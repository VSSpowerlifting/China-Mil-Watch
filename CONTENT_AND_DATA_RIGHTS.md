# Content and data rights

This repository holds several different kinds of material, and they are **not**
under the same terms. One licence file covering all of them would be wrong in
both directions: it would give away rights that are not this project's to give,
and it would claim rights over public facts that nobody owns.

This document says which is which.

---

## 1. Software — MIT

All source code in this repository is licensed under the MIT License. The full
grant is in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE); it is the canonical OSI text, unmodified.

This covers the collectors, adapters, pipeline, renderers, migrations, tests,
scripts and tooling — the machinery. Copyright (c) 2026 Benjamin Yang.

## 2. Original editorial work — all rights reserved

Benjamin Yang's original editorial analysis, issue text, site copy, and original
visual and editorial material remain **copyright Benjamin Yang, all rights
reserved**, unless a specific item is separately marked otherwise.

That includes the written analysis in The PLA Watch editions, executive
readouts, editorial framing and commentary, and original imagery and design
work produced for the publication.

**The MIT licence on the software does not relicense any of it.** Someone who
takes the code under MIT takes the machinery, not the journalism.

## 3. Official source documents — the originating institution's terms

Official source documents, quotations, images and other third-party material
collected or referenced by this project remain subject to the rights, licences
and terms of the institutions that published them — defence ministries, joint
staffs, commands and their equivalents.

This project does not acquire, transfer or sublicense any right in that
material. It links to and quotes from official publications; it does not
relicense them. Anyone reusing a collected document is dealing with the
originating institution's terms, not with this repository's.

**Nothing here is a claim that government or ministry material is owned by this
project.** It is not.

## 4. Underlying facts — owned by nobody

Indo-Pacific Record claims no ownership over the underlying public facts: that
an exercise occurred, that a vessel transited, that an official said a thing on
a date. Facts are not copyrightable and this project does not pretend otherwise.

What is protectable is the expression — the selection, arrangement, translation
choices and written analysis — not the events.

## 5. Corpus metadata and provenance

Corpus metadata and provenance records — canonical URLs, publication dates,
issuing institution, content hashes, collection-run evidence and extraction
status — are provided for **research, verification and reference**.

They exist so a reader can check the record rather than trust it. Reuse for
verification, citation and research is the purpose they were built for.

Note that metadata describing a document is not the document. Metadata being
available for reference does not carry any right to the underlying official
text, which stays under §3.

## 6. What the MIT licence does not silently do

Stated plainly, because this is the confusion the document exists to prevent:

* it does **not** relicense collected official texts (§3)
* it does **not** relicense original editorial publications (§2)
* it does **not** transfer rights in third-party images or quotations (§3)
* it does **not** convert the corpus into a public-domain dataset (§3, §5)

The MIT grant applies to the software in this repository. Nothing more.
