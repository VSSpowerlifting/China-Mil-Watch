China Coast Guard spokesman Jiang Lue issued a statement Thursday warning the Philippines to stop 'infringing provocations and false narratives' after Philippine Coast Guard vessel 4409 approached Ren'ai Reef to evacuate an injured crew member from the grounded BRP Sierra Madre, which Beijing allowed via small boat transfer on humanitarian grounds. The statement reasserts China's standard sovereignty claim over the Nansha Islands and Ren'ai Reef while framing Manila's public account of the incident as deliberate disinformation — a pattern China Coast Guard has used consistently to contest the Philippine narrative in the ongoing resupply standoff. Stored source text

Source record

Text captured from the source page. Extraction may omit material or include unrelated page elements; consult the original URL when exact wording or completeness matters.

China Coast Guard has warned the Philippines to cease conducting infringing provocations and stop making false narratives, its spokesman said in a statement on Thursday night. Jiang Lue said Philippine Coast Guard vessel 4409 approached waters near Ren'ai Reef to collect an injured crew member from an illegally "grounded" ship there. For humanitarian reasons, China promptly allowed the Philippines to use small boats for the transfer. China Coast Guard handled the situation in accordance with the law and provided necessary assistance, Jiang said. "However, the statements from the Philippine side disregard the facts and distort the truth, attempting to mislead public opinion," he said. "China holds indisputable sovereignty over the Nansha Islands, including Ren'ai Reef, and the surrounding waters. We sternly warn the Philippine side to immediately cease their infringing provocations and false narratives."
