Toward the Centenary · Military Journalists at the Frontier | May: Threading Through the Sweltering, Treacherous Dense Forest

向百年·军事记者走边关丨五月，穿梭在湿热艰险的密林里
PLA Daily (解放军报) 31 May 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A PLA media team embedded with the Shili Village Border Defense Company, a brigade of the Southern Theater Command Army (Unit 31640), documenting a foot patrol to Border Marker No. 72 through the Ailao Mountains of Yunnan — a route that requires hours of off-road movement through dense primary forest with no vehicle access. The piece is standard political work content from the 'Toward the Centenary' series; its value is as a record of how official military media frames frontier hardship, generational loyalty, and the 最可爱的人 ('most beloved people') trope for a domestic audience, not as evidence of changed posture or capability along the southwestern border.
Translation

Reporter's Notes　In May, the southwestern frontier is humid, stifling, and lush green in every direction. The dense forests of the Ailao Mountains layer upon layer, branches and vines intertwined, vegetation sprawling in every direction; the cries of cicadas weave together with the sound of wind, and mist drifts through the mountain passes.

At dawn, I followed the patrol detachment of the Shili Village Border Defense Company, a certain brigade of the Southern Theater Command Army, as it advanced toward Border Marker No. 72. It was my first time setting foot on this border patrol route. Before departure, officers and soldiers donned their gear and formed up in neat ranks. At a single command from Company Commander Du Jiqilin, the search element, command element, and reserve element moved as one. Watching their swift and decisive bearing, I felt a deepening sense of awe at the trek ahead.

The patrol vehicles wound their way along a serpentine mountain road; once they reached the foot of the mountain, there was no road left. The remainder of the patrol had to be completed entirely on foot through primeval dense forest.

Thick vines spread unchecked and blocked the way ahead; rain-carved paths varied in depth and were muddy and slippery; rushing streams invariably lay across the route; unidentified snakes and insects startled us at every turn — the dense forest was fraught with danger at every step. Throughout the hours-long trek, the sound of labored breathing, the steady beat of footsteps, and the warnings called out between comrades echoed through the deep mountain forest.

This border patrol route is a dual test of the officers' and soldiers' physical endurance and willpower. Every step forward requires them to contend with hidden dangers.

As the point man of the formation, Squad Leader Yang Guangkai kept his eyes fixed on the state of the march the entire way, reminding the new recruits behind him to watch their footing on the slippery ground, stay alert to surrounding hazards, and maintain proper spacing in the column.

Amid the perilous mountain forest, there is one ordinary fruit tree — his and his wife's love tree.

During a patrol several years ago, Squad Leader Yang happened upon a young woman who had come into the mountains to pick fruit and had accidentally sprained her ankle. One act of assistance planted the seed of a bond. With the dense forest as matchmaker and the mountains and rivers as witness, an encounter on the patrol route grew into mutual understanding and then into a life together, adding a touch of human warmth to the years of frontier duty and making the solitary vigil at the deep-mountain border post no longer lonely.

Among the patrol party, Fan Dingxin — a new recruit who had enlisted just six months earlier — was the youngest soldier on this patrol route. Traveling more than 2,200 kilometers from Henan to Yunnan, he resolutely answered the call of the frontier. When asked about his original motivation, the young man's gaze was clear and resolute.

Fan Dingxin's father is a veteran. Raised from childhood under his father's influence, he loved military life and longed for the uniform. After enlisting, he has always kept his father's admonition in mind: "Talk less, do more; fear no hardship; be willing to take the short end." In his heart, the hard places and the exhausting places are precisely the good places to build merit and accomplish something.

After hours of trekking, we finally reached Border Marker No. 72. All officers and soldiers formed up and stood at solemn attention, facing the national flag and taking a grave oath. "The border has me here — the motherland and the people may rest assured…"

The oath rang out with resounding force, echoing through the forested mountains and rivers. The wilderness speaks not, yet the mountains and rivers bear witness: generation after generation of border defense officers and soldiers, using youth as their brush and steadfast duty as their ink, continue to write their answer of loyalty along the motherland's border line.

"The mountains know me, the rivers know me; the motherland will not forget, will not forget me…" As dusk gradually fell, the last rays of the setting sun gilded the rolling peaks with a golden edge, and the officers and soldiers set out on the return journey, singing aloud. I walked in the middle of the patrol column, listening to the hoarse yet powerful singing of the soldiers around me, gazing at the silent mountains and dense forest in the gathering dusk, and found myself softly humming along. As I hummed, a pang rose at the tip of my nose, and tears welled in my eyes.

Though not many people know their names, and not many have seen their young faces, they are here nonetheless. In this sweltering dense forest, on this lonely frontier, they walk and stand watch day after day — so that we may live in peace and sleep soundly.

There is no such thing as a tranquil and blessed life that simply exists on its own; it only exists because someone is carrying the burden on our behalf (哪有什么岁月静好，不过是有人替我们负重前行).

On the gentle slope beside the guard post stands a border-defense sculpture, built by veteran soldiers for the company. On its weathered stone base are engraved the names of generation after generation of officers and soldiers. Beside the sculpture, bougainvillea blooms in the wind —

Its symbolic meaning is "indomitable perseverance, passionate sincerity, and silent watchfulness (坚韧不拔、热情赤诚、默默守望)" — precisely like these frontier sentinels rooted in the southwestern borderland, who neither fear the humid heat and barren conditions nor shrink from the perils of the deep mountains, who answer their mission with their lives and guard the mountains and rivers with their hearts, silently blooming a flower of loyalty along the border line.

During my days with the Shili Village Border Defense Company, I was with the officers and soldiers morning and night — eating together, living together, training together, finding joy together — immersed in the daily life of frontier duty. Their eyes are clear and pure; their hearts are sincere and burning with passion… These plain-spoken and fearless sons and daughters of the people use their steadfast watch to fortify the gateway of the nation, and use their devotion to guard the lights of ten thousand homes. They are, without question, the most beloved people (最可爱的人) of the new era.

This May, threading through the sweltering, treacherous dense forest of the frontier, I encountered the most unspoiled youth, the most moving steadfastness, and the most fervent love of country and home (家国情怀).

记者手记 五月的西南边陲，潮湿闷热、满目葱茏。哀牢山脉的密林层叠，枝蔓交错、草木横生，蝉鸣与风声交织，雾气萦绕山间。 清晨，我跟随南部战区陆军某旅十里村边防连巡逻分队向着72号界碑挺进。这是我第一次踏上这条边境巡逻路。临行前，官兵们携带装具，整齐列队。连长杜吉七林一声令下，搜索组、指挥组、预备组闻令而动。看着大家雷厉风行的作风，我对即将到来的跋涉，多了几分敬畏。 巡逻车沿着蜿蜒山路盘旋前行，抵达山脚后便再无通路。余下的巡逻路程，全部需要徒步穿越原始密林。 茂密的藤蔓肆意蔓延遮挡前路，雨水冲刷形成的小径深浅不一、泥泞湿滑，湍急的溪流总是横在前路，不知名的蛇虫不时惊扰……密林之中险象环生。数小时的跋涉过程中，沉重的喘息声、坚定的脚步声、战友间的提醒声，回荡在幽深山林。 这条边境巡逻路，是对官兵体能与意志的双重考验。官兵的每一次前行，都要与潜藏的危险周旋。 作为队伍的排头兵，班长杨光恺全程紧盯行进状态，提醒身后新兵注意脚下湿滑、警惕周边风险、保持队伍间距。 惊险的山林间，有一棵普通的果树，那是他和妻子的爱情树。 几年前的一次巡逻途中，杨班长偶遇一名进山摘果、意外崴脚的女生。一次出手相助，埋下了缘分的种子。密林为媒，山河为证，巡逻路上的相遇、相知直至相守，让戍边岁月多了一份人间温情，让深山边关的坚守不再孤单。 队伍里，半年前刚入伍的新兵范鼎昕，是这次巡逻路上最年轻的兵。从河南远赴云南，跨越两千两百多公里，他毅然奔赴边关。被问及初心时，少年的眼神清澈而坚定。 范鼎昕的父亲是一名退伍军人。从小受父亲熏陶，他热爱军旅、向往戎装，入伍后始终铭记父亲“少说多干、不怕吃苦、甘于吃亏”的叮嘱。在他心中，苦地方、累地方，才是建功立业的好地方。 经过数小时跋涉，我们终于来到了72号界碑。全体官兵列队肃立，面向国旗庄严宣誓。“边防有我在，请祖国和人民放心……” 誓言铿锵，回荡在密林山河之间。山野无言、山河作证，一代代边防官兵，以青春为笔、坚守为墨，在祖国的边境线上不断续写忠诚答卷。 “山知道我，江河知道我，祖国不会忘记，不会忘记我……”暮色渐临，夕阳余晖为连绵的群山镀上金边，官兵们踏上返程的路途，放声唱起了歌谣。我走在巡逻队伍中间，听着身边官兵们沙哑却有力的歌声，望着暮色中沉默的群山和密林，也跟着轻轻哼唱起来。唱着唱着，鼻尖一酸，泪水在我的眼眶里打转。 尽管没有多少人知晓他们的名字，没有多少人看过他们年轻的面孔，可他们依然在这里。在这样湿热的密林里，在这样寂寞的边关上，他们日复一日地走着、守着，为的是让我们安心地生活、安稳地睡去。 哪有什么岁月静好，不过是有人替我们负重前行。 哨位旁的平缓坡地上，矗立着一座戍边雕塑，是老兵们为连队修建的。斑驳的石质基座上，镌刻着一代代官兵的姓名。雕塑旁，三角梅迎风盛放—— 它的花语是“坚韧不拔、热情赤诚、默默守望”，恰如这群扎根西南边陲的戍边人，不惧湿热贫瘠、不畏深山艰险，以生命赴使命、以寸心护山河，在边境线默默绽放着忠诚之花。 在十里村边防连的这段日子，我与官兵朝夕相伴，同吃同住、同训同乐，沉浸感受着戍边日常。他们的眼神清澈纯粹，他们的内心赤诚滚烫……这群质朴无畏的子弟兵，用坚守筑牢国门屏障，用热爱守护万家灯火。他们，当之无愧是新时代最可爱的人。 这个五月，我穿梭在湿热艰险的边关密林，遇见了最纯粹的青春、最动人的坚守、最滚烫的家国情怀。 策划：柳刚、李砺寒 监制：龙文志、贺方林 统筹：杨明博、马鹏远、姚成博 记者：韩启扬、王慧 编导：王慧、韩启扬 撰稿：韩启扬、王慧 摄像：韩启扬、梁通、罗显升 剪辑：罗显升、梁通 后期：王慧、罗显升 设计：梁通 协调：秦庆凡、张富康、曹继可、刘萱玮 鸣谢 南部战区陆军 中国人民解放军31640部队
