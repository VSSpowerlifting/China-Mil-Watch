Dog Days Outpost Visit | Emerald Sea, Lonely Island: Agency Cadres Move to the Front to Solve Problems

三伏探哨丨碧海孤岛：机关干部靠前解难
PLA Daily (解放军报) 9 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Supply section chief Bu Di of an unidentified PLA Air Force radar unit conducted an embedded inspection (蹲点) at a remote island radar station during the current dog-days heat, documenting welfare problems—stranded family members, school enrollment difficulties, parent-child separation—and driving the unit's Party committee to establish a formal 'family visit transit station' (家属来队中转站) mechanism covering transport, accommodation, and meals. The article documents the institutional problem the PLA calls 'passing items between departments until they come to nothing,' and presents the new 'rigid mechanism' (刚性机制) requiring inspecting cadres to return with a prioritized problem list and jointly implement solutions with relevant departments as the fix. The framing—anchored in a 1958 precedent of Air Force Deputy Commander Cheng Jun serving as a common soldier and the station's founding figure Fang Heyi—points to this as political work content reinforcing the 'grassroots first, soldiers first' (基层至上、士兵第一) line, making the article useful as a record of how the institution frames agency-grassroots relations rather than as evidence of a capability or posture change.

A Reporter Visits a Radar Station of a Certain Air Force Unit——

Emerald Sea, Lonely Island: Agency Cadres Move to the Front to Solve Problems

■ Li Zihao, PLA Daily Reporter Zhang Dongpan Illustration: Fang Han

Since the onset of the dog days (入伏), temperatures have surged sharply.

Early in the morning, the reporter boarded a vessel at the dock and, after nearly five hours of rough sailing, finally made out the faint outline of an island on the horizon — the garrison of a radar station belonging to a certain Air Force unit. Traveling with the reporter were Li Shuang, wife of radar station cadre Deng Junsheng, their daughter Yingying, and Bu Di, chief of the supply section of the unit, who was on the island for an embedded inspection (蹲点).

At noon, the boat came ashore. Deng Junsheng, who had been waiting for some time, came running over, took the luggage from his wife's hands, held his daughter's hand, and placed a carefully prepared gift into her hands.

"What looks like an ordinary family visit is extraordinarily precious to the officers and soldiers on the island." Watching the family of three reunite happily before him, Bu Di recounted for the reporter the experience of Li Shuang's first family visit two years earlier.

That summer, Li Shuang set out from her hometown in Tianjin, transferring through multiple modes of transportation over a long and arduous journey before finally reaching the port of departure for the island. Unexpectedly, a sudden change in weather at sea caused wave heights to reach the threshold for a navigation ban, and all ferry services were suspended. Li Shuang had no choice but to find a guesthouse near the port and wait for ferry service to resume. By the time she finally made it to the island, more than half of her leave had passed, and the braised pork she had made by hand for her husband had already spoiled.

At the time, Bu Di happened to be on the island for an embedded inspection. Upon learning of this, he wrote his own reflection in his inspection diary: "What can I do for this island? What can I do for the officers and soldiers on this island?"

[Caption] Officers and soldiers observe air conditions. Photo by Hou Jun.

In conversation, the reporter learned that Bu Di had previously served as the commander of another island radar station and knew firsthand the hardships of island garrison duty. During his embedded inspection, he slept in the squad and platoon barracks, ate in the company mess hall, and through daily life alongside the officers and soldiers, came to understand clearly the real difficulties they faced in work and life.

"Quite a few officers and soldiers on the island have experienced family members getting stranded by extreme weather during visits." Recounting this experience, Bu Di told the reporter: "The difficulties that officers and soldiers are too embarrassed to raise are precisely the important matters that the agency (机关) must make every effort to resolve."

During the inspection, Bu Di covered every squad, platoon, and fighting position on the island, broadly gathering the various problems officers and soldiers faced: family members enduring long transit times during visits, with large expenses incurred from being stranded during navigation bans; difficulties coordinating children's school enrollment; the impact of living apart in two places on parent-child relationships… His inspection diary was filled densely with the difficulties and demands of officers and soldiers, annotated with varying numbers of stars according to priority, with the corners of several pages deliberately folded over.

When that inspection concluded and he returned to the agency, Bu Di immediately reported his research findings to the Party committee. After deliberation and review by the Party committee leadership, a support mechanism for a "family visit transit station" (家属来队中转站) was quickly introduced — covering everything from transportation pickup to transit accommodation, from meal support to itinerary coordination, all centrally arranged by the agency. In response to the reality of unpredictable island weather and frequent navigation suspensions, they began compiling weekly plans for the following week's family visits; for any family members unable to reach the island on time due to transit delays or holdups, the agency would uniformly arrange food and lodging.

"Officers and soldiers have sacrificed so much to garrison this island — it is our responsibility to resolve their difficulties." Following Bu Di's footsteps, the reporter arrived at the radar station's honor room. Upon entering, a yellowed photograph on the wall caught the reporter's attention —

In the photograph, a soldier grips a shovel tightly, his gaze resolute.

"This is the radar station's old company commander, Fang Heyi, who was once received by Chairman Mao." Pointing to the photograph, Bu Di told the reporter the old company commander's story.

In the early days of the radar station's construction, the mess hall was situated halfway up a hillside backed by a sheer cliff face. One misstep while transporting supplies could mean falling into the sea. Fang Heyi, who was then the cook squad leader, used his rest time every day to haul stones, lay gravel, and build a path. Two months later, the path was finally widened, and he then set about planning an activity ground for the officers and soldiers — he would not rest even as his hands became covered in blisters. Inspired by Fang Heyi, the company's officers and soldiers worked together, and over the course of a year leveled an activity ground at the edge of the cliff face. After becoming company commander, he took the lead in opening up wasteland, improving facilities, and doing everything in his power to improve conditions on the island garrison, earning him the affectionate nickname "Island Foolish Old Man" (海岛愚公).

"What our predecessors had in their minds was not how to rack up achievements, but how to build the company well and guard the island well." Bu Di said with feeling. "Faced with harsh conditions, it was not only ordinary officers and soldiers — even generals of high rank did not seek special treatment."

In 1958, Chairman Mao called on all senior cadres of the armed forces to go down to the companies to serve as soldiers. Cheng Jun, then Deputy Commander of the Air Force, voluntarily went to a radar company on a certain island with harsh conditions, eating, living, and training together with the soldiers: standing guard in winter, he proactively took the coldest late-night shifts; caring for the officers and soldiers, he conducted extensive research into the various difficulties facing work and training on the island… After his soldier-tempering period ended, Cheng Jun faithfully reported on conditions at the grassroots level and actively pushed for continuous improvements to island logistics support, setting a benchmark for subsequent leading cadres going down to companies to serve as soldiers.

Leaving the honor room, it was nearly evening. The sky suddenly filled with dark clouds, a fierce wind rose, and pellets of rain came crashing down. The reporter followed Bu Di as they hurried toward the position. The bluestone steps on the mountain path were soaked by rain and extremely slippery, and Bu Di nearly fell several times.

"A typhoon is coming — we must immediately take protective measures for the equipment!" After reaching the position, Bu Di, heedless of being soaked through, stepped forward quickly and worked together with the officers and soldiers to secure equipment, protect devices, and reinforce the radar antenna.

Listening to the sound of the pouring rain, the reporter thought of Li Shuang and her daughter and decided to go check on them at the family quarters. Pushing open the door, the room was clean and tidy, with furniture and appliances all in place. Comparing her two visits to the island, Li Shuang's expression relaxed: "I really didn't expect that in just a few short years, the unit would have introduced so many heartwarming measures for military families."

"In the past, when cadres came for embedded inspections and encountered matters outside their own area of responsibility, most would simply note them down and bring them back, and many items would get passed around between departments and then come to nothing." Zhang, the radar station's political instructor, who was accompanying the reporter, said: "Now, the agency has established a rigid mechanism (刚性机制) requiring cadres to go down to the front lines to ascertain the real situation and compile a list of problems, then immediately upon returning to jointly formulate countermeasures with relevant departments and implement them item by item according to division of responsibilities."

The next morning, the reporter stood at the edge of the activity ground that Fang Heyi had led the officers and soldiers to build all those years ago, watching the surging, unceasing waves before him, as if hearing in his ears the resounding melody of the radar station's unit song: "Enemy conditions are in our eyes — like steel nails we hold fast at the red forward outpost (红色前哨)…"

Always "Sitting on the Same Bench" (坐在一条板凳上) with Officers and Soldiers

■ Zhang Dongpan

A deputy commander eating, living, and training together with soldiers, proactively taking the coldest late-night guard shifts; an old company commander using his rest time to build roads for officers and soldiers, taking the lead in opening up wasteland and improving facilities; agency cadres going down to the front lines to learn of soldiers' conditions and resolve soldiers' difficulties, handling every practical matter in a way that reaches the hearts of officers and soldiers… The times are different, the positions are different, the methods are different — but the core of the spirit has always been the same: always "sitting on the same bench" (坐在一条板凳上) with officers and soldiers.

Going to the island to report, the reporter heard one story after another about reunion. Many of the difficulties that had plagued generation after generation of island garrison officers and soldiers have been effectively resolved with the help of increasingly refined support mechanisms, so that visiting family members no longer gaze at the sea and sigh. Behind these moving changes lies the fervent original aspiration (初心) of agencies at all levels whose hearts are tied to the grassroots, and the sincere care of agency cadres whose warmth reaches the officers and soldiers. It is precisely because they have always treated soldiers as brothers and military families as their own kin that officers and soldiers have the soaring morale and steel-like conviction to "hold fast like steel nails at the red forward outpost."

Where the heart is at peace, that is home. Looking back on the history of the radar station's construction, the old generation of island garrison personnel took root on a barren island, with cadres at all levels taking the lead in enduring the greatest hardships and doing the most exhausting work, laying a solid foundation for guarding the red forward outpost; the new generation of builders carries on and opens up new ground, with cadres at all levels always keeping the welfare of soldiers in their hearts, working together with soldiers to overcome difficulties and tackle hard problems. Within this fine tradition of "grassroots first, soldiers first" (基层至上、士兵第一) lies the complete answer to why the People's Army has been able to march from victory to victory.

记者探访空军某部某雷达站—— 碧海孤岛：机关干部靠前解难 ■李子浩 解放军报记者 张东盼 插图：方 汉 入伏以来，气温骤升。 一大早，记者从码头登船，颠簸近5个小时，终于隐约看见一座海岛的轮廓——那是空军某部某雷达站驻地。与记者同行的，还有雷达站干部邓俊生的妻子李爽、女儿莹莹，以及上岛蹲点的该部供应科科长卜迪。 正午时分，船靠岸边。等候已久的邓俊生快步跑来，一边接过妻子手中的行李，一边牵起女儿的手，把精心准备的礼物放到女儿手上。 “看似寻常的家属来队，对岛上官兵来说却异常珍贵。”看着眼前幸福团聚的一家三口，卜迪为记者讲起两年前李爽第一次来队探亲时的经历。 那年夏天，李爽从天津老家启程，一路辗转换乘多种交通工具，几经奔波才抵达登岛出发港口。不料，因海上天气突变，浪高达到禁航标准，轮渡全部停航。李爽只好在港口附近找旅店住下，盼着轮渡航线尽快恢复。直到假期过了大半，亲手为爱人制作的酱肉已经变质，李爽才终于登岛。 当时，卜迪正好在岛上蹲点。得知此事，他在蹲点日记中写下自己的反思：“我能为这个岛做些什么？能为岛上的官兵做些什么？” 官兵观察空情。侯 俊摄 记者在交谈中了解到，卜迪曾担任另一个海岛雷达站站长，深知守岛官兵的艰辛不易。蹲点的日子里，他睡在班排宿舍，吃在连队食堂，在与官兵朝夕相处中，把他们工作生活中的现实困难摸了个一清二楚。 “家属探亲赶上极端天气的情况，岛上不少官兵都经历过。”谈及这段经历，卜迪告诉记者，“官兵不好意思提的难处，正是机关应着力解决的要事。” 蹲点期间，卜迪跑遍岛上的每个班排、每个战位，广泛搜集官兵面临的种种难题：家属来队中转时间长，停航滞留开销大；孩子入学协调困难，两地分居影响亲子关系……他的蹲点日记里，密密麻麻记满了官兵的困难诉求，并按轻重缓急标注了不同数量的五角星，其中几页还特意折了一个角。 那次蹲点结束，回到机关，卜迪第一时间向党委汇报调研情况。党委一班人研究论证后，很快出台“家属来队中转站”保障机制——来队家属从交通接送到中转住宿，从餐饮保障到行程协调，全部由机关统筹安排。针对海岛天气多变、容易停航滞留的实际情况，他们每周统计下周家属来队计划，凡是因中转、延误无法及时登岛的，由机关统一安排食宿。 “官兵为守岛付出了太多，我们有责任把他们的难题解决好。”跟随卜迪的脚步，记者来到雷达站荣誉室。一进门，墙上一张泛黄的照片引起了记者的注意—— 照片上，一名军人紧握铁锹，目光坚毅。 “这是雷达站的老连长方和义，曾经受到毛主席接见。”指着照片，卜迪为记者讲起老连长的故事。 雷达站建设初期，食堂坐落于背靠陡峭崖壁的半山腰，运送物资时稍不留神，便有失足坠海的危险。当时还是炊事班班长的方和义，每天利用休息时间运石、铺渣、修路。两个月后，小路终于拓宽了，他又筹划着为官兵开辟一个活动场地，双手布满血泡也不肯歇一歇。在方和义的带动下，连队官兵齐心协力，历时一年在崖壁边平整出一块活动场地。担任连长后，他又带头开荒造地、完善设施，全力改善驻岛条件，被大家亲切地称作“海岛愚公”。 “先辈们脑子里装的不是怎么出政绩，而是怎么把连队建好、把海岛守好。”卜迪感慨地说，“面对艰苦条件，不仅普通官兵，身居高位的将军也不搞特殊。” 1958年，毛主席号召全军高级干部下连当兵。时任空军副司令员成钧主动奔赴条件艰苦的某海岛雷达连，与战士们同吃同住同训练：冬日站哨，他主动值守深夜最冷时段；关心官兵，他广泛调研岛上工作训练面临的各种难题……当兵锻炼结束后，成钧如实反映基层情况，积极推动海岛后勤保障不断完善，为后续领导干部下连当兵立起标杆。 走出荣誉室，已近傍晚。天空突然乌云密布，狂风骤起，豆大的雨点劈头盖脸地砸下来。记者跟随卜迪疾步朝阵地跑去。山路上的青石台阶被雨水打湿，格外湿滑难行，卜迪几次险些摔倒。 “台风要来了，得立即做好装备防护！”赶到阵地后，卜迪顾不上全身被雨水淋透，快步上前与官兵一起紧固器材、防护设备、加固雷达天线。 听着哗哗的雨声，记者惦记李爽母女，决定去家属院看看。推开房门，屋里干净整洁，家具家电一应俱全。谈及两次登岛的对比，李爽眉眼舒展：“真没想到，短短几年，部队为军属推出了这么多暖心措施。” “以前干部蹲点，遇上不是自己业务口的事，大多只是记下来、带回去，不少事项在各部门间转来转去就没了下文。”同行的雷达站张指导员告诉记者，“如今，机关建立了刚性机制，要求干部下沉一线摸清实情、梳理问题清单，返回后立即联合相关部门制订对策，再按职责分工逐项落实。” 第二天清晨，记者站在当年方和义带领官兵建起的那块活动场地边，看着眼前奔涌不息的海浪，耳畔仿佛响起雷达站站歌的铿锵旋律：“敌情在我眼中，像钢钉坚守在红色前哨……” 始终同官兵“坐在一条板凳上” ■张东盼 副司令员与战士同吃同住同训练，站哨主动值守深夜最冷的时段；老连长利用休息时间为官兵修路，带头开荒造地、完善设施；机关干部下沉一线察兵情、解兵难，把件件实事办到官兵心坎上……年代不同、职务不同、方式不同，但精神的内核始终如一：始终同官兵“坐在一条板凳上”。 登岛采访，记者听到一个个关于团聚的故事。曾困扰一代代守岛官兵的很多难题，在日益完善的保障机制助力下有效破解，让探亲家属不再望海兴叹。感人变化的背后，是各级机关心系基层的火热初心，是机关干部情暖官兵的真挚关爱。正是因为他们始终把战士当兄弟、把军属当亲人，才有了官兵“像钢钉坚守在红色前哨”的士气如虹、信念如钢。 此心安处是吾乡。回望雷达站的建设历程，老一辈守岛人扎根荒岛，各级干部带头吃最大的苦、干最累的活，为守护红色前哨打下了坚实基础；新一代建设者继往开来，各级干部始终把战士的冷暖放在心上，与战士同心克难、合力攻坚。这份“基层至上、士兵第一”的优良传统里，藏着人民军队为什么能够从胜利走向胜利的全部答案。
