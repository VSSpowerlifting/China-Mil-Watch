Stationed Year-Round in the Desert, He Uses a Paintbrush to Tell the Stories of This Land

常年驻守大漠，他用画笔绘出那些发生在这片土地的故事
PLA Daily (解放军报) 6 June 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Staff Sergeant Second Class Zhou Zhijie, assigned to a unit under the Xinjiang Military Command on the edge of the Taklimakan Desert, painted a compound wall mural depicting garrison life as part of a unit-level cultural-construction initiative (文化建设活动); the article also profiles Corporal Liang Ziliang, who won the unit's 'Desert Vanguard' professional skills competition after training specifically to control racing drones in high-wind, blowing-sand conditions. This is standard political-work content — the dual profile format (artist soldier plus technical achiever) documents how the unit frames perseverance and local identity as morale resources for troops in an austere border posting. Its value is as a record of grassroots cultural-work methods in a Xinjiang garrison, not as evidence of a capability or posture change; the drone-racing competition detail does confirm that small-unit drone operator training is routine enough to be featured in a morale narrative rather than a capability announcement.
Translation

The Desert Beneath the Paintbrush ■ Li Quanjun, Liu Changwei

Soldiers of a unit under the Xinjiang Military Command sit gathered beneath a poplar tree, sharing what is on their minds. Photo by PLA Daily special correspondent Wang Junqiang.

A fierce wind sweeps across the banks of the Tarim River, lashing the gnarled branches of the desert poplars and lifting a murky yellow cloud of dust and sand. A sentry stands facing into the wind, eyes sharp and fixed on the distance… This is the scene Staff Sergeant Second Class Zhou Zhijie, of a unit under the Xinjiang Military Command, painted on the wall of the garrison compound.

Not long ago, the unit launched a cultural-construction initiative, and Zhou Zhijie volunteered to take on the wall-mural assignment. Having been stationed in the desert year-round, he holds deep affection for every grain of sand and stone, every blade of grass and leaf here. This time, he decided to draw on what was right at hand—gathering stones of every shape, scooping up fine yellow sand, and collecting branches and leaves from the desert poplars—and began creating with the desert's distinctive yellow as his dominant tone.

As the brush moved lightly across the wall, memories accumulated over many years flickered in and out of Zhou Zhijie's mind.

The unit is stationed on the edge of the Taklimakan Desert. Many years ago, Zhou Zhijie came to the unit from the humid southern slopes of the Qinling Mountains and was hit hard by the dry climate and raging sandstorms. "My nasal passages kept drying out, and at night I tossed and turned, unable to sleep." The most unbearable thing was being caught in a sudden sandstorm while on duty—visibility dropped to nothing, and in no more than a few dozen seconds, sand had worked its way into every part of his body.

Not long after, the unit joined local residents to plant trees along the desert's edge. That day the wind was still blowing hard, strong enough to force people's eyes shut. Officers, soldiers, and villagers gripped shovels and cradled seedlings, laboring with their heads down in the swirling yellow sand.

Watching everyone working together as one, then gazing toward the golden desert poplars in the distance, Zhou Zhijie felt a sudden clarity in his heart: "Generation after generation, the border guards have stood firm—no matter how fierce the sandstorm, they do not retreat." That day, Zhou Zhijie—who had always loved painting—picked up his sketchbook and captured the scene of soldiers and civilians planting trees together to hold back the desert, stroke by stroke.

Gradually, Zhou Zhijie began fixing more of the stories around him onto the page. His most recent painting is titled "Taking the Championship." The backdrop is still the desert with its rolling yellow sand; the subject is his comrade Liang Ziliang.

Last winter, the unit held a drone-racing simulation competition in the heart of the desert. Corporal Liang Ziliang had devoted himself to studying racing drones for a long time and had hoped to show what he could do at the competition—but an accident occurred while he was acquiring a target. The drone's flight path was thrown off by erratic wind directions, and it crashed straight into a branch of a desert poplar.

Watching the equipment fall, Liang Ziliang was filled with regret. The judge walked over and said quietly, "The weather was terrible—mistakes were inevitable." Liang Ziliang shook his head and clenched his fist: "My skills are just not strong enough."

The defeat did not bring Liang Ziliang down. From that point on, in order to master the techniques for controlling equipment in sandy, windy conditions, he deliberately chose to train in bad weather. When fierce winds made the drone's body shake, he drilled repeatedly on stabilization techniques; when blowing sand blocked his line of sight, he familiarized himself over and over with the terrain of the poplar groves and practiced blind operation and target identification.

Through the bone-chilling cold winds of deep winter and the scorching sun of the height of summer, day after day of persistence made Liang Ziliang's skills ever more refined. Last month, the unit organized the "Desert Vanguard" professional skills competition. Liang Ziliang, stepping onto the field again, was calm and composed, controlling the racing drone in steady flight. In the end, he successfully completed every assessed event and took the championship in one stroke.

The wind still howls and races across the boundless desert. In front of the wall mural Zhou Zhijie created, his comrades spontaneously broke into applause. In the eyes of the officers and soldiers guarding the border, the deep and pale yellows of this land are no longer a monotonous backdrop, but a magnificent ground color soaked through with loyalty, struggle, and glory.

画笔下的大漠 ■李泉军 刘昌炜 新疆军区某部战士围坐在胡杨树下畅谈心声。解放军报特约记者 王军强摄 狂风掠过塔里木河沿岸，抽打着胡杨苍劲的枝干，掀起一片昏黄的尘沙，哨兵迎风而立，目光炯炯凝望远方……新疆军区某部二级上士周志杰在营院的墙壁上画下这样一幅景象。 不久前，营区开展文化建设工作，周志杰主动揽下墙面绘画任务。常年驻守大漠，他对这里的一沙一石、一草一木饱含深情。这一次，他决定就地取材——捡拾形态各异的石头，捧来细腻黄沙还有胡杨枝叶，以大漠独有的黄色为主调开始创作。 画笔轻轻移动，多年来的记忆在周志杰的脑海中若隐若现。 单位驻守于塔克拉玛干沙漠边缘，多年前，周志杰从湿润的秦岭南麓来到部队，被干燥的气候和肆虐的风沙冲击，“鼻腔一阵阵发干，夜晚翻来覆去难以入眠”。最让人难熬的是执勤中遭遇突如其来的沙尘暴，什么也看不清，不过几十秒时间，身上每个地方都钻满黄沙。 不久后，单位联合驻地群众在沙漠边缘植树。那天依旧刮着大风，吹得人睁不开眼。官兵和村民握着铁锹、捧着幼苗，在漫天黄沙中埋头劳作。 看着大家齐心协力的身影，再望向远处金黄的胡杨，周志杰心头豁然开朗：“一代代戍边人坚强挺立，哪怕风沙再大也不退缩。”那天，喜欢画画的周志杰拿起画册，一笔一画将军民携手植树治沙的场景描摹下来。 渐渐地，周志杰将更多身边的故事定格在纸页之中。最近的一幅画作，名字叫作《夺冠》，背景仍然是黄沙漫卷的大漠，主角则是战友梁子良。 去年冬天，该部在沙漠腹地开展穿越机模拟比武。中士梁子良潜心钻研穿越机许久，本想借着比武一展身手，却在查证目标时出了意外。穿越机被杂乱的风向扰乱了飞行轨迹，一下子撞上胡杨树枝。 看着坠落的设备，梁子良满心遗憾。裁判走到他身旁轻声说道：“天气太差，失误在所难免。”梁子良摇了摇头，握紧拳头：“是我本领还不够强。” 比武失利没有让梁子良消沉。自那以后，为了掌握风沙环境下的设备操控技巧，他专挑恶劣天气训练。狂风刮得机身摇晃，他反复练习稳控技巧；黄沙遮挡视线，他一遍遍熟悉胡杨林地形，练习盲操与目标识别。 寒冬腊月冷风刺骨，盛夏酷暑烈日灼人，日复一日的坚持，让梁子良的技术愈发精湛。上个月，单位组织“大漠尖兵”专业比武，再次站上赛场的梁子良沉着冷静，操控穿越机平稳飞行。最终，他顺利完成全部考核课目，一举夺冠。 风，依旧在无垠大漠中呼啸穿行。在周志杰创作的墙画前，战友们不约而同鼓起了掌。在戍边官兵眼中，这片土地深深浅浅的黄，早已不是单调的背景，而是浸透着忠诚、奋斗与荣光的壮美底色。 大漠中的哨兵。解放军报特约记者 王军强摄
