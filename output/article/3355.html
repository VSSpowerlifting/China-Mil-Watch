Barracks Observation | The Torch Light of "Heirlooms" Deep in the Mountains

军营观察丨深山“传家宝”的薪火之光
MOD China (国防部) 19 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A base under the Zhengzhou Joint Logistic Support Center has institutionalized a 'heirloom' (传家宝) political education program built around ten recovered artifacts—including a maintenance wrench, patrol lantern, rubber shoes, and a 1989 work diary—rotating them monthly through subunit display cases and embedding them in promotion ceremonies, pre-mission briefings, and Party Day micro-lessons. The article documents a named institutional problem: the base's Party committee identified a 'wait, rely, and demand' (等靠要) mentality and weakened conservation habits among younger soldiers as material conditions improved, and this program is the direct organizational response. The framing points to a broader PLA concern about ideological erosion in logistics units during modernization, though whether the behavioral changes described—Private First Class Long Yu repairing rather than replacing a component, Master Sergeant Wang Zuwen's 'High-Skill Sergeant Workshop'—represent durable shifts or are themselves political education content remains unverifiable from this source.
Translation

On a midsummer morning, two soldiers stood before a cultural display case at a subunit of a certain base under the Zhengzhou Joint Logistic Support Center, carefully wiping down an old open-end wrench on exhibit. The wrench's edges were pitted and scarred, covered in marks from knocks and strikes; several conspicuous weld lines along the middle of the handle bore silent witness to its years of wear. "Looking at it is like seeing the figures of our predecessors fighting hard back then," said the subunit's political commissar, Meng Xiaodong, feeling a sudden warmth in his chest.

The wrench's "rebirth" traced back to a storeroom cleanout several months earlier. When the officers and soldiers pulled this blackened piece of iron from a pile of scrapped parts, someone suggested: "It's rusted this badly—let's just scrap it." At the very moment the wrench was about to be consigned to the discard pile, the senior squad leader leading the detail, Chen Chi, reached out and stopped them. He took the wrench, fingertips tracing the worn jaw, and said with feeling: "We can't throw this away—it's the 'bread and butter' of our old-generation mechanics."

Those words brought instant silence to the noisy storeroom. The soldiers who had gathered around sat down on the storeroom steps and listened as the old squad leader recounted stories from deep in the mountains decades ago.

When the depot was first established, the mountains were remote and supplies desperately scarce. Excavating the underground storage tunnels, laying pipelines, maintaining facilities—all of it depended on a few simple tools. This wrench accompanied generation after generation of mechanics as they split rock by day and conducted depot inspections and repairs by night. When a tool broke, they ground it back into shape; when a part wore out, they made do with what they had. Never requisition what you can repair yourself; never replace what can be reused—a single wrench served for decades.

No fine appearance, no technological enhancement—yet this unassuming old object stirred a powerful resonance in the hearts of the young soldiers.

Comparing that era to today's well-supplied, advanced-equipment barracks, they felt for the first time, concretely, that the efficient and complete logistic support capability of the present did not arise from nothing—it was wrested from hardship by their predecessors, "one turn of a bolt and one repair at a time, one pickaxe and one shovel at a time," through sheer hard work. The spirit of enduring hardship, which had once seemed intangible, now crystallized—through the mottled marks on the old wrench and the stories of those predecessors—into the soldiers' deep understanding of the original aspiration and steadfast commitment of guarding a depot deep in the mountains.

The next day, on the unanimous proposal of the subunit's officers and soldiers, the old wrench that had nearly been discarded was solemnly placed in the barracks' cultural display case, becoming the subunit's "heirloom" of building the military through diligence and thrift (勤俭建军).

An old storeroom, a story told by a veteran soldier—these gave a long-silent old wrench new luster, and also prompted reflection among the base's leadership: the best form of inheritance does not rely on rigid constraints and rote preaching, but on using tangible, touchable objects and weighty, vivid stories to awaken deep resonance within the soldiers' hearts.

Immediately, a "treasure hunt" activity themed "Seeking the Base's Old Objects, Inheriting the Dabie Mountain Spirit of Struggle" was launched with great enthusiasm across the base's various units. Searching storerooms, scouring corners, recalling the past—old tools, old ledgers, and old photographs together sketched out a red lineage (红色谱系) of the base's troops forging ahead through hardship, educating and inspiring soldiers to carry forward the red gene (红色基因) and shoulder the responsibility of strengthening the military.

**The Torch Light of "Heirlooms" Deep in the Mountains**

The mountains stand tall; the years pass in silence. Decades ago, predecessors answered the call, shouldered their packs, and plunged into the heart of the Dabie Mountains.

No buildings, no roads, no water, no electricity, no equipment, no support—they relied on sheer passion to blast open the mountains and carve out the depot tunnels, some leaving their lives forever on this land. As time has passed, the objects they left behind have long since transcended their utilitarian value, becoming vivid teaching materials for political education.

At a certain base under the Zhengzhou Joint Logistic Support Center, an exhibition themed on building the military through diligence and thrift is drawing the attention of officers and soldiers. An old oil lantern covered in dents, a handwritten diary with yellowed pages, old rubber shoes with cracked uppers—these objects and photographs bearing the hardships of the founding era, accompanied by detailed background stories, speak silently of years gone by.

Over the past two years, the base has continuously conducted activities to seek out "heirlooms" of struggle, allowing silent old objects to become the "main narrators" of history and occupy an important place in the barracks' displays. This spiritual torch, lit from deep within history, is quietly illuminating the soldiers' conscious drive to train and prepare for war. On the eve of the exhibition, the base followed the thread of the "heirlooms," going deep into the grassroots to seek out veterans and record these moving stories—rooted in the mountains, about original aspirations and inheritance.

**Echoes from the Mountains: The Spiritual Power Contained in Old Objects**

Entering the headquarters barracks of a certain base under the Zhengzhou Joint Logistic Support Center, a brightly lit display case immediately caught this writer's eye.

Behind the glass, an old oil lantern covered in scratches sat quietly. The dents in the lamp shade and the smoke-blackened lamp opening told of more than sixty years of wind and rain. Senior Sergeant First Class Lu Tao of the base stood before the display case and explained: "Back then, there was no electricity in the mountain gully. Tunnel inspections, mountain patrols, hazard checks—all of it depended on this lantern."

He paused, then continued: "Lamp oil was precious then. Everyone agreed to use it only on the most treacherous stretches of the path. That faint glimmer of the lantern not only lit up cold nights deep in the mountains—it also planted in the hearts of generation after generation of soldiers the original aspiration of careful calculation and treating the depot as home." Looking at the wear marks of varying depths on the lantern body, this writer seemed to see a line of figures groping their way forward in the faint light along a winding mountain path.

Driving deep into the mountains, in the honor room of a certain subunit, a pair of old rubber shoes in the central display case moved all who saw them. The uppers had yellowed, densely covered with five or six patches; at the heel and ball of the sole, thick layers of rubber sheeting obscured the shoe's original tread pattern.

"These shoes literally trod a path through the rocky mountains." The senior squad leader accompanying the visit spoke with pride. He recounted the hardships of the 1960s: supply storage points were scattered across more than a dozen hilltops, with no paths to patrol. The soldiers relied on their own hands and feet to carve roads out of the steep slopes. "The subunit Party branch organized everyone to set a goal: if the road isn't open, don't stop even if you wear through the soles of your feet." For a full eight months, patch upon patch was added, and the road extended inch by inch. On the day the road was completed, the soldiers kept the pair of shoes with the most patches as a memento.

In the archives room of a certain subunit, a 1989 work diary is displayed with great solemnity. The pages have yellowed, but the handwriting remains neat and clear. "Use the kitchen's rice-washing water to irrigate the vegetable garden; strip usable parts from scrapped equipment and build a 'second-hand parts depot'; sew old rain gear into dust covers…" Line after line of handwritten requirements for managing the depot with diligence and thrift—rigorous and plain. "This is a set of rules and regulations, but it is also the thrift gene (勤俭基因) inscribed in action by the older generation," said Subunit Commander Wen Chao with feeling. Running through every line is the wisdom of using every resource to its absolute limit, and the commitment to hard work without complaint.

Walking through the various grassroots subunits of the base, more old objects bearing the weight of the years have been carefully "awakened": a canvas tool bag with dense, net-like repair stitching; an old wrench with layers of weld marks; a mountain-splitting pickaxe worn blunt at the tip, engraved with the hardship of carving out the underground tunnels; a toolbox assembled from spare parts, a vivid embodiment of turning waste into treasure… They have been "invited" out from the corners of storerooms and placed in the barracks' display cases—not merely to exhibit history, but to illuminate the present.

These mottled old objects have, in truth, lost all practical value. But each of them reveals a single truth: every brick and tile, every road and tunnel in this mountain fastness is a position wrested inch by inch, a fortress carved out blow by blow, a reserve saved up cent by cent by the predecessors. What these old objects embody is not merely nostalgic stories, but a spiritual power that transcends time and space, silently nourishing the hearts of those who came after.

**Transforming Silently: Fine Traditions Taking Root in Soldiers' Hearts**

Now that the old objects are on display, how can the spirit they carry truly be integrated into the thinking of the new generation of soldiers and transformed into conscious action? This has become a deep-seated challenge facing the base's Party committee.

As the force has undergone transformation and development and the composition of its personnel has changed, certain phenomena have gradually emerged: some young soldiers, upon first arriving in the mountains, find their thinking prone to wavering when confronted with the relatively harsh environment and monotonous pace of life; awareness of conservation has faded somewhat in the daily use of water and electricity and the requisitioning and use of supplies and equipment; and a mentality of "fearing hardship and avoiding difficulty" (怕苦避难) has occasionally surfaced during training and duty. These problems reflect the real-world challenge of effectively inheriting the fine tradition of arduous struggle and building the military through diligence and thrift in an era of improved material conditions.

Focused on this, the base's Party committee has a clear approach: the "heirlooms" that have been carefully recovered must truly become educational vehicles that touch the soul and guide action. They went deep to seek out discharged veterans, meticulously combed through long-sealed historical materials, and repeatedly screened and selected objects, ultimately identifying 10 red "heirlooms" most representative of the tradition, and worked to build a series of educational courses:

Each month, these carefully selected "heirlooms" rotate on display among the various subunits. Ten minutes are reserved at each unit affairs meeting to organize soldiers to discuss their understanding and share their reflections in connection with their own posts, drawing spiritual nourishment from the old objects. Before setting out on major missions, all personnel conduct a special "heirloom" education session together in the honor room, reviewing the arduous journey of the predecessors' founding efforts and inspiring a sense of mission through looking back at history. When new soldiers join or when cadres are promoted in rank, they are organized to retrace the patrol route, with veteran comrades recounting the stories of the predecessors' founding struggles, allowing soldiers to feel the weight of inheritance with every step. In conjunction with Party Day activities, "heirloom micro-Party lessons" (传家宝微党课) are routinely conducted, with Party members each selecting an old object, examining their own shortcomings against the work style of their predecessors and setting goals against their post responsibilities, inspecting their own gaps in diligence and thrift and work standards, and clarifying the direction of their efforts.

At the same time, the base has also relied on its internal network to open an interactive platform called "Xiong Guan Xin Sheng" (雄关心声, "Voices from the Mighty Pass") to hear soldiers' opinions and suggestions on the "treasure hunt activity." By mining typical examples from among the soldiers themselves, it regularly organizes "Ordinary People, Ordinary Things" (凡人凡事) deeds report meetings and has established a "Pioneer Lecture Hall" (先锋讲堂), bringing to the podium those who have practiced the "heirloom" spirit in ordinary posts, using real stories to move and inspire more people.

Like a spring breeze bringing gentle rain, the message has entered hearts drop by drop. The ideological transformation of Private First Class Long Yu of a certain subunit under the base is quite representative.

When he first joined the company, his first instinct whenever equipment had a minor malfunction was always "replace it with something new." However, after listening again and again to the stories of his predecessors, his thinking quietly changed. During one equipment maintenance session, faced with a worn small component, he did not rush to requisition a replacement but instead pondered repeatedly and attempted to repair and reuse it. In his study notes he wrote: "The lamp oil in the lantern will eventually burn out, but the red spark of arduous struggle must not be extinguished; the rubber shoes will always wear through, but the forward steps of taking root in the mountains and ensuring victory in battle will never stop."

Facing the new changes in soldiers' thinking, the base's leadership clearly recognized that while some soldiers' understanding of the core spirit of building the military through diligence and thrift still needs to deepen, the seed of identification has been planted—the moment to effectively transform ideological identification into conscious action has arrived.

**Passing the Torch: Diligence and Struggle Becoming Conscious Action**

The power of the spirit will ultimately shine forth in hard work.

Not long ago, the technical assault team led by Master Sergeant First Class Wang Zuwen of the base was formally "upgraded" into the base's first "High-Skill Sergeant Workshop" (高技能军士工作室). This veteran, who has taken root in the mountains for 33 years, spoke plainly but powerfully: "The predecessors' spiritual torch has been passed into our hands; the red gene will be passed down from generation to generation on the road to strengthening the military."

This is not only a pledge—it is a call to action. Over the past two years, the base's Party committee has taken the lead in deeply integrating the spiritual core of the "heirlooms" into its central work: on one hand, leading soldiers to carefully manage resources and live through "lean times" (紧日子), starting from saving a single drop of water, a single sheet of paper, a single drop of oil, weaving the spirit of diligence and thrift into daily life; on the other hand, carrying forward the predecessors' wisdom of managing the depot with diligence and thrift, integrating limited resources to overcome difficulties, successfully completing the military's first batch of autonomous supply tasks within quota standards, and achieving a deep integration of fine traditions with the digital logistics support system.

Under the Party committee's lead by example and the inspiration of the "heirloom" spirit, diligence and struggle have been internalized as the soldiers' conscious action. When facilities in the barracks sustain minor damage, soldiers no longer sit and wait for repairs to be reported—they proactively pick up tools and fix things themselves. Careful calculation in the use of consumables and the recycling and full utilization of waste materials have become a shared understanding. The use of every resource to its maximum has become consensus.

Even more commendable is the enthusiasm for innovation and creation that has burst forth in practice, with the "small innovations, small modifications, small inventions" (小革新、小改造、小发明) activity yielding abundant results: an "Intelligent Rapid Search System for Ordnance Maintenance Materials" has significantly improved support efficiency; an "Automatic Identification System for War Reserve Materials" ensures precise management and control; a "Depot Inventory Management System" has optimized resource allocation; and a "Fully Automatic Ultrasonic Degreasing and Rust Removal Process" has effectively reduced labor and material costs. Several of these innovative achievements, born from practice and serving combat readiness, have received national invention patents and have been promoted and applied across the force.

The inheritance of the spirit is also giving rise to "heirlooms" belonging to the new era.

Today, the precious objects carefully preserved by each subunit have gained new members: new-style combat boots with soles worn through during major exercise and training support missions; hand-drawn design sketches recording ideas for equipment innovation; multi-function tool kits used repeatedly during field training; handwritten optimization ledgers from digitized depots… These new objects record the footprints of struggle of the new generation of soldiers.

The mountains speak no words, yet they witness the relay of protection by generation after generation of soldiers; the old objects make no sound, yet they tell of an original aspiration and mission that will never fade. Ten "heirlooms" from deep in the mountains carry not only the founding years of pioneering hardship (筚路蓝缕), but also the spiritual power of building the military through diligence and thrift, working hard and working practically, and ensuring victory in battle.

**The Spirit of Arduous Struggle Will Never Be Outdated**

Ten plain old objects string together decades of self-reliance and diligent entrepreneurship in a single unit.

These weathered "heirlooms," as the concrete material embodiment of the fine tradition of building the military through diligence and thrift, also offer profound lessons for grassroots units in conducting solid ideological education and honing a combat-winning work style: no matter how much living conditions improve or how much support equipment is upgraded, the fundamental quality of arduous struggle must never be abandoned; deeply integrating this precious spiritual wealth into support practice will efficiently empower the accomplishment of combat readiness tasks.

Arduous struggle is the red gene born with the people's army; building the military through diligence and thrift is a fine tradition our military has carried forward for a hundred years. Looking back on the journey—from the Red Army making their own straw sandals and subsisting on wild vegetables during the revolutionary war years, to island-garrison soldiers opening wasteland and building their own barracks during the period of peaceful construction, to generation after generation of joint logistic support soldiers blasting roads through mountains and repairing their own equipment, laying a solid support foundation in harsh environments—historical practice has fully confirmed: the revolutionary spirit of building the military through diligence and thrift and arduous struggle is not only our heirloom, but also the most solid foundation of confidence for our military to overcome hardship and obstacles and effectively fulfill its mission.

Entering the new era, the modernization level of our military's joint logistic support system has comprehensively leaped forward, with large transfer vehicles, intelligent warehousing facilities, and digital maintenance and support equipment being fielded in large numbers, and the duty environment and material support conditions for grassroots soldiers are incomparably better than before. However, the improvement of material conditions has also brought ideological challenges: a small number of soldiers have developed a "wait, rely, and demand" (等靠要) mentality; field support has become excessively dependent on large equipment; minor equipment malfunctions routinely trigger requisitions for new parts; daily consumables are used extravagantly; and the concept of diligence and thrift has quietly weakened. This erroneous understanding—that "conditions have improved, so arduous struggle is outdated"—runs directly counter to our military's glorious traditions.

A certain base under the Zhengzhou Joint Logistic Support Center has transformed the stories of struggle behind old objects into immersive, real-scene lessons (沉浸式实景课), guiding soldiers to thoroughly correct ideological deviations and establish correct behavioral standards, effectively inspiring soldiers' conscious drive to integrate the thinking of building the military through diligence and thrift into training and combat readiness—an approach worthy of study and emulation.

The mountains stand firm; the torch burns without cease. The faint light emitted by 10 "heirlooms" from deep in the mountains illuminates the eternal value of the spirit of arduous struggle across time and space. Joint logistic support missions in the new era are arduous and demanding; the better equipped and the more favorable the conditions, the more firmly we must grip the heirloom of building the military through diligence and thrift, and let the spirit of arduous struggle shine brilliantly on the support front line. All officers and soldiers should take history as a mirror and examine themselves, consciously abandon thoughts of ease and slackness, stand firm at their posts, temper hard skills through suffering and hard work, improve efficiency through diligence and thrift, and with an unrelenting spirit of struggle and solid support capabilities, shoulder the historic responsibility of strengthening and rejuvenating the military in the new era.

**The "Treasures" Hidden in the Mountain Gullies**

A rusted maintenance wrench, a chipped mountain-splitting pickaxe, a mottled patrol lantern, a yellowed work diary… These old objects engraved with the marks of the years, interwoven with the rapidly changing light of the times, silently tell of the remarkable years deep in the Dabie Mountains.

They are historical witnesses to generation after generation of officers and soldiers of a certain base under the Zhengzhou Joint Logistic Support Center taking root in the mountains and struggling through hardship, and they are also the spiritual wellspring from which joint logistic support soldiers of the new era draw strength and strive to strengthen the military.

随即，一场以“寻访基地老物件、传承大别山奋斗精神”为主题的“寻宝”活动在基地各单位火热展开。翻库房、寻角落、忆往事，一件件老工具、老台账、老照片，共同勾勒出一张基地部队艰苦创业的红色谱系，教育激励官兵传承红色基因、担当强军重任。 深山“传家宝”的薪火之光 群山巍峨，岁月无声。几十年前，前辈们响应号令，背起行装扎进大别山腹地。 他顿了顿，继续说道：“当时灯油金贵，大伙儿约定，只在险要路段使用。马灯的那一点微光，不仅点亮了深山寒夜，更在几代官兵心里，种下了精打细算、以库为家的初心。”看着灯身上深浅不一的磨痕，笔者仿佛看到蜿蜒山路上，一队身影在微光中摸索前行。 驱车辗转至大山深处，某分队荣誉室中央的展柜里，一双老胶鞋令人感慨。鞋面泛黄，密密麻麻缀着五六块补丁；鞋底的后跟与脚掌处，厚厚摞起的胶皮掩盖了胶鞋原本的纹路。 “这双鞋，硬是在乱石山中踏出了一条路。”陪同的老班长语气里透着自豪。他讲述起上世纪六十年代的艰难：物资存放点散落在十几个山头，巡逻无路可走。官兵们硬是靠双手双脚，在陡坡上开出路来。“分队党支部组织大伙儿立下目标，路不通，脚板磨穿也不停步。”整整8个月，补丁摞着补丁，路一寸寸延伸。道路贯通那天，官兵们留下了一双补丁最多的胶鞋，作为纪念。 在某分队的档案室，一本1989年的工作日记被郑重展出。纸页泛黄，字迹却依然工整清晰。“食堂淘米水浇菜地；报废装备拆可用件，建‘二手库’；旧雨衣缝补作防尘罩……”一条条手写的勤俭管库要求，严谨而朴素。“这是规章制度，更是老一辈用行动刻下的勤俭基因。”分队长文超感慨道，字里行间流淌的，是把每一份资源用到极致的智慧，是苦干实干不叫屈的担当。 行走在该基地各个基层分队，更多承载着岁月重量的旧物被精心“唤醒”：一个帆布工具袋，缝补针脚细密如网；一把老扳手，焊痕堆叠；一根磨秃了尖的开山镐，镌刻着开凿洞库的艰辛；一个零件拼凑的工具箱，则是变废为宝的生动诠释……它们从库房的角落被“请”出，置于营区的展窗，不仅是为了陈列历史，更是为了照亮当下。 这些斑驳的老物件，其实已经没有了实用价值，但它们无不揭示着一个道理：这座深山里的一砖一瓦、一路一库，是先辈们一寸寸拼出来的阵地、一镐镐凿出来的堡垒、一分分省出来的家底。这些老物件所汇聚的，不仅仅是怀旧的故事，还是跨越时空的精神力量，无声滋养着后来者的心田。 润物无声：优良传统融入官兵心田 老物件上了展台，如何让它们承载的精神真正融入新一代官兵的思想观念，转化为自觉行动？这成为该基地党委面临的深层课题。 伴随部队转型发展和官兵成分结构变化，一些现象也逐渐显现：部分年轻官兵初入深山，面对相对艰苦的环境和单调的生活节奏，思想易生波动；日常用水用电、物资器材申领使用中，节约意识有所淡化；训练执勤中，“怕苦避难”的心态也偶有露头。这些问题反映出，在物质条件改善的今天，如何有效传承艰苦奋斗、勤俭建军优良传统所面临的现实挑战。 聚焦于此，基地党委思路清晰：必须让精心寻回的“传家宝”真正成为触及灵魂、引领行动的教育载体。他们深入寻访退伍老兵，细致梳理尘封史料，反复甄别筛选实物，最终确立10件最具代表性的红色“传家宝”，着力构建起一系列教育课程—— 每月，这些精心挑选的“传家宝”会在各分队流动展出。每次队务会预留10分钟，用来组织官兵结合自身岗位，谈认识、讲体会，从老物件中汲取精神养分；重大任务出征前，全体人员统一在荣誉室开展“传家宝”专题教育，回顾先辈创业的艰辛历程，在历史的回望中激发使命感；新兵入营或骨干晋衔时，组织大家重走巡逻路，请老同志讲述前辈创业故事，让官兵在脚步丈量中感悟传承；结合党日活动，常态开展“传家宝微党课”，组织党员自选一件老物件，对照先辈作风查不足、对照岗位职责定目标，检视自身在勤俭节约、工作标准上的差距，明确努力方向。 同时，基地还依托内部网络，开通了“雄关心声”互动平台，倾听官兵对“寻宝活动”的意见建议；通过挖掘官兵身边典型，定期组织“凡人凡事”事迹报告会和开设“先锋讲堂”，让那些在平凡岗位上践行“传家宝”精神的身边人走上讲台，用真实的故事感染带动更多人。 春风化雨，点滴入心。该基地某分队上等兵龙宇的思想转变颇为典型。 初下连队时，装备稍有故障，他的第一反应总是“换新的”。然而，在一次次聆听前辈的故事后，他的观念悄然改变。一次装备维护中，面对一个磨损的小零件，他没有急于申领更换，而是反复琢磨，尝试修复利用。他在学习心得笔记中写道：“马灯里的灯油终会燃尽，但艰苦奋斗的红色火种不能熄灭；胶鞋总会磨破，但扎根深山、保障打赢的前行脚步永远不会停下。” 面对官兵的思想新变化，该基地领导清醒认识到，虽然部分官兵对勤俭建军精神内核的理解尚需深化，但认同的种子已经播下——将思想认同有效转化为行动自觉的时机，已经到来。 薪火相传：勤俭奋斗成为行动自觉 精神的力量，终将在实干中绽放光芒。 不久前，由该基地一级军士长王祖文领衔的技术攻坚小组，正式“升级”为基地首个“高技能军士工作室”。这位扎根深山33年的老兵话语朴实而有力：“先辈的精神火把递到了我们手中，红色基因必将在强军路上代代相传。” 这不仅是誓言，更是行动号角。近两年，基地党委带头将“传家宝”精神内核深度融入中心工作：一方面，带领官兵精打细算过好“紧日子”，从节约一滴水、一张纸、一滴油入手，将勤俭作风融入日常；另一方面，发扬先辈勤俭管库智慧，整合有限资源攻坚克难，成功完成全军首批限额标准内物资自主供应任务，实现了优良传统与数字化保障体系的深度融合。 在党委率先垂范和“传家宝”精神感召下，勤俭奋斗已内化为官兵的行动自觉。营区设施出现小破损，官兵们不再坐等报修，而是主动拿起工具自己动手修补；耗材使用处处精打细算，废旧物资想方设法循环利用、物尽其用成为共识。 更令人称道的是，官兵们创新创造的热情也在实践中迸发，“小革新、小改造、小发明”活动硕果累累——“军械维修器材智能快速查找系统”大幅提升了保障效率，“战储器材自动识别系统”确保了精准管控，“库存物资管理系统”优化了资源配置，“全自动超声波除油除锈工艺”有效节省了人力物力成本。这些源于实践、服务备战的创新成果中，有多项获得国家发明专利，在部队推广应用。 精神的传承，也在催生着属于新时代的“传家宝”。 如今，各分队精心保存的珍贵物品，增添了新成员：重大演训保障任务中磨穿鞋底的新式作战靴、记录着装备革新思路的手绘设计草图、野外驻训反复使用的多功能工具包、数字化库房手写的优化台账……这些新物件，记录着新生代官兵的奋斗足迹。 群山无言，见证着一代代官兵的接力守护；旧物无声，诉说着永不褪色的初心使命。10件深山“传家宝”，承载的不仅是筚路蓝缕的创业岁月，更是勤俭建军、苦干实干、保障打赢的精神力量。 艰苦奋斗精神永远不会过时 10件朴素老物件，串联起一支部队数十年自力更生、勤俭创业的峥嵘岁月。 这些饱经风霜的“传家宝”，是勤俭建军优良传统具象化的实物载体，也为基层部队抓实思想教育、砥砺胜战作风提供深刻启示：无论生活条件如何改善、保障装备如何升级，艰苦奋斗的底色永远不能丢；将这笔宝贵精神财富深度融入保障实践，必将为完成备战打仗任务高效赋能。 艰苦奋斗是人民军队与生俱来的红色基因，勤俭建军是我军赓续百年的优良传统。回望征程，从革命战争年代红军自制草鞋、野菜果腹，到和平建设时期守岛官兵开荒种地、自建营房，再到一代代联勤官兵开山筑路、自修装备，在艰苦环境中筑牢保障根基……历史实践充分印证：勤俭建军、艰苦奋斗的革命精神，不仅是我们的传家宝，更是我军战胜艰难险阻、有效履行使命任务最坚实的底气。 步入新时代，我军联勤体系现代化水平全面跃升，大型转运车辆、智能仓储库房、数字化维保设备批量列装，基层官兵执勤环境、物质保障条件今非昔比。然而，物质条件的改善，也伴生着思想上的挑战：少数官兵滋生了“等靠要”心态，野外保障过度依赖大型设备，装备小故障动辄申领新件，日常耗材大手大脚，勤俭节约观念悄然弱化。这种“条件好了，艰苦奋斗过时了”的错误认识，与我军光荣传统背道而驰。 郑州联勤保障中心某基地通过把老物件背后的奋斗故事，转化为沉浸式实景课，引导官兵深纠思想偏差，树正行为标准，有效激发了官兵将勤俭建军思想融入练兵备战的行动自觉，值得学习借鉴。 群山巍然，薪火不息。10件深山“传家宝”散发的微光，映照着艰苦奋斗精神穿越时空的永恒价值。新时代联勤保障任务艰巨繁重，越是装备精良、条件优越，越要牢牢握紧勤俭建军传家宝，让艰苦奋斗精神在保障一线熠熠生辉。广大官兵当以史为鉴、反躬自省，自觉摒弃安逸懈怠思想，立足本职岗位，在吃苦实干中锤炼硬功，于勤俭节约中提升效能，以永不懈怠的奋斗姿态和过硬的保障本领，扛起新时代强军兴军的历史重任。
