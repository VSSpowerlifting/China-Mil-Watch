China's Border | On the Banks of the Ussuri River, Standing Watch for the Motherland

中国边关丨在乌苏里江畔，为祖国放哨

About this analysis
Summary
Officers and soldiers of the Zhuaji Outpost, assigned to an unnamed brigade of the Northern Theater Command Army, conduct rotating boat patrols and fixed-post duty along a several-dozen-kilometer stretch of the Ussuri River from Belahong Township each April through November, living in mobile modular shelters (移动方舱) with intermittent communications and no permanent infrastructure. The article is a human-interest profile in official military media; its value is as a record of how the PLA frames hardship duty at a remote border outpost — invoking the television drama 士兵突击 and its motto 不抛弃、不放弃 — to cultivate perseverance and mission identity among junior enlisted soldiers, not as evidence of a changed posture on the Sino-Russian border.
Translation

On the Banks of the Ussuri River, Standing Watch for the Motherland

■ Li Yingbo, Wang Gan

The Ussuri River has thawed from its winter freeze, and officers and soldiers of the Zhuaji Outpost, belonging to a certain brigade of the Northern Theater Command Army stationed at the Belahong checkpoint, are conducting boat patrols.

Aboard the vessel, a soldier is observing conditions on the river's surface. Photo by Li Gen.

Belahong Township, in the far northeast borderlands, sits along the banks of the Ussuri River. Each year from April through November, once the river breaks free of its frozen period, officers and soldiers of the Zhuaji Outpost of a certain brigade of the Northern Theater Command Army arrive here to carry out boat patrols and fixed-post duty.

In the television drama "Soldiers' Sortie" (士兵突击), "Steppe Squad Five" was located deep in a remote grassland, a five-hour drive from the regimental headquarters, yet a handful of soldiers grew steadily and solidly there, truly embodying what it means to "never abandon, never give up" (不抛弃、不放弃) and what it means to be a genuine soldier.

The officers and soldiers of the Belahong checkpoint are the same. They are stationed on a remote and desolate riverbank, and through day after day of duty and patrol they have cultivated an extraordinary spirit of perseverance — which is why people also call them "Riverside Squad Five" (江畔五班).

Stationed on the banks of the Ussuri River, the outpost's officers and soldiers cover a duty range spanning dozens of kilometers of river, requiring uninterrupted observation of the river's surface around the clock. Whether under blazing sun or raging storms, there can be not the slightest slackening.

In summer, Belahong is a kingdom of insects. The horseflies and mosquitoes here are large-bodied; their bites are both painful and itchy. Even wearing thick combat uniforms and mosquito-proof hats, soldiers on duty can hardly avoid being covered in red welts. At midday, surface temperatures reach over 40 degrees Celsius. Sweat streams down the soldiers' faces, and after a single patrol their combat uniforms are soaked through and cling tightly to their bodies. Yet the officers and soldiers often say: "Grit your teeth through the mosquito bites, bear up under the scorching sun — there can be not half a moment's carelessness on duty."

Every July and August, violent rainstorms strike Belahong frequently, and the Ussuri River enters its flood season. The river surface widens and the current runs extremely swift. The soil along the banks is washed loose and soft, and landslides or river backflow can occur at any moment, threatening the lives and property of nearby villagers.

The outpost's officers and soldiers proactively provide flood-season early warnings for villagers, safeguarding the people's safety. They monitor water-level changes at fixed times each day, closely tracking weather forecasts and hydrological information. The moment they detect that water levels are approaching the warning threshold or that signs of danger are emerging, they immediately notify villagers along the banks to move their belongings in advance and evacuate hazardous areas.

The dead of night is often when various emergencies and special situations (险情、特情) are most likely to occur, making nighttime duty an even greater test of the soldiers' willpower. One night around midnight, Private First Class Shi Weiye was vigilantly observing the river surface when he suddenly spotted several small boats quietly approaching the border line. He immediately reported the situation, and Deputy Company Commander Wa Shengjiu led a team that rushed to the scene and promptly stopped an incident of cross-border fishing. "The deeper the night, the more vigilant we must be. Every extra measure of perseverance on our part means one more measure of security for the border," said Shi Weiye, adding that after that incident he gained a far deeper understanding of the word "mission."

Beyond the harsh natural environment, the long duty routes are also full of challenges. Officers and soldiers must travel back and forth between various positions every day, and in many cases can only make their way on foot across tidal flats, with muddy weeds and sharp gravel underfoot. On one occasion, Sergeant Feng Yongjian accidentally sprained his ankle. So as not to delay the duty schedule, he persisted and completed the full route, only to return to the outpost and discover that his ankle had swollen enormously. Yet he showed not the slightest grievance, and said with a smile: "A small injury like this is nothing."

The outpost's officers and soldiers live at Belahong in cramped mobile modular shelters (移动方舱). During the day they are stifling hot, like a steamer; at night the river wind carries a bone-chilling cold, and the soldiers often have to wrap themselves tightly in quilts and clothing to sleep. Mobile phone signals come and go. For most of the time, the soldiers' world consists of nothing but the sound of the water on the river and the light of the observation post.

In the four years Deputy Company Commander Wa Shengjiu has held his post, he has come to the Belahong checkpoint for duty every year. By now his face has been tanned dark and gleaming, and his eyes carry a look of toughness and resolve. Wa Shengjiu knows well the hardships his comrades endure, so he uses rest time to lead everyone in hands-on improvements to the shelter environment. They build simple tables and chairs from discarded wooden planks they have gathered, fashion empty plastic bottles into flower pots, and plant a few wild flowers — bringing a touch more vitality and warmth to the spartan outpost.

"Look up at the Five-Starred Red Flag flying before the shelter door, and no matter how bitter or exhausting it gets, it's all worth it!" As the outpost's officers and soldiers say, standing guard at Belahong and keeping watch for the motherland is both a mission and a duty — and even more, an honor and a happiness.

Original Chinese
在乌苏里江畔，为祖国放哨 ■历莹博 王 淦 乌苏里江结束封冻，驻守别拉洪哨点的北部战区陆军某旅抓吉哨所官兵乘艇巡逻。 船艇上，一名战士正在观察江面情况。李 根摄 东北边陲的别拉洪乡，地处乌苏里江沿岸。每年的4月至11月，江水结束封冻期，北部战区陆军某旅抓吉哨所官兵便会抵达此处，执行乘艇巡逻、驻点执勤任务。 在电视剧《士兵突击》中，“草原五班”位于人迹罕至的草原深处，距离团部有5小时车程，然而，几名战士却在那里一步步扎实成长，真正诠释了何为“不抛弃、不放弃”，何为真正的军人本色。 别拉洪哨点的官兵同样如此。他们驻守于偏远荒凉的江畔，在日复一日的执勤巡逻中孕育出不平凡的坚守精神，因而大家也称他们是“江畔五班”。 驻守乌苏里江畔，哨点官兵的执勤范围覆盖数十公里江段，需要昼夜不间断观察江面动静，无论烈日炎炎还是狂风暴雨，都不能有丝毫懈怠。 夏日的别拉洪是蚊虫的王国。这里的牛虻、蚊子体型大，叮咬起来又疼又痒，执勤官兵即使穿着厚厚的作训服、戴着防蚊帽，也难免被叮咬得满身红包。每当正午时分，地表温度高达40多摄氏度，汗水顺着官兵脸颊往下淌，巡逻一趟下来，作训服都湿透了，紧紧贴在身上。可官兵常说一句话：“蚊子咬了忍一忍，太阳晒了扛一扛，执勤可不能有半点马虎。” 每到七八月份，别拉洪暴雨频发，乌苏里江进入汛期，江面变得宽阔、水流十分湍急。岸边的泥土被冲刷得松软，随时可能发生滑坡或江水倒灌险情，威胁附近村民的生命财产安全。 哨点官兵主动为村民提供汛期预警，守护群众安全。他们每天定时监测水位变化，密切关注天气预报和水文信息，一旦发现水位接近警戒值、出现险情苗头，就第一时间通知沿岸村民提前转移物资、撤离危险区域。 深夜往往更易发生各种险情、特情，因而夜间执勤更加考验官兵的毅力。一次夜半时分，上等兵时伟业正警惕观察着江面，突然发现有几艘小船正在悄悄靠近边境线。他立刻上报情况，副连长佤生久带队迅速赶到现场，及时制止了一起越界捕捞行为。“越是深夜，越要提高警惕，我们多一分坚守，边境就多一分安全。”时伟业说，经过那次事件，他对“使命”二字有了更深刻的理解。 除了恶劣的自然环境，漫长的执勤路也充满了挑战。官兵每天要往返于各个点位，很多情况下只能在滩涂上徒步穿行，脚下是泥泞的荒草、尖锐的碎石。一次，中士封永键不小心崴了脚，为了不耽误执勤进度，他坚持走完全程，直到回到哨点，才发现脚踝已经肿得老高。可他没有丝毫委屈，笑着说：“这点小伤不算什么。” 哨点官兵在别拉洪的驻勤居所是空间狭小的移动方舱，白天闷热得像个蒸笼，晚上却能感到江风刺骨的凉意，官兵常常要裹紧被褥和衣物才能安睡。手机信号也时有时无，大部分时间，官兵的世界里只有江面的水声和观察哨的灯光。 佤生久担任副连长4年来，每年都来到别拉洪哨点驻勤。如今，他的脸庞被晒得黝黑发亮，眼神更透出一股坚强和刚毅。佤生久深知战友们的不易，便利用休息时间带着大家动手改造方舱环境，他们用捡来的废弃木板搭建简易桌椅，把收集的空塑料瓶做成花盆，种上几株野花，让简陋的哨点多了几分生机与暖意。 “仰头看一看方舱门前飘扬的五星红旗，再苦再累也值了！”正如哨点官兵所说，驻守别拉洪、为祖国放哨，既是使命和职责，更是荣耀和幸福。
