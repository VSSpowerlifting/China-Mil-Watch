From the Clouds to the Ground: Pilots of an Air Force Brigade Temper Themselves at the Maintenance Front Line

从云端到地面，空军某旅飞行员机务一线砺精兵
PLA Daily (解放军报) 3 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Pilots at an unidentified PLA Air Force brigade are rotating through mechanical and ordnance maintenance sections for structured soldier-tempering exercises (当兵锻炼), performing borescope inspections, flight-control system ground tests, and joint troubleshooting sessions with ground crews after a transition-trained pilot's unfamiliarity with an external pod caused a sortie to abort. The program documents a recognized institutional gap: aircrew and maintenance personnel operate in entirely separate knowledge domains, leaving pilots unable to anticipate how external equipment behavior or component wear affects flight performance and safety. The cross-functional 'Zhuge Liang meetings' (诸葛亮会) that emerged from the program — where pilots raise tactical problems and technicians respond with maintenance-side analysis — extend a pattern of PLA aviation units trying to close the aircrew-groundcrew integration deficit, though the brigade, aircraft type, and whether this is a theater-wide directive or a single-unit initiative remain unspecified.
Translation

On a summer day, a routine combat aircraft inspection and maintenance operation was underway on the flight line apron of an Air Force brigade. Reporters at the scene observed that those conducting the aircraft "physical examinations" were not maintenance personnel but pilots who had shed their flight gear. Holding professional equipment and under the guidance of maintenance personnel, they carried out inspection and maintenance work on the aircraft with meticulous care. This was one scene from the brigade's program organizing pilots to go deep into the maintenance front line for soldier-tempering exercises (当兵锻炼). The impetus for launching this program came from an experience of a mission not completed.

Not long ago, a young pilot who had just completed transition training on a new aircraft was executing a training mission when, due to unfamiliarity with the performance of the external pod on the aircraft, he mistakenly left the pod in an extreme position for an extended period. Although this operation caused no damage to the equipment, it resulted in the training mission for that sortie failing to be completed smoothly.

"This kind of situation is not an isolated case," the brigade leadership found during their investigation. Because the working environments of aircrew and ground crew are completely separate and their areas of specialized knowledge differ entirely, pilots' training focus is ordinarily concentrated on cockpit instruments, weapons systems, and tactics and methods of operation (战术战法), leaving them with insufficient understanding of the key points of aircraft inspection and maintenance support and the performance of external equipment.

To address this, the brigade decided to organize pilots to go deep into maintenance positions such as mechanical and ordnance sections for soldier-tempering exercises, following along to train and learn. They began with basic tasks such as lubricating and cleaning the aircraft, then gradually participated in component adjustment, testing, and troubleshooting, deepening their understanding of the aircraft at the maintenance front line.

"In the past, when conducting flight training, I mostly paid attention to engine thrust; now, directly participating in borescope inspection of engine blades has given me a firsthand, intuitive sense of the impact that sand, dust, and foreign objects have on aircraft performance," one pilot remarked. "When conducting low-altitude and ultra-low-altitude flight training, I will pay closer attention to subtle changes in flight state to ensure flight safety."

In the brigade's special equipment operations room, reporters observed several pilots gathered around densely packed circuit diagrams conducting a ground joint test of the flight control system. Under the guidance of maintenance personnel, after the pilots entered parameters, the operating status of the internal components of the actuators was immediately clear. "The action of pushing and pulling the control stick looks simple, but behind it many components are operating efficiently in support. Any loose solder joint or poor connector contact on any one component could potentially trigger a safety incident," said one pilot, adding that this soldier-tempering exercise gave him a far deeper understanding of the maintenance personnel's ethos of taking extreme responsibility (极端负责) and carrying out meticulous repairs.

During the soldier-tempering exercise period, in addition to participating in routine support operations, the pilots also joined maintenance personnel in convening "Zhuge Liang meetings" (诸葛亮会) to analyze in detail the causes of occasional equipment malfunctions that arose during training and to jointly study improvement plans.

At one such "Zhuge Liang meeting," the flight squadron commander raised from a tactical employment perspective the problem of a certain type of radar easily losing targets in complex electromagnetic environments, and opened a discussion with maintenance personnel. An electronics technician from the maintenance squadron analyzed the issue in conjunction with the radar's operating principles, not only helping the pilots identify the root cause of the problem but also compiling a set of mnemonics to make it easier for pilots to operate the equipment, effectively improving the quality and efficiency of flight training.

"Pilots, as the direct operators of the aircraft, have the most immediate and profound experience with the equipment, and their feedback and suggestions can also help ground support work focus more sharply on the battlefield," said the maintenance squadron commander, adding that they would compile and organize the issues raised by pilots into a key-points reference handbook, striving to make it something pilots can understand at a glance and master after one study.

"Flight is the endeavor of the brave. Lifting the war eagle to fly high and far cannot be achieved without the joint efforts of pilots and maintenance personnel." The brigade leadership told reporters that pilots have broadly reflected that since going deep into the maintenance front line for soldier-tempering exercises, they have studied and grasped the operating principles of the equipment, come to understand the aircraft's "temperament and characteristics" (脾气秉性), and feel more confident when handling in-flight emergencies (空中特情). Moreover, maintenance personnel, through their exchanges with pilots, have also come to understand pilots' combat intentions and operating habits, promoting an improvement in maintenance support capabilities.

Original Chinese
夏日，空军某旅外场停机坪上，一场例行战机检修保养作业正在进行。 记者在现场看到，为战机“体检”的并非机务官兵，而是脱下装具的飞行员。只见他们手持专业设备，在机务官兵指导下，一丝不苟地为战机进行检修保养。这是该旅组织飞行员深入机务一线当兵锻炼的一个场景。开展这项活动的初衷，源于一次未完成任务的经历。 不久前，一名刚完成新机改装的年轻飞行员执行训练任务时，因对战机舱外吊舱性能不熟悉，误将吊舱长时间置于极限位置。这个操作虽未对装备造成损坏，但导致该架次训练任务未能顺利完成。 “这样的情况并非个例。”该旅领导在调研中发现，空勤与地勤工作场景完全分离、专业知识各不相同，飞行员平时训练重点聚焦在座舱仪表、武器系统和战术战法上，导致对战机检修保障重点、舱外装备性能等了解不深。 为此，该旅决定组织飞行员深入机械、军械等机务岗位当兵锻炼、跟训跟学。他们从给战机涂油润滑、清洁保养等基础工作做起，逐步参与机件调试检测和故障排查，在机务一线加深对战机的了解。 “以往开展飞行训练，大多关注发动机推力大小；如今直接参与孔探发动机叶片，让我直观感受到沙尘、异物对战机性能的影响。”一名飞行员感慨地说，“开展低空、超低空飞行训练时，我将更注意飞行状态的细微变化，确保飞行安全。” 在该旅特设操作间，记者看见，几名飞行员围着密密麻麻的线路图，正在进行飞控系统地面联合测试。在机务官兵指导下，飞行员们输入参数后，舵机内部组件的运行状态一目了然。“推拉操纵杆这个动作看似简单，背后却有很多元器件在高效运转支撑。任何一个元器件焊点松动、插头接触不良，都有可能引发安全事故。”一名飞行员说，这次当兵锻炼，让他对机务战友极端负责、精心维修的作风有了更深刻的理解。 当兵锻炼期间，除了参与日常保障作业外，飞行员们还结合训练中出现的装备偶发故障，与机务官兵联合召开“诸葛亮会”，详细剖析故障原因、共同研讨改进方案。 一次“诸葛亮会”上，飞行大队长从战术运用方面提出某型雷达在复杂电磁环境下目标容易丢失的问题，并与机务官兵展开讨论。机务中队一名电子师结合雷达运行机理进行分析，不仅帮飞行员找到问题原因，还梳理出一套方便飞行员操作装备的口诀，有效提升飞行训练质效。 “飞行员作为战机的直接操纵者，对装备体验最直接、感受最深刻，他们的反馈建议也能让地勤保障工作更加聚焦战场。”机务中队长说，他们将对飞行员提出的问题进行总结梳理，编写要点提示手册，力争让飞行员一看就懂、一学就会。 “飞行是勇敢者的事业。托举战鹰高飞远航，离不开飞行员和机务官兵的共同努力。”该旅领导告诉记者，飞行员普遍反映，深入机务一线当兵锻炼以来，他们学习掌握了装备运行原理、摸清了战机“脾气秉性”，处置空中特情时底气更足。不仅如此，机务官兵在与飞行员的交流中，也了解了飞行员的作战意图和操作习惯，促进了机务保障能力提升。
