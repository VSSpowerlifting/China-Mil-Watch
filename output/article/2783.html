About this analysis
Summary
A political work essay published in PLA official media, authored by Liu Guangming and Liu Ming, uses Xi Jinping's April 8 address at the National Defense University senior cadre training class opening ceremony as its anchor to mount a sustained argument against 特权思想 (privileged thinking) and 特权现象 (privileged phenomena) in the Party and military, explicitly naming the recently investigated Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli alongside earlier cases as evidence that no rank confers immunity. This is standard political education content keyed to the ongoing anti-corruption campaign; its value is as a record of how the institution frames discipline enforcement to senior cadres, documenting the rhetorical move of yoking Xi's self-revolution (自我革命) doctrine to historical CCP legitimacy narratives and the 历史周期率 (historical cycle) problem. The explicit naming of Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli—both CMC-level figures—as disciplinary cases alongside Guo Boxiong and Xu Caihou is the one detail that extends beyond boilerplate, confirming that official messaging has now fully incorporated those investigations into the standard anti-privilege canon.

There Are No Special Persons Beyond Constraint

■ Liu Guangming　Liu Ming

Heaven moves in constant patterns; the sun, moon, and stars each follow their own orbits, never overstepping one another. Governing a nation has its way; Party discipline and state law define clear boundaries that must not be breached. Under the sky of Red China, there have never been special persons beyond constraint!

On April 8 of this year, a spring breeze swept across the campus of the National Defense University. At the opening ceremony of the senior cadre training class for the entire military, Chairman Xi's important address rang out with resounding force: "One must understand laws and regulations, know the rules, and hold them in reverence; before laws and regulations, everyone is equal, there are no exceptions in complying with laws and regulations, and there are no exceptions in implementing them." This is not merely a command — it is also a declaration of the supreme commander's resolute will to thoroughly purge privileged thinking (特权思想) and privileged phenomena (特权现象) from our Party and our military, a manifestation of his powerful determination to ensure the red country's mountains and rivers remain forever secure by keeping the People's Army from changing its nature, and an expression of his broad-minded commitment to leading the entire Party and the entire military in steadfastly upholding and practicing their founding mission (初心使命).

Looking back on the new era, words that resound like spring thunder string together the magnificent trajectory of governing the Party and the military with strict discipline —

"No organization or individual may enjoy the privilege of overriding the Constitution and the law."

"Within the Party, it is absolutely impermissible to have special Party members who are not bound by Party discipline and state law, or who even place themselves above the Party Constitution and Party organizations."

"Under the rule of law, no one can harbor the hope of getting away with it, no one can expect to receive grace outside the law, there is no 'iron certificate of immunity from crime' (丹书铁券), and there is no 'iron-capped prince' (铁帽子王)."

……

These are the great bells and drums (黄钟大吕), resounding in the heart of every Party member and cadre; they are a bright mirror held high, reflecting the soul of every revolutionary soldier; they are a sharp blade, cutting out the deep-rooted chronic ailments and corrupt practices that remain embedded in the body of our Party and our military.

Time speaks without words; rivers and mountains bear witness. Since the 18th National Congress of the Party, from the investigation and punishment of Guo Boxiong, Xu Caihou, Fang Fenghui, and Zhang Yang, to the investigation and punishment of He Weidong, Miao Hua, and He Hongjun, and then to the investigation and punishment of Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli — each heavy blow of anti-corruption has split the sky like a thunderclap, proclaiming the justice and majesty of law and discipline. In the sacred halls of our Party and our military, there are no protective talismans that grant immunity from punishment, only touchstones of loyalty. The Party Central Committee and the Central Military Commission's (中央军委) absolute resolve in the fight against corruption — with no forbidden zones, full coverage, and zero tolerance — will surely lead the People's Army to expel corruption and regenerate, to shed old feathers and be reborn, and with a pure and glorious new political bearing, to write a new chapter in building a strong military and revitalizing the military that is worthy of our revolutionary forebears, worthy of the century-old enterprise, and worthy of this great era.

I

Red flames smelt gold; years forge the soul. The political character of the People's Army is rooted in the years of war and fire, and has accumulated over a journey of nearly one hundred years.

The green pines of Jinggang Mountain remember: Commander Zhu De's carrying pole bore the large characters "Do not take things without permission," manifesting the deep warmth of officers and soldiers sharing hardships alike. The yellow earth of Yan'an bears witness: Chairman Mao's cotton-padded trousers were washed and mended for seven years, his sleeping garments covered in patches, yet he sternly refused special supplies. From "cadres yielding their rations and caring for the wounded and sick" on the Long March, to "leaving the apples for comrades, keeping the thirst for oneself" during the War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea — generation after generation of revolutionary forebears demonstrated through their own conduct: in this People's Army of ours, there are no "official lords" (官老爷) who lord it over others, only comrades and brothers who share life and death.

Today, studying Chairman Xi's earnest admonition to "put aside the airs of officialdom and return to the true character of a revolutionary soldier" is both a matter of restoring and carrying forward the fine traditions of our Party and our military, and a matter of winning the political initiative to defend the red country's mountains and rivers and the historical initiative to build a strong military and revitalize the military.

— Only by eliminating special persons beyond constraint can we break out of the historical cycle (历史周期率).

The poison of "special status" is the key variable that gives rise to the historical cycle. Tracing back through the tunnel of time, countless dynasties that once shone brilliantly were hollowed out at their foundations precisely amid the wine, meat, and music behind vermilion gates by those "special persons" who operated outside the bounds of law, becoming cold footnotes in the historical record that attest to the historical cycle.

In 1945, in the cave dwellings of Yan'an, Mr. Huang Yanpei was deeply troubled and posed the timeless question of how to break out of the historical cycle of "rising swiftly and falling suddenly." Chairman Mao replied with composure: let the people supervise the government. Entering the new era, Chairman Xi provided a second answer: self-revolution (自我革命). Both answers strike at the poison of "special status." Like a bolt of lightning, they pierced through the long dark night of "the realm as family property" (家天下) across a thousand years, using the sharp gaze of the people to keep watch over power, and using the sharp weapon of self-revolution to purge the poison and eliminate abuses, absolutely refusing to allow special persons beyond constraint to appear within the Party.

From firing the first shot against corruption during the Ruijin period, to executing Liu Qingshan and Zhang Zishan in the early days of the founding of New China; from the stern warning to be vigilant against "sugar-coated bullets" (糖衣炮弹), to the new era's anti-corruption declaration of "offending thousands, not letting down 1.4 billion" — it is precisely through the consciousness of relying externally on the supervision of the people and internally on self-revolution, in the ongoing process of practicing the "two answers" and purging the poison of "special status," that our Party has steadied crises, consolidated foundations, and opened new horizons.

— Maintain a high degree of political clarity; always be soldiers of the people.

The military takes up arms to defend the state and raises its forces to protect the mountains and rivers — it is the most solid shield of the red country and cannot tolerate even the slightest taint of privilege. During the revolutionary war years, it was precisely because there was no distinction of high and low, noble and base, that officers and soldiers could be forged into a single piece of steel, creating one miracle of war after another. In peacetime, once privileged thinking takes root and privileged phenomena emerge, they will dissolve trust, scatter military morale, and shake the very foundation on which the military is built and on which victory rests.

The poison of "special status" is an invisible arsenic that corrodes the military soul and undermines military morale. Once contaminated, it is as if one has drunk a bewitching potion and been marked with a death warrant, ultimately falling into the bottomless abyss of corruption, degeneration, and divided loyalties (心怀二志). Some senior military cadres who have fallen from grace in recent years were, on the surface, guilty of economic corruption and misconduct, but in essence they had been poisoned politically by "special status."

The lessons of the past are before us; the warning bell rings long. No matter how times develop or conditions change, we need the battle cry of "follow me," not the arrogance of "charge ahead without me"; we need the deep affection of "sharing the same coat with you" (与子同袍), not the estrangement of rigid barriers. Leading cadres must take the lead in breaking down official authority and eliminating official airs, always bearing in mind "I am a soldier, come from among the common people," always serving as a positive force for being "politically strong," injecting powerful positive energy into solidifying the political character of the People's Army, and ensuring that our military always remains a heroic force that the Party and the people can completely trust.

II

In 1944, the U.S. Army Observer Group visited Yan'an and was deeply struck by the contrast between the vigorous, vibrant Yan'an and the decadent, oppressive Chongqing — two entirely different worlds, two entirely different atmospheres. Upon returning, they lavished praise on the Yan'an style to Song Meiling. After listening, Song Meiling responded: "That is because the Chinese Communist Party has not yet truly tasted the flavor of power." Without question, Song's words carried arrogance and prejudice, yet they also pointed to a truth: human nature often wavers between hardship and ease. In times of bitter cold, sinews are easily tempered and resolve is easily steeled; but once one holds the reins of power and lives in comfort, thoughts of extravagance easily arise, a fondness for pleasure easily grows, and one imperceptibly becomes a special person beyond constraint. This poison of "special status" is a sugar coating wrapped around the handle of power — the most severe test of human nature.

For Communists and revolutionary soldiers, the founding mission (初心) is not a memory sealed away in dust, but an answer sheet that is always fresh; not a momentary oath, but a lifelong commitment. Preventing the taste of power from corrupting the founding mission, and guarding against the poison of "special status" causing one to lose one's way — these are eternal subjects that transcend time and space, political interrogations that are always ongoing.

— Contamination by the poison of "special status" often begins with the dissolution of thought.

"Flies do not bite an egg without a crack." Countless cases warn us that the erosion of the poison of "special status" often begins with the loosening of the ideological dam. When there are reckless thoughts within, there will inevitably be reckless actions without — when power is regarded as a badge of identity rather than a weighty responsibility, when applause is seen as "something I deserve" rather than the people's tribute, the vigilant line of defense quietly falls.

Listen to those belated confessions. Some have painfully stated: "I always felt I was a 'special figure,' that the rules were for managing others, not for managing me." Others have lamented: "I grew accustomed to being surrounded by an entourage, could not bear a single word of honest counsel, and gradually became isolated and alone." These monologues reveal precisely the chain of cause and effect from "reckless thought" to "reckless action": a sense of superiority that places oneself above others leads one to become intoxicated by the illusion of power; a carefully woven "hunting ground" causes one to lose vigilance amid warmth and affection.

The rusting of thought is more fearsome than the rusting of a gun barrel. Once one places oneself above the masses, one becomes like a kite with a broken string, drifting away from the earth in self-inflation. Those figures who were once full of vigor ultimately came to understand behind iron bars: so-called "special status" has always been a pass to the abyss; only by maintaining the purity of the soul and rejecting privileged thinking can one grow the armor to resist temptation.

— One must not only see through it, but also endure it, in order to resist the poison of "special status."

Resisting the poison of "special status" tests not only the momentary clarity to see through the temptations of vanity, but also the long-lasting self-discipline to endure the stirrings of private desire.

The trajectories of degeneration of many fallen senior officials all share the same origin and the same logic. They all understood that enjoying privileges beyond one's station is wrong, that indulging in ease and pleasure violates the rules, and that exercising power arbitrarily and without restraint is improper. Yet when faced with temptations that escalated step by step and fawning flattery, they harbored the hope of getting away with it, relaxed their guard, and ultimately fell.

At first it was merely acquiescing to minor preferential treatment and accepting small conveniences; then came the comfortable enjoyment of privileges and immersion in ease and vanity. The ideological line of defense loosened inch by inch; the bottom line of discipline was breached step by step. From clear self-awareness to numb submersion, from self-restraint to unbridled willfulness — the inner commitment was slowly dissolved by gentle temptation, and the pure founding mission was completely numbed by the taste of power.

The greatest test in the world is not an open and brazen attack, but a covert and creeping corrosion. The taste of power is the most grinding of all; the poison of ease is the most harmful of all. Only when Communists and revolutionary soldiers constantly harbor a heart of self-discipline — seeing through temptation clearly, withstanding the lure of vanity, and holding fast to simplicity — can they escape the trap of "boiling a frog in warm water" and forever preserve their revolutionary character.

— Answer well the "question of the founding mission" (初心之问); guard well the source of a pure heart.

The long river of years surges onward; the fire of faith never extinguishes. For every Communist and revolutionary soldier, directly confronting "why did you join the Party, why did you join the military, why did you become a commander" is absolutely not a perfunctory response for a moment in time, but a solemn answer for an entire lifetime — one that must be constantly cultivated, constantly refined, constantly reflected upon, and constantly advanced.

In February 1950, Chairman Mao stayed in Songjiang. Feeling quite uncomfortable at the sight of the delicious food on the table and the magnificent Western-style villa, after listening to reports from the original Songjiang Provincial Committee and Harbin Municipal Committee, he took up his brush and wrote "study," "struggle," and "do not become contaminated with the style of bureaucratism." It was precisely because he had the people in his heart and responsibility in his heart that Chairman Mao regarded preferential treatment as a burden and deeply detested the phenomenon of becoming divorced from the masses.

Those whose founding mission is pure regard privilege as shackles, even as an enemy. At the very outset of Chairman Xi's leadership of the central work, he formulated the Eight-Point Regulation of the Central Committee and the Ten-Point Regulation of the Central Military Commission, targeting the "Four Winds" (四风), and resolutely rectifying and thoroughly eliminating privileged conduct and bad habits. This is precisely the clear-headed choice illuminated by the founding mission — only by severing the roots of privilege can the nature and purpose of our Party and our military be forever preserved.

The founding mission is easy to hold at the start; it is difficult to uphold throughout. For Communists and revolutionary soldiers, the "question of the founding mission" is an eternal examination, and the source of a pure heart must be guarded for a lifetime. Only by constantly warming the founding mission and constantly examining oneself, not being bewitched by power, not being confused by profit, and not being moved by extravagance, can one remain steadfast in one's founding mission and true to one's original character in the great political examination.

III

Looking back over more than one hundred years of Party history and more than ninety years of military history, from the "Three Main Rules of Discipline and Eight Points for Attention" (三大纪律八项注意) to the Eight-Point Regulation of the Central Committee and the Ten-Point Regulation of the Central Military Commission — it is precisely those springs of discipline tightened from generation to generation that have driven the People's Army through wind and rain, ever victorious.

Can one not see that every one of those "special persons" who fell into the abyss began with "not being fussy about small matters," slid down through "this will be the last time," and ended in "an unmanageable mess"? Once the switch of discipline slips, the floodwaters of power will breach the dam of law and discipline. History has long made clear: the true iron Great Wall lies not only in the sharpness of guns and cannons, but even more in the strictness of discipline; and all "special status" that places itself above the law will ultimately be exposed in the sunlight of discipline.

— The "broken windows effect" (破窗效应) amplifies privileged phenomena.

"A dike of a thousand zhang collapses from the hole made by an ant; a room of a hundred feet burns from a crack in the chimney." Han Feizi's warning from more than two thousand years ago still rings out deafeningly today.

Throughout history, corruption and degeneration have all begun with the loosening of discipline and the loss of small matters. Once the string of discipline slackens even slightly, the ideological dam will quietly begin to seep. Seemingly trivial flaws that appear harmless and go uncorrected will, over time, grow inch by inch and become impossible to reverse, leading Party members and cadres to gradually break through the bottom lines and red lines in a state of unchecked drift, ultimately falling into the abyss of violating discipline and the law.

Even more fearsome is the "broken windows effect." When principal leading cadres fail to inspect and manage for extended periods, or even take the lead in overstepping boundaries, those below will follow suit, causing privileged thinking to spread everywhere and the entire political ecosystem to deteriorate accordingly.

If unhealthy tendencies are not eliminated, righteous spirit cannot be uplifted. Under a corrupt political ecosystem, those who scheme and exploit privilege run rampant, while Party members and cadres who uphold principles and remain upright are instead squeezed out, suppressed, and even subjected to reverse elimination. Only by catching problems early and when they are small, preventing minor issues from becoming major ones, enforcing discipline strictly, and tackling problems head-on — resolutely breaking down the walls of privilege and purging unhealthy tendencies — can a clean and upright political ecosystem be cultivated, and a broad stage be built where the loyal, courageous, and upright can "leap freely as fish in open seas, soar freely as birds in high skies."

— Use the satisfaction of the people and the masses as a "calibration instrument."

The stricter the discipline, the better the image and prestige of leading cadres; the stricter the self-requirements of leading cadres, the higher the satisfaction of the masses and the officers and soldiers. This principle has always operated throughout history and in reality. Governing officials strictly and enforcing discipline strictly may appear to be a constraint on Party members and cadres, but in reality it establishes prestige and credibility, and consolidates military morale and popular sentiment.

History has repeatedly proven that the scale of discipline is the temperature of credibility. Revolutionary forebears dedicated themselves to the Party and the nation, were strict with themselves in serving the public, proactively gave up personal ease and rejected special treatment, shared hardships with officers and soldiers, and weathered storms together with the masses. It was precisely this consciousness of not seeking special treatment and strictly maintaining Party character that made the image of our Party and our military shine brilliantly and won the wholehearted allegiance of the people.

Chairman Xi has pointed out: "Rules are meant to be constraining, so they need to be tight. Being tight naturally makes one uncomfortable, and there is a problem with comfort — the point is to be a little uncomfortable, a little ill at ease. If we are a little uncomfortable and a little ill at ease, the comfort level of the common people will be a little better, their satisfaction will be a little higher, and their feeling toward us will be a little better." This is the iron law of history, and also the call of the times.

— Use strict discipline to manage unconstrained words and actions.

In governing the Party, the state, and the military, strictness is paramount; in establishing the heart, the word, and the deed, discipline is the foundation. Looking back on the journey of suffering and glory, the reason our Party and our military have been able to weather storms with their integrity ever more firm, and to endure repeated trials with their enterprise ever more flourishing, lies importantly in the genetic code of always putting discipline in the forefront — acting swiftly and decisively with a tight pace, rectifying unhealthy tendencies with a strict keynote, and honing conduct with a solid standard.

In 1937, Zhang Lingfu was reinstated to his post two years after shooting his wife dead in a fit of hatred born of passion. That same year, Mao Zedong personally approved the execution of Huang Kegong, who had killed a female student when his attempt to force her into marriage failed. Similar cases, different dispositions — two different underlying characters laid bare. A Nationalist general who committed a grave crime could rely on the privilege of his status and the protection of his connections to escape severe punishment; a meritorious figure in our revolutionary ranks, no matter how many battle achievements he had accumulated, could not be exempted from punishment by virtue of his merits if he broke the law. One lenient, one strict; one loose, one tight — this makes clear: the law and discipline of our military represent the will of the Party and the people; the law shows no favoritism, and the law stands as firm as a mountain; no one may place themselves above the law.

Today, we must likewise tighten up, become stricter, and become more substantive in terms of law and discipline. "Tight" means keeping the string of discipline taut at all times, never relaxing at any moment. "Strict" means zero tolerance for all violations and transgressions, with no "this will be the last time" and no "there are extenuating circumstances." "Substantive" means implementing discipline and rules in every word and action, managing those "free-ranging" (天马行空) thoughts and "willful and reckless" (任性妄为) conduct. Unconstrained words and actions must be struck down the moment they appear and corrected the moment they surface; they must absolutely not be allowed to fester.

Those who enforce discipline must be absolutely firm; those who correct abuses must not yield an inch. One must use thunderous means to break the psychology of hoping to get away with it, use strict and substantive discipline to purge unhealthy tendencies, thoroughly eradicate the soil and conditions in which privilege breeds, and forge a crack force that is strictly disciplined, with a solid work style, loyal and pure.

IV

If the soul of a system is justice, the earth of society will not become barren. A system is like a riverbed — it determines whether the water of social operation flows as a surging clear stream or stagnates as a putrid backwater. Truly stable order lies in casting "the public heart" (公心) into the bones and sinews of the legal system. If the design of a system harbors hidden partiality — using the name of "fairness" to practice "special favor," allowing a minority to place themselves above the rules — then the justice written on paper will ultimately become a mirage.

History has long made clear: when the scales of a system tilt toward power and influence, privilege will grow wildly and erode the foundations of society. Only by ensuring that every rule embodies the essence of fairness, and that every ruling reflects the light of justice, can lofty values and principles be transformed into happiness and beauty that can be felt and reached.

— Let system design align with theoretical orientation.

Truth is like a torch, illuminating the entire system; a system is like a channel, guiding the clear spring of truth. When the light of scientific theory penetrates the page and is deeply embedded in the workings of the legal system, society becomes like a precision timepiece, meshing together a symphony of order and vitality. Conversely, if grand theories hang in mid-air while footsteps sink into the mud, and "principles on paper" and "the path underfoot" point in opposite directions, the cornerstone of trust will silently crumble.

In the long scroll of history, those moments when the sunlight of fairness and justice pierced through the haze have all been moments of passionate embrace between truth and system — from the Party's system of absolute leadership over the military breaking with the feudal military system, to the modern rule of law placing power in a cage — all attest: only by ensuring that every regulation is imbued with the sweetness of truth can the authority of the system stand like a lighthouse, and the torch of faith in the hearts of officers and soldiers burn ever brighter.

Today's China is calibrating its course with the compass of thought. By deeply studying and carefully comprehending the Party's innovative theories, and letting the light of truth of Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era — especially Xi Jinping's Thought on Strengthening the Military (习近平强军思想) — illuminate every vein of governing the military according to law, we must condense nourishment in the words and lines of regulations, let the entitlements that are due be steadily upheld by the arms of the system, and let overstepping privilege be blocked and dissolved before the iron rules. When the seeds of theory take root in the soil of the system, we will ultimately see — a brighter future with more warmth and more strength is growing and flourishing in the concerto of truth and system.

— Let system design align with the orientation of consolidating foundations.

Power is a double-edged sword; once the one who wields it harbors narrow-mindedness and allows thought to rust, it will turn and wound oneself. When a political party holds the power and resources of state governance, a kind of hidden inertia easily takes root and grows in secret — always thinking of lightly turning the gears of the system to find a convenient hidden path for oneself to avoid wind and rain. Little does one know that this seemingly comfortable small door is in fact the door of calamity through which the poison of "special status" enters the body of the political power and erodes the roots of faith.

Starting an enterprise is difficult; maintaining it is even harder. Breaking the old world and seizing power relied on the courage to sacrifice one's life and the popular will of united hearts; but guarding the country and consolidating the foundation of governance tests the clarity to turn the blade inward and the resolve for self-revolution. The most difficult thing of all is to always wage resolute struggle against one's own private interests, to anchor the gears of the system to fairness and justice, to strictly prevent privilege from operating openly under a cloak of legality, and to strictly prevent public power from being perverted into a tool for a minority to seek profit.

The Communist Party of China always holds high the mirror of vigilance and strictly prevents the operation of the system from deviating from the right path. Our system design is deeply rooted in the underlying color of fairness and justice, ensuring that power always operates in the sunlight, that resources always flow within the rules, that overstepping privilege has nowhere to hide, that backroom dealings lose their space, and that public power always serves the warmth and welfare of every household. This system, rooted in the people and guarding justice, protects tens of millions of ordinary citizens in living and working in peace and contentment and sharing in the fruits of development. A political power that so accords with the great way will surely remain as steady as a rock through wind and rain, and grow ever stronger with the passage of time.

— Let system design align with the direction of popular sentiment.

The people's heart is like a mirror — it can reflect one's innermost being; the people's heart is like a ruler — it can measure the legal system. In the mind of the leader, the two characters "people's heart" (民心) weigh a thousand jun. This is not only a deep and abiding affection, but also the benchmark for governing the state. Our Party's theories are created from practice; the Party's will is tempered in history — at bottom, all of this is the echo of the voices of hundreds of millions of people. The greatest politics, in the final analysis, is the question of whether the hearts of the people are won or lost.

A good system must necessarily accord with popular sentiment and guard justice. One must constantly use the mirror of the people's heart to examine all kinds of laws and regulations, and routinely calibrate systemic deviations. For the welfare and benefits that align with military and popular sentiment and align with the essential needs of performing one's duties, these must be solidified, upheld, and implemented with rigidity. For special treatment that breeds privilege, departs from the public heart, and is resented by officers and soldiers, it must be resolutely cleaned up, banned, and thoroughly rectified. Use the iron hand of the system to break down the chaos of privilege and sever private greed and desire, so that special persons who overstep their authority and act arbitrarily, and special acts that violate rules and transgress boundaries, have absolutely nowhere to exist — truly allowing every system to shine with the brilliance of fairness and justice.

Ahead lies the vastness of the sea of stars; behind us are the hopes of hundreds of millions of soldiers and civilians. Let us forge a torrent of steel with iron discipline, sweep all the poison of "special status" into the dustbin of history, and forge the sturdy grass in fierce winds and the true gold in raging fire of the new era and the new journey!

这是黄钟大吕，响彻在每一名党员干部的心头；这是高悬的明镜，照鉴每一名革命军人的灵魂；这是锋利刀刃，剜除我党我军肌体深处残留的沉疴流弊。 时光无言，江河为证。党的十八大以来，从查处郭伯雄、徐才厚、房峰辉、张阳，到查处何卫东、苗华、何宏军，再到查处张又侠、刘振立，一记记反腐重拳如惊雷划破长空，昭示着法纪的公正与威严。在我党我军的神圣殿堂里，没有免罪的护身符，只有忠诚的试金石。党中央、中央军委正风反腐无禁区、全覆盖、零容忍的决绝强韧，必将引领人民军队去腐生肌、换羽新生，以纯洁光荣的崭新政治面貌，书写无愧于革命先辈、无愧于百年基业、无愧于伟大时代的强军兴军新篇章。 一 赤焰熔金，岁月铸魂。人民军队的政治本色，植根于烽火岁月，积淀于近百年征程。 井冈山的青松记得，朱德军长的扁担刻着“不准乱拿”的大字，彰显着官兵同甘共苦的深厚温情。延安的黄土铭记，毛主席棉裤洗补七载、睡衣补丁累累，仍严词拒绝特殊供给。从长征路上“干部让粮、照顾伤病”，到抗美援朝“苹果留给战友、干渴留给自己”，一代代革命先辈用躬身践行昭示：我们这支人民军队，没有高高在上的“官老爷”，只有生死与共的战友兄弟。 今天，学习习主席“放下官架子、回归革命军人本色”的谆谆告诫，既是为了恢复和弘扬我党我军优良传统，也是为了赢得捍卫红色江山的政治主动和强军兴军的历史主动。 ——清除不受约束的特殊人，才能跳出历史周期率。 “特殊”之毒是导致历史周期率的关键变量。沿着时光隧道回溯，无数烜赫一时的王朝，恰恰是在朱门酒肉笙歌中，被那些游离于法度之外的“特殊人”蛀空根基，沦为史册中佐证历史周期率的冰冷注脚。 1945年，延安窑洞里，黄炎培先生忧思难平，发出如何跳出“其兴也勃焉，其亡也忽焉”历史周期率的千古一问。毛主席从容作答：让人民来监督政府。进入新时代，习主席又给出第二个答案：自我革命。这两个答案，都剑指“特殊”之毒，如一道闪电，划破了千年来“家天下”的沉沉暗夜，用人民的犀利目光盯住权力，用自我革命的锐利武器肃毒除弊，决不允许党内出现不受约束的特殊人。 从瑞金时期打响反腐第一枪，到新中国成立初期处决刘青山、张子善；从警惕“糖衣炮弹”的严正警醒，到新时代“得罪千百人、不负十四亿”的反腐宣言——我们党正是以外靠人民监督、内靠自我革命的自觉，在不断践行“两个答案”、肃清“特殊”之毒的进程中扶危定倾、固本开新。 ——保持政治上的高度清醒，始终做人民的子弟兵。 军队执干戈以卫社稷、举甲兵以护山河，是红色江山最坚实的屏障，容不得半点特权之弊。革命战争年代，正是因为没有尊卑贵贱之别，官兵才能铸成一块整钢，创造一个又一个战争奇迹；和平时期，一旦特权思想滋生、特权现象冒头，就会消解信任、涣散军心，动摇立军之本、胜战之基。 “特殊”之毒，是腐蚀军魂、瓦解军心的无形砒霜，沾染后便如同喝了迷魂汤、贴了催命符，最终堕入贪腐变质、心怀二志的万丈深渊。近年来落马的一些军队高级干部，表面是经济贪腐、作风败坏，本质是政治上中了“特殊”之毒。 殷鉴在前，警钟长鸣。无论时代如何发展、条件如何变化，我们都需要“跟我上”的呐喊，而不是“给我上”的傲慢；都需要“与子同袍”的深情，而不是森严壁垒的隔膜。领导干部必须带头破官威、除官气，时刻牢记“我是一个兵，来自老百姓”，始终做“政治上强”的正向因子，为强固人民军队的政治本色注入强大正能量，确保我军始终是党和人民完全可以信赖的英雄部队。 二 1944年，美军观察组踏访延安，深感朝气蓬勃的延安与奢靡沉郁的重庆相较，迥然是两个世界、两种气象。他们归来后向宋美龄盛赞延安作风。宋美龄听罢回应，“那是中共还没有真正尝到权力的滋味”。毋庸置疑，宋氏话中带着傲慢与偏见，却也挑明了一个道理：人性常在清贫与安逸之间摇摆。苦寒之时，筋骨易砺，心志易坚；一旦手握权柄、身处安逸，奢靡之念易生，享乐之心易长，不知不觉就成为不受约束的特殊人。这“特殊”之毒，是权柄包覆的糖衣，是对人性最严峻的考验。 对共产党人、革命军人而言，初心不是尘封的记忆，而是常新的答卷；不是一时的誓言，而是一生的坚守。防止权力滋味诱变初心，防范“特殊”之毒迷失心志，是穿越时空的永恒课题，是永在路上的政治拷问。 ——“特殊”之毒的沾染，往往是从思想上涣散开始的。 “苍蝇不叮无缝的蛋”。无数案例警示我们，“特殊”之毒的侵蚀，往往始于思想堤坝的松动。内有妄思，外必妄动——当权力被视作身份的勋章而非责任的重托，当掌声被视为“我应享有”而非人民的礼赞，警惕的防线便悄然失守。 听听那些迟来的忏悔吧。有人痛陈：“总觉得自己是‘特殊人物’，规矩是管别人的，不是管我的。”有人追悔：“习惯了前呼后拥，听不得半句诤言，慢慢就成了孤家寡人。”这些独白，揭示的正是从“妄思”到“妄动”的因果链路：自以为高人一等的优越感，让人沉迷于权力的幻觉；精心编织的“围猎场”，让人在温情中丧失警觉。 思想的锈蚀比枪膛的锈蚀更可怕。一旦把自己凌驾于群众之上，就会像断了线的风筝，在自我膨胀中远离大地。那些曾经意气风发的身影，最终在铁窗里悔悟、读懂：所谓“特殊”，从来都是走向深渊的通行证；只有保持灵魂的纯净，摒弃特权思想，才能长出抵御诱惑的铠甲。 ——既要识得破、更要忍得过，才能抵御“特殊”之毒。 抵御“特殊”之毒，考验的不只是识得破浮华诱惑的一时清醒，更是忍得过私欲躁动的长久自持。 很多落马高官的堕落轨迹，皆是同源同理。他们都懂得，特权享受逾矩，安逸享乐违规，权力任性失度。可面对步步加码、大献殷勤的诱惑，却心存侥幸、放松戒备，最终败下阵来。 起初只是默许细微优待、接受小小便利，继而坦然享受特权、沉迷安逸浮华。思想防线一寸寸松动，纪律底线一步步失守。从清醒自知到麻木沉沦，从心存克制到肆意妄为，内心的坚守被温柔的诱惑慢慢瓦解，纯粹的初心被权力的滋味彻底麻醉。 世间最大的考验，不是明火执仗的攻击，而是暗中侵染的腐蚀。权力之味最是磨人，安逸之毒最是伤人。共产党人、革命军人唯有常怀律己之心，看得清诱惑、扛得住浮华、守得住清贫，才能挣脱“温水煮青蛙”的陷阱，永葆革命本色。 ——回答好“初心之问”，守护好净心之源。 岁月长河奔涌，信仰之火不灭。对于每一名共产党人、革命军人而言，直面“入党干什么、当兵干什么、为将干什么”，绝不是一时一刻的敷衍应声，而是一生一世的庄严作答，必须常修常炼、常悟常进。 1950年2月，毛主席在松江下榻，面对桌上的美味佳肴和富丽堂皇的西式别墅颇觉不适，在听取原松江省委和哈尔滨市委汇报后，提笔写下“学习”“奋斗”和“不要沾染官僚主义作风”。正因为心中有民、心中有责，毛主席将优待视为包袱，对脱离群众现象深恶痛绝。 初心纯粹之人，将特权视为桎梏甚至寇仇。习主席主持中央工作伊始，即制定中央八项规定、军委十项规定，剑指“四风”，对特权做派、不良习气坚决纠治、除弊务尽。这正是初心映照下的清醒抉择——唯有斩断特权根系，方能永葆我党我军性质宗旨本色。 初心易得，始终难守。对共产党人、革命军人而言，“初心之问”是永恒考题，净心之源需终身守护。唯有常温初心、常省己身，不为权迷、不为利惑、不为奢移，方能在政治大考中初心如磐、本色如初。 三 回望百余年党史、九十余载军史，从“三大纪律八项注意”，到中央八项规定、军委十项规定，正是那代代拧紧的纪律发条，驱策着人民军队穿越风雨、无往不胜。 可曾见，那些跌落深渊的“特殊人”，哪个不是从“不拘小节”开始，从“下不为例”滑落，终至“不可收拾”？纪律的开关一旦滑丝，权力的洪水便会冲毁法纪的堤坝。历史早已昭示：真正的钢铁长城，不仅在枪炮之利，更在纪律之严；而所有凌驾于法度之上的“特殊”，终将在纪律的阳光下照出原形。 ——“破窗效应”放大特权现象。 “千丈之堤，以蝼蚁之穴溃；百尺之室，以突隙之烟焚。”韩非子两千多年前的警示，至今读来仍振聋发聩。 纵观历史，腐化堕落皆起于纪律松弛、小节失守。纪律之弦一旦稍有松弛，思想堤坝便会悄然渗水。看似无伤大雅、无人纠治的微小纰漏，日积月累便会得寸进尺、积重难返，让党员干部在放任自流中逐步突破底线红线，最终堕入违纪违法的深渊。 更可怕的是“破窗效应”。主要领导干部长期失察失管，甚至带头逾矩，就会上行下效，使特权思想蔓延遍野，整个政治生态随之恶化。 歪风不除，正气难扬。污浊的政治生态之下，投机钻营、依附特权者大行其道，坚守原则、刚正不阿的党员干部反受排挤打压甚至被逆淘汰。唯有抓早抓小、防微杜渐，从严执纪、较真碰硬，坚决破除特权壁垒、肃清歪风邪气，方能营造风清气正的政治生态，搭建起让忠勇正直之士“海阔凭鱼跃，天高任鸟飞”的广阔舞台。 ——将人民群众满意度作为“校正器”。 纪律严一点，领导干部的形象威信就好一点；领导干部自我要求严一点，群众和官兵的满意度就高一点。这样的哲理，始终在历史和现实中发挥作用。从严治吏、从严执纪，看似对党员干部是一种约束，实则立起了威信公信、凝聚了军心民心。 历史反复证明，纪律的尺度，就是公信的温度。革命先辈以身许党许国、严于律己奉公，主动舍弃个人安逸、摒弃特殊待遇，与官兵同甘共苦、与群众风雨同舟。正是这种不搞特殊、严守党性的自觉，让我党我军的形象熠熠生辉，赢得万众归心。 习主席指出：“规矩是起约束作用的，所以要紧一点。紧一点自然就不舒服了，舒适度就有问题了，就是要不舒服一点、不自在一点，我们不舒服一点、不自在一点，老百姓的舒适度就好一点、满意度就高一点，对我们的感觉就好一点。”这是历史的铁律，也是时代的呼唤。 ——用严格纪律管住不受约束的言行。 治党治国治军，从严为要；立心立言立功，纪律为基。回望苦难辉煌的征程，我党我军之所以能历经风雨而风骨弥坚、屡经考验而基业长青，重要的基因密码就在于始终把纪律挺在前面，以紧的步伐雷厉风行、以严的基调纠风治弊、以实的标准正风砺行。 1937年，张灵甫在因情生恨枪杀自己妻子两年后官复原职；同年，毛泽东亲自批准对逼婚不成打死女学生的黄克功处以极刑。相似的案件、不同的处置，照见两种底色。国民党将领犯下重罪竟能依仗身份特权、人脉庇护而逃脱严惩；我们革命队伍中的功臣，即使屡立战功，只要以身试法就不能因功脱罪。一宽一严，一松一紧，鲜明昭示：我军的法纪代表的是党和人民的意志，法不容情、法度如山，任何人都不得凌驾于法律之上。 今天，我们同样要从法纪上紧起来、严起来、实起来。紧，就是时刻绷紧纪律这根弦，任何时候都不松懈；严，就是对一切违规逾矩行为零容忍，不搞下不为例、情有可原；实，就是把纪律规矩落实到一言一行，管住那些“天马行空”的念头、“任性妄为”的做派。对不受约束的言行，必须露头就打、现形就纠，绝不能养痈遗患。 执纪者必刚性十足，纠弊者必寸步不让。要以雷霆手段破除侥幸心理，以严实纪律肃清不良风气，彻底铲除特权滋生的土壤和条件，锻造纪律严明、作风过硬、忠诚纯粹的过硬队伍。 四 制度的灵魂若是公正，社会的大地便不会荒芜。制度如河床，决定社会运行之水是奔涌清流，还是淤塞腐臭。真正稳固的秩序，在于将“公心”熔铸于法制筋骨之中。若制度设计暗藏偏私，以“公平”之名行“特惠”之实，让少数人凌驾于规则之上，那么写在纸上的正义终将沦为镜花水月。 历史早已昭示：当制度的天平向权势倾斜，特权便会野蛮生长，侵蚀社会的根基。唯有让每一项规则都蕴含公平的精义，让每一次裁决都折射正义的光芒，才能让崇高的价值理念转化为可感可及的幸福美好。 ——让制度设计契合理论导向。 真理如炬，照彻制度体系；制度如渠，引流真理清泉。当科学理论的光芒穿透纸背，深深楔入法制运行的肌理，社会便如精密的钟表，咬合出秩序与活力的交响。反之，若宏论悬于半空，脚步陷于泥沼，“纸上道理”与“脚下道路”南辕北辙，信任的基石便会在无声中崩裂。 历史长卷里，那些让公平正义的阳光刺破阴霾的瞬间，无不是真理与制度的热烈相拥——从党对军队绝对领导制度对封建军制的决裂，到现代法治对权力的笼锁，皆印证着：唯有让每一部法规都浸润着真理的甘甜，才能让制度的威严如灯塔般矗立，让官兵心中的信仰之炬愈燃愈亮。 今日之中国，正以思想的罗盘校准航向。深学细悟党的创新理论，让习近平新时代中国特色社会主义思想特别是习近平强军思想的真理之光，照亮依法治军的每一条脉络。要在法规的字里行间凝结养分，让应有的权益被制度的臂弯稳稳托举，让越界的特权在铁规面前碰壁消弭。当理论的种子在制度的土壤里扎根，我们终将看见——更有温度、更具力量的光明未来，正在真理与制度的协奏中拔节生长。 ——让制度设计契合固本取向。 权力是一把双刃剑，执剑之人一旦心生偏狭、思想锈蚀，就会反伤己身。当一个政党执掌国家治理的权柄与资源，一种隐蔽的惰性便容易潜滋暗长——总想着轻拨制度齿轮，为自身寻一处规避风雨的便利暗道。殊不知，这看似安逸的小门，实则是“特殊”之毒侵入政权肌体、侵蚀信仰根基的祸乱之门。 创业维艰，守业更难。打破旧世界、夺取政权，靠的是舍生忘死的勇气与众志成城的民心；而守好江山、巩固执政根基，考验的却是刀刃向内的清醒与自我革命的定力。最难之处，莫过于始终与一己私利作坚决斗争，让制度的齿轮锚定公平正义，严防特权披着合法外衣大行其道，严防公权异化为少数人谋利的工具。 中国共产党始终高悬警醒之镜，严防制度运行偏离正道。我们的制度设计，深植公平正义的底色，让权力始终在阳光下运行，让资源始终在规则内流转，让越界特权无处遁形，让暗箱操作失去空间，让公权始终服务万家冷暖。这套扎根人民、守护公义的制度体系，护佑万千百姓安居乐业、共享发展成果。如此顺应大道的政权，必能历经风雨而稳如磐石，跨越岁月而历久弥坚。 ——让制度设计契合民心所向。
