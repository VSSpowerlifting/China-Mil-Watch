Officers and Soldiers' Family Affairs Are Tied to Military Morale — When the "Small Family" Is Stable and Harmonious, the "Greater Family's" Cause Flourishes

官兵家事连着军心士气，"小家"安定和睦，"大家"事业兴旺
PLA Daily (解放军报) 27 May 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
PLA Daily's sixth installment in a series on the 正确政绩观 ("correct outlook on political achievement") documents how Party committees in a Rocket Force brigade, a Northern Theater Command Army brigade, and an Eastern Theater Command Army brigade are institutionalizing welfare casework — coordinating hospital access at the PLA General Hospital, brokering civilian employment for military spouses, adjusting mission assignments to accommodate family medical emergencies, and maintaining brigade-level difficulty-resolution lists — to reduce family-driven morale problems among enlisted personnel and technical backbone members. The series documents a PLA-wide framing effort that repositions soldier welfare work as a combat-readiness input rather than a political-work afterthought, though the articles do not establish whether the described mechanisms are new policy mandates or local initiatives being held up as models.
Translation

"Small Family" Stable and Harmonious, "Greater Family's" Cause Flourishing

— News Investigation No. 6 on Party Committees and Organs at All Levels Helping Grassroots Officers and Soldiers Overcome Difficulties with the Correct Outlook on Political Achievement (正确政绩观)

■ PLA Daily Reporters Peng Hongxia, Zhang Dongpan, Peng Bingjie

"The child's condition has stabilized and improved. I feel an unprecedented sense of relief, and there will be far fewer conflicts at home." In early summer, Shi Chengliang, a Grade-2 Master Sergeant (二级军士长) in a certain brigade of the Rocket Force, took his second son to the hospital for a follow-up examination. Upon learning that the treatment results had exceeded expectations, much of the pressure that had been weighing on him dissipated.

Shi Chengliang and his wife have three children, who are ordinarily cared for by his wife alone at their hometown in Shandong Province. After their second son was diagnosed with tethered spinal cord syndrome, the family's previously stable life was completely upended: Shi Chengliang was not by his child's side, consumed with worry yet powerless to share the burden; his wife toiled day and night, anxious about the child's condition, exhausted in body and spirit with no one to turn to.

"The economic burden and psychological pressure compounded each other, and friction between me and my wife inevitably increased," said Shi Chengliang. Ever since the child fell ill, disharmonious factors affecting the family relationship had gradually emerged.

"As the saying goes, 'when the family is at peace, all things prosper.' Whether officers' and soldiers' families are harmonious is not a purely 'private family matter' — it is a major issue that affects military morale and unit stability." A leader of the brigade explained that they treat helping officers and soldiers manage their families and foster a harmonious atmosphere as a "Party committee project" (党委工程), actively working to relieve officers and soldiers who encounter family conflicts and difficulties.

This leader told reporters that when the brigade Party committee studied solutions to Shi Chengliang's "nagging problem," it concluded that only by thoroughly resolving the difficulty of getting the child medical treatment could the underlying threat to Shi Chengliang's family relations be eliminated at the source. To that end, they made contact through multiple channels and coordinated with the PLA General Hospital to provide treatment for Shi Chengliang's second son.

As it happened, just as the hospital notified Shi Chengliang to bring the child to Beijing for examination, he received orders to carry out a mission at a location in northwest China. Upon learning of the situation, the brigade Party committee deliberated and decided to temporarily adjust the mission personnel, ensuring that Shi Chengliang could accompany his child to the hospital without worry.

Moreover, once Shi Chengliang met the conditions for his family to join him at his duty station, the brigade organs immediately processed the family relocation (家属随军) and also helped complete the enrollment procedures for his child. Seeing one concrete difficulty after another effectively resolved, Shi Chengliang's wife was deeply moved: "I will do everything in my power to support my husband in serving with peace of mind, and I will absolutely not let down the organization's care and generosity."

"When the 'small family' is stable and harmonious, the 'greater family's' cause flourishes." On the topic of "officers' and soldiers' family affairs being tied to military morale," Political Commissar Ma of a certain brigade under the Northern Theater Command Army also had profound feelings. During the interview, he recounted an experience.

On one occasion, the brigade's "Political Commissar's Mailbox" received an anonymous letter: "My spouse and I have been living apart in different locations for years. The heavy workload and family chores have left me physically and mentally exhausted, and marital conflicts have grown increasingly frequent…" In just a few words, a military spouse's bitterness and helplessness were laid bare.

After reading the letter, Political Commissar Ma was deeply troubled and decided to investigate further. Through various means, the brigade organs eventually identified the letter writer — the wife of a technical backbone (技术骨干) in one of the companies.

In a candid heart-to-heart conversation, this backbone member revealed the truth: the unit had been away for extended field training exercises with heavy mission loads, leaving him no time to care for his family; his wife was busy with her own work and also had to care for elderly parents in poor health at home, leaving her worn out by the dual pressures of work and family. Over the past six months or so, the two had communicated less and less, and phone calls frequently ended in arguments — the husband felt his wife did not understand his difficulties, while the wife felt the husband ignored her hardships. The prolonged emotional drain had made this backbone member withdrawn and silent, with no energy for work or training.

After getting a clear picture of the situation, the brigade Party committee tackled the blockages and pain points through multiple channels: on one hand, they proactively coordinated with local education authorities, leveraging the military-civilian co-construction work mechanism to help this military spouse successfully secure employment at a kindergarten near the unit's garrison, resolving the hardship of living apart in different locations; on the other hand, the organs dispatched cadres to separately conduct ideological communication work with both husband and wife, guiding them to view things more from each other's perspective, to empathize with each other's difficulties through role reversal, and to be grateful for each other's contributions.

As concrete difficulties were resolved and family relations were gradually sorted out, this backbone member quickly regained his working form. In a skills competition organized by the brigade, he won the top prize for "proficiency in one specialty with competence in multiple skills" (一专多能).

The brigade organs drew broader lessons from this case, focusing on concrete problems affecting the harmony of officers' and soldiers' families — sorting out a list of difficulties to resolve, studying countermeasures item by item, and pressing ahead with implementation.

"In the past, we always worried that family conflicts among officers and soldiers would affect safety and stability. But really, what family is perfect and flawless? The key is whether problems can be discovered in time and resolved effectively." Not long ago, a certain brigade of the Eastern Theater Command Army organized a major discussion on "What is the correct outlook on political achievement?" (什么是正确的政绩观). The remarks of one brigade leader resonated deeply among officers and soldiers.

This clear-headed understanding grew from deep reflection. Previously, the brigade had conducted an in-depth investigation into the family situations of officers and soldiers, and the results were unexpected: many officers and soldiers faced real problems affecting family harmony, yet the vast majority had not raised them with the organization. When asked why, some felt "the organization should not have to worry about family matters," others felt "family shame should not be aired in public," and still others believed "family difficulties involve personal privacy."

"Only when the organization takes the initiative to step forward will officers and soldiers feel a measure of warmth." To that end, the brigade explored and innovated mechanisms for resolving difficulties: the brigade organs established a cooperative mechanism with local judicial, civil affairs, and people's armed forces departments to promptly follow up and resolve urgent and difficult matters involving serious illness or legal disputes (涉法涉诉) affecting officers, soldiers, and their family members; companies and battalions regularly investigate changes in officers' and soldiers' family circumstances, and on the basis of respecting the wishes and privacy of officers and soldiers, formulate plans for resolving difficulties; cadres and backbone members are encouraged to routinely conduct heart-to-heart conversations to ensure that early signs are detected early and problems are resolved early.

During the interviews, reporters found that on the desks of the brigade's leaders, there was a list of difficulties to be resolved. Children's school enrollment, family members' employment, serious illness relief… behind each item of need, the person responsible and the progress of the work were clearly noted. Pointing to the list on his desk, one brigade leader stated candidly: "Improving the capability to win battles is the greatest political achievement for commanders at all levels. Only when officers and soldiers are free from worries at the rear can they go into battle unburdened and devote themselves wholeheartedly to training and war preparation."

【Do Not Let Officers and Soldiers Take "Burdens" to Their Battle Positions】 Reporters visited barracks after barracks. Party committees and organs at all levels, guided by the correct outlook on political achievement (正确政绩观), are working hard to help grassroots officers and soldiers overcome difficulties — making great efforts to resolve problems related to children's schooling, welfare benefits, and legal rights protection, handling the heartwarming matters that officers and soldiers most care about. (Zhang Tianye, Li Haoqi, Shao Bokang)

Eyewitness Account

The Organization Is Our Greatest Pillar of Support

■ Grade-2 Master Sergeant of a Certain Rocket Force Brigade, Shi Chengliang

The moment the doctor told me that my child had begun to recover after treatment, my eyes grew moist. After several years of traveling from place to place seeking treatment, the child's condition has finally seen a ray of hope, and my wife and I can finally breathe a sigh of relief.

Looking back on these years of seeking medical care, I have a thousand feelings in my heart — there was hardship, there was warmth, but above all there is gratitude toward the organization. After the child fell ill, my wife and I had more arguments and disagreements due to living apart in different locations and the financial pressure. The unit's leaders saw this clearly and took it to heart, doing everything they could to help us relieve our worries and resolve our conflicts.

The brigade processed the family relocation for my wife, helping us end years of living apart. The enrollment procedures for the child were all handled by the organs on our behalf. With the whole family together, we face difficulties and solve problems together — there are fewer arguments, more warmth, and I feel far more settled in my heart.

When the timing of accompanying my child to Beijing for treatment conflicted with a unit mission, the brigade decisively coordinated arrangements so that I could accompany my child through the surgery with peace of mind. During the child's post-operative recovery, brigade leaders called multiple times to ask about the recovery progress. This meticulous care made my wife and me truly feel the warmth of the organization. In the past, my wife often complained that I had no time to look after the child; now, she always urges me to work hard in the unit.

As my wife wished, I have channeled the organization's care into the drive to strive. For two consecutive years I have achieved outstanding results in the brigade's professional skills competitions, and I have also participated in drafting and revising multiple professional training manuals.

In recent years, during the discharge seasons, whenever comrades-in-arms have consulted me about whether to stay or leave the service, I always draw on my own experience to tell them: "On the question of staying or leaving, listen to the organization — because the organization is our greatest pillar of support."

(Compiled by PLA Daily reporter Peng Hongxia)

Sincerely Resolving Problems — Actions Speak Louder Than Words

■ Wang Donglei, Staff Officer, Human Resources Section, a Certain Northern Theater Command Army Brigade

In recent years, as the military's combat-realistic training (实战化训练) has deepened, the training tasks of grassroots officers and soldiers have grown heavier and the pace of work faster. Long-term separation from family and infrequent reunions have left many officers and soldiers facing a variety of concrete difficulties that affect family harmony.

During one visit and investigation, a company commander's words were filled with bitterness: while on field training in the desert for several months, he led officers and soldiers in battling wind and sand to overcome training challenges, while his pregnant wife made repeated solo trips to the hospital for prenatal checkups and also managed the household and cared for the elderly. Over time, estrangement between husband and wife was inevitable.

This company commander's experience is representative of many officers and soldiers. Seemingly ordinary family matters — getting along as a couple, children's schooling, caring for elderly parents — are often what consume officers' and soldiers' energy the most and affect their morale the most. If worries about the rear are not properly resolved, officers and soldiers will find it difficult to devote themselves fully to training and war preparation. Resolving family conflicts among officers and soldiers and promoting family harmony fundamentally requires identifying the root of the problem and applying targeted measures to break through it.

"Sincerely resolving problems — actions speak louder than words." The brigade Party committee has a clear-headed understanding of this and has introduced a series of practical measures: comprehensively optimizing the system for officers' and soldiers' leave and rotation, giving priority to ensuring home visits and leave for those facing family difficulties; proactively coordinating with relevant local departments to make great efforts to resolve concrete difficulties such as employment placement for military spouses, school and kindergarten enrollment for children, and relief for families in hardship; regularly organizing mental health counseling sessions, forums for officers', soldiers', and their families' members, and other activities to help servicemembers and their families relieve emotional stress and resolve conflicts, eliminating estrangement and fostering affection through deepening mutual understanding.

"When officers and soldiers have difficulties, the organization is their backstop." Treating officers' and soldiers' family matters as major matters, important matters, and matters close to the heart — and doing everything possible to help everyone resolve the "three rear-area concerns" (三后) problems — is the only way to ensure that officers and soldiers can devote themselves wholeheartedly to their work and careers, and to consolidate strength for achieving the centenary goal of army building (建军一百年奋斗目标) on schedule.

(Compiled by PLA Daily reporter Zhang Dongpan)

“小家”安定和睦 “大家”事业兴旺 ——各级党委机关以正确政绩观为基层官兵解难帮困新闻调查之六 ■解放军报记者 彭洪霞 张东盼 彭冰洁 “孩子病情稳定好转，心里感到前所未有的轻松，家里也会少不少矛盾。”初夏时节，火箭军某旅二级军士长石成良带着二儿子去医院做完检查，得知治疗效果好于预期，堆积在心里的压力散去不少。 石成良和妻子育有3个孩子，平常由远在山东老家的妻子独自照顾，二儿子查出患有脊髓栓系综合征后，原本安稳的生活被彻底打破：石成良不在孩子身边，成天牵挂却无力分担；妻子日夜操劳又担心孩子病情，心力交瘁却无处诉说。 “经济负担和精神压力交织叠加，我和爱人的磕磕绊绊难免增多。”石成良说，自从孩子生病后，影响家庭关系的不和谐因素渐渐出现。 “都说‘家和万事兴’，官兵家庭是否和谐和睦，不是单纯的‘家事’，而是影响军心士气和部队稳定的大事。”该旅一位领导介绍，他们把帮助官兵经营家庭、营造和谐氛围作为一项“党委工程”来抓，积极为遇到家庭矛盾问题的官兵解难纾困。 这位领导告诉记者，该旅党委围绕石成良的“挠头事”研究解决办法时感到，只有彻底解决好孩子的看病就医难题，才能从源头上消除影响石成良家庭关系的隐患。为此，他们多方联系，协调解放军总医院为石成良的二儿子进行治疗。 不巧的是，医院刚通知石成良带上孩子赴北京检查，他却接到赴西北某地执行任务的通知。得知情况，该旅党委研究决定，临时调整任务人员，保障石成良安心带孩子看病。 不仅如此，石成良符合随军安置条件后，该旅机关第一时间为他办理了家属随军，还帮他办好了孩子入学手续。眼见一个个现实难题得到有效解决，石成良的妻子感动不已：“我一定全力支持爱人安心服役，绝不辜负组织的关怀厚爱。” “‘小家’安定和睦，‘大家’事业兴旺。”谈及“官兵家事连着军心士气”这一话题，北部战区陆军某旅马政委同样感触颇深。采访中，他给记者讲起一段经历。 一次，该旅“政委信箱”收到一封匿名信：“我与爱人常年两地分居，繁重工作和家庭琐事让我身心俱疲，夫妻矛盾日渐增多……”寥寥数语，道出一名军属的心酸与无奈。 读完来信，马政委揪心之余，决定进一步了解情况。该旅机关通过多种方式，终于弄清了写信人的身份——某连一名技术骨干的妻子。 一番促膝长谈，这名骨干道出实情：部队长期在外驻训、任务繁重，他顾不上照顾家庭；妻子平时工作很忙，还要照顾家中体弱多病的老人，工作和家庭的双重压力让她疲惫不堪。近半年，两人沟通越来越少，打电话时常一言不合就吵——丈夫觉得妻子不体谅自己的难处，妻子觉得丈夫无视自己的辛苦。长期的精神内耗，让这名骨干变得沉默寡言，工作训练提不起劲头。 摸清情况后，该旅党委多管齐下打通堵点瘀点：一方面主动对接地方教育部门，依托军地共建相关工作机制，帮助这名军嫂成功入职部队驻地一家幼儿园，解除了两地分居的烦恼；另一方面，机关派出干部分头为夫妻二人做好思想沟通工作，引导他们多站在对方的视角，换位思考体谅对方的难处、感恩对方的付出。 随着现实难题被解决、家庭关系慢慢捋顺，这名骨干迅速找回了工作状态，在旅里组织的比武中，一举夺得“一专多能”桂冠。 该旅机关举一反三，聚焦影响官兵家庭和谐的现实问题，梳理解难清单、逐项研究对策、加紧推进落实。 “过去，我们总担心官兵家庭矛盾影响安全稳定。其实，哪有完美无瑕的家庭？关键在于有问题能不能及时发现、有效解决。”前不久，东部战区陆军某旅组织一场“什么是正确的政绩观”大讨论，一位旅领导的发言，在官兵中引起共鸣。 清醒的认识，源于深刻的思考。此前，该旅围绕官兵家庭情况组织一次深入调研，结果出乎意料：不少官兵面临影响家庭和谐的实际问题，但大多数人并未向组织开口。问及原因，有人觉得“不能让组织为家事操心”，有人感到“家丑不可外扬”，还有人认为“家庭困难涉及个人隐私”。 “组织主动靠前一步，官兵才能心暖一分。”为此，该旅探索创新解难工作机制：旅机关与驻地司法、民政部门和人武部建立协作机制，对官兵及家庭成员重大疾病、涉法涉诉等急难事项及时跟进解决；营连定期排查官兵家庭变故等情况，在尊重官兵意愿和隐私基础上制订解难方案；鼓励干部骨干常态开展谈心交心，确保苗头早发现、问题早解决。 采访中记者发现，该旅领导的办公桌上，都有一份解难清单。子女入学、家属就业、大病救助……每一条需求背后，都清晰标注了承办人和工作进度。指着桌上的解难清单，该旅一位领导坦言：“提高打赢能力是各级带兵人最大的政绩，后顾无忧才能让官兵轻装上阵、心无旁骛投入练兵备战。” 【不让官兵带着“包袱”上战位】记者走访座座军营，各级党委机关以正确政绩观为基层官兵解难帮困，下大力解决子女入学、福利待遇、涉法维权等问题，办好官兵心之所向的暖心事。（张田野、李浩琦、邵博康） 亲历者说 组织是最大的依靠 ■火箭军某旅二级军士长 石成良 当医生告诉我，孩子经过治疗开始好转的那一刻，我的眼眶湿润了。经过好几年的奔波治疗，孩子的病情终于迎来曙光，我和爱人终于可以松一口气了。 回顾这几年的求医路，我心中感慨万千，其中既有艰辛也有温暖，但更多的是对组织的感恩。孩子生病后，我和爱人因两地分居、经济压力等原因，多了一些争吵和分歧。单位领导看在眼里、急在心上，想方设法帮我们排忧解难、消除矛盾。 旅里为我爱人办理了随军，帮助我们结束了多年的分居状态，孩子的上学手续也全由机关代办。一家人聚在一起，困难一起扛、难题一起解，争吵少了、温馨多了，我的心里踏实了许多。 当陪孩子赴北京治疗和部队任务的时间发生冲突，旅里果断协调安排，让我安心陪着孩子做手术。孩子术后康复期间，旅领导多次打电话询问恢复情况。细致入微的关心，让我和爱人真切感受到组织的温暖。以前，爱人对我没时间照顾孩子颇有微词；如今，她总叮嘱我要在部队好好干。 如爱人所愿，我把组织的关爱化作拼搏的动力，连续两年在旅里组织的专业比武中取得佳绩，并参与编写修订多部专业教材。 这几年退伍季，每当有战友就自己的进退走留咨询我的意见时，我都会结合亲身经历告诉他们：“进退走留听组织的，因为组织是我们最大的依靠。” （解放军报记者彭洪霞整理） 真心解难题 行动胜千言 ■北部战区陆军某旅人力资源科干事 王东磊 近年来，随着部队实战化训练深入推进，基层官兵训练任务更重、工作节奏更快。长期两地分居、与家人聚少离多，让不少官兵面临影响家庭和谐的种种现实困难。 一次走访调研，一名连长言语间饱含辛酸：大漠驻训数月，他带领官兵战风斗沙攻克训练难题，有孕在身的妻子一次次独自前往医院产检，还要操持家务、照顾老人。时间一长，夫妻之间难免产生隔阂。 这名连长的经历，在官兵中具有一定代表性。夫妻相处、子女上学、老人赡养等看似寻常的家事，往往最牵扯官兵精力、影响官兵情绪。后顾之忧没有妥善解决，官兵便难以全身心投入练兵备战。化解官兵家庭矛盾、促进官兵家庭和谐，最根本的在于找准问题症结、精准施策破解。 “真心解难题，行动胜千言。”旅党委对此有清醒认识，推出一系列务实举措：统筹优化官兵休假轮休制度，优先保障面临家庭困难的官兵探亲休假；主动对接地方有关部门，下大力解决军属就业安置、子女入学入园、家庭困难救济等实际难题；定期组织心理健康辅导、官兵家属座谈会等活动，为军人军属疏导情绪、化解矛盾，通过增进彼此理解消除隔阂、融洽感情。 “官兵有困难，组织是后盾。”把官兵家事当作大事、要事、心头事，尽心竭力帮助大家解决好“三后”问题，才能确保官兵心无旁骛干事创业，为如期实现建军一百年奋斗目标凝聚力量。 （解放军报记者张东盼整理）
