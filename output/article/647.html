Adding "Solving Fertility Problems" to the Party Committee's Annual List of Practical Tasks, They Help Officers and Soldiers with the Problem of "Offspring"

将“解决生育难题”列入党委机关年度办实事清单，他们为官兵解决“后代”问题
PLA Daily (解放军报) 25 May 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
PLA Daily's fourth installment in a series on Party committee welfare work documents how units across the force—including a brigade of the Tibet Military District and a Rocket Force base—have built coordinated support chains to help married but childless officers and soldiers access infertility treatment at 36 military medical institutions, including Southwest Hospital of Army Medical University and the 960th Hospital of the Joint Logistic Support Force, under a force-wide Implementation Plan for Routine Diagnosis and Treatment of Infertility Among Military Couples of Childbearing Age. The article's institutional weight lies in its detailed account of deliberate concealment: the Tibet Military District brigade's political commissar explicitly removed fertility assistance from the publicly posted annual Party committee task list to avoid stigmatizing affected personnel, while a Rocket Force base recorded spousal reunion scheduling under the euphemism 'strengthening humanistic care' (加强人文关怀) in working minutes. This documents a specific tension between the PLA's push to institutionalize welfare programs and its recognition that visibility itself can undermine uptake—a retention and morale problem the framing attributes directly to long-duration remote postings on the plateau and border regions.
Translation

Care Delivered in Silence, Good News Arriving Gently

— A News Investigation into Party Committees at All Levels Helping Grassroots Officers and Soldiers Overcome Difficulties with the Correct Outlook on Political Achievement, Part Four

■ PLA Daily Reporters Xu Xiaolong, Ma Jialong, and Zheng Maoqi

A pediatric nurse at Southwest Hospital of Army Medical University tends to a newborn military baby. Photo by Wei Hongping.

Early summer. The Qinghai-Tibet Plateau teems with life. One evening, Xiao Wang, a Staff Sergeant Second Class (二级上士) in a certain brigade of the Tibet Military District, returned to his dormitory to find a message suddenly pop up on his phone — his wife had sent him an ultrasound report. Staring at the outline of the gestational sac on the form, Xiao Wang's face broke into an instant smile.

Xiao Wang had waited years for that report. In more than a decade of service, his list of honors had grown steadily, yet one thing had always weighed on his heart — "a barrier harder to cross than a mountain pass in the snow." His fellow soldiers from the same enlistment year already had children in kindergarten, while his wife had yet to conceive.

Year after year, the pressure on Xiao Wang mounted. When his political instructor learned of his difficulty, he called Xiao Wang to his room after evening roll call one day and handed him a document the headquarters had compiled based on higher-level directives — Answers to Questions on the Policy for Routine Diagnosis and Treatment of Infertility Among Couples of Childbearing Age (育龄夫妇不孕不育症常态化诊治政策答疑) — along with a Diagnosis and Treatment Application Form (诊治申请表): "Tomorrow, experts from Southwest Hospital of Army Medical University will be conducting a medical tour at our higher-level unit. You're going on a temporary duty trip — take the opportunity to find out more."

What Xiao Wang did not expect was that this carefully arranged "incidental" duty trip would bring him good news: "Your personal condition is quite good — the probability of success is very high!" The expert's words filled Xiao Wang with joy, and the cloud hanging over his heart largely lifted.

At the time, Xiao Wang did not yet know that his name had already been entered into a roster kept by the brigade Party committee.

Late last year, in order to identify problems that officers and soldiers were "unable to bring up themselves," the brigade's relevant headquarters departments, medical team, and battalion and company commanders conducted a quiet, three-level coordinated survey of married but childless officers and soldiers through systematic screening and one-on-one conversations.

"Officers and soldiers have things they find hard to say. The organization cannot stay silent." The brigade's political commissar told reporters that resolving fertility difficulties for married officers and soldiers might not yield visible results in the short term, but it is an important measure for clearing "blockages" (淤堵) in unit cohesion and opening the "channels" (经络) of morale. Drawing on higher-level documents such as the Implementation Plan for Routine Diagnosis and Treatment of Infertility Among Military Couples of Childbearing Age (军队育龄夫妇不孕不育症常态化诊治实施方案), they established and refined a multi-level coordinated support chain: headquarters handling "urgent matters quickly" (急事快办), hospitals providing "dedicated points of contact" (专人对接), and battalions and companies conducting "precision scheduling" (精准调配), all in an effort to help officers and soldiers resolve the problem of "offspring" (后代).

Shortly after returning from his duty trip, Xiao Wang submitted a treatment application to his unit. Three days later, he received approval from higher command. Soon, Xiao Wang and his wife traveled to Southwest Hospital. In the consultation room, the hospital's expert reviewed Xiao Wang's test results and pulled up his physical examination records, not only formulating a specific treatment plan for him but also working with the health management department to develop a physical conditioning regimen.

Hospital leadership at Southwest Hospital explained that in recent years the hospital's Party committee has continuously improved its service measures for military personnel, requiring relevant departments to do their utmost to provide priority medical services to officers, soldiers, and their family members. In addition, the hospital has aligned itself with unit needs, proactively expanded the scope of its medical tours, promoted online remote processing of approval procedures, and ensured full-cycle tracking and guidance for high-risk cases. In recent years, experts from the hospital's Reproductive Medicine Center have gone deep into frontline units on multiple occasions to provide specialized screening services for grassroots officers and soldiers.

According to reports, 36 military medical institutions across the entire armed forces (全军) currently carry out routine diagnosis and treatment of infertility. Military couples may, following the principle of proximity and convenience based on "the individual's place of work, family residence, or place of household registration," visit qualified military medical institutions to receive routine diagnosis and treatment of infertility and related services free of charge. Reporters visited multiple military hospitals and learned that as one green channel (绿色通道) after another has been opened for military service, a solid network safeguarding the family happiness of officers and soldiers has been woven together at all levels, from hospitals to grassroots units.

The Seventh Medical Center of PLA General Hospital has taken the lead in establishing a specialized alliance in obstetrics-gynecology and pediatrics, with membership spanning multiple military and civilian hospitals, dedicated to providing high-quality and efficient medical support for military dependents and military children (军娃); Northern Theater Command General Hospital has integrated its neonatology, pediatrics, and other departments to establish a Children's Medical Center, enabling military children to access care with a single registration number (一号通行); in Jinan, Shandong, a military-civilian joint "Green Channel for Military Spouses' Pregnancy and Childbirth" (军嫂孕产绿色通道) has been established, allowing military spouses to enjoy priority prenatal checkups and other services throughout the entire process at multiple local hospitals…

"Fertility is a highly private matter. Married but childless officers and soldiers have all kinds of reservations and rarely seek help from the organization." During interviews, leaders from many units raised this issue and shared their approach: do it quietly.

At the beginning of this year, Staff Officer Li (李干事) at the headquarters of a certain Army unit stationed on the plateau added "resolving fertility difficulties" to the Party committee headquarters' annual list of practical tasks, listing support measures, responsible units, and funding in considerable detail. When submitting the document, the brigade's political commissar made his position clear: this work must be done, but it should not go on the list. He explained at the Party committee meeting: "The annual list of practical tasks for Party committee headquarters must be publicly posted and is subject to assessment and review — but fertility is both a private matter and a source of anxiety for officers and soldiers. Discussing it openly would invisibly add to their psychological burden."

Reporting in depth across multiple units, reporters discovered further details of Party committees "doing things quietly." A certain Rocket Force base has incorporated spousal reunions into mission planning and scheduling, but uses the phrase "strengthening humanistic care" (加强人文关怀) in its working minutes; a certain Army unit strictly maintains confidentiality regarding the list of married but childless officers and soldiers who have submitted treatment applications, designating a specific individual to continuously follow up and strictly controlling the circle of those with knowledge…

At the close of the interview, reporters saw that Xiao Wang had shared the good news of his wife's pregnancy on his social media (朋友圈). His political instructor gave it a "like" in the comments and shared with reporters a remark from the brigade's political commissar: helping officers and soldiers solve the problem of "offspring" is like cultivating Tibetan primrose (格桑花) — quiet tending of the soil is better than fanfare, waiting in ordinary days for the flowers to bloom in silence.

Eyewitness Account

The Small Family's Dream Fulfilled, Thanks to the Big Family's Support

■ Military Spouse, Ms. Liu

As I type these words, my baby sleeps peacefully beside me, and everything before my eyes fills me with happiness. Before, a scene like this felt as distant and unreal as a dream.

My husband is a border defense servicemember. Exchanging greetings by phone is the norm of our married life. What comes naturally to ordinary couples — having children — has been extraordinarily difficult for us. The long separations made it hard for us to precisely time conception, and combined with my own physical condition, the probability of natural conception was very low. Since our marriage, I had gone from hospital to hospital on my own, seeking medical help, always without result. The repeated journeys and disappointments left both my husband and me physically and emotionally exhausted.

After learning of our situation, the leadership of my husband's unit proactively sought him out for a heart-to-heart talk, explained the military's specialized medical policies in detail, and repeatedly assured him: "When you run into difficulties, the organization will coordinate."

With the unit's help, my husband and I walked, with anxious hearts, into the consultation room of the 960th Hospital of the Joint Logistic Support Force (联勤保障部队第960医院). The doctor asked about our situation in careful detail and tailored a diagnosis and treatment plan specifically for us. After a period of treatment and conditioning, I successfully conceived.

Today, our baby is growing up healthy. Looking back on this experience, the care and concern of the unit's leadership and the patient treatment by the hospital's experts have made me deeply feel the warmth and wholehearted protection of the military family. As a military spouse, I will certainly take good care of our small family and support my husband's career through concrete action.

(Compiled by PLA Daily reporter Zhou Jiangchuan)

Doing Everything to Safeguard the Happiness of Military Families

■ Military Physician He Wei, Southwest Hospital of Army Medical University

Some time ago, military spouse Ms. Sai came specially to bring me wedding candy. Looking at the adorable infant in her arms and the smile radiating from her face, I felt genuinely happy for her and immensely proud of our team. As military medical workers, over the years we have helped multiple military families realize their dream of "offspring."

Wholeheartedly protecting grassroots officers and soldiers is the first lesson for every military medical worker upon joining the profession, and a mission etched into our very being. The family happiness of officers and soldiers is bound up with morale and fighting spirit. In recent years, we have focused on cutting-edge technology, built a multidisciplinary collaborative platform, and played our proper role on the invisible battlefield, doing everything in our power to safeguard the happiness of military families one by one.

In military camps and sentry posts at the peaks of snow-capped mountains, generation after generation of border defense officers and soldiers have taken root on the plateau and devoted their youth to it. They shoulder the heavy responsibility of defending the nation's borders, yet because of their long separations they find it hard to fulfill the dream of a small family. Witnessing their sacrifice and dedication has only strengthened our resolve to move the service frontier forward. Taking advantage of our plateau medical tour missions, we have established family health records for border defense officers and soldiers and implemented dynamic tracking and management, promoting early detection, early intervention, and early treatment of health problems.

The border is far away, but care is always present. Over the years, the hospital has, in accordance with the spirit of relevant higher-level documents, continuously issued multiple institutional measures, done its utmost to provide specialized medical support services, and made every effort to help border defense officers and soldiers and their families resolve the difficulty of "offspring." Border defense officers and soldiers guard the nation's mountains and rivers with their youthful blood; we guard the happiness of their small families with the benevolent art of medicine.

(Compiled by PLA Daily reporter Ma Jialong)

Original Chinese
关怀默默送 喜讯款款来 ——各级党委机关以正确政绩观为基层官兵解难帮困新闻调查之四 ■解放军报记者 徐小龙 马嘉隆 郑茂琦 陆军军医大学西南医院儿科护士看护照顾新出生的军娃。魏红平摄 初夏，青藏高原生机盎然。傍晚，西藏军区某旅二级上士小王回到宿舍，手机上突然弹出一条消息——妻子发来了一张B超检查单。盯着单子上的孕囊轮廓，小王脸上瞬间乐开了花。 这张检查单，小王等了好几年。入伍十多年来，小王获得的荣誉不断增加，可那个“比雪山垭口更难逾越的关口”一直横在他的心头——同年兵的孩子都上幼儿园了，妻子却没有怀上孩子。 一年年过去，小王的压力与日俱增。得知小王的困难，一天晚点名后，指导员把小王叫到房间，递给他一份机关根据上级文件梳理的《育龄夫妇不孕不育症常态化诊治政策答疑》和一张《诊治申请表》：“明天陆军军医大学西南医院的专家到咱上级单位巡诊，你去出趟差，顺便了解一下情况。” 让小王没想到的是，这趟组织精心安排的“顺路”公差，竟给他带来一个好消息——“个人条件不错，成功概率很大！”医院专家的话让小王大喜过望，心头的阴霾散去一大半。 那时的小王还不知道，他的名字已被写入旅党委的一份名册。 去年底，为了摸清官兵“开不了口”的难题，该旅机关相关科室、卫生队、营连主官三级联动，通过系统筛查、一对一谈心等方式，针对已婚未育官兵展开一次“静悄悄”的排查。 “官兵有难言之隐，组织不能默不作声。”该旅政委告诉记者，解决已婚官兵生育难题，短期内未必能看到成果，却是为部队凝聚力除“淤堵”、为军心士气通“经络”的重要举措。他们根据上级下发的《军队育龄夫妇不孕不育症常态化诊治实施方案》等文件，建立完善机关“急事快办”、医院“专人对接”、营连“精准调配”的多级联动守护链，努力为官兵解决“后代”问题。 出差归队不久，小王向单位提交了治疗申请。3天后，他就收到上级的批复。很快，小王和妻子赶往西南医院。诊室里，医院专家看完小王的检查结果，又调出他的体检信息，不仅为他制订具体治疗计划，还联合健康管理科为他制订了身体调理方案。 西南医院领导介绍，近年来，医院党委持续改进为兵服务举措，要求医院相关科室全力为来院官兵及家属提供优先诊疗服务。此外，他们对接部队需求，主动扩大巡诊范围，推动审批手续线上远程通办，做好高危案例全周期跟踪指导。近年来，该医院生殖医学中心专家多次深入部队一线，为基层官兵提供专项筛查服务。 据介绍，目前，全军共有36所医疗机构承担不孕不育症常态化诊治任务，军人夫妻可按照“本人工作地、家庭居住地或户籍所在地”就近就便原则，前往具备相应资质和条件的军队医疗机构，免费接受不孕不育症常态化诊治等服务。记者走访多家部队医院了解到，随着一条条为军服务绿色通道开通，从医院到基层部队，各级共同编织起一张守护官兵家庭幸福的坚实网络。 解放军总医院第七医学中心牵头创办妇产、儿科专科联盟，成员涵盖军地多家医院，致力于为军属军娃提供优质高效医疗保障；北部战区总医院整合新生儿科、儿科等多个科室，成立儿童医学中心，军娃就医实现“一号通行”；山东济南军地联合建立“军嫂孕产绿色通道”，军嫂可在多家地方医院全流程享受优先孕检等服务…… “生育是私密性很强的事情，已婚未育官兵存在各种各样的顾虑，极少向组织寻求帮助。”采访中，很多单位的领导提到这个问题，并分享了他们的解决办法——默默做。 今年初，驻守高原的陆军某部机关李干事将“解决生育难题”列入党委机关年度办实事清单，帮扶措施、责任单位、经费支持等列得很详细。呈报文件时，该旅政委明确表示：这件事要做，但不上清单。他在党委会上解释：“党委机关年度办实事清单要公示还要考核讲评，但生育问题是官兵私事更是愁事，公开讨论会无形增加他们的心理负担。” 深入多个部队单位采访，记者发现了更多党委机关“默默做”的细节。火箭军某基地将夫妻团聚纳入任务筹划安排，但工作纪要中用“加强人文关怀”来表述；陆军某部对提交诊治申请的已婚未育官兵名单严格保密，指定专人持续负责跟进，严格控制知悉范围…… 采访结束时记者看到，小王在朋友圈里分享了妻子怀孕的喜讯，他的指导员在评论区点了个赞，并向记者分享了旅政委的一段话：帮助官兵解决“后代”问题，就像培育格桑花，大张旗鼓不如静静培土，在平凡日子里静待花开。 亲历者说 小家圆梦全靠大家托举 ■军嫂 刘女士 敲下这些文字时，身旁的宝宝安稳入睡，眼前的一切让我倍感幸福。以前，这样的场景对我来说，就像梦一样缥缈。 我爱人是一名边防军人，通过电话互致问候，是我们婚后生活的常态。对普通夫妻而言顺理成章的生儿育女，对我们来说却格外艰难——聚少离多让我们难以精准把握受孕时机，再加上我个人身体原因，自然受孕概率很小。结婚以来，我一个人辗转多家医院寻医问诊始终无果。一次次奔波与失望，让我和爱人身心俱疲。 得知我们的情况后，爱人单位领导主动找他谈心，详细介绍军队专项医疗政策，并反复叮嘱：“遇到困难，组织来协调。” 在部队帮助下，我和爱人怀着忐忑的心情，走进了联勤保障部队第960医院诊室。医生细致询问我们的情况，为我们量身定制了一套诊疗方案。经过一段时间的治疗调理，我成功怀上了宝宝。 如今，我们的宝宝健康成长。回想这段经历，部队领导的关心关怀、医院专家的耐心诊治，让我深深感受到军营大家庭的温情托举和真心守护。作为军嫂，我一定照顾好我们的小家，用实际行动支持丈夫的事业。 （解放军报记者周江川整理） 全力守护军人家庭幸福 ■陆军军医大学西南医院军医 何 畏 前段时间，军嫂赛女士专门给我送来喜糖。看着她怀中可爱的婴儿和脸上洋溢的笑容，我为她感到由衷高兴，也为我们团队感到无比自豪。作为军队医疗工作者，这些年，我们帮助多个军人家庭圆了“后代”梦。 倾情守护基层官兵，是每名军队医疗工作者的入职第一课，也是我们刻进血脉的使命。官兵的家庭幸福连着军心士气。近年来，我们聚焦前沿技术，搭建多学科协作平台，在无形战场上发挥应有作用，全力守护一个个军人家庭的幸福。 在雪山之巅的军营哨所，一茬茬边防官兵扎根高原、奉献青春，他们扛起卫国戍边的重任，却因聚少离多难圆小家梦。目睹他们的牺牲奉献，更加坚定了我们将服务关口前移的决心。结合高原巡诊时机，我们为边防官兵建立家庭健康档案、动态跟踪管理，推动健康问题早发现、早干预、早治疗。 边关虽远，关怀常在。这些年，医院根据上级有关文件精神，连续出台多项制度，全力做好专项医疗保障服务，尽最大努力帮助边防官兵与家属解决“后代”难题。边防官兵用青春热血守护祖国山河，我们用医者仁术守护他们小家幸福。 （解放军报记者马嘉隆整理）
