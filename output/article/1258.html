Only for That One "Blossoming" in the Sky

■ Gao Zhenming, PLA Daily Correspondent Qu Kaiming

On the exercise and training ground, gun barrels raised high toward the sky, a live-fire exercise by a brigade of the 83rd Group Army was about to begin. Staff Sergeant Second Class Chen Tifeng, assigned to the target support detachment responsible for drone support, stood watch at the control panel, eyes fixed on the parameters displayed on the screen.

In air defense system operations, target drones are an indispensable "adversary." The air defense blade is sharpened on the exercise and training ground, and the target support detachment is the "whetstone" of the open sky.

"Target drone, launch!" On receiving the order, Chen Tifeng pressed the button, and the drone screamed into the sky, simulating an "enemy" surprise attack. Before long, the air defense detachment successfully locked onto the target and rapidly adjusted the gun barrels.

"Long burst, fire!" With a command from the position, multiple gun barrels simultaneously spat tongues of flame, and shells tore through the air toward the drone. In the billowing gunsmoke, the gunners' cheeks were flushed red by the firelight.

Moments later, a muffled explosion came from the sky, and the drone turned into a ball of flame and slowly fell. Cheers erupted across the position.

At the same time, Chen Tifeng and his comrades shouldered their tool bags and immediately headed for the drone's crash site. "Recovering the drone and inspecting the damage allows us to salvage usable components and also provides a reference for follow-on support," Chen Tifeng said, wiping the dust from his face as he spoke to the reporter.

Where the drone wreckage fell was a stretch of desolate Gobi desert. The soldiers followed the positioning signal, picking their way over gravel, and before long spotted a charred black shape in the distance. The drone lay quietly on the ground, its fuselage still reeking of acrid gunsmoke.

The wing had been struck by shells and the fuselage was covered in scratches, but the engine section was intact and still had salvage value. Chen Tifeng carefully cleared away the rubble and weeds around the drone to prevent sharp stones from scraping the remaining components, while reminding the younger soldiers: "Watch your movements — shut off the onboard power first, then protect the air intake!"

Once sand and dust enter a drone engine's air intake, it easily causes cylinder wear and component failure, wasting parts that could otherwise be recovered. The detachment personnel divided up the work: some gently wiped the air intake with cloths, carefully cleaned off loose dust, and then covered it with protective fabric; some bent down to disassemble the less-damaged fuselage components, inspecting each one to determine whether it was intact and sorting usable parts into dedicated storage bags; some carefully cleaned the dust and shell marks from the drone's surface, recording the location and extent of damage to collect data for subsequent drone maintenance and parameter adjustment…

As the excitement on the position gradually subsided and other detachments withdrew from the exercise and training ground one after another, the Gobi desert returned to silence — only the target support detachment remained busy amid the swirling dust.

In the setting sun, the shadows of Chen Tifeng and his comrades stretched long behind them. The daily routine of these soldiers holds no heart-stopping moments, only the endless cycle of rushing about and holding the line. As the "whetstone" of the open sky, they have never stood in the spotlight, yet they have always been an indispensable link in the generation of combat capability for air defense forces.

The footsteps they never cease to take are for the sake of waiting for that one "blossoming" of flame in the sky; every piece of drone wreckage they collect bears the quiet honor of an ordinary post.

Target Drones Hone the Sword: Serving as the "Whetstone" of the Open Sky

■ Li Qianqian, Gao Zhenming, PLA Daily Correspondent Qu Kaiming

Target drone taking off. Photo by Jiang Zhenliang

In the sky, the target drone soars through the clouds, wings spread wide. On the ground, fingers dance across controls, making precise adjustments.

Every precise strike on the exercise and training ground depends on the seamless cooperation of the target drone. For the officers and soldiers of the target support detachment of a brigade of the 83rd Group Army, keeping company with drones and working with data is the most ordinary of daily routines.

When a missile cleaves the sky and strikes its target with precision, people are often awed by the instant the flames blossom — yet they do not know that far from the launch position, a group of people are silently watching over every ascent and every flight path of the target drone.

The target drone may seem ordinary, yet it carries a unique mission; the officers and soldiers responsible for its support are plain and tenacious. Through one smooth flight after another, they sharpen the long sword in the open sky, contributing their own light and heat to the cause of strengthening the military.

Patience

Every step forward is a matter of using perseverance to overcome fatigue and temper oneself

Watching the red dot on the screen disappear, Staff Sergeant Second Class Chen Tifeng raised his hand and pressed the earpiece on his head. Through the crackle of static, a burst of jubilant cheering reached his ears:

"Hit! Hit! It 'bloomed' in the air — Squad Leader Chen, you've all worked hard!"

Hearing those words, Chen Tifeng's tense shoulders slowly relaxed. He let out a long breath and carefully picked up his pen to write down the details of this support mission in the flight log.

For Chen Tifeng, ensuring "zero errors" in drone flight support is a responsibility weightier than Mount Tai. "The target drone is like a weather vane for realistic training — it carries the important responsibility of providing air defense forces with precise simulated targets and helping to improve their real-combat capabilities," he told the reporter.

Drone launches demand precision, and composure during operations is indispensable. Chen Tifeng still remembers a mission from years ago when the primary operator was suddenly absent and he, having studied the role for some time, stepped up to fill the gap.

It was Chen Tifeng's first time standing watch independently. Faced with the complex information on the control panel, he was not timely enough in reporting data, his commands were not clear enough, and the transmission of instructions with the remote-control operator was not smooth — he felt acutely anxious. During the mission, a drone in a dive urgently needed to gain altitude, and it was not until the drone had entered a dangerous altitude that Chen Tifeng finished reporting the flight data. Fortunately, the remote-control operator reacted quickly and adjusted immediately, saving the drone from crashing.

This experience made Chen Tifeng realize: "To forge the patience to stay composed in any situation, you must train your professional skills until they are hard and strong." From then on, after every flight training session, he would repeatedly replay the flight data and recite the reporting commands over and over, building muscle memory through continuous practice, until he had finally honed the calm and fluid emergency techniques he needed.

For aircraft launch assistant Wang Zhenguang, holding his post means enduring the hardship of wind and rain.

At a coastal range, the weather was changeable. That day, all the drones had been fully calibrated, and Wang Zhenguang and his comrades stood by on site, ready and waiting. Then, without warning, a violent wind swept in carrying pelting rain that hammered against the fuselages.

To protect the drone about to take off, Wang Zhenguang quickly stripped off his combat uniform top, rushed to the drone's side, and used his body and jacket to shield the key components from the raging wind and rain as best he could. The seconds ticked by, and only after his comrades had firmly secured the rain cover over the drone did Wang Zhenguang lower his arms — which he had been holding up the whole time — and put away the jacket he had used as a rain shield.

When he returned to the tent, water was still dripping from his hair. Wang Zhenguang found that he was soaked through, his combat boots were caked with mud, and his arms were stiff and aching.

"Protecting the drone is my duty. No matter how fierce the wind and rain, I will complete the mission," Wang Zhenguang said.

Comrades who know Wang Zhenguang well know that he loves sports, especially running. Whether in the early morning or during breaks in training, his running figure can often be seen on the track. For Wang Zhenguang, running is not only physical conditioning but also a contest with himself.

"In my view, running and drone support are sometimes very similar," Wang Zhenguang said. "Every step forward is a matter of using perseverance to overcome fatigue and temper oneself."

The perseverance of running has helped Wang Zhenguang better understand the steadfastness required at his post. On the flyleaf of his notebook, he wrote: "Every minute and second spent holding the combat position must be used to accumulate strength for combat capability."

Not only Wang Zhenguang — every officer and soldier in the target support detachment is putting this weighty commitment into practice through day-after-day action.

Perseverance

Only by checking every unremarkable detail can you guard every segment of the flight path

"The pitot tube looks unremarkable, but it is a critical component for signal transmission in the drone…" Inside the drone repair workshop, Chen Tifeng held up a cracked pitot tube and explained it to the young soldiers beside him.

Drone support missions are often conducted in harsh environments such as desert and Gobi terrain. Drone components are easily damaged and signals are easily disrupted; any single oversight could cause the drone to lose control and affect the exercise mission.

The cracked pitot tube in his hand is a constant reminder to Chen Tifeng that executing support missions requires unwavering perseverance.

He still remembers that late autumn when the brigade went to the Gobi to conduct live-fire exercises. Shortly after one drone became airborne, it suddenly showed abnormal data and was forced to make an emergency landing. Chen Tifeng was the first to reach the drone, crouching down to assess the situation.

He swiftly removed the drone's outer casing and carried out a series of diagnostic checks, finally pinpointing the root cause: the pitot tube had been improperly handled during installation, was compressed and damaged, and could not withstand the impact of the airflow after takeoff, causing it to crack and interrupting signal transmission.

"A small pitot tube takes only a few minutes to install, but troubleshooting the fault takes several times as long," Chen Tifeng said. Afterward, he kept the cracked pitot tube in his breast pocket and carried it with him at all times. This small component serves as a warning: to properly support a drone, you must understand the characteristics of every component, and there is no room for the slightest carelessness.

"There are no 'shortcuts' in drone support. Only by checking every unremarkable detail on every mission can you guard every segment of the flight path," he told the younger soldiers.

For Detachment Commander Zheng Jincheng, maintaining perseverance means completing every mission and meeting every challenge with solid effort, no matter what difficulties arise.

One year, the brigade went to a high-altitude area to participate in exercises and training. It was the first time Zheng Jincheng had led a team to a high-altitude area to execute a support mission, and with no established experience to draw on, everything had to be figured out from scratch.

The low air pressure and complex air currents of high-altitude areas posed enormous challenges to the support work. Some could not help but waver: "The high-altitude environment is special — the drone has been calibrated multiple times and still can't meet the standard. Why don't we ask the manufacturer's personnel to come and service it first, and then we can conduct training afterward?" Hearing this, Zheng Jincheng said sternly: "The exercise and training mission brooks no delay. If we were actually on the battlefield, would we wait for the manufacturer to arrive before fighting?"

"The high-altitude environment is special, but as long as we understand how the environment affects the drone's power, signals, and flight path and make precise parameter adjustments, there is no problem we cannot solve." With that, he led the soldiers in burying themselves in reference materials, feeling their way forward step by step and optimizing bit by bit.

During that period, Zheng Jincheng worked overtime continuously. During the day, he led everyone in repeated test flights and parameter adjustments in the field; at night, he organized after-action reviews and optimized the plan for the following day. Driven by Zheng Jincheng's example, the detachment personnel pooled their efforts to tackle the problem and gradually worked out the operational techniques for high-altitude drone support.

On the day of the exercise, the first drone to take off experienced slight power insufficiency and speed fluctuation. The young operators were somewhat flustered. Zheng Jincheng stood beside the control console and said in a steady voice: "Stay calm. Follow the procedures we practiced in advance and adjust slowly."

Under his direction, everyone quickly calmed down and worked in close coordination. The remote-control operator made fine adjustments to the drone's attitude; the telemetry operator kept a constant watch on signal transmission throughout… The drone quickly stabilized, and the mission was ultimately completed successfully. Through the efforts of the detachment personnel, they successfully overcame the technical challenges of high-altitude drone support.

Unity of Purpose

Drone support is not any one person's affair — every link is indispensable

Altitude, air pressure, wind speed… For a drone in the sky, every one of these factors bears on its flight status. And from the moment a drone becomes airborne to the moment it safely returns, the vigilance of every combat position is of critical importance.

What engine operator Jiang Zebing most hopes to hear is the continuous, steady roar of the engine. This means the engine speed is stable within the prescribed range and is the prerequisite for the drone's safe takeoff. "The engine is the heart of the drone. Once it cuts out in the air and loses power, it not only affects the mission but also renders all the efforts of the other comrades futile," he said.

As the flight control system operator, Wang Kai's job is to calibrate the onboard parameters before the drone takes off and to conduct real-time monitoring and emergency fine-tuning after takeoff. "I am responsible for calibrating parameters, the remote-control operator is responsible for controlling the heading, the engine operator is responsible for checking components…" For him, drone support is not any one person's affair — every link is indispensable.

During rest periods, Wang Kai enjoys sweating it out with his comrades on the basketball court. In his mind, basketball and drone support share a common underlying logic.

"Basketball requires teammates to cooperate with tacit understanding to complete every link — passing, screening, defending, shooting; launching a drone likewise requires seamless coordination among specialized positions such as remote control, power inspection, and signal calibration," Wang Kai said. "That kind of tacit understanding is worked out through day-after-day repetitive practice."

Wang Kai still remembers how, on the court, he and his comrades went from initial poor coordination and frequent mistakes to eventually being able to grasp each other's intentions with a single glance — a process that took countless training sessions to achieve. They brought that same tenacity to their posts: in every mission and every exercise, they continuously summed up experience and identified gaps through cooperation. To make parameter calibration and heading control link up more smoothly, and to make power inspection and emergency response more efficient, they rehearsed their coordination and refined their plans again and again — they have lost count of how many days and nights passed, how much sweat was shed.

"Cooperation is not achieved overnight. Every practice session, every instance of working together, is a step toward completing the mission better," Wang Kai said.

Not long ago, an unexpected incident occurred during a training session: the drone's positioning signal was cut off and the telemetry link went silent. For a moment, remote-control operator Yang Jiamin was at a loss.

"Don't panic!" Beside him, telemetry operator Chen Qian rapidly pulled up the flight parameters; Wang Kai immediately reviewed the flight trajectory and cross-checked the historical parameter records. The detachment personnel quickly activated the emergency plan, and Yang Jiamin also calmed down, working with the other operators, each performing their own duties in efficient coordination.

As Chen Qian calculated the drone's approximate position, Yang Jiamin kept his eyes fixed on the control console and precisely adjusted the control parameters; aircraft launch assistant Wang Zhenguang simultaneously reported the handling progress to the command post. Through everyone's concerted effort, the drone's position was successfully locked. Before long, the drone re-entered its planned flight path and continued flying as scheduled.

Outside the shelter vehicle, wind and sand swirled freely; inside, the soldiers let out a long collective breath and raised their hands to wipe the sweat from their foreheads. Wang Kai and several comrades exchanged smiles, and a sense of achievement from fighting side by side welled up within them.

Eyewitness Account

Drone Support Has No Trivial Matters

■ Staff Sergeant Second Class Chen Tifeng

Instructor conducting basic drone knowledge instruction. Photo by Wang Yang

I still remember that when I was studying at the academy years ago, the professor would often emphasize one thing to us: "Drone support has no trivial matters — a single small oversight could cause irreversible losses." At the time I was young and did not take the professor's words to heart. I thought that as long as I mastered the control techniques, nothing else mattered much. It was not until a field support mission that I truly understood the importance of details.

That late autumn, our cadet unit executed a field support mission for the first time. To ensure nothing went wrong, my comrades and I spent three days at the range beforehand, checking equipment, calibrating parameters, and verifying flight paths, going through every link multiple times — I thought we had done a thorough enough job.

On the day of the mission, watching the drone take off smoothly and complete all the course objectives along the planned flight path, the weight lifted from my heart. What I did not expect was that an accident occurred during recovery: after the recovery parachute deployed and slowed the drone, it separated from the drone without any warning. Watching the drone fall out of control, my mind went blank.

That afternoon, the professor personally led us through disassembling the drone wreckage and checking the equipment components. After repeated investigation, the cause was finally confirmed: the igniter had been subjected to friction in the air, accidentally activating the triggered separation device, causing the recovery parachute to detach and the drone to crash.

A small igniter, an inconspicuous friction hazard, had destroyed the entire mission. At that moment, I finally understood what "drone support has no trivial matters" truly means.

In the past, when faced with the tedious work of checking components, calibrating parameters, and investigating hidden hazards, I only wanted to get through the task as quickly as possible. It was not until this drone accident that I understood: what I called "doing my best" was actually still far from sufficient; to achieve genuine "thoroughness," you must pay attention to every single detail.

Now, more than twenty years have passed. Before every support mission, I still ask myself: "If I were a comrade in another detachment, what kind of support would I hope to receive?" The answer is: careful scrutiny of every screw, rigorous verification of every igniter — the rigor and sense of responsibility to give everything so that comrades can have peace of mind.

(Compiled by Gao Zhenming)

Every Post Is Connected to Combat Capability

■ Junior Sergeant Wang Ke

Before I was transferred to this detachment, I had served three years in another support detachment and considered my technical foundation solid enough — wherever I was assigned, I could get up to speed quickly and handle things with ease. However, the first time I set foot on the drone position deep in the Gobi desert and saw that simple shelter vehicle, I was brought up short.

At the time, I was responsible for fueling the drones. Faced with boundless solitude and repetitive work, my mood began to grow restless, and as a result I encountered a "harrowing moment" I have regretted ever since.

Different drones require different fuels. That time, I picked up a fuel drum and was about to open the valve and start fueling when Squad Leader Chen suddenly reached out and stopped me: "Hold on! Have you smelled this drum?"

I was taken aback and said: "It can't be wrong — it's jet fuel." Squad Leader Chen said nothing more, bent down and leaned in for a sniff, then immediately frowned: "Wrong — this is gasoline! You grabbed the wrong drum!"

My back instantly went cold and cold sweat soaked through my combat uniform — if the wrong fuel had actually been added, at best it would have clogged the engine lines, at worst it would have burned out the equipment outright, rendering the entire support mission a complete failure.

I stood to one side, still shaken, watching Squad Leader Chen fuel the drone according to procedure. The words he repeated again and again — "Drone support has no 'close enough,' only 'zero errors'" — struck me hard.

This experience of nearly making a mistake made me fully realize: drone support tolerates no empiricism; an oversight in any single detail could cause an irreversible error.

Over the past two years, my professional skills have continued to improve, and I have come to understand more deeply the meaning of drone support — everything we do is not for the sake of mechanically completing a workflow, but to safeguard the safety of exercises and training and to underpin battlefield victory. Whether routine fuel management or field bivouac support; whether parameter calibration or equipment maintenance… every post and every procedure is connected to combat capability.

Now, every time I open a fuel valve and fuel a drone, I always think back to that "harrowing moment" and to Squad Leader Chen's earnest admonition. I know that drone support work is plain and unremarkable — only by holding firm to the principle of rigor and meticulousness can we lay a solid foundation for winning battles in the cause of strengthening the military.

(Compiled by Gao Zhenming)

海边靶场，天气阴晴不定。那天，靶机已全部调试完毕，负责现场保障的王振光和战友守在一旁严阵以待。这时，狂风裹挟着豆大的雨点突然来袭，噼里啪啦砸在机身上。 为了保护即将起飞的靶机，王振光迅速脱掉作训服上衣，快步冲到靶机旁，尽力用身体和外衣遮挡住狂乱的风雨，保护靶机关键部位。时间一分一秒流逝，等战友们将防雨布牢牢盖在靶机上，王振光才放下一直高举的手臂，收起用来挡雨的上衣。 回到帐篷时，他的头发还在不停滴着水珠。王振光发现自己身上早已被雨水淋透，作训鞋满是泥泞，手臂也有些僵硬酸痛。 “保护好靶机是我的职责，不管遇到多大风雨，我都要完成任务。”王振光说。 熟悉王振光的战友都知道，他喜欢运动，尤其爱跑步。无论清晨还是训练间隙，跑道上时常能看到他奔跑的身影。对王振光而言，跑步不仅是体能锻炼，更是一场与自我的较量。 “在我看来，跑步和靶机保障有时很相似。”王振光说，“迈向前方的每一步，都是在用坚持战胜疲惫、磨砺自己。” 奔跑时的坚持，让王振光更加理解岗位上的坚守。在笔记本扉页，他写下了这样一句话：“坚守战位的每分每秒，都要为战斗力积蓄力量。” 不仅是王振光，目标保障分队的每一名官兵，都在用日复一日的行动，践行这份沉甸甸的承诺。 恒 心 将所有不起眼的细节检查到位，才能守好每一段航迹 “空速管看着不起眼，却是靶机信号传输的关键部件……”靶机修理工间内，陈体峰拿出一根破裂的空速管，对身边的年轻战士讲解道。 靶机保障任务，常常要在沙漠戈壁这样的恶劣环境中进行。而靶机零部件易受损、信号易受干扰，任何一个疏忽，都可能导致靶机失控，影响演练任务。 手中这根破裂的空速管，时刻提醒着陈体峰，执行保障任务要常怀恒心。 他还记得那年深秋，旅队赴戈壁开展实弹演练。一架靶机在升空后不久，突然出现数据异常，被迫紧急降落。陈体峰第一个来到靶机旁，蹲下身查看情况。 他动作麻利地拆掉靶机外壳，展开一系列检测调试，终于锁定问题根源——空速管在安装时力道把控不当，挤压受损，在升空后承受不住气流冲击而破裂，因此中断了信号传输。 “一根小小的空速管，安装只需要几分钟，排除故障却要耗费成倍的时间。”后来，陈体峰一直将这根破裂的空速管放在胸前口袋，随身携带。这个小小的部件警示着他，要保障好靶机必须摸清每个部件的特性，容不得半点疏忽。 “靶机保障没有‘捷径’可走，我们只有每次任务都将所有不起眼的细节检查到位，才能守好每一段航迹。”他对年轻战友说。 对于分队长郑锦程来说，保持恒心，意味着不论遇到什么难题，都要扎扎实实地完成任务、迎接挑战。 那年，旅队赴高原参加演训。这是郑锦程第一次带队上高原执行保障任务，由于没有成熟经验可循，一切都要从头摸索。 高原地区气压偏低、气流复杂，为保障工作带来极大挑战。有人不禁打起退堂鼓：“高原环境特殊，靶机调试多次都达不到标准，要不先请厂家工作人员过来检修，我们之后再开展训练？”听到这话，郑锦程严肃地说：“演训任务刻不容缓，真上了战场，难道还要等厂家来了再打仗吗？” “高原环境虽然特殊，但只要我们摸清环境对靶机动力、信号、航线的影响，精准调整参数，就没有搞不定的难题。”就这样，他带着官兵一头扎进资料堆，一步步摸索，一点点优化。 那段时间，郑锦程连续加班加点。白天，他带领大家在野外反复试飞、调试参数；晚上，他牵头组织复盘总结，优化次日方案。在郑锦程的带动下，分队官兵聚力攻坚，逐渐探索出高原靶机保障的操作技巧。 演练当天，一架靶机率先升空，却出现轻微动力不足、速度波动情况。年轻的操作手们有些慌乱，郑锦程站在操控台旁，语气沉稳地说：“沉住气，按咱们提前练过的流程来，慢慢调整。” 在他的指挥下，大家迅速冷静下来，密切配合。遥控操作手精细调整靶机姿态，遥测操作手全程紧盯信号传输……靶机很快恢复稳定，任务最终顺利完成。在分队官兵的努力下，他们成功攻克高原靶机保障的技术难题。 同 心 靶机保障不是某个人的事，每一个环节都不可或缺 高度、气压、风速……对于天空中的靶机来说，每一项要素，都关系着飞行状态。而从靶机升空到安全返航，每一个战位的守护，都至关重要。 发动机手姜泽兵最希望听到的，就是发动机持续平稳的轰鸣。这意味着发动机转速稳定在规定范围，也是靶机安全升空的前提。“发动机是靶机的心脏，一旦空中停车、失去动力，不仅影响任务，也会让其他战友的努力付诸东流。”他说。 作为飞行控制系统操作手，王凯的工作是在靶机起飞前调试机上参数，在起飞后进行实时监测和应急微调。“我负责调试参数，遥控操作手负责操控航向，发动机手负责排查零件……”对他而言，靶机保障不是某个人的事，每一个环节都不可或缺。 休息时，王凯喜欢和战友在篮球场上挥洒汗水。在他心中，打篮球和靶机保障蕴藏着一种异曲同工的道理。 “打篮球需要队友间默契配合，完成传球、掩护、防守、投篮各个环节；放飞靶机，同样需要遥控操控、动力排查、信号校准等专业岗位的无缝协同。”王凯说，“这种默契，是在日复一日的重复练习中磨合出来的。” 王凯依然记得，当年在球场上，他和战友从最初的衔接不畅、频频失误，到后来一个眼神就能领会彼此意图，经历了一次次训练打磨。他们也把这种韧劲带到了岗位上——每一次任务、每一次演练，都在协作中不断总结经验、查漏补缺。为了让参数调试与航向操控衔接更顺畅，让动力排查与应急处置更高效，他们一次次演练配合、优化方案，记不清经历了多少日夜，挥洒了多少汗水。 “协作不是一蹴而就的。每一次练习、每一次配合，都在向更好地完成任务迈进。”王凯说。 前段时间，某次训练中意外突发——靶机定位信号中断，遥测链路失联。一时间，遥控操作手杨嘉敏有些手足无措。 “别慌！”在他身旁，遥测操作手陈乾迅速调取飞行参数，王凯立即复盘飞行轨迹，核对过往参数记录。分队官兵快速启动应急预案，杨嘉敏也冷静下来，与其他操作手各司其职、高效配合。 随着陈乾测算出靶机大致方位，杨嘉敏紧盯控制台，精准调整操控参数，机体助飞手王振光同步向指挥所报告处置进度。在大家的通力合作下，靶机位置被成功锁定。很快，靶机重新驶入预定航线，按计划继续飞行。 方舱外，风沙恣意飞舞；方舱内，官兵们长舒一口气，抬手擦去额头的汗珠。王凯和几名战友相视一笑，一股并肩作战的成就感油然而生。 亲历者说 靶机保障无小事 ■二级军士长 陈体峰 教练员进行靶机基础知识教学。王 杨摄 还记得多年前在院校学习时，教授常跟我们强调一句话：“靶机保障无小事，一个微小的疏忽，都可能酿成无法挽回的损失。”那时我年轻，没把教授的话放在心上。我想，只要把操控技术练精，其他都不打紧。直到一次外场保障任务，我才真正理解了细节的重要性。 那年深秋，学员队第一次执行外场保障任务。为确保万无一失，我和战友提前3天就泡在靶场，检查设备、调试参数、核对航线，把每个环节都过了多遍，自认为干得足够扎实。 任务当天，看到靶机顺利升空，按预定航线完成全部课目，我心里的石头落了地。没想到，回收环节突发意外——回收伞张开减速后，毫无征兆地与靶机分离。看着靶机失控下坠，我的脑子一片空白。 那天下午，教授手把手带着我们拆解靶机残骸、核对设备部件，反复排查后终于确认：是点火头在空中受到摩擦，意外启动了触发分离装置，导致回收伞脱落、靶机坠毁。 一个小小的点火头，一个不起眼的摩擦隐患，竟然毁了整个任务。那一刻，我终于明白，什么叫“靶机保障无小事”。 过去，面对检查部件、调试参数、排查隐患这些琐事，我只想着尽快完成任务就好。直到这次靶机意外坠毁，我才明白：自己所谓的“尽力”，其实还差得很远；想要做到真正的“扎实”，必须关注到每一个细节。 如今，20多年过去。每次执行保障任务前，我依然会反问自己：“如果我是其他分队的战友，希望得到怎样的保障？”答案，是对每一颗螺丝钉的仔细检视、对每一个点火头的严格校验，是拼尽全力让战友放心的严谨与担当。 （高朕铭整理） 岗位连着战斗力 ■下士 王 克 调入分队前，我曾在其他保障分队服役3年，自认为技术基础足够扎实，无论到哪个岗位，都能快速上手、从容应对。然而，第一次踏上戈壁深处的靶机阵地，见到那间简陋的方舱，我就被泼了一盆冷水。 当时，我负责为靶机加注油料。面对无边的寂寞和重复的工作，我的情绪开始变得浮躁，也因此遇见了一次让我懊悔万分的“惊险时刻”。 不同靶机需要加注不同油料。那一次，我拎起一个油桶就要拧开油阀加注，身旁的陈班长突然伸手拦住我：“停！这桶油闻过了吗？” 我愣了愣，说道：“不会错，就是航空煤油。”陈班长没多说，俯身凑近轻嗅，随即皱起眉：“不对，这是汽油！你拿错桶了！” 我瞬间后背发凉，冷汗浸湿了作训服——如果真的加错油料，轻则堵塞发动机管路，重则直接烧毁装备，让整个保障任务功亏一篑。 我心有余悸地站在一旁，看着陈班长按流程加注油料。他反复重申的那句 “靶机保障没有‘差不多’，只有‘零差错’”，重重砸在了我心里。 这次险些出错的经历，让我彻底意识到：靶机保障容不得经验主义，任何一个细节的疏忽，都可能酿成无法挽回的错误。 这两年，我的专业技术不断精进，也更深刻理解了靶机保障的意义——我们所做的每件事，不是为了机械地完成工作流程，而是为了守护演训安全、支撑战场打赢。无论日常油料管理，还是野外驻训保障；无论参数校准，还是装备检修……每一个岗位、每一道工序，都连着战斗力。 如今，每当我拧开油阀、为靶机加注油料时，总会想起那次“惊险时刻”，想起陈班长语重心长的叮嘱。我知道，靶机保障的工作平凡且平淡，只有坚守住严谨细致的原则，才能为强军事业筑牢打赢根基。 （高朕铭整理）
