From the Jieshou Ferry Crossing to Both Banks of the Xiang River, These Historic Sites Have Become "A Classroom That Never Closes Its Doors"

从界首渡口到湘江两岸，这片旧址如今成了“不关门的课堂”
PLA Daily (解放军报) 13 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
The Guilin Garrison uses the Jieshou Town ferry-crossing site in Xing'an County, Guangxi — where residents removed door planks to help the Red Army cross the Xiang River during the 1934 Long March — as a recurring on-site political education venue for newly commissioned officers, newly enrolled militia members, and units undergoing reorganization, with garrison staff officer Yin Tiejun leading annual visits and militia platoon leader Xie Zhaoli drawing an explicit line from the 1934 episode to the garrison's 2024 flood-relief operations. This is standard political work content — 'party-history education at historic sites' (红色教育基地) tied to the PLA's ongoing 'never forget the people' loyalty framing — and its value is as a record of how the Guilin Garrison institutionalizes that framing across the officer, enlisted, and militia pipeline, not as evidence of a new policy direction or changed operational posture.
Translation

Every Household Lost a Door, the Red Army Gained a Road

■ Chen Qi'an, PLA Daily Reporter Li Zhibin

On Red Army Street in Jieshou Town, Xing'an County, Guangxi, several figurative sculptures freeze a single moment in time: ordinary people, carrying door planks on their shoulders and in their hands, ferrying them down to the riverbank. A guide told reporters that these sculptures depict the scene in which local residents removed the door planks from their own homes to help the Red Army cross the Xiang River in a desperate rush.

In early winter of 1934, to ensure the main Red Army force could cross the river, pontoon bridges had to be constructed at ferry crossings including Jieshou and Daping. Residents along the riverbank, seeing that the troops lacked timber, removed their door planks and floorboards without a second word. The river water was bitterly cold, yet they waded barefoot into the muddy shallows and worked alongside the Red Army soldiers to anchor the bridge footings and tighten the ropes.

After the main force had crossed, the rear-guard units fought through the night and, before departing, did everything they could to return the door planks to each household. But in the urgency of the moment, a small number of planks were fitted back into the wrong door frames. When daylight came, the villagers found that the Zhang family's door had been hung at the Li family's house, and some door pivots simply would not fit their hinges. Yet no one had the heart to go find the Red Army and ask for an exchange, so they made do with the mismatch, and things stayed that way for years.

On the wooden door planks of the old houses lining the street, many pivot holes no longer align with their original sockets, and the gaps between planks vary in width. These mismatched door planks have now become teaching materials for on-site instruction conducted by the Guilin Garrison. Garrison staff officer Yin Tiejun organizes newly appointed officers and newly enrolled militia members to visit the site every year. He says that every time he stands on that old street and looks at those old door planks, a question rises in his mind: what is it that carried this force through to where it stands today?

Party history experts and descendants of Red Army soldiers told those assembled: before the Red Army entered Jieshou, enemy forces had spread rumors far and wide that "the Red Army kills people and burns houses," and many residents had fled into the mountains. But after the advance units entered the town, they did not enter private homes, did not take so much as a needle or a thread, slept in the open on the streets, and swept the lanes and alleys clean. One soldier had borrowed a water bucket from a resident to fetch water; when he was done, he returned it immediately, and before leaving he filled the household's water vat to the brim. The residents who had been hiding in the mountains gradually returned home, found that not a single grain of rice was missing from their jars and not a single door plank had been touched, and the rumors collapsed on their own.

Sergeant Zhu Lingxiu wrote in a reflection piece after visiting the site: "Why were the people of that time willing to remove the door planks from their own homes? Because they had seen with their own eyes who this army fought for. Today, wearing this uniform, we too must follow in our forebears' footsteps and safeguard that trust."

This past summer, Guangxi was struck by sustained heavy rainfall; tributaries of the Xiang River surged, and multiple areas were cut off by floodwaters. The Guilin Garrison activated its emergency response, with officers, soldiers, and militia emergency detachments standing by in full kit, preparing through the night assault boats, life rings, sandbags, and other flood-control and rescue supplies. During the mobilization, militia emergency platoon leader Xie Zhaoli suddenly recalled those mismatched door planks, and said to his comrades: "Back then, the people used door planks to build a bridge and carry the Red Army across the river. Today, we will use assault boats to open a lifeline for our fellow villagers."

Reflecting on the multiple flood-relief and rescue missions he had taken part in, Xie Zhaoli said that whenever people trapped by floodwaters caught sight of camouflage uniforms, they would always wave and cheer. That sight struck him deeply: "The people's trust is the most solid pontoon bridge beneath our feet."

From the Jieshou ferry crossing to both banks of the Xiang River, these historic sites have become "a classroom that never closes its doors." Whenever new soldiers join the ranks, officers rotate into new posts, or militia units are reorganized, commanders bring their troops before the Red Army Hall and out onto the old street, letting officers and soldiers hear a lesson on the spot and walk a stretch of road — so they will never forget the people whose door planks once bore the footsteps of the people's soldiers.

Original Chinese
每家少了一扇门，红军多了一条路 ■陈麒安 解放军报记者 李志彬 在广西兴安县界首镇红军街，几尊人物雕塑定格着这样一个动作：百姓肩扛手抬，把一块块门板送向江边。讲解员告诉记者，这些雕塑正是当年老百姓卸下自家门板、帮助红军抢渡湘江的场景。 1934年初冬，为保障红军主力过江，必须在界首、大坪等渡口架设浮桥。沿江百姓见部队缺木料，二话不说卸下门板、铺板。江水寒凉刺骨，他们赤脚踩进泥滩，和红军一道压桥脚、紧绳索。 主力过江后，后卫部队连夜转战，临行前想尽办法把门板送回各家各户。可由于时间紧急，少数门板还是装错了门框。天亮后乡亲们发现，张家的门安到了李家，有的门轴根本挂不进去。可谁也不忍心再找红军调换，就这样将错就错，一用就是多年。 街边老屋的木质门板，不少门轴对不上原有的孔洞，板缝宽窄不一。这些错位的门板，如今成了桂林警备区开展现地教学的教材。警备区干事殷铁军每年都要组织新任职干部、新入队民兵到此见学。他说，每次站在这条老街，看着这些老门板，心里都会响起一个声音：我们这支队伍，是靠什么走过来的。 党史专家和红军后人告诉大家：红军进入界首前，敌军四处散布“红军杀人放火”的谣言，不少群众躲进山里。可先头部队进镇后，不进民房、不拿一针一线，露宿街头，还把街巷打扫得干干净净。有战士借了群众的水桶打水，用完立刻归还，临走还挑满一缸水。躲在山里的百姓陆续返回家中，见米缸没少一粒，门板没动一块，谣言不攻自破。 军士祝令修在一篇参观体会里写道：“当年的百姓为什么甘愿卸下自家的门板？因为他们亲眼看见，这支军队为谁而战。今天我们穿上这身军装，也要像先辈那样，守护这份信任。” 今年入夏，广西遭遇持续暴雨，湘江支流水位猛涨，多地被洪水围困。桂林警备区启动应急响应，官兵和民兵应急分队整装待命，连夜准备冲锋舟、救生圈、沙袋等防汛抢险物资。在进行动员时，民兵应急排排长谢肇力的脑海中突然闪过那些错位的门板，他对战友说：“当年老百姓用门板搭桥托举红军渡江，今天我们要用冲锋舟为乡亲们开辟生命通道。” 谢肇力回顾自己数次参加抢险救援任务时说，每当被困的百姓们看到迷彩身影，总会挥手欢呼。这样的场景让他心头一震：“人民的信任，就是我们脚下最坚实的浮桥。” 从界首渡口到湘江两岸，这片旧址如今成了“不关门的课堂”。每逢新兵入队、干部调整、民兵整组，他们都会把队伍带到红军堂前、拉到老街上，让官兵在现场听一堂课、走一段路，永远记住那些承托子弟兵脚步的百姓的门板。
