A Trajectory That Never Deviates

——Reporters Trace the Original Aspiration and Journey of Martyr Zhu Rui, Founder of the People's Artillery (Part II)

■ PLA Daily Reporters Zhang Kejin and Wang Desai

A certain brigade of the 79th Group Army organizes live-fire training for artillery units. Photo by Fu Zhixiang.

During the Qingming Festival, people carrying fresh flowers walked into two cemeteries, one in the south and one in the north.

At the Menglianggu Martyrs' Cemetery in Shandong, two graves—one large, one small—rest close together, sheltering a mother and daughter in eternal sleep. The mother, Chen Ruoke, was 22 years old. Her daughter had been born only 19 days earlier and had not yet been given a name.

This mother and daughter were the wife and child of Zhu Rui, founder of the People's Artillery. During the anti-"mopping-up" (反"扫荡") campaign of 1941, both were killed by Japanese invaders.

At the Zhu Rui Martyrs' Cemetery in Yixian County, Jinzhou, Liaoning, fresh flowers bloom before the grave. On October 1, 1948, Zhu Rui was killed by a landmine during the battle to liberate Yixian County.

From the Yimeng Mountain area to the land of Liaoshen, husband and wife—a family of three—shed their blood on the sacred soil of the motherland, seven years apart.

Our reporters, in relay, traced the footsteps of Martyr Zhu Rui, extending their steps and their pens into the more distant mists of history. When Zhu Rui—who had declared he was "willing to give the second half of his life to artillery"—threw himself like a blazing projectile, without hesitation, onto the battlefield, his brilliant first half of life still shines like a trajectory that never deviates, drawing our reverent gaze.

Faith in the Heart, Responsibility on the Shoulders

In those years, the television drama The Right Path Is the World of Vicissitudes (人间正道是沧桑) was enormously popular. Regarding the prototype for the protagonist Yang Liqing, audiences generally believed the character blended the traits of four founding generals: Chen Geng, Song Shilun, Yang Zhicheng, and Xiao Jinguang.

"In fact, Zhu Rui was also one of the prototypes for Yang Liqing." At the Zhu Rui General Memorial Hall in Suqian City, Jiangsu Province, Huang Xinhe—a non-commissioned officer school student from the Army Arms University who had come to trace his roots—was connecting online with classmates to discuss this. Comparing the drama's plot of Yang Liqing "risking his life to save his mentor" with Zhu Rui's own deed of "fighting to the death to save his comrades," this member of the post-2000 generation was deeply moved.

In October 1930, Wuhan was shrouded in a White Terror. At the time, the CCP Yangtze River Bureau had been compromised by the enemy and urgently needed capable cadres to carry out its work.

At this critical juncture, Zhu Rui—then serving as a staff officer in the General Staff Section of the Central Military Commission—stepped forward, declaring he was prepared to "carry his own coffin" to take up the post, and resolutely departed for Wuhan. He evaded repeated enemy searches, provided cover for leaders including Guan Xianying and Ren Bishi to successfully transfer to safety, and at the risk of his life withdrew a large number of clandestine Party members and united-front contacts.

The non-commissioned officer school where Huang Xinhe studies traces its lineage to the Yan'an Artillery School. Before one exhibit in the school history museum, many people stopped to look closely. It was a photocopy of My History and Ideological Autobiography (《我的历史与思想自传》), written by Zhu Rui during the Yan'an Rectification Movement (延安整风)—a precious historical document through which later generations can approach him in the first person.

Zhu Rui was born in 1905 into a prosperous family in Suqian, Jiangsu. In 1925, he went to the Soviet Union, studying successively at Sun Yat-sen University in Moscow and the Krasin Artillery School; in 1928, he joined the Chinese Communist Party. In 1930, after returning to China, Zhu Rui lost contact with the Party organization. Amid the storm of blood and terror, some withdrew from the Party, some defected. Old friends urged him to "stop doing revolution—let's go into business together," offering him important positions; he refused them all with stern words.

After six months of desperate searching, Zhu Rui finally returned to the embrace of the organization. He later recalled: "From leaving Moscow to finding the Party—six months in all—was the loneliest, most anguished period of my life. Before finding the Party, we were very much like orphans who had lost their mother…"

These heartfelt words moved the students participating in the discussion to reflect: a true revolutionary always has a "Zhu Rui" living inside—faith in the heart, responsibility on the shoulders.

"Revolutionary work is not a pastoral idyll; it cannot possibly proceed without a hitch." Every time she enters the Zhu Rui General Memorial Hall, local Party history researcher Wang Qiuwen finds new insights. She says that on the revolutionary road, some fell behind or became lost because their faith was not firm; some even betrayed and degenerated because their faith wavered. It is precisely for this reason that those who kept walking are especially worth remembering.

Through all the twists and hardships, why was the Chinese revolution able to march toward victory? The stories that happened to Zhu Rui and his family gave our reporters a profound understanding of "the power of faith (信仰的力量)"—

In 1936, the Red Army marched north through Gansu and Shaanxi. To clear the way for the three main forces to converge, Zhu Rui risked his life to make contact with the Northeastern Army. Before his secret meeting with the commander of the 6th Cavalry Division, he handed a notebook containing classified information to a member of his party to be buried in a sorghum field, having prepared himself for death.

Before her martyrdom, Zhu Rui's wife, Martyr Chen Ruoke, extended her bleeding hand and said: "Child, you came into this world without drinking a single drop of your mother's milk, and now you must leave this world together with your mother. Then drink a mouthful of your mother's blood!"

In Zhu Rui's hometown of Zhu Daxingzhuang, more than twenty of his relatives and descendants successively joined the revolutionary ranks. During the anti-"mopping-up" campaign in southern Shandong, his niece Zhu Hua—who had followed Zhu Rui into the revolution—was martyred in the area of Cangshan and Feixian counties…

When the seas run wild, the true colors of heroes are revealed. These stories resonated with Huang Xinhe and his classmates: "The more magnificent the light, the more fiercely it must burn. True faith is the willingness to become the ash within that light. Those who refuse to burn are forever nothing more than opportunists in the course of history, sifted out like sand by the waves."

Since the deepening of national defense and military reform, this non-commissioned officer school has undergone multiple rounds of reorganization. Faced with relocating to teach in a different place, changing posts, and restructuring specializations, the entire faculty and staff have not a word of complaint. Everyone says: "Old Commandant Zhu Rui sacrificed his family and his own life for the Party's cause. What does our small personal sacrifice amount to?"

Always Ready to Uphold Truth, Always Ready to Correct Errors

Linshu—situated at the center of the Rectification Movement in the Shandong Anti-Japanese Base Area—was once known as "Little Yan'an."

Visiting multiple former sites of the CCP Shandong Sub-bureau, what left the deepest impression on our reporters was that the exhibits presented, in their original form, the criticism directed at Zhu Rui during the Rectification Movement, as well as his candid self-examination and reflective self-criticism.

"Failure to complete the Central Committee's task of 'gaining superiority in Shandong'; insufficient performance in the struggle against the stubborn (顽固) forces"… "The 'rent and interest reduction' (减租减息) campaign was not carried out deeply enough; the masses were fundamentally not mobilized and organized"… "Historical opportunities to establish political power were lost"… The sharp criticisms, more than eighty years later, remain striking.

As the top leader (一把手) of the Shandong Sub-bureau—described by Comrade Mao Zedong as a "regional overlord (一方诸侯)"—how did Zhu Rui confront these "grating" criticisms? Zhong Xingfan, then deputy director of the Dazhong Daily (《大众日报》) and a participant in the criticism meetings, recalled that Zhu Rui's attitude was sincere and his expression solemn; he nodded from time to time in agreement, and calmly told Zhong to record these opinions faithfully.

Beyond that, Zhu Rui took the lead in delivering a three-hour self-criticism at the Sub-bureau meeting, proactively assuming responsibility for the "primary problems" in Shandong's work—neither deflecting blame nor pushing it downward. Liu Shaoqi, who had come to guide the work, said: "Comrade Zhu Rui showed courage in self-criticism, corrected his shortcomings and errors, and made an important contribution to Shandong's Rectification Movement."

Visiting the old Yimeng base area, our reporters heard a Rectification story still passed down by word of mouth there today—

In the southern Shandong region, a signals unit once received a "first-priority combat mission": to set up a radio station to receive Rectification documents from Yan'an. Because they encountered Japanese puppet "mopping-up" operations and their supplies were cut off, the officers and soldiers could only eat "national salvation rations (救国粮)" while carrying out the mission. These "national salvation rations" were, in fact, animal fodder.

On the day the mission ended, Japanese puppet forces came up the mountain to search and sweep. The soldiers finished decoding the final manuscript at the last possible moment before withdrawing down the mountain. That final manuscript was Mao Zedong's "Oppose Stereotyped Party Writing" (《反对党八股》). After the text was printed and distributed, the Rectification Movement in southern Shandong was set in motion.

A docent told our reporters that at the time, some people felt that with the War of Resistance situation so tense, there was no need to "conduct mopping-up outside while conducting rectification inside." Zhu Rui, however, firmly demanded: one "gun" aimed at the enemy, one "gun" aimed at ourselves.

It was precisely because Zhu Rui led by example—starting from the top, insisting that senior cadres go first, and treating rectification as a hard battle to be fought—that the cadre corps of the Shandong Anti-Japanese Base Area underwent a thorough ideological transformation. Everyone was of one mind, truly aligning with the Central Committee.

From Shandong to Yan'an, Zhu Rui's utterly genuine and pure Party spirit (党性) moved us time and again. A historical document disclosed by the Central Institute of Party History and Documentation reveals that at the Seventh National Congress of the CCP, Zhu Rui spoke as follows: "When I was working in Shandong, I did not show proper respect to Comrade Luo Ronghuan, whose abilities and leadership were both superior to mine. I not only apologized to him in Shandong—I should also apologize to him today, at this Party congress."

"Rectification means a complete transformation—a cleansing of the heart and a renewal of the face. Let us senior cadres start first, examine ourselves first, then criticize others"… "Some of you young people used to think I was remarkable, even admired me. In fact, many of my shortcomings were things you took for strengths, and that will affect you"… More than forty years later, Zhu Rui's secretary Mao Pengyun recalled these heartfelt words and still marveled: "For a senior cadre to dissect himself so plainly and openly in public—that commands respect!"

"Always ready to uphold truth, always ready to correct errors! (时刻准备坚持真理，时刻准备修正错误！)" Inside the Central Auditorium at Yangjialing, this slogan seemed to transport our reporters back more than eighty years. Zhu Rui of that era must have heard Mao Zedong's report "On Coalition Government" (《论联合政府》), and must have felt these words deeply—

"Communists must always be ready to uphold truth, because all truth is in accord with the interests of the people; Communists must always be ready to correct errors, because all errors are contrary to the interests of the people."

Credit for Success Need Not Be Mine; Credit for Success Must Include Me

Yixian County—the northern gateway to Jinzhou. The local county gazetteer records: the city walls stood 10 meters high and 4 meters thick, with a wide and deep moat outside; the enemy relied on the solid walls to construct networks of blockhouses and bunkers, making the fortifications formidable and easy to defend, difficult to attack.

In early summer, our reporters stood beneath the "Antai Gate"—the only surviving section of the ancient city wall—and looked up, filled with awe: in that battle of October 1948, how did the People's Liberation Army storm this fortified city in just six hours?

The answer lies in the exhibits at the Zhu Rui Martyrs' Cemetery. The battle records of that time document the following—

"In the Battle of Yixian, our forces deployed over a hundred artillery pieces—including 150mm heavy howitzers, 105mm howitzers, field guns, mountain guns, and mortars—organized into three artillery groups, deployed in close forward positions."

"The sustained artillery bombardment completely suppressed the defending forces. Enemy division commander Wang Shigao lamented in his confession: 'Every firing position inside the city walls was completely sealed off by artillery fire—unable to move. Men were either killed outright or pinned down by shellfire, unable to raise their heads.'"

The Battle of Yixian set a new record for our army in urban assault warfare—the shortest time to completely annihilate the defending enemy—and served as a "firepower rehearsal" for the assault on Jinzhou. It was the crowning moment when the light of Zhu Rui's life shone most brilliantly.

Who could have imagined that this founder of the People's Artillery—who had ranked first in both the graduation examination and live-fire shooting at the Soviet Krasin Artillery School—would spend more than ten years after returning to China without being able to work in artillery at all?

As Zhu Rui himself said, "there were no conditions for it when I returned in 1930." The Red Army of that time—and later the Eighth Route Army and the New Fourth Army—had extremely few artillery pieces, far too few to constitute a branch of service. Zhu Rui, who had received systematic artillery education in the Soviet Union, simply had no arena in which to apply his skills. During those more than ten years, Zhu Rui did underground work, united-front work, and most extensively and for the longest time, Party affairs work—none of it connected to artillery.

Yet one who aspires to be a great tree harbors the dream of reaching the sky.

At the end of 1944, when Mao Zedong and Zhou Enlai met with the U.S. Army Observer Group (驻华美军观察小组) in Yan'an, Lieutenant Colonel Bird posed a question: "Mr. Mao, will the Eighth Route Army launch a major counteroffensive in the near future?" Mao Zedong said firmly: "It will—and very soon we will fight our way into the cities!" Bird followed up: "I know your army has no artillery or armored forces. How will you take cities?" Zhou Enlai smiled and replied: "There is a Chinese saying: when the cart reaches the mountain, a road will appear." "A cart? Mr. Zhou, we are talking about artillery!" Bird was thoroughly baffled.

One year later, Zhu Rui stepped forward, volunteering with passion, and became the first "hauler" to pull the war chariot of the People's Artillery. In 1948, the artillery of the Northeast Field Army (东北野战军), with the force of thunder, stormed and took the defenses of Jinzhou in a single stroke.

For that day, Zhu Rui had politely declined the Central Military Commission's appointment as Deputy Chief of the General Staff in Yan'an—choosing to be a teacher rather than a high official. For that day, Zhu Rui had gone to the Northeast, leading officers and soldiers across the mountains and fields to "scavenge for guns (捡炮)." For that day, before the Liaoshen Campaign (辽沈战役) was launched, Zhu Rui made yet another "selfless choice (无我抉择)"—

At that time, the Northeast artillery had been built up, but Zhu Rui found that military-industrial production could not keep pace, and once again volunteered to take sole charge of that work. In a letter to Liu Yalou, his words were plain and resolute: "For the long-term development of the artillery, I set aside any consideration of title or rank. I request to be relieved of my post as Artillery Commander and to take on this work—to be an administrator for the rest of my life."

The day Jinzhou fell was fifteen days after Zhu Rui's death. At the Northeast Martyrs' Memorial Hall, our reporters saw a letter to his mother and elder brother that he had not had time to send. It read: "…The Northeast is developing very rapidly. I think before long we will fight our way through the Pass and link up with North China. Victory—this time it is true victory—and we will meet again at home. I hope Mother, Elder Brother, Sister-in-law, and the little nephew are all in good health, and that we may all be reunited!"

"Credit for success need not be mine; credit for success must include me (功成不必在我，功成必定有我)." For a leader like this, the people will keep him in their hearts forever. Zhu Rui Road, Zhu Rui Square, Zhu Rui Primary School, Zhu Rui Park… Although General Zhu Rui ultimately never got to see his family again, our reporters found, all along the way, that the people have long since inscribed his name into the mountains and rivers of the motherland.

(This reporting received strong support from Yu Cheng, Zhang Qian, Zhou Shiyu, and Shi Wenting.)

Reporter's Notes

The Original Aspiration Is the Soul's Home

In the season when wisteria blooms, our reporters came to General Zhu Rui's former residence—a courtyard compound on the bank of the Longhe River in Suqian. It is utterly inconspicuous amid the bustle of passersby, yet it nurtured the man recorded in history as the "Father of New China's Artillery."

The original aspiration (初心) is the soul's home. Throughout more than twenty years of revolutionary life, Zhu Rui never changed his original aspiration, forging the resolve to "not shrink from difficulty (不避难)," the candor of "harboring no private thoughts (无私念)," and the wisdom to "act with capability (善作为)." Today, as the entire military deepens political consolidation training (政治整训) and carries out in-depth study and education on establishing and practicing a correct view of political achievement (正确政绩观), General Zhu Rui's original aspiration—which has never faded—is precisely a question posed to every leading cadre: today, for whom do we exercise power, and for whom do we seek benefit? Only by not forgetting the original aspiration and keeping the mission firmly in mind, and by firmly establishing a correct view of power (权力观), a correct view of political achievement (政绩观), and a correct view of one's undertaking (事业观), can we truly shoulder the weighty trust placed in us by the Party and the people.

永不偏航的弹道 ——记者追寻人民炮兵奠基人朱瑞烈士的初心与征程（下） ■解放军报记者 张科进 王德赛 第79集团军某旅组织炮兵分队实弹射击训练。付支祥摄 清明时节，手捧鲜花的人们，走进一南一北两座陵园。 山东孟良崮烈士陵园，一大一小两座坟茔紧紧依偎，里面长眠着一对母女。母亲陈若克，22岁；女儿出生仅仅19天，未及取名。 这一对母女，就是人民炮兵奠基人朱瑞的妻子和女儿。1941年反“扫荡”时，母女二人死于日寇屠刀之下。 辽宁锦州义县，朱瑞烈士陵园，墓前鲜花吐艳。1948年10月1日，朱瑞在解放义县的战斗中触雷牺牲。 从沂蒙山区，到辽沈大地，夫妻二人，一家三口，相隔7年，都把鲜血洒在祖国的热土上。 记者接力追寻朱瑞烈士的足迹，将脚步和笔触伸向更远的历史云烟。当“愿意把后半生交给大炮”的朱瑞，像一颗火热的弹丸义无反顾投身战斗的沙场，他光彩夺目的前半生，依然犹如永不偏航的弹道，吸引着我们崇敬的目光。 心中有信仰，肩上有担当 那些年，电视剧《人间正道是沧桑》热播。关于主角杨立青的原型，观众普遍认为融合了陈赓、宋时轮、杨至成、萧劲光等4位开国将领的特点。 “其实，朱瑞也是杨立青的原型之一。”在江苏省宿迁市朱瑞将军纪念馆，前来寻根的陆军兵种大学某士官学校学员黄新贺，正与同学们连线讨论。对比剧中杨立青“冒险救恩师”的情节与朱瑞“拼死救同志”的事迹，这名“00后”深受触动。 1930年10月，武汉笼罩在白色恐怖之中。当时，中共长江局遭敌破坏，急需得力的干部前去开展工作。 危急关头，时任中央军委参谋科参谋的朱瑞站了出来，表示准备“扛着棺材板”上任，毅然前往武汉。他躲过敌人重重搜捕，掩护关向应、任弼时等领导人成功转移，并冒死撤出大批秘密党员与统战对象。 黄新贺所在的士官学校，前身是延安炮兵学校。校史馆的一处展陈前，不少人驻足细看。那是朱瑞在延安整风期间撰写的《我的历史与思想自传》复印件，也是后人从第一人称视角走近他的珍贵史料。 1905年，朱瑞出生在江苏宿迁的一个殷实家庭。1925年，朱瑞赴苏联，先后在莫斯科中山大学、克拉辛炮兵学校学习；1928年，加入中国共产党。1930年，归国的朱瑞与党组织失去联系。血雨腥风中，有人退党，有人叛变，旧友劝他“别干革命了，一起经商吧”，并许以重要职位，均被他严词拒绝。 苦苦找寻半年，朱瑞终于重回组织怀抱。他后来回忆：“从离开莫斯科到找到党，前后6个月，是我一生中最孤零、最苦闷的时候。在找到党以前，我们很像失去母亲的孤儿……” 这番掏心窝子的话，让参与讨论的同学们感叹：真正的革命者，心里都住着一个“朱瑞”——心中有信仰，肩上有担当。 “革命事业不是田园牧歌，不可能一帆风顺。”每次走进朱瑞将军纪念馆，当地党史研究人员王秋雯都有新的感悟。她说，革命路上有人因信仰不坚定掉队、走散，甚至有人因信仰动摇背叛、堕落。正因为如此，那些一直走下去的人，才尤其值得铭记。 历经曲折坎坷，中国革命为什么能够走向胜利？发生在朱瑞和他的亲人身上的故事，让记者深刻体悟到“信仰的力量”—— 1936年，红军北上甘陕。为给三大主力会师开路，朱瑞冒死联络东北军。在与骑六师团长秘密接触前，他把记录机密的笔记本交给随行人员埋进高粱地，做好了牺牲的准备。 朱瑞的妻子陈若克烈士牺牲前，伸出流血的手说：“孩子，你来到世上，没有喝妈妈一口奶，现在就要和妈妈一起离开这个世界了，你就吸一口妈妈的血吧！” 朱瑞的家乡朱大兴庄，先后有他的20多位亲朋后辈加入革命队伍。鲁南反“扫荡”中，跟随朱瑞投身革命的侄女朱华牺牲于苍山、费县一带…… 沧海横流，方显英雄本色。这些故事，引起黄新贺和同学们共鸣：“越宏大的光，越需要炽烈的燃烧。真正的信仰，便是自愿成为那光里的灰烬。拒绝燃烧的人，永远只是历史进程中的投机客，被大浪淘沙般淘汰。” 深化国防和军队改革以来，这个士官学校历经多轮调整，面对异地任教、岗位变换、专业重构，全校教职员工毫无怨言。大家说：“朱瑞老校长为党的事业牺牲了家庭和自己的生命，我们这一点个人付出，又算得了什么？” 随时准备坚持真理，随时准备修正错误 临沭，处在山东抗日根据地整风运动中心，曾有“小延安”之称。 走访中共山东分局多处旧址，记者印象最深的，是展陈原汁原味地呈现了整风运动中对朱瑞的批评，以及他毫不讳言的自我剖析与检讨反思。 “对中共中央‘在山东争取优势’的任务没能完成，在反顽斗争上表现不力”“‘减租减息’不够深入，群众基本没能深入发动和组织起来”“丧失了一些建立政权的历史先机”……尖锐的批评意见，时隔80多年依然令人震撼。 身为山东分局“一把手”、毛泽东同志口中的“一方诸侯”，朱瑞如何直面这些“刺耳”的批评？当年参加批评会的《大众日报》副社长仲星帆回忆，朱瑞态度诚恳、神色庄重，还不时点头表示赞同，并平静地告诉他如实记录这些意见。 不仅如此，朱瑞还带头在分局会议上作了长达3小时的自我批评，主动承担山东工作“主要问题责任”，不推过、不诿下。前来指导工作的刘少奇说：“朱瑞同志勇于自我批评，改正缺点错误，为山东的整风作出重要贡献。” 走访沂蒙老区，记者听到当地至今口口相传的一个整风故事—— 鲁南地区，一支通信部队曾受领一个“头等重要的战斗任务”：架设电台接收延安的整风文件。由于遭遇日伪“扫荡”、补给断绝，官兵只能吃“救国粮”执行任务。所谓“救国粮”，其实就是牲口饲料。 任务结束当天，日伪上山搜剿，战士们赶在最后一刻译完文稿才转移下山。这最后一篇文稿，正是毛泽东的《反对党八股》。该文刊印后，鲁南地区的整风运动拉开帷幕。 一名讲解员告诉记者，当时有人认为，抗战形势紧张，没必要“外边扫荡、里边整风”。朱瑞却坚决要求：一把枪指向敌人，一把“枪”对准自己。 正是朱瑞以身作则、以上率下，坚持从高级干部抓起，把整风当硬仗来打，山东抗日根据地干部队伍经历了一次彻底的思想洗礼，大家上下一条心，真正向中央看齐。 从山东到延安，朱瑞那至真至纯的党性，一次次触动我们。中央党史和文献研究院披露的一则史料显示，在党的七大上，朱瑞发言时这样说道：“在山东工作时，我对能力、领导水平都比我强的罗荣桓同志，是不尊重的。不但在山东向他道歉，在今天党的大会上，我也应该向他道歉。” “整风就是要脱胎换骨、洗心革面，先从我们高级干部做起，先检讨自己，再批评别人”“你们有些年轻人过去都觉得我很了不起，甚至崇拜过我。其实不少地方是我的缺点，你们却当成了优点，这会影响你们”……40多年后，朱瑞的秘书毛鹏云忆及朱瑞这些肺腑之言，依然感慨：“一位高级干部如此直白地当众解剖自己，令人敬佩！” “时刻准备坚持真理，时刻准备修正错误！”杨家岭中央大礼堂内，这句标语让记者仿佛回到80多年前。那时的朱瑞，应该听过毛泽东所作的《论联合政府》报告，一定对这句话感同身受—— “共产党人必须随时准备坚持真理，因为任何真理都是符合于人民利益的；共产党人必须随时准备修正错误，因为任何错误都是不符合于人民利益的。” 功成不必在我，功成必定有我 义县，锦州北大门。当地县志记载：城墙高10米、厚4米，外有宽深护城河，敌人以坚固城墙为依托，构筑碉堡、地堡群，工事坚固、易守难攻。 初夏，记者站在古城仅存的“安泰门”下仰望，敬畏感油然而生：1948年10月的那场战斗，人民解放军是如何仅用6小时就攻克了这座坚城？ 答案藏在朱瑞烈士陵园的展陈里。当年的战斗记录如此记载—— “义县战斗中，我军出动上百门火炮，含150毫米重榴弹炮、105毫米榴弹炮、野炮、山炮、迫击炮等，分3个炮群，前沿抵近部署。” “持续的炮火打击彻底压制守军，敌师长王世高供述时哀叹：‘城墙内火力点完全被炮火封锁，动弹不得，不是被打死，就是被炮弹压得抬不起头’。” 义县之战，刷新我军城市攻坚战最短时间全歼守敌纪录，也成了锦州攻坚战的“火力预演”，是朱瑞生命之光大放异彩的巅峰时刻。 谁能想到，这位人民炮兵的奠基人、苏联克拉辛炮兵学校毕业考试和实弹射击双项第一的高材生，在回国后10余年里都没能从事炮兵工作。 正如朱瑞所说，“1930年回国时没有条件”。当时的红军，包括后来的八路军、新四军，火炮都极为稀少，还远远不能成为一个兵种。在苏联接受系统炮兵教育的朱瑞，实在是没有用武之地。这10余年中，朱瑞做过地下工作，做过统战工作，做得最多、最久的是党务工作，都和炮兵不沾边儿。 然而，欲做大树，就有参天的梦想。 1944年底，毛泽东和周恩来在延安会见驻华美军观察小组时，伯德中校抛出一个问题：“毛先生，在不远的将来，八路军会不会大举反攻？”毛泽东坚定地说：“会的，而且很快要打到城里去！”伯德接着问：“我知道贵军没有炮兵和坦克兵，如何才能攻城呢？”周恩来笑着接过话：“中国有句俗话，车到山前必有路。”“车？周先生，我们说的是炮的问题！”伯德大惑不解。 一年之后，朱瑞挺身而出，慷慨请命，成为牵引人民炮兵战车的第一名“纤夫”。1948年，东北野战军炮兵以雷霆之势，一举攻克锦州城防。 为了这一天，朱瑞在延安婉辞中央军委副总参谋长的任命，不当高官当教员；为了这一天，朱瑞奔赴东北，带领官兵漫山遍野去“捡炮”；为了这一天，朱瑞在辽沈战役打响前，再次作出一个“无我抉择”—— 当时，东北炮兵建立起来了，朱瑞发现军工生产跟不上，再次请缨专门负责这项工作。在一封写给刘亚楼的信中，他的语气直白而坚定：“为了炮兵的长远发展，我不计较任何名义，请求免去我炮兵司令的职务，担负这一工作，做一辈子的办事人。” 攻克锦州之日，是朱瑞牺牲之后的第15天。东北烈士纪念馆里，记者看到他没有来得及发出的一封写给母亲和哥哥的家信，信中写道：“……东北发展很快，我想不久我们就要打进关，与华北会合，胜利（这次是真正的胜利了）与家乡见面，希望母亲、哥哥、嫂子及小侄等健康，均团圆见面才好！” “功成不必在我，功成必定有我”。对于这样的领导干部，人民会永远把他记在心里。朱瑞路、朱瑞广场、朱瑞小学、朱瑞公园……虽然朱瑞将军最终没能与亲人相见，但记者一路走来发现，人民早已将他的英名刻在祖国的山河中！ （采访得到余成、张茜、周士钰、史文婷大力支持） 记者手记 初心，是灵魂的家园 紫藤盛开的季节，记者来到朱瑞将军的故居——宿迁龙河畔那栋四合院。它在熙熙攘攘的行人中毫不起眼，却孕育了彪炳史册的“新中国炮兵之父”。 初心，是灵魂的家园。在20多年的革命生涯中，朱瑞始终不改初心，淬炼出“不避难”的担当、“无私念”的坦荡与“善作为”的智慧。在全军深化政治整训、深入开展树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育的今天，朱瑞将军那颗永不褪色的初心，恰是对每一名领导干部的叩问：今天，我们权为谁用、利为谁谋？只有不忘初心、牢记使命，树牢正确的权力观、政绩观、事业观，才能真正肩负起党和人民的如山重托。 请扫描二维码 浏览更多内容
