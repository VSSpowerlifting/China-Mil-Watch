How Should Young Officers and Soldiers of the New Era Walk "Our Long March Road"?

新时代年轻官兵如何走好“我们的长征路”？
PLA Daily (解放军报) 20 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
The People's Armed Police Jiangxi General Corps Ganzhou Branch's Yudu Detachment, a brigade under 72nd Group Army, and a brigade under 82nd Group Army are the subjects of 'Youth Long March Road' (青春长征路), a three-episode documentary produced by National Defense and Military Channel's 'Military Chronicle' (军事纪实) program and released this year to mark the 90th anniversary of the Long March. The documentary uses experiential activities, live-fire exercise footage, and individual soldier narratives — new recruit Tao Liang, volunteer guide Luo Ji, and squad leader Liu Shaojie — to address a specific institutional problem the production team encountered directly: post-1995 and post-2000 recruits acknowledge Long March history intellectually but report feeling emotionally disconnected from it, describing 'a wall' between themselves and their predecessors. The framing points to a recognized gap in political education effectiveness that the PLA and PAP are trying to close through immersive experience rather than didactic instruction, though whether this approach is being adopted systematically or remains a media-production initiative is not established by the article.
Translation

"After the detachment organized a straw-sandal 'march' experience activity, Tao Liang started repeating 'from quantitative change to qualitative change — don't fear slowness, only fear stopping' during every 5-kilometer armed cross-country training run!" When the National Defense and Military Channel's "Military Chronicle" (军事纪实) production team revisited the Yudu Detachment of the People's Armed Police Jiangxi General Corps Ganzhou Branch, and learned of new recruit Tao Liang's recent progress, the days spent creating "Youth Long March Road" (青春长征路) came flooding back.

This year marks the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Chinese Workers' and Peasants' Red Army's Long March. The "Military Chronicle" program recently released a three-episode documentary, "Youth Long March Road," turning its lens on young officers and soldiers born after 1995 and 2000 to explore the generational inheritance of the Long March spirit.

Each generation has its own Long March. Ninety years on, as we read this heroic epic that shook the mountains and rivers, and look up at this immortal monument that will shine through the ages, how should young officers and soldiers of the new era walk "our Long March road"?

From the very start of production, this question never left the minds of our core creative team. The answer gradually came into focus after we went deep into the grassroots and confronted the growth, transformation, and inner questioning of officers and soldiers — to use their real stories as the vehicle for a dialogue that transcends time and space.

"Youth" (青春) is the theme of this dialogue. Leafing through historical records, one set of figures gave us pause: 20 was the average age of Red Army soldiers who participated in the Long March; 25 was the average age of many Red Army commanders at the time. That great expedition was a breakout by a group of young people.

Today, the officers and soldiers stationed on the red soil along the Long March route are equally in the prime of their youth. Two generations of young people, separated by nearly a century, are "marching" along the same spiritual journey.

Yet when we attempted to use "the resonance of youth across two generations" as our narrative thread, new questions arose during our embedded reporting: young officers and soldiers are not unaware of Long March history and the Long March spirit, but they sometimes cannot find an emotional connection to the Red Army soldiers. A sense of distance — "our forebears were too great; we cannot compare" — made them feel as though there was "a wall" between themselves and that great expedition.

How to break down this wall? We came to realize that conveying the inheritance of spirit cannot be a simple splicing together of "historical replay + modern examples" with forced connections. More than 90 years ago, Red Army soldiers fought bloody battles, cutting a path through encirclement and pursuit; today, officers and soldiers of the new era charge toward battle, actively striving toward the centenary goal of building a strong military. We decided to break the Long March spirit down into three dimensions that young officers and soldiers could perceive — drawing out the contemporary resonance of the Long March spirit from their breakthroughs in training, their choices in the course of missions, and their breakouts from adversity.

Tao Liang felt that the distance between himself and his forebears was "closed" by a pair of straw sandals. When we were filming at the Yudu Detachment, the detachment was preparing to take new recruits to the local Red Army Long March trail for an experiential activity — this was an important road along which the main force of the Central Red Army crossed the Yudu River and broke through the first blockade line. To heighten the sense of immersion, the new recruits specifically changed into straw sandals and set out on a 3-kilometer "march."

In front of the camera, the new recruits who had put on straw sandals were full of novelty. After walking barely a hundred meters of mountain path, their ease and confidence had completely vanished: the mountain road was rugged and slippery, coarse hard rope bit into their ankles, straw stalks jabbed the soles of their feet, and pebbles worked through the gaps in the sandals to grind painfully into their palms… The camera faithfully recorded their transformation from light-footed strides to furrowed brows, and then to figures leaning on one another for support.

Tao Liang was among that group. Earlier, we had followed him with the camera as he participated in long-distance running training in full combat gear. Although he ran with great difficulty, he did not retreat: "The closer you get to the end, the stronger the suffocating feeling — but if you want to improve, don't stop."

This hiking experience made Tao Liang feel ever more deeply the Red Army's gritted-teeth perseverance that could not stop: "We knew from textbooks that the Long March was very bitter. It was only when we actually put on straw sandals that we discovered how concrete that bitterness is — even walking is 'bitter.' Yet the Red Army soldiers, for the faith in their hearts, pressed on without hesitation."

As more and more footage captured scenes of new recruits breaking through their own limits and challenging their extremes in training, what appeared before the audience was a shared spiritual core — struggle knows no era; only with indomitable perseverance and the courage to face difficulties head-on can one continue to walk the road ahead.

Luo Ji, a new recruit of a brigade under Army Group 72 of the Army, is a volunteer guide at the Ruijin Revolutionary Martyrs Memorial Hall. His experience as a guide was originally the focus of our narrative, but it changed because of one on-site shoot.

During the filming of a mountain combined demolition exercise, Luo Ji and his comrades split up and advanced separately, preparing to destroy an "enemy" machine-gun bunker. After simultaneously igniting their explosive packs, Luo Ji suddenly discovered that the fuse on his comrade's explosive pack had failed to ignite. In that critical moment, Luo Ji did not hesitate for an instant — he threw himself toward his comrade's explosive pack and, with calm composure, completed the detonation, ensuring the mission was successfully accomplished.

During the interview, we learned that Luo Ji carries a card with him at all times, on which are written the names of 17 Red Army martyrs from Huawu Village in Ruijin City. As a guide, he is very familiar with the stories of the 17 martyrs: before setting out on the Long March, 17 young men from Huawu Village each planted a pine tree; they all later died heroically, leaving only the green pines to silently watch over their hometown.

"Walking our Long March road means being able to step up when the moment calls for it." Luo Ji said plainly that the forebears were willing to give their lives for victory, and that for revolutionary soldiers of the new era, the mission always comes before personal safety. Luo Ji's words made us realize: red history has never been archival material displayed in a memorial hall — it is the spiritual yardstick engraved in the hearts of young officers and soldiers.

The Long March is a precious historical legacy; savoring it carefully, one can find the key to solving real-world problems. The story of the Miao soldier "Yun-Gui-Chuan" (云贵川) brought inspiration to Liu Shaojie, a newly appointed squad leader in a brigade under Army Group 82 of the Army.

Liu Shaojie is the top physical fitness performer in the entire brigade and has repeatedly won honors in higher-level competitions. However, less than six months after transferring from driver to squad leader, his squad ranked last in the entire battalion in assessments. Convinced that "sweat earns results," Liu Shaojie led the whole squad in extra physical fitness training — but the squad's assessment ranking remained at the bottom, plunging him into a period of self-doubt.

In the company's honor room, the account of "Yun-Gui-Chuan's" deeds in the Battle of Lazikou (腊子口战役) opened his eyes: facing the natural barrier of Lazikou and the enemy's heavy garrison, this young soldier used only a bamboo pole fitted with an iron hook to scale a sheer cliff, then used a long rope to help his comrades climb up, outflanking the enemy's rear and capturing the formidable stronghold in one stroke.

"The 'surprise attack on Lazikou' was not won by brute force — it was won by precisely leveraging the strengths of officers and soldiers and achieving clever coordinated cooperation." With his mind suddenly clear, Liu Shaojie adjusted his training approach: he taught each soldier according to their individual strengths and weaknesses, while also clarifying the division of labor and reinforcing coordination in subjects such as fortification construction and battlefield medical care, and refining tactical details. He later led the entire squad in a successful reversal, winning second place in the entire company.

Facing our camera, Liu Shaojie said with deep feeling: "As a troop leader of the new era, only by breaking through the 'Lazikou' of habitual thinking can one achieve real, tangible growth and progress."

On a journey of ten thousand li, straw sandals and combat boots "meet" across 90 years. The flames of war have long since faded, but the torch of spirit lit by that generation of young people has crossed time and space and never been extinguished — it will guide every generation to continue walking the Long March road.

Original Chinese
“中队上次组织穿草鞋体验‘行军’活动后，陶亮在5公里武装越野训练时，总把‘从量变到质变，不怕慢，就怕停’挂在嘴边！”当国防军事频道《军事纪实》栏目组回访武警江西总队赣州支队于都中队，了解到新兵陶亮的近况时，那些创作《青春长征路》的日子又浮现在眼前。 今年是中国工农红军长征胜利90周年。《军事纪实》栏目近日推出3集专题片《青春长征路》，把镜头对准95后、00后年轻官兵，探寻长征精神的时代传承。 一代人有一代人的长征。90年过去，品读这部气壮山河的英雄史诗，仰望这座彪炳千秋的不朽丰碑，新时代年轻官兵如何走好“我们的长征路”？ 创作伊始，这个问题一直萦绕在我们主创人员心头。答案，在深入基层、直面官兵的成长蜕变与心灵叩问后，逐渐清晰——以他们的真实故事为载体，完成一次跨越时空的对话。 “青春”，是这次对话的主题——翻阅史料时，一组数据让我们心头一颤：20岁，是参加长征红军战士的平均年龄；25岁，是当时诸多红军将领的平均年龄。那场伟大的远征，是一群年轻人的突围。 如今，驻守在长征沿线红色热土的官兵，同样风华正茂。两代人的青春，相隔近一个世纪，“走”在同一条精神征途上。 然而，当我们试图以“两代青年的青春共振”为叙事主线时，蹲点采访中又产生了新的追问：年轻官兵并非不了解长征历史和长征精神，但有时找不到与红军将士的情感连接点，“先辈太伟大，我们根本比不了”的距离感，让他们觉得似乎与那场远征之间“隔着一堵墙”。 如何打破这堵墙？我们意识到，精神传承的呈现，不是“历史回放+现代事例”的简单拼接和生硬勾连。90多年前，红军将士浴血奋战，是在围追堵截下杀出一条血路；如今，新时代官兵向战冲锋，是攻坚建军百年奋斗目标的主动求索。我们决定把长征精神拆解为年轻官兵可感知的3个维度，从他们训练中的突破、任务中的抉择、困境里的突围，挖掘长征精神的当代回响。 陶亮觉得自己与先辈的距离，正是一双草鞋“拉近”的。我们在于都中队取景时，中队正准备组织新兵到当地的红军长征小道体验，那是中央红军主力部队渡过于都河后突破第一道封锁线的重要道路。为了更有沉浸感，新兵们特意换上草鞋，踏上一段3公里的“行军”路。 镜头前，换上草鞋的新兵们新鲜感十足。走了不过百米山路，他们的轻松自信就荡然无存：山路崎岖湿滑，粗硬草绳勒紧脚踝，草梗直扎脚底，碎石从鞋缝钻入磨痛脚掌……镜头真实记录下他们从脚步轻盈到眉头紧锁，再到相互搀扶的身影。 陶亮就在这支队伍里。前期，我们曾跟拍他全副武装参加长跑训练。尽管跑得十分吃力，但他没有退缩：“越跑到最后，窒息感越强，但想进步就不要停。” 这次徒步体验，让陶亮愈发体会到当年红军那种不能停的咬牙坚持：“我们从课本上知道长征很苦，真的穿上草鞋才发现，这种苦很具象，连走路都是‘苦’的。可红军将士为了心中的信仰，还是义无反顾地走下去。” 当更多的镜头捕捉到新兵在训练中突破自我、挑战极限的场景时，呈现在观众眼前的，是一脉相通的精神内核——奋斗不分年代，坚韧不拔、迎难而上，才能继续走好脚下的路。 陆军第72集团军某旅新兵罗吉，是瑞金革命烈士纪念馆一名志愿讲解员。他的讲解员经历，原本是我们的叙事重点，却因为一次实地拍摄而发生改变。 拍摄一场山地协同爆破演练时，罗吉与战友分头突进，准备炸毁“敌”机枪堡。同步点燃炸药包后，罗吉突然发现战友炸药包的引线未能引燃。千钧一发之际，罗吉没有丝毫犹豫，扑向战友的炸药包，沉着冷静完成引爆处置，保证了任务顺利完成。 采访中，我们了解到，罗吉随身带着一张卡片，上面写着瑞金市华屋村17位红军烈士的姓名。作为讲解员，他对17位烈士的故事十分熟悉：长征出发前，华屋村17名青年栽下17棵松树，后来他们都英勇牺牲，唯留青松默默守护家乡。 “走好我们的长征路，就是在需要的时候能顶得上去。”罗吉直言，先辈们为了胜利甘愿付出生命，对新时代革命军人来说，任务永远排在个人安危前面。罗吉的话，让我们意识到：红色历史从来不是陈列在纪念馆里的史料，而是刻在青年官兵心底的精神标尺。 长征是宝贵的历史财富，细细品味能找到破解现实难题的钥匙。苗族战士“云贵川”的故事，就带给陆军第82集团军某旅新任班长刘少杰启迪。 刘少杰是全旅体能训练尖子，在上级比武中屡获佳绩。然而，从驾驶员转岗班长不足半年，全班考核在全营排名垫底。坚信“汗水换成绩”的刘少杰带领全班加练体能，但之后考核排名依旧垫底，让他一度陷入自我怀疑。 在连队荣誉室，“云贵川”在腊子口战役中的事迹点醒了他：面对腊子口天堑和敌军重兵把守，这个小战士仅凭带铁钩的竹竿攀上绝壁，用长绳帮助战友们攀援而上，迂回敌后一举攻克天险。 “‘奇袭腊子口’不是靠蛮力硬冲，而是精准发挥官兵特长，巧妙协同配合取得胜利。”茅塞顿开的刘少杰调整训练思路，针对每名战士优势短板因材施教，同时在掩体构筑、战场救护等课目中明确分工、强化配合，打磨战术细节。后来他带领全班成功实现逆袭，夺得全连第二名。 面对我们的镜头，刘少杰深有感触地说：“作为新时代的带兵人，只有突破思维惯性的‘腊子口’，才能取得实实在在的成长进步。” 万里征途上，草鞋与战靴在90年后“相逢”。烽火硝烟早已远去，但那群青年点燃的精神火炬，穿越时空，从未熄灭，将指引每一代人继续走好长征路。
