Reporter's Notes | Two Encounters: Collecting a Moment of Quiet Strength

记者手记｜两度相逢，收藏一段温柔的力量
PLA Daily (解放军报) 10 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A PLA Daily or defense-media reporter reflects on two rounds of embedded coverage of Wang Ji, a computer science professor at the National University of Defense Technology (NUDT) who received the Central Propaganda Department's 'Model of the Era' (时代楷模) designation, focusing on his team's software research conducted through serious illness. The piece is a reporter's notebook entry, not a news report; its value is as a record of how PLA institutional media frames civilian-track defense researchers — sustained, self-sacrificing, indifferent to recognition — as moral exemplars for both military audiences and journalists covering them. It documents the propaganda architecture around NUDT's computer science faculty but provides no information about Wang Ji's specific research program, his team's technical outputs, or the software capabilities at issue.
Translation

In June, I walked once again into the College of Computer Science at the National University of Defense Technology for an interview. Earlier this year, when I was writing a piece for the column "Forging a Centenary Army: New Faces, New Actions (攻坚建军百年·新样貌 新作为)," I had the opportunity to interview Professor Wang Ji for the first time. This was our second meeting, half a year later, with a whole spring in between. He had grown thinner — his round cheeks had sunken in somewhat, and he looked older. His short sleeves made the scars on his arms more visible, like a winding red river. But his eyes were the same as before: bright, carrying a smile. Professor Wang is truly someone who loves to smile; the layered creases at the corners of his eyes seem to harbor an optimistic wisdom.

In conversation, his sense of genius is not particularly heavy — he is like a kind, humorous elder who speaks at an unhurried pace, telling stories, sharing feelings, one thing at a time. But Professor Wang at work is sharp, direct, and brilliant. The first time I went to film him running a group meeting in winter, he rejected my request for an opening remark. He said, "We don't say things like that before our discussions," even though I had only shallowly hoped for something like "Now let's have a certain student begin." When filming started in earnest, Professor Wang ran the group meeting, firing questions one after another at the student presenting the paper, with no buffer.

But he was still smiling. Professor Wang never speaks of hardship — or rather, never speaks of his own hardship. "Everyone is like this; everyone works very hard, everyone feels a sense of urgency." His colleagues and his students all say the same. On the journey of a great undertaking (伟大事业), he and they are exhausted, will be exhausted — but they have never feared exhaustion. Those all-night lights, that invested toil: "it is all as it should be."

Professor Wang says that software is his battlefield. On the way to Tianhe Tower one day, on a sudden impulse, I added a line to my interview outline: "Professor Wang, has this battle been fought well?" Everyone else says it has been fought well — what does Professor Wang himself think? His answer I included in the written piece. There was actually a follow-up question, directed at his colleagues and students: "And do you think you have fought it well?" Mao Xiaoguang, a long-time colleague of Professor Wang's and a core member of the team, waved his hand and said: "Not bad, I suppose. Being able to play a useful role — for us, that is already very good." Liu Xingpeng, one of Wang Ji's doctoral students, said: "I have confidence that I can fight it as well as my teacher."

On the morning of the day Wang Ji was awarded the title "Model of the Era (时代楷模)" by the Central Propaganda Department, he held a meeting with his students from eight-thirty in the morning straight through to noon. For Professor Wang, this day seemed to remain an ordinary day. I thought of the words Professor Wang had inscribed on the title page of Battle Song from a Wheelchair (《轮椅上的战歌》) — the same words his mentor, Professor Chen Huowang, had once inscribed for him: "Indifference to fame clarifies one's aspirations; tranquility enables one to reach far (淡泊以明志，宁静以致远)."

Two follow-up interviews, full-process coverage — for me, this had long since ceased to be an ordinary work assignment. This encounter spanning half a year has been a quiet and precious form of growth.

The first thing I came to understand was pure love (纯粹的热爱). Professor Wang has led his team through decades of devoted work in the laboratory, rising early and retiring late, quietly cultivating. In a time of hurried rhythms and the demand for speed in all things, this steadfast commitment made without calculation of gain or loss is especially moving. I have finally come to truly understand that love is enough to withstand every hardship. In the work that lies ahead, I too am willing to hold onto this purity — to carefully polish every line of text, every frame of footage, and to approach every story with sincerity.

The second thing I gained was the strength to grow by confronting difficulties head-on and pressing forward in the face of adversity (迎难而上). From the moment I received the assignment to the broadcast of the completed news series, I thought of how Professor Wang endured more than a hundred days in intensive care, more than ten surgeries, and hundreds of dialysis sessions, yet persisted in traveling back and forth among the ward, the laboratory, and the classroom to press forward on his research — and so what is there that I cannot overcome?

This stretch of interviews has now drawn to a close. Illness and pain cannot restrain Professor Wang's forward steps; within his cheerful smile lies a perseverance that does not cease. I will carefully keep what I have learned from him — his passion, tenacity, rigor, and humility — and, carrying the strength gathered from this encounter, I will move forward steadily and earnestly cultivate my craft.

Original Chinese
六月，我再一次走进国防科技大学计算机学院采访。今年年初撰写《攻坚建军百年·新样貌 新作为》专栏稿的时候，我有幸去采访过王戟老师一次。这是半年后我再一次见到他，跨过一个春天，他变瘦了，圆圆的脸颊有些凹陷进去，老了一些，短袖让他手臂上的疤更明显起来，像是一条蜿蜒的红河。但是他的眼睛还是那样，明亮的，带着笑意。王老师真是很爱笑的人啊，眼尾的层层褶皱里似乎都藏着乐观的智慧。 在对谈的时候，他的天才感其实是不太重的，像一个和蔼的、幽默的、语速不太快的长辈，讲故事，讲感受，娓娓道来。但工作中的王老师，犀利、直接、天才。冬天第一次去拍王老师开组会时，他拒绝了我想要一个开场白的要求，他说“我们讨论之前不会说这种话”，即使我只是浅薄地想要一句“下面让某个同学开始”。正式开拍，王老师开组会，一个一个问题砸给讲论文的学生，没有缓冲。 但他还是笑着的。王老师从来不谈苦，或者说，从来不谈自己的苦，“大家都是这样的，都很辛苦，都有紧迫感”。他的同事，他的学生，都这样说。在伟大事业的征程中，他和他们很累，会累，但是从来不怕累。那些彻夜的灯光，付出的辛劳，“都是应该的”。 王老师说，软件是他的战场。某个去天河楼的路上，我突发奇想，在采访大纲里加了一句，“王老师，这场仗打得漂亮吗？”大家都说漂亮，王老师自己觉得呢？他的回答被我放进了稿件里。其实还有后面一个问题，是给王老师同事和学生的：“那您觉得，您打得漂亮吗？”王老师多年的同事，团队核心骨干毛晓光老师摆摆手说：“还可以吧，能发挥作用，对于我们来说就很好。”刘兴鹏是王戟的博士生，他说：“有信心打得和老师一样漂亮。” 王戟被中央宣传部授予“时代楷模”称号后的那天上午，他跟学生开会，从早上八点半一直到中午十二点。这一天，对于王老师，似乎仍是平凡的一天。我想到王老师题在《轮椅上的战歌》扉页上的话，也是他的导师陈火旺先生题给他的那句话——“淡泊以明志，宁静以致远”。 两次跟踪采访、全程跟进宣传，于我而言，早已不只是一份普通的工作任务。这场跨越半年的相逢，是一场安静又珍贵的成长。 我最先读懂的，是纯粹的热爱。王老师带领团队数十年沉心实验室，夙兴夜寐，默默耕耘。在节奏匆忙、凡事求快的当下，这份不计得失的坚守格外动人。我终于真切体会，热爱足以抵御万难。往后工作，我也愿守住这份纯粹，认真打磨每一段文字、每一帧画面，用心对待每一个故事。 其次收获的，是直面难题、迎难而上的成长力量。从接到采访任务，到系列新闻成片播出，我想到王老师历经百余天重症监护、十余次手术、数百次透析，仍坚持往返病房、实验室、教室三点一线攻关，那么我又有什么不能战胜的呢？ 这段采访落下帷幕。病痛束缚不住王老师向前的脚步，他开朗的笑容里，藏着的是长久不息的坚持。我会把从他身上习得的热忱、坚韧、严谨与谦和妥帖收好，带着这份相逢收获的力量，踏实稳步，认真耕耘。
