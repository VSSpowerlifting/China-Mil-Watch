Wu Rui, Political Instructor of a Certain Subunit of the PAP Nanchang Detachment: Wherever We Redeploy, the "Loyalty Stone" Follows

武警南昌支队某中队指导员吴睿：移防到哪,"忠诚石"就跟到哪
PLA Daily (解放军报) 10 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Wu Rui, political instructor of a subunit of the PAP Nanchang Detachment, recounts how a stone inscribed with the characters 忠诚 (Loyalty), carved by veterans in 1987, has been physically transported through four garrison relocations—including the 1998 Yangtze flood response and the 2018 PAP reorganization under CMC centralized command—and now stands at the compound gate as a unit heritage object. This is standard political work content in the '传统话今天' (Traditions in Today's Conversation) format; its value is as a record of how the PAP frames institutional loyalty and continuity across organizational disruption for junior officers, not as evidence of a new policy line. The 2018 detail—repositioning the stone to the gate specifically at the moment of CMC centralized command—documents how unit-level political instructors are narrating the reorganization as a loyalty inflection point rather than an administrative change.
Translation

In a certain subunit of the People's Armed Police (PAP) Nanchang Detachment, a blue-gray stone stands silent guard at the camp gate, the two characters "忠诚" (Loyalty) blazing red on its face. Since the subunit first moved into its original garrison in 1987, this "Loyalty Stone" has followed the officers and soldiers through four relocations (移防), becoming a "spiritual anchor point" for generations of the subunit's personnel. In this installment of "Traditions in Today's Conversation," we invited the subunit's political instructor Wu Rui to share the story behind the "Loyalty Stone."

Answer: A single stone bears witness to the journey of strengthening the military (强军历程) and embodies a set of values. The two characters "Loyalty" carved on the stone represent both the conviction passed down from generation to generation among the subunit's officers and soldiers, and speak to our bond of blood with this land.

Ninety-nine years ago, the Party led the people's army in firing the first shot of armed resistance against the Kuomintang reactionaries from the city of Nanchang. After Nanchang was liberated in 1949, the subunit's predecessor took on urban garrison duties, putting down roots in this heroic land. At that time, Nanchang faced a complex threat environment and had everything yet to be built; our predecessors started from nothing and devoted themselves fully to safeguarding local stability and the people's peace. "Loyalty" had long since been fused deep into the blood and soul of the subunit's officers and soldiers.

In 1987, the subunit was commended as an "Advanced Duty Subunit." Several veterans on the verge of discharge wanted to leave the subunit a gift. They searched the hills, forests, gullies, and ravines near the garrison, found a large boulder, and chiseled the two characters "Loyalty" into it.

On the day the characters were painted red, the subunit held a solemn ceremony. Officers and soldiers stood straight as pines and raised their fists in an oath before the "Loyalty Stone": "Loyal to the Party, devoted to the mission; when the subunit flourishes, I share its glory; when the subunit falters, I share its shame." From that day on, the stone became our heirloom.

Answer: Fine traditions are the "true gold" panned from the long river of history. They must not be discarded — to discard them is to lose one's soul. They must not be altered — to alter them is to corrupt their essence. And so, each time the subunit relocated and the question arose of whether to move the "Loyalty Stone," every generation of officers and soldiers gave the same answer.

In 1988, the subunit was relocated in its entirety due to a mission adjustment. Some said the cost of transporting the "Loyalty Stone" would be better spent on extra rations to reward the soldiers. The political instructor at the time, Sun Chuankui, felt that while officers and soldiers certainly needed material encouragement, spiritual motivation would produce a more lasting and profound effect. And so the "Loyalty Stone" moved with the officers and soldiers for the first time.

Ten years passed in an instant. The Yangtze River basin suffered catastrophic flooding; surging floodwaters poured into the city, and the people's lives hung in the balance. The subunit's officers and soldiers moved on order — by day they hauled sandbags, plugged seepage channels, and reinforced levees; by night they patrolled the embankments to check for danger, keeping watch through the night. They fought for dozens of days on the flood-control front line and returned with a collective Third-Class Merit citation. Shortly after returning to their garrison, the subunit was to move to another compound. What was to be done with the "Loyalty Stone"? Everyone's thinking was the same — winning the hard battle of flood control had depended on the firm conviction of loyalty to the Party, and the two characters "Loyalty" must be forever engraved in the heart. And so the "Loyalty Stone" completed its second transfer.

The "Loyalty Stone's" third relocation came in 2007. To accommodate the local city's development plan, higher authorities built the subunit a new garrison compound. Someone suggested that the "Loyalty Stone," weathered and somewhat worn, clashed with the brand-new barracks, and proposed taking the opportunity to replace it with a more attractive and imposing stone — but the officers and soldiers rejected the idea. They said that every mark on the stone is a witness to the subunit's growth and struggle. What does it matter that it looks plain? That cannot conceal the spiritual radiance contained within. Officers and soldiers worked together and moved the "Loyalty Stone" into the new garrison.

In the course of deepening national defense and military reform, the detachment's organizational structure was adjusted and the subunit's designation changed. Beginning at midnight on January 1, 2018, the PAP came under the centralized and unified leadership of the Party Central Committee and the Central Military Commission (CMC). The subunit decided to move the "Loyalty Stone" from the cultural corridor to a prominent position at the garrison gate, so that officers and soldiers entering and leaving the compound would see it the moment they looked up.

Answer: Compared with the older generation, young officers and soldiers have grown up in a very different environment — but every generation has its own mountains to climb and passes to break through; that has not changed. The "Loyalty Stone" is like a spiritual "refueling station." When we look at it, we recall the stories of struggle of generation after generation of officers and soldiers, and it stirs in us the will to strive and refuse to fall behind.

Not long ago, higher authorities organized a mass military skills competition (群众性比武竞赛). The eight competitors the subunit sent were not deeply experienced, but they carried the vigorous energy of youth. Before setting out, they assembled in front of the "Loyalty Stone" and revisited the stories of their predecessors' determined charges forward —

That year, many areas were struck by rare low-temperature rain and snow disasters. In one location, roads were blocked and the power grid had collapsed, leaving large numbers of people stranded in the rain and snow. At the critical moment, subunit backbone Dai Bing volunteered for the mission, leading his team members through knee-deep snow with power cables on their shoulders and repair tools in hand, fighting continuously for dozens of hours. After long hours of high-intensity work, his strength gave out as he climbed a steep slope, and he collapsed unconscious in the snow…

"We must shout our own declaration of loyalty (忠诚宣言)!" In the end, carrying that strength with them, the officers and soldiers worked continuously through intense heat and achieved outstanding results in the competition.

Original Chinese
在武警南昌支队某中队，一块青灰色的石头静静守卫着营门，石面上“忠诚”二字鲜红夺目。从1987年第一次进驻原来的营区，这块“忠诚石”跟着官兵4次“移防”，成为中队几代官兵的“精神锚点”。本期“传统今日谈”，我们请该中队指导员吴睿讲讲“忠诚石”背后的故事。 答：一块石头，见证的是强军历程，彰显的是价值取向。石头上的“忠诚”二字，既代表了中队官兵代代相传的信念，也诉说着我们与这片土地的血脉联结。 99年前，党领导人民军队在南昌城头打响了武装反抗国民党反动派的第一枪。1949年南昌解放后，中队前身担负城市警备任务，把根扎在这片英雄的土地上。那时的南昌，敌情复杂、百废待兴，前辈们白手起家，全力守护一方稳定和百姓安宁，“忠诚”早已深深融入中队官兵的血脉灵魂。 1987年，中队获评“执勤先进中队”。几位即将退役的老兵想给中队留下一份礼物，于是跑遍营区附近的山林沟壑，寻来一块巨石，凿上“忠诚”二字。 描红当天，中队组织了庄重的仪式。官兵们挺拔如松，在“忠诚石”前举拳宣誓——“忠诚于党、献身使命，队兴我荣、队衰我耻”。从此，这块石头成了我们的传家宝。 答：优良传统是历史长河中淘出的“真金”，它不能丢，丢了就丢了魂；更不能变，变了就变了质。因此每次中队搬迁，“忠诚石”面临是否搬家的选择时，每代官兵都给出了相同的答案。 1988年，中队因任务调整需整体搬迁。有人说，搬运“忠诚石”的费用，还不如用来加餐，犒劳一下战士们。时任指导员孙传魁感到，官兵固然需要物质层面的鼓励，但精神上的激励会产生更久远、更深刻的作用。就这样，“忠诚石”跟着官兵第一次搬家。 10年转瞬即逝。长江流域遭遇特大洪涝灾害，滔滔洪水涌入城内，人民群众危在旦夕。中队官兵闻令而动，白天扛沙袋、堵管涌、固堤坝，夜里巡堤查险、通宵值守，数十天鏖战在抗洪一线，捧回了集体三等功。归建后不久，中队要迁至其他营区。“忠诚石”怎么办？大家的想法很统一——打赢抗洪攻坚战，靠的就是忠诚于党的坚定信念，“忠诚”二字要永远刻在心间。于是，“忠诚石”完成了第二次转场。 “忠诚石”第三次搬迁是2007年。为配合驻地城市发展规划，上级为中队盖了新营区。有人提出，这块历经风雨、略显陈旧的“忠诚石”和崭新的营房不协调，提议趁机换一块更美观大气的石头，但遭到官兵反对。大家说，石头上的每一道痕迹，都是中队成长奋斗的见证。外观朴素又如何？遮不住蕴藏其中的精神光芒。官兵们齐心协力，将“忠诚石”搬进了新营区。 在深化国防和军队改革中，支队的编制体制进行了调整，中队番号也有了变化。自2018年1月1日零时起，武警部队由党中央、中央军委集中统一领导，中队决定将“忠诚石”从文化长廊迁移到营区门口的醒目位置，让出入营区的官兵第一眼就能看见。 答：与老一辈相比，年轻官兵成长环境大不一样，但每一代人都有要翻越的山峰、攻克的隘口，这一点是不会变的。“忠诚石”就像精神“加油站”，我们看到它，就想起一代代官兵的奋斗故事，激扬起不甘落后的奋斗意志。 前不久，上级组织群众性比武竞赛。中队派出的8名参赛者资历不深，但身上有股朝气蓬勃的青春力量。临出发前，他们在“忠诚石”前集结，重温前辈矢志冲锋的故事—— 那一年，多地遭遇罕见的低温雨雪灾害，某地道路封堵、电网瘫痪，大量群众被困在雨雪中。危急时刻，中队骨干戴兵主动请战，在齐膝的积雪中带领队员肩扛线缆、手拎抢修工具，连续奋战数十小时。由于长时间高强度工作，他攀爬陡坡时体力不支，晕倒在雪中…… “我们要喊出属于自己的‘忠诚’宣言！”最终，带着这份力量，官兵们顶着高温连续作业，在比武中夺得优异成绩。
