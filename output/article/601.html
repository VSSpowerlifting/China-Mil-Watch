The Road Home to Visit Family: Mountains No Longer High, Waters No Longer Long

探亲之路：山不再高，水不再漫长
PLA Daily (解放军报) 23 May 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
PLA Daily's second installment in a series on the 正确政绩观 (correct outlook on political achievement) campaign documents concrete welfare improvements at three remote garrisons: a People's Armed Police 2nd Mobile Corps brigade along the Sichuan-Tibet and Xinjiang-Tibet highways established a military-local government transportation support system for family visits; a Rocket Force brigade in southern China built children's recreational facilities in family quarters ahead of the summer visit peak; and a naval observation and communications brigade on an East China Sea island coordinated regular flights and emergency transport with local carriers to protect leave entitlements. The article documents how the campaign is being operationalized at brigade level as a mechanism for converting political-work directives into measurable logistics and welfare outputs—the framing that 'stabilizing the rear consolidates the front' positions family support as a combat-readiness input, which fits a recurring PLA effort to reduce attrition pressure on personnel serving in geographically isolated postings.

The Road Home to Visit Family: Mountains No Longer High, Waters No Longer Long

— A News Investigation into Party Committees and Organs at All Levels Using the Correct Outlook on Political Achievement (正确政绩观) to Help Resolve Difficulties for Grassroots Officers and Soldiers, Part II

■ PLA Daily Reporters Wang Yue, Xie Fei, Jin Meizi

A People's Armed Police officer and family members who came to visit pose for a photo. Photo by Lu Wenkai.

In the early summer on the plateau, a minibus bearing a "Double Support (双拥)" emblem traveled steadily along National Highway 318.

A dedicated vehicle to meet them at the airport, oxygen supply, a military doctor accompanying them along the way… Yang Di, wife of Luo Qizhi, a staff officer with a brigade of the People's Armed Police 2nd Mobile Corps, told reporters that throughout this family visit, she was surrounded by warmth at every moment. Yet recalling her very first visit to the unit, she said: "It was a bit like being stuck in a terrible journey."

"After getting off the plane, I took the airport shuttle to the city, then hailed a ride-hailing car to the long-distance bus station, then took a long-distance coach to the county town, then took a taxi from there to the garrison… I dragged two suitcases through transfer after transfer and didn't reach the unit's gate until late at night," said Yang Di.

"Why didn't you apply in advance to the organ for a vehicle, or request leave to pick up your family member?" Some time ago, when the brigade's leadership learned of Yang Di's experience during a research visit, they asked Luo Qizhi this question. Luo explained: "Long-distance vehicle dispatch requires advance application to the organ, and whether a vehicle can be sent also depends on the weather. Day-to-day missions are heavy, and I felt embarrassed asking for leave—I could only ask my wife to bear with it."

It is understood that this brigade has long been responsible for maintenance and traffic-keeping operations along the Sichuan-Tibet and Xinjiang-Tibet highways. Its positions are dispersed and remote, and conditions at the garrison are harsh. As the research deepened, many officers and soldiers noted that inconvenient transportation is the primary reason family members are reluctant to come for visits.

"Over the years, although we have invested a great deal of funding and effort in housing and living support, the number of family members coming to visit remains low." At a brigade Party committee meeting, this leader spoke based on his research experience: the brigade's garrison is remote with almost no civilian support infrastructure. We must not only work hard on food and accommodation, but also strive to solve the transportation problem—to provide good service and support for military family members coming to visit, and to create convenient conditions for officers, soldiers, and their families to reunite.

Shortly thereafter, the brigade established a special working group to go deep into the grassroots and understand the transportation needs of military family members coming to visit. It drew up a list of difficulties to be resolved targeting frequently raised issues such as "inconvenient pickup and drop-off at stations," and designated specific personnel to follow up on solutions. The working group also made multiple visits to relevant local government departments to coordinate solutions for transportation support during family visits. Ultimately, the military and local authorities jointly formulated a Detailed Plan for Transportation Services and Support for Military Family Members and a Special Preferential Treatment Implementation Plan for "Supporting the Military and Giving Preferential Treatment to Military Families—Warming the Hearts of Servicemembers (拥军优属 情暖军心)," based on the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Status and Rights and Interests Protection of Military Personnel and the Opinions on Strengthening Preferential Treatment Work for Servicemembers and Their Families, Veterans, and Other Entitled Groups jointly issued by the Ministry of Veterans Affairs and 19 other departments. This established a three-in-one support system of "expense reimbursement + dedicated vehicle pickup and drop-off + efficient service," effectively enhancing the sense of honor (荣誉感), sense of gain (获得感), and sense of happiness (幸福感) of servicemembers and their families.

From the snow-capped plateau to the desert Gobi to high-mountain islands, reporters visited garrison after garrison over consecutive days and learned that since the launch of study and education on establishing and practicing the correct outlook on political achievement (正确政绩观), Party committees across units have continuously recalibrated the orientation of political achievement, rolling out a series of concrete measures to address real difficulties in areas such as family visits to units and officers' and soldiers' home leave, making the road home so that "mountains are no longer high and waters are no longer long."

Deep in the mountains of southern China, reporters entered a Rocket Force brigade where a brigade leader was leading organ cadres in revising and optimizing the support plan for visiting family members, drawing on the newly revised joint regulations and the Several Provisions on Regulating and Improving Certain Welfare Benefits for Military Personnel. Reporters learned that, in addition to improving transportation services, the brigade—looking ahead to the summer peak period for family visits—had collected and compiled officers' and soldiers' opinions and suggestions in advance. In response to the frequently raised grassroots request to "establish children's recreational areas," the brigade organ flexibly utilized space in the family quarters compound, designating a dedicated children's zone. They assigned specific personnel to procure equipment such as slides and swings, closely monitored the measurement and installation process, and ensured the children's play area would be ready for use by summer.

On the shore of the East China Sea, Navy Petty Officer He Chunlin of a naval observation and communications brigade, having just completed a specialized mission, submitted a leave application to his company. The brigade's leadership introduced that in recent years, the entire brigade has faced overlapping major missions and heavy training demands, but the brigade Party committee has made clear: no matter how saturated the mission load, the leave system must be strictly implemented and officers' and soldiers' home leave time must be fully protected. Building on the implementation of the Interim Regulations on Military Personnel Leave and relevant provisions of the newly revised joint regulations, the brigade scientifically coordinates officers' and soldiers' home leave arrangements according to actual mission conditions, and has explored multiple effective mechanisms to ensure that all personnel who are entitled to leave take their leave (应休尽休).

During interviews at one of the brigade's observation and communications stations, officers and soldiers told reporters that the island they garrison is far from the mainland, has no air service, and transportation is extremely inconvenient. In the past, leave could only be taken by catching a supply ship off the island, and supply ships came only once every many days. Master Sergeant Pan Jixu recalled that one summer he applied for leave, but an approaching typhoon prevented the supply ship from docking for an extended period, his departure from the island was delayed again and again, and in the end he had no choice but to cancel his leave plans.

Today, the situation is very different. The brigade has actively communicated with the local government to coordinate the opening of regular flights, making the transport of supplies and the movement of personnel on and off the island far more convenient than before. Moreover, with strong support from higher authorities, the brigade has also established a cooperation mechanism with local carriers to meet the emergency transportation needs of island-garrison personnel.

"Matters that concern the vital interests of officers and soldiers must absolutely never remain at the level of slogans." With a gentle sea breeze blowing, a brigade leader told reporters: "Serving officers and soldiers is serving combat effectiveness. Only when everyone is free from worries at home can they devote themselves wholeheartedly to training and war preparation."

**Feeling the Honor of Being a Military Family Member on the Plateau**

■ Military Spouse Yang Di

This visit to the plateau left me feeling warmly embraced, and I deeply experienced the unit's tender, genuine care for military family members.

The moment I walked out of the terminal, I saw my husband's comrades holding a pickup sign waiting at the exit. When we met, everyone rushed to help carry my luggage and saw me onto a minibus bearing a "Double Support (双拥)" emblem—these warm gestures moved me deeply.

On the road from the airport to the unit's garrison, everyone not only thoughtfully handed me an oxygen bottle, but also carefully shared tips for preventing altitude sickness. The vehicle traveled steadily along National Highway 318. Outside the windows, snow-capped mountains stretched into the distance and white clouds hung low; inside the vehicle, several military spouses traveling together chatted and laughed, filling the long journey with cheerful voices. The "one-stop" service and support gave us the feeling of going home.

Arriving at the garrison and stepping into the apartment, the room was bright and clean, the bedding was neatly folded, and the kitchen was fully stocked with oil, salt, soy sauce, and vinegar, as well as pots, pans, and utensils—truly a move-in-ready stay. Especially when I heard my husband's comrades calling out "sister-in-law" one after another, my heart felt warm, and all the exhaustion of the long journey melted away.

In the past, I always felt that the four words "military family members are honorable (军属光荣)" sounded impressive, but in real life, the identity of "military family member" meant for me more than anything the hardship of "carrying a whole family on one's own." On this visit, from the inland to the plateau, from departure to arrival, there was a "green channel" all the way and thoughtful arrangements throughout. The other military spouses and I truly felt the warmth and deep affection carried by "military family members are honorable."

The thousand-li road home to visit family—at one end is an ordinary household, at the other is the nation's frontier. While feeling the honor of being a military family member on the plateau, I also deeply appreciated the hardships of frontier soldiers. As a military spouse, I will continue as always to keep our home in good order and wholeheartedly support my husband in taking root on the plateau and rendering meritorious service in the military.

(Compiled by PLA Daily reporter Wang Yue)

**"Stabilizing the Rear (稳后方)" Is "Consolidating the Front (固前方)"**

■ Ma Liang, Assistant Officer, Quartermaster and Barracks Section, a Brigade of the 76th Group Army

Late at night, when word came that the visiting family members had safely returned home, the weight I had been carrying finally lifted.

Some time ago, I was responsible for support work for family members temporarily visiting the unit. In fact, well before the family members arrived, the brigade Party committee had set the tone: treat "stabilizing the rear" as a major matter of "consolidating the front," with organs proactively moving forward to serve and getting real things done in ways that truly reach the hearts of military family members.

To that end, we conducted a detailed survey across the entire brigade, gathering information on family members' arrival times, accompanying personnel, and actual difficulties, and compiled a service and support checklist for visiting family members. For every item on the checklist, we designated a specific person responsible and set a clear deadline for completion.

In the past, what furniture to put in the apartments and what decorating style to use was typically decided by the organ. This time, the brigade broke with convention and organized a special "Military Camp Furniture Selection Exhibition," working with manufacturers to screen dozens of environmentally friendly household items. To increase the sense of participation among officers, soldiers, and family members, the brigade also adopted both an "in-person experience" and an "online cloud selection" model, allowing officers and soldiers in the garrison as well as family members in different locations to vote for the apartment furnishings of their choice. In addition, the brigade simultaneously advanced renovation of water and electrical pipe networks, installation of gas pipelines, and provision of daily necessities, ensuring that visiting family members could move in without bringing anything extra.

Military spouse Zhao Ruimin came to visit from her hometown more than 2,000 kilometers away. When she pushed open the apartment door, the complete set of daily necessities and the warm living environment moved her deeply: "The unit has thought of everything—this is what home feels like!" The recognition from family members and comrades made me feel that every bit of effort had been worthwhile.

Serving officers and soldiers is serving combat effectiveness development. Not long ago, the brigade organized a live-force confrontation training exercise. The officers and soldiers of a certain battalion tasked with the main assault won engagement after engagement, efficiently completing their assigned tasks and earning unanimous recognition from the exercise control group. After training concluded, one sergeant said candidly: "The unit has been very thoughtful—a series of caring and heartwarming service measures has made everyone's enthusiasm for training and war preparation even higher."

(Compiled by PLA Daily reporter Wang Yue)

探亲之路：山不再高，水不再漫长 ——各级党委机关以正确政绩观为基层官兵解难帮困新闻调查之二 ■解放军报记者 王 越 谢 菲 金美姊 武警某部干部与来队探亲的家人合影。陆文凯摄 初夏高原，一辆贴着“双拥号”标识的中巴车，平稳行驶在318国道上。 专车接机、氧气保障、军医随行……武警第二机动总队某支队参谋罗启智的妻子杨迪告诉记者，这次探亲之路，她时刻都被温暖包围着。然而，回忆自己第一次来队探亲的经历，她说：“有点像人在囧途。” “下飞机后乘机场大巴到市区，然后坐网约车前往长途汽车站，接着乘长途客车赶往县城，下车后再乘出租车到营区……我拖着两个行李箱，先后多次辗转，直至深夜才到了部队大门口。”杨迪说。 “为什么不提前向机关申请派车，或者请假去接一下家属？”前段时间，该支队领导调研时，得知罗启智家属的这段经历。面对领导的询问，罗启智解释：“长距离派车需提前向机关申请，能否派车还要视天气而定，平时任务重，自己也不好意思请假，只能劝家属多担待些……” 据了解，该支队常年担负川藏线、新藏线的养护保通等任务，点位分散、位置偏远，驻地条件艰苦。随着调研深入，不少官兵谈道，交通不便是家属不愿来队探亲的主要原因。 “这些年，尽管我们在住房和生活保障上投入了很多经费和精力，但来队探亲的家属依然不多。”支队党委会上，这名领导结合调研经历发言，支队驻地偏远，几乎没有社会依托，我们不仅要在吃住方面下功夫，也要努力解决出行难题，为军属来队探亲做好服务保障工作，给官兵和家人团聚创造便利条件。 不久后，该支队成立专项工作组，深入基层了解军属来队探亲出行需求，针对反映较多的“接送站不便”等问题制订解难清单，并指派专人跟进解决。工作组还多次赴驻地有关部门沟通，协调解决军属来队探亲期间的出行保障问题。最终，军地双方根据《中华人民共和国军人地位和权益保障法》，以及退役军人事务部等20部门联合印发的《关于加强军人军属、退役军人和其他优抚对象优待工作的意见》等，联合制订《军属出行服务保障细化方案》和《“拥军优属 情暖军心”专项优待实施方案》，构建起“费用报销+专车接送+高效服务”三位一体的保障体系，有效提升了军人军属的荣誉感、获得感和幸福感。 从雪域高原到大漠戈壁再到高山海岛，连日来，记者探访座座军营了解到，开展树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育以来，各部队党委不断校准政绩导向，连续推出一系列务实举措，着力解决军属来队探亲、官兵休假探亲等方面的现实难题，让探亲之路“山不再高，水不再漫长”。 南国深山，记者走进火箭军某旅，一名旅领导正带领机关干部结合新修订的共同条令、《关于规范完善军队人员有关福利待遇的若干规定》等制度规定，改进优化来队探亲家属的保障方案。记者了解到，除了完善出行方面的服务，他们着眼暑期将迎来家属来队探亲高峰这一实际，提前收集梳理官兵意见建议。针对基层反映较多的“设立儿童娱乐场地”需求，旅机关灵活利用家属院场地，划出一块儿童专区。他们指定专人采购滑梯、秋千等器材，紧盯测量、安装等环节，确保儿童乐园在暑期投入使用。 东海之滨，刚刚完成某专项任务的海军某观通旅军士何春林，向连队递交了休假申请。该旅领导介绍，近年来，全旅大项任务交织、训练任务繁重，但旅党委明确要求：即便任务再饱和，也要严格落实休假制度，全力保障官兵的休假探亲时间。该旅在落实《军人休假工作暂行规定》、新修订的共同条令有关规定的基础上，根据任务实际，科学统筹官兵探亲休假安排，探索出多种行之有效的机制，确保官兵应休尽休。 在该旅某观通站采访期间，官兵们告诉记者，他们驻守的岛屿远离陆地，不通航班，出行十分不便，以往休假只能搭乘补给船离岛，而补给船很多天才来一趟。三级军士长潘积绪回忆，一年夏天，他申请休假，不料台风逼近，补给船迟迟无法靠岸，离岛时间一拖再拖，最后只能取消休假计划。 如今，情况大不同，该旅与驻地政府积极沟通，协调开通定期航班，物资补给和官兵进出岛比以前方便多了。不仅如此，在上级大力支持下，该旅还与地方承运企业建立合作机制，满足守岛官兵紧急进出岛需求。 “事关官兵切身利益的事，绝不能停留在口号上。”海风轻拂，该旅一位领导告诉记者，“服务官兵就是服务战斗力，没有后顾之忧，大家才能心无旁骛地投入练兵备战。” 在高原感受军属荣光 ■军嫂 杨 迪 到了营区，走进公寓房，屋内窗明几净，被褥叠放得整整齐齐，厨房里油盐酱醋、锅碗瓢盆样样不缺，真正是拎包入住。尤其是听着爱人的战友一声声喊“嫂子”，我心里暖暖的，一路的舟车劳顿烟消云散。
