About this analysis
Summary
An unnamed Rocket Force brigade—identified by its propaganda moniker 'Great Nation's Long Sword'—has restructured training, command, and logistics around three explicit shifts: from live-fire launches to combat launches, from single-unit operations to modular combined-arms integration, and from trailing support to embedded support, with the brigade Party committee framing the overhaul explicitly as a correction of a flawed 'outlook on performance' (政绩观). The article documents specific institutional failures that drove the changes: launch elements that could not coordinate with support sub-elements under dynamic conditions, an awards culture that systematically favored launch crews over logistics and maintenance personnel, and an exercise-control library that had relied on scripted scenarios until it was rebuilt with dozens of new variables including intelligent jamming and extreme-condition communications. The framing points to a brigade-level effort to internalize system-of-systems (体系) doctrine at the crew and company level, though the article does not identify the brigade's missile type, garrison location, or the specific live-fire exercises cited.

The "Great Nation's Long Sword" Honed to a Sharper Edge

— An Investigative Report from a Rocket Force Brigade

■ PLA Daily Special Correspondent Li Bingfeng, Special Correspondent Li Hongjie

Officers and soldiers of the brigade conduct missile operations training in the field. Photo by PLA Daily Special Correspondent Liu Mingsong

In front of the dormitory building of Launch Battalion One of a certain Rocket Force brigade, a countdown board stands erect, its display fixed on a single number: "0."

Every time he returns from leading a launch element through training, Master Sergeant First Class He Xianda habitually stops and stands before the countdown board for a moment. That conspicuous "0" reminds him at all times that only by treating every ordinary day as the day war arrives can he ensure the unit is ready to answer the call, ready to fight upon arrival, and certain to win when it fights.

One soldier's combat consciousness reflects the combat readiness posture of an entire unit. Confronting the real challenges of transformation and development, the brigade Party committee has trained its "performance sights" on winning battles, leading officers and soldiers to continuously study new equipment, new skills, and new methods of warfare. Toward the goal of every individual being battle-hardened, every launcher being capable of firing, and the entire brigade winning as a whole, they have pressed forward with all-out effort to overcome key difficulties, successfully completing a series of major tasks including live-fire launches and combat readiness duty shifts. The "Great Nation's Long Sword" has been honed to a sharper edge.

From "Live-Fire Launch" to "Combat Launch"——

Pressing the Attack: Closing the Distance of "One Word's Difference"

"Whether it was the configuration of crew positions or the operating procedures, the changes were comprehensive across the board." Reflecting on his experience when the unit first transitioned to new equipment, He Xianda—who has worked in the missile specialty for nearly 30 years—said that during that period he suddenly felt like a complete novice at the controls.

In years past, He Xianda had followed his unit into dense forests, onto snow-covered plains, and through the Gobi Desert, launching missiles skyward on multiple occasions, forging the hard skills of "crystal-clear recitation (一口清)," "single-keystroke precision (一键准)," and "unshakeable under questioning (问不倒)." Yet, faced with new equipment of ever-increasing informatization levels, and looking through the lens of the smoke and fire of wars in various parts of the world, he and his comrades felt an unprecedented sense of capability panic: Does being able to conduct a live-fire launch necessarily mean being able to win a battle? Does a successful launch in peacetime training equal a successful launch in wartime?

This was also a subject of deliberation for the brigade Party committee leadership. They came to a deep understanding that a servicemember's record of performance must be written on the future battlefield and must withstand the test of war. Closing the distance of "one word's difference"—from "live-fire launch" to "combat launch"—urgently required an all-out assault on key difficulties.

Pressing the attack first requires calibrating the target direction. The brigade used its mission tasks as the driving force, bearing down hard on combat readiness training from a starting point of difficulty, strictness, and combat realism, guiding officers and soldiers out of their training "comfort zone," and working to solidify the foundation of capabilities. Constructing environmental conditions that closely approximate actual combat, setting up complex enemy situations that might be encountered on the battlefield, and broadly conducting battalion-versus-battalion confrontations and launcher-sequencing activities… Reviewing the brigade's training summary reports from recent years, this reporter found that various difficult, complex, and dangerous situations are appearing on the exercise and training grounds with increasing frequency.

This change is inseparable from the exercise control group, the behind-the-scenes "operator." They discarded scripts that had been in use for many years, and with an eye toward the characteristics of modern warfare, upgraded and expanded the "special-situation library (特情库)," adding dozens of new special situations including intelligent jamming and deception and extreme-condition communications, making "catching officers and soldiers off guard" the norm.

"In the past, we acted on orders. Now we frequently have to independently assess the battlefield situation and determine a course of action. This demands that we forge the hard skills of zero reaction-time delay (反应零时差), zero operational errors (操作零差错), and zero data misjudgments (数据零误判)." Squad Leader Li, commander of a launch element, said that his comrades have come to understand that being proficient in operating weapons and equipment is far from sufficient—they must also improve their ability to read a situation, control a situation, and break out of a situation.

After one confrontation training exercise concluded, they promptly conducted an after-action review, and based on the problems exposed during training, designated equipment operations in complex electromagnetic environments and emergency situation handling as key areas for subsequent intensive and specialized drilling. The commander of a certain launch company introduced that the brigade organizes a weekly specialty spot-check examination, forcing officers and soldiers to accelerate their pace and achieve strong results.

Pressing the attack most clearly reveals how strong the shoulders of Party member cadres are. In this brigade, from the brigade leadership down to new platoons commanders, everyone has further clarified their combat position responsibilities according to combat standards: train intensively in whatever fighting requires, urgently fill in whatever one personally lacks, and go all-out to overcome whatever difficulties exist in combat capability development—taking the practical action of leading from the front and setting the example, striving to create a record of real achievements that can withstand the test of actual combat. During the interview, this reporter noted the following set of figures: more than ninety percent of launch sub-element political instructors across the entire brigade are capable of independently serving as launch rack commanders. The spirit of officers and soldiers pressing forward and overcoming difficulties can be glimpsed from this alone.

From "Fighting Alone" to "Modular Combination"——

Breaking Through: Accelerating the Enhancement of System-of-Systems Combat Capability

"To forge oneself into the 'blade tip' of a strategic force, one must put boldness first and action foremost, dare to take responsibility, and be skilled at getting things done." Sitting in the battalion command post, eyes fixed on the screen before him, Battalion Commander Li of a certain launch battalion in the brigade cannot forget that experience.

At the time, the battalion was participating in a confrontation exercise organized by a higher echelon. The entire battalion's officers and soldiers trained under intensified pressure at their posts, and their scores in several preliminary assessments were quite good. Battalion Commander Li was fully confident in defeating the opposing force.

The exercise proceeded at a tense pace. However, just as the battalion's officers and soldiers were preparing to enter the launch countdown, the exercise control group issued an on-the-spot directive requiring them to immediately maneuver to another area and complete a combat launch within a specified time.

Withdrawing equipment while simultaneously planning routes and coordinating with support sub-elements… All manner of unexpected situations arrived one after another, throwing officers and soldiers into momentary confusion. In the end, due to poor individual coordination and element synchronization, the launch zero-hour was delayed.

At the after-action critique, many backbone personnel attributed the failure to insufficient combat realism in training. But after the pain of reflection, Battalion Commander Li cut through the surface to identify the essence: "In day-to-day training, we only focused on our own 'one-third of an acre (一亩三分地),' thinking that as long as we could operate proficiently and troubleshoot faults, everything would be fine. We rarely proactively thought about how to coordinate with other support elements, always feeling that was someone else's business…" This profound reflection ultimately pointed its spearhead at the battalion Party committee's outlook on performance. The Party committee leadership turned the blade inward and conducted self-examination, anchoring the basis for improvement in exploring methods and approaches for system-of-systems integration——

Breaking down organizational barriers, strengthening cross-specialty training, conducting tactical coordination training, and progressively achieving the "clenching of all elements into a fist (攥指成拳)" across all elements; distinguishing categories such as command modules and technical modules, establishing more than 50 subjects with hundreds of training items, and embedding more than 200 special-situation variables; organizing mutually-conditioned training, and verifying and improving integration effectiveness through live-force confrontations with other services, branches, and sister units…

From "fighting alone" to "modular combination," accelerating the enhancement of system-of-systems combat capability quickly became a common action across the entire brigade.

Immediately after one combined-arms training exercise concluded, Company Commander Wang of a certain launch company led officers and soldiers in organizing all data and identifying problems and shortcomings. He said: "The subject configuration of this training emphasized randomness and continuity, the conditions were constructed to more closely approximate actual combat, and many situations were encountered for the first time. We must urgently formulate corrective measures. When it comes to matters of fighting, we cannot wait and cannot afford to drag our feet."

Battalion Commander Li and his comrades share this same sense of urgency. Today, as their system-of-systems combat capability has grown stronger, they have become increasingly composed on the exercise and training grounds. During one combat-realistic training exercise conducted under nighttime conditions, several launch elements of the battalion responded to orders, simultaneously handling all manner of exercise-control special situations in real time while closely coordinating with support elements. On the large screen, flowing alongside the tracks of the missile vehicles came information and data from every element. This reporter observed that in this brigade, a command "single chain (一条链)" and a support "single network (一张网)" are becoming reality.

From "Accompanying Support" to "Embedded Support"——

Building Capability: There Is No Primary or Secondary on the Battlefield

Deep in the mountains of southern China, a fine rain falls steadily.

"Tweet-tweet-tweet——" A burst of urgent whistle blasts suddenly rang out, and various types of combat vehicles of the brigade surged forward like the wind, advancing into the depths of the dense forest. Immediately, a fire assault training exercise commenced. Beneath the launch rack, Sergeant First Class Ke and his comrades prepared to erect the missile.

"The erection arm cannot be extended!" Faced with this sudden special situation, the launch element immediately organized to handle it. Unexpectedly, multiple additional special situations arrived in rapid succession: "crew member attacked and wounded," "equipment damaged," "fuel tank penetrated"… Although informed that a neighboring unit was rushing to provide support, Sergeant Ke still felt uncertain: whether they could effectively respond to these situations was a severe test of comprehensive support capability.

Shortly thereafter, the maintenance sub-element arrived on scene and began emergency repairs; the medical sub-element urgently treated and evacuated the "casualties"; the fuel sub-element calculated remaining travel mileage and urgently commenced equipment emergency repairs… The brigade's various support elements acted swiftly, each fulfilling its responsibilities according to the plan and division of labor. Before long, the special situations were resolved and training proceeded in an orderly manner.

Witnessing this scene, the brigade leadership reflected: "On the future battlefield, there will be no clear distinction between primary and secondary. Every element is an indispensable link in the combat chain." He explained to this reporter that in previous exercises and training, support elements always considered themselves "supporting actors," following behind the main combat sub-elements "step by step." Now, combat elements and support elements are mixed into combined groupings, clenched into a fist, and have long since ceased to distinguish between "yours" and "mine."

The shift from "accompanying support" to "embedded support" once went through an intense collision of ideas.

"Everyone understands the principle that support capability is combat capability. But for a period of time, support units were not built as combat units." During the study and education campaign on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on performance (树立和践行正确政绩观), a brigade leader raised this old matter again, demanding that each unit plan its development against the standard of actual combat while also conducting in-depth analysis of its own existing problems.

"Take the evaluation of merits and awards, for example. The brigade Party committee leadership used to always look more favorably upon and show more affection for launch sub-elements, not realizing that a missile's flight into the sky depends on the combined lifting force of every element. The spotlight cannot always be on the soldiers who press the launch button." This leader reflected with feeling: "Future warfare is fought as a system; peacetime troop training trains as a system. So why, when it comes to year-end commendations and awards, must we distinguish between those who are close and those who are distant?"

Correct action originates from corrected thinking. The brigade Party committee promptly adjusted the direction and volume of resource allocation, convened a collective consultation on the development approach for support sub-elements, simultaneously optimized the support model, implemented modular grouping, and drove support elements to organically integrate into the combat system. The brigade support department leadership introduced that in organizing combined-arms training today, launch sub-elements and support sub-elements plan together and operate in an integrated and linked manner—each present within the other—ensuring that any sudden situation can be handled immediately.

During the interview, the brigade happened to be organizing a comprehensive training exercise. Were it not for the conspicuous insignia on the officers' and soldiers' arms, this reporter would have had difficulty distinguishing which personnel came from support sub-elements. "Ready to respond at any moment, ready to deploy at any moment" has become their shining calling card.

"Crew members, take your positions!" Another resounding command rang out. Missile crew members moved out rapidly and operated with precision; combat vehicle after combat vehicle occupied its position; missile after missile stood poised and ready to launch… Bustling figures and resolute faces—a snapshot of this missile brigade's fine bearing as it prepares for war and builds its capabilities.

这也是该旅党委一班人思考的课题。他们深刻感到，军人的政绩答卷，必须写在未来战场上、经得起战争检验。从“实弹发射”到“实战发射”，缩短“一字之差”的距离，急需一场全力攻坚。 这一变化，离不开导调组这个幕后“操盘手”。他们摒弃沿用多年的“脚本”，着眼现代战争特点，对“特情库”进行升级扩容，新增智能干扰与诱骗、极限通信等数十种特情，让“出乎官兵意料”成为常态。 “过去是闻令而动，如今经常要自主研判战场态势、确定行动方案。这要求我们必须练就反应零时差、操作零差错、数据零误判的过硬本领。”发射单元指挥长李班长说，战友们开始懂得，只会熟练使用武器装备远远不够，还要提升识局、控局、破局能力。 一次对抗训练结束，他们及时复盘总结，并结合训练中暴露出的问题，将复杂电磁环境下装备操作、应急情况处置等作为后续专攻精练重点。某发射连连长介绍，旅里每周组织一次专业抽考，倒逼官兵跑出“加速度”、打出好成绩。 攻坚，最能看出党员干部的肩膀有多硬。在该旅，从旅领导到新排长，人人按照打仗标准进一步明确战位职责，打仗需要什么就重点练什么、自身缺什么就抓紧补什么、战斗力建设有什么难题就全力攻克什么，以身先士卒、率先垂范的实际行动，争创经得起实战检验的实绩。采访中，记者看到这样一组数据：全旅九成以上发射分队指导员，都能独立担任发射架指挥长。官兵奋进攻坚的精神状态，从中可见一斑。 从“单打独斗”到“模块组合”—— 突破，加快提升体系作战能力 “把自己锻造成战略力量的‘刀尖子’，就要敢字为先、干字当头，勇于担当、善于作为。”坐在营指挥所里，盯着眼前的屏幕，该旅某发射营李营长忘不了那段经历。 当时，该营参加上级组织的对抗演练。全营官兵立足岗位加压严训，几次摸底成绩都不错，李营长对打败对手信心满满。 演练紧张进行。然而，当该营官兵准备进入发射倒计时的时候，导调组临机下达指令，要求他们立即机动到另一地域，在规定时间内完成战斗发射。 一边撤收装备，一边规划路线、对接保障分队……各种意想不到的情况接踵而至，让官兵一时间手忙脚乱。最终，因单兵协作和要素协同不畅，发射零时延后。 复盘检讨会上，很多骨干把失利原因归结为训练贴近实战不够。但痛定思痛，李营长透过表象道出本质：“平时训练只盯着自己的‘一亩三分地’，认为只要能熟练操作、排除故障就万事大吉，很少主动思考与其他保障要素如何配合，总感觉那是别人的事……”这场深刻反思，矛头最终指向营党委的政绩观。党委一班人刀刃向内、自我检视，把改进的基点铆定在探索体系融合的方法路子上—— 打破建制壁垒，加强跨专业训练，开展战术协同训练，逐步实现各要素“攥指成拳”；区分指挥模块、技术模块等类别，设置50多个课目数百项训练内容，嵌入200多个特情变量；组织互为条件训练，通过与其他军兵种和兄弟单位实兵对抗，检验提升融合成效…… 从“单打独斗”到“模块组合”，加快提升体系作战能力，很快成为该旅上下的共同行动。 一次合成训练刚结束，某发射连王连长就带领官兵整理各项数据、查找问题不足。他说：“这次训练的课目设置突出随机性和连贯性，条件构设更加贴近实战，很多情况都是第一次遇到。我们必须抓紧制订整改举措，事关打仗的事，等不得、拖不起。” 李营长和战友们同样有这种紧迫感。如今，随着体系作战能力增强，他们在演训场上变得越来越从容。一次夜暗条件下实战化训练，该营数个发射单元闻令而动，一边实时处置各类导调特情，一边与保障要素密切配合。大屏幕上，伴随导弹战车行迹奔涌而来的，是来自各要素的信息数据。记者发现，在该旅，指挥“一条链”、保障“一张网”正变为现实。 从“伴随保障”到“嵌入保障”—— 强能，战场上没有主次之分 南国深山，细雨绵绵。 “嘟嘟嘟——”一阵急促的哨音骤然响起，该旅各型战车动若风发，向密林深处挺进。随即，一场火力突击训练展开。发射架下，三级军士长柯班长与战友一起，准备起竖导弹。 “起竖臂无法展开！”面对突发特情，发射单元立即组织处置。没想到，多个特情又接连传来：“号手遭袭受伤”“装备受损”“油箱被击穿”……尽管得知友邻单位正赶来支援，柯班长心里仍没底：能否有效应对这些情况，是对综合保障能力的严峻考验。 很快，维修分队抵达现场展开抢修，卫勤分队紧急救治后送“伤员”，油料分队计算剩余行驶里程、紧急展开装备抢修……该旅各保障要素迅疾行动，按照预案和分工各司其职。不多时，特情处置完毕，训练有序推进。 目睹这一幕，该旅领导感慨：“未来战场，没有明显的主次之分，每一个要素都是作战链条上不可或缺的一环。”他向记者介绍，以前演训，保障要素总认为自己是“配角”，跟在主战分队后面“亦步亦趋”。现在，作战要素与保障要素混合编组、攥指成拳，早已不分你我。 从“伴随保障”到“嵌入保障”，保障方式的转变，曾经历一次激烈的思想碰撞。 “保障力是战斗力的道理，谁都懂。但有一段时间，没有把保障队当战斗队来建。”在树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育中，该旅一名领导旧事重提，要求各单位对标实战筹划建设发展，同时深入剖析自身存在问题。 “就拿评功评奖来说，旅党委一班人以前总是对发射分队高看一眼、厚爱几分，殊不知导弹飞天靠的是各要素合力托举，聚光灯不能一直对着那些按下发射按钮的战士。”这名领导感慨地说，“未来战争打的是体系，平时练兵练的是体系。为啥到了年底表彰奖励，却要分出个远近亲疏？” 行动正向，源于思想纠偏。该旅党委及时调整资源投向投量，集中会诊保障分队建设思路，同步优化保障模式，实行模块编组，推动保障要素有机融入作战体系。旅保障部领导介绍，如今组织合成训练，发射分队与保障分队一同筹划、一体联动，你中有我、我中有你，确保遇有突发情况能够迅即处置。 采访中，正值该旅组织一次综合训练。要不是官兵胳膊上戴着醒目标识，记者很难分辨出哪些人来自保障分队，“随时能响应、随时能出动”已成为他们的闪亮名片。 “号手就位！”又是一阵响亮的号令，导弹号手快速出击、精准操作，一辆辆战车占领阵地，一枚枚导弹引而待发……奔忙的身影、坚毅的脸庞，定格出这支导弹劲旅备战强能的好样子。
