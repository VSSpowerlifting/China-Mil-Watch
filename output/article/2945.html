Navy's Flotilla 83 and Indonesian Navy Organize Mutual Ship Visit Activity — "Bima Suci" Appears Again

海军83舰编队与印尼海军组织舰艇互访活动，又见“毕玛苏吉”
PLA Daily (解放军报) 5 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Navy Flotilla 83, on a friendly visit to Indonesia, conducted a mutual ship visit with the Indonesian Navy at Surabaya Port in early August, with Chinese cadets and officers from the training ship Qi Jiguang boarding the Indonesian sail training ship Bima Suci — the vessel's fourth engagement with the Chinese Navy following port calls to China in 2019, 2024, and May 2025. The article documents the bilateral naval training relationship at the working level, with both sides exchanging views on sail training's role in building seamanship fundamentals and the concept of a 人才培养链条 (talent-cultivation chain) combining sail and motor training ships. The framing — quoting Tang poetry, emphasizing shared commission years, and noting a Chinese officer's prior internship aboard Chile's Esmeralda — points to a deliberate effort to normalize multilateral sail-training exchanges as a soft-power instrument, though the article does not indicate any new formal training agreement between the two navies.
Translation

"Bima Suci" Appears Again

■ PLA Daily Reporter Zhang Dan, People's Navy News Reporter Cui Xiaohan

The Indonesian Navy sail training ship "Bima Suci," moored at Surabaya Port. Photo by Li Kunpeng.

August. Surabaya Port, Indonesia. Friends have come from afar.

Navy Flotilla 83, currently conducting a friendly visit to Indonesia, organized a mutual ship visit activity with the Indonesian Navy. Chinese naval cadets and officers and soldiers boarded the Indonesian Navy sail training ship "Bima Suci" for yet another reunion with this "old friend."

"Bima Suci" is a genuine "old friend" of the Chinese Navy, having visited China three times — in 2019, in 2024, and again this past May — and having carried Chinese naval cadets on open-ocean navigation training, forging a deep friendship between the two sides.

Ascending the gangway of "Bima Suci," Indonesian Navy Lieutenant Husni stood on deck to welcome the visiting Chinese cadets and officers and soldiers. "Every time our ship visits China, we receive a warm welcome. This time, receiving Chinese friends who have come from afar at our home port, we must return the same heartfelt sentiment." Husni has participated in every visit "Bima Suci" has made to China, which has given him a special affection for China; he deeply admires the achievements of Chinese naval construction and the Chinese military's tireless pursuit of safeguarding peace.

In exchanges with the officers and soldiers of the Qi Jiguang, Husni learned that "Bima Suci" and Qi Jiguang were both commissioned in 2017 and are both training ships — a discovery that surprised and delighted him. "Long-term reliance on electronic equipment is not conducive to cultivating officers' and soldiers' traditional seamanship skills," Husni said. "On a sail training ship, cadets must master skills such as reading the sky and sea, climbing aloft, and setting and furling sails. This can complement motor training ships and form a complete talent-cultivation chain (人才培养链条). I hope there will be an opportunity in the future to train together with China's 'Polang' sail training ship."

Walking through the wooden decks and compartments, personnel from both sides conversed on topics ranging from ship structure and sail-rig maintenance to open-ocean training and meteorological support. Flotilla 83 staff officer Zhu Zhiwei, who came to visit, was particularly interested in "Bima Suci's" practice of carrying cadets for graduation internships. He told reporters that this was his second time boarding the ship. When "Bima Suci" made its first visit to China in 2019, he had just graduated from a military academy and had gone aboard to visit, encountering a foreign tall ship for the first time. He later also participated, as a Chinese Navy representative, in an open-ocean internship aboard the Chilean Navy sail training ship "Esmeralda." "The long ocean voyages, the ever-changing maritime environment, and the high-intensity deck work can effectively temper officers' and soldiers' qualities of resilience and their sense of cooperation," said Zhu Zhiwei.

For Zhang Yiteng, an intern cadet aboard Qi Jiguang, this was his first time boarding a sail training ship. The towering masts piercing the blue sky made him involuntarily imagine what it would look like when this ship raised its white sails and set out across the vast open ocean.

The "Bima Suci" sail training ship relies on sails for propulsion. The wind gives the ship the power to move forward, but also brings surging waves and violent rolling. What Zhang Yiteng was most curious about was how the crew copes with wind and waves during open-ocean voyages. An Indonesian Navy crew member who accompanied the tour explained: "We must possess the fighting spirit (拼搏精神) to battle wind and waves; the entire crew shares honor and disgrace, life and death. On a sail training ship, a great deal of sail-setting and mast-climbing work must be accomplished through crew coordination." Zhang Yiteng responded with delight: "This is entirely in keeping with the spirit of solidarity and cooperation (同舟共济、团结协作) that we uphold."

Slowly making his way through every part of the ship, Zhang Yiteng was still reluctant to leave even after stepping off the deck. He looks forward to one day having the opportunity to set sail on a sail training ship.

After the visit concluded, representatives of the Chinese and Indonesian militaries exchanged ship crests and ship caps. Kunlun Mountain cadet Yang Yiheng expressed his gratitude to the Indonesian Navy representatives: "We sincerely thank you for your warm hospitality and detailed explanations." Citing the lines of Tang dynasty poet Wang Changling, he said: "The same clouds and rain pass over the green mountains that connect us; the bright moon has never shone on two separate homelands. China and Indonesia are close neighbors separated only by a strip of water, and even more so friends bound by deep friendship. May the friendship between our two countries and two militaries endure forever."

A sea breeze gently blew. In the firm clasp of joined hands, the friendship between China and Indonesia stretching across the azure sea grew ever more enduring.

(PLA Daily dispatch from Surabaya, Indonesia, August 4)

又见“毕玛苏吉” ■解放军报记者 张 丹 人民海军报记者 崔晓涵 靠泊在泗水港的印尼海军“毕玛苏吉”号风帆训练舰。 李鲲鹏 摄 8月，印度尼西亚泗水港，有朋自远方来。 正在印尼进行友好访问的海军83舰编队，与印尼海军组织舰艇互访活动。中方海军学员和官兵登上印尼海军“毕玛苏吉”号风帆训练舰，与这位“老朋友”又一次相见。 “毕玛苏吉”号是中国海军名副其实的“老朋友”，曾于2019年、2024年和今年5月三次访问中国，还曾搭载中国海军学员远海航行训练，双方结下深厚的友谊。 登上“毕玛苏吉”号舷梯，印尼海军上尉胡斯尼正站在甲板上迎接前来参观的中方学员和官兵。“我们这艘舰每次到访中国，都受到热情欢迎。这次在母港接待远道而来的中国朋友，我们也必须还以同样诚挚的情意。”每一次“毕玛苏吉”号到访中国，胡斯尼都参与其中，这让他对中国有着别样的感情，他非常钦佩中国海军的建设成就和中国军队守护和平的不懈追求。 在与戚继光舰官兵的交流中，胡斯尼得知“毕玛苏吉”号与戚继光舰都是2017年入列，也同样是训练舰，这让他感到意外和欣喜。“长期依赖电子设备不利于培养官兵的传统航海能力。”胡斯尼表示，“在风帆训练舰上，学员必须掌握观天辨海、高空攀桅、扬帆收帆等技能，可以与机动训练舰相互补充，形成完整的人才培养链条。希望以后能有机会与中国的‘破浪’号风帆训练舰共同训练。” 行走在木质甲板和船舱内，双方人员从舰船结构、帆装保养聊到远海训练、气象保障等内容。前来参观的83舰编队参谋朱志伟对“毕玛苏吉”号搭载学员开展毕业实习尤为感兴趣，他告诉记者，这是他第二次登上这艘舰。2019年“毕玛苏吉”号首次访问中国，刚从军校毕业的他就曾上舰参观，首次接触外国高桅帆船。后来他还作为中国海军代表，参与过智利海军“埃斯梅拉达”号风帆训练舰的远海实习。“漫长的远洋航行、多变的海上环境、高强度的甲板作业，能有效磨砺官兵的坚韧品质和协作意识。”朱志伟说。 对于戚继光舰实习学员张乂腾来说，这是他第一次登上风帆训练舰。巍峨高耸的桅杆直刺蓝天，让他不禁想象这艘舰白帆扬起、驶向万顷远洋之时，会是怎样的景象。 “毕玛苏吉”号风帆训练舰依靠风帆航行。风赋予舰船前行的动力，也带来汹涌波涛与剧烈摇晃。张乂腾最好奇的是舰员们在远海航行时如何应对海上风浪。随行讲解的印尼海军舰员向他介绍：“我们必须拥有战风斗浪的拼搏精神，全体舰员荣辱与共、生死相依。在风帆训练舰上，大量升帆、攀桅作业需要依靠舰员协同完成。”张乂腾欣喜地回应道：“这与我们推崇的同舟共济、团结协作精神不谋而合。” 缓步走完舰船各处，直至走下甲板，张乂腾意犹未尽。他期待以后能有机会随风帆训练舰扬帆远航。 参观活动结束后，中印尼两军代表互赠舰徽与舰帽。昆仑山舰学员杨以恒向印尼海军代表致以谢意：“由衷感谢你们的热情招待与细致讲解。”他援引唐代诗人王昌龄的诗句说：“青山一道同云雨，明月何曾是两乡。中印尼两国是一衣带水的近邻，更是有深厚情谊的朋友，祝愿两国两军友谊长存。” 海风轻拂，紧紧相握的双手之间，中印尼跨越碧海的友谊愈发绵长。 （解放军报印度尼西亚泗水8月4日电）
