Let the Photos Return to the Heroic Unit! A Korean War Veteran "Sends Home" a Photo of Huang Jiguang

让照片回归英雄部队！抗美援朝老兵将黄继光的照片"送回家"
PLA Daily (解放军报) 29 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Ninety-one-year-old Korean War veteran He Yixiu, who served as a clerk in the 4th Company, 2nd Battalion, 135th Regiment, 45th Division, 15th Army during the 1952 Battle of Shangganling, donated two archival photographs to an Airborne Corps unit via video call — one documenting the 1953 cliff inscription on Wusheng Mountain commemorating Huang Jiguang, the other showing Huang's mother Deng Fangzhi visiting the unit after his death. The article is standard heroic lineage (英雄血脉) content; its value is as a record of how the Airborne Corps institutionally links contemporary training performance — including international competitions and massed parachute assault (集群伞降) tactics — to Korean War sacrifice narratives, not as evidence of a capability or posture change.
Translation

Huang Jiguang's wartime comrade, a Korean War veteran in his nineties, donates two old photographs to a unit of the Airborne Corps——

The hero's figure has never left

■ Lin Qingji, Gao Di, Li Jianqing

Li Pengchao, the 37th squad leader of the "Huang Jiguang Squad," reports to He Yixiu on the unit's current situation. Photo by Ma Zi'an.

"Today, I have finally sent the photos of my old comrade Huang Jiguang back 'home'!" Recently, inside the Special-Grade Hero Huang Jiguang Memorial Hall of a unit of the Airborne Corps, a video call gave rise to a bond of care and remembrance that spans time and space.

On the other side of the screen, 91-year-old Korean War veteran He Yixiu slowly rose to his feet and made every effort to stand straight. On this side of the screen, officers and soldiers of the Airborne Corps unit stood in neat formation and saluted solemnly — the old man was sending "home" two old photographs he had treasured for more than half a century, and once again telling the story of Huang Jiguang to the heirs of the hero.

On May 6 of this year, a unit of the Airborne Corps received a letter from Xinyang, Henan. The sender was He Yixiu, a wartime comrade of Huang Jiguang who had once served in the predecessor unit of this formation.

"I am already 91 years old this year, and there is one matter I have never been able to set my mind at rest about," the old man wrote in the letter. "I wish to donate the two photographs of hero Huang Jiguang that I have treasured to the old unit I have always kept close to my heart."

A commemorative stone inscription on the cliff face of Wusheng Mountain on the Shangganling battlefield. Archival photo.

The two old photographs are like keys that open the door to a bygone era. One is a commemorative stone inscription carved into the cliff face of Wusheng Mountain on the Shangganling battlefield: "Comrade Huang Jiguang, a Matveyev-type (马特洛索夫式) Special-Grade Combat Hero of the Chinese People's Volunteer Army, gave his life for his country and shall live forever." The other is a photograph taken after Huang Jiguang's death, when his mother, Deng Fangzhi, came to the unit to visit her son's comrades — in the photo, Mother Deng sits side by side with two soldiers, her gaze resolute yet gentle.

In October 1952, the Battle of Shangganling began. He Yixiu, then serving as a clerk in the 4th Company, 2nd Battalion, 135th Regiment, 45th Division, 15th Army of the Volunteer Army, was responsible for battlefield statistical records. He remembers clearly that at a critical moment when the unit's assault had been stopped, Huang Jiguang, dragging his already-wounded body, resolutely threw himself onto the enemy's firing position and used his chest to block the gun port. That heroic figure was forever fixed in his heart.

A stone carved as a monument, an immortal name. On New Year's Day 1953, the mountains were sealed by heavy snow. Bi Kaiwen, then serving as a cultural officer of the 45th Division, was ordered to lead six soldiers and, braving temperatures dozens of degrees below zero Celsius, climb step by slipping step to the top of the Wusheng Mountain cliff. They heated the rock face with fire and, hammer blow by hammer blow, chiseled into the hard stone, carving the hero's name deep into the mountain. Bi Kaiwen held firm in his heart: "As long as spring comes, this place will bloom with blazing red azaleas (金达莱) and wildflowers covering the hillside, to adorn this hero's monument."

Huang Jiguang's mother, Deng Fangzhi (center), visits her son's comrades at the unit. Archival photo.

These two photographs — one recording the heroic act of carving a name in stone, the other preserving the image of the hero's mother — were regarded by He Yixiu as precious as life itself. After returning to the country, from the fertile fields of the Northern Wilderness (北大荒) to the workshops of the Xinyang Glass Factory, the album containing the photographs was always with him. As the years passed, the old soldier's long-held wish never changed — to let the photographs return to the heroic unit.

Upon receiving the letter and photographs, a unit of the Airborne Corps immediately arranged for a dedicated person to respond and express gratitude, invited military history experts to assess the historical value and archival significance of the photographs, and located an eyewitness from that time, 96-year-old Bi Kaiwen, to reconstruct that chapter of wartime history. At the same time, considering He Yixiu's advanced age, the officers and soldiers decided to welcome the old photographs "home" through a "cloud reunion" (云端归队).

The moment the video connected, the gazes of two generations of soldiers — inside and outside the screen — met, and history and the present overlapped in an instant.

Private First Class Zhou Kai asked: "Elder He, what is the battle scene you remember most?"

He Yixiu's spirits suddenly surged: "When the bugle sounded the charge, everyone threw themselves forward with everything they had! Through a hail of bullets, not one person fell back!" As he reached the most stirring part, he swung his right arm forcefully, his voice resonant and powerful, and the three characters for "charge" rang out like thunderclaps: "Charge! Charge! Charge!" (冲！冲！冲！) The fighting spirit of more than seventy years ago crashed instantly into the hearts of every young officer and soldier.

Li Pengchao, who had served as the 37th squad leader of the "Huang Jiguang Squad," reported to He Yixiu: "Every time things get grueling at international competitions, the image of the old squad leader sacrificing himself to block the gun port rises before my eyes. As heirs of the hero, we have only the duty to charge forward — we have no right to give up!" He Yixiu nodded repeatedly: "No matter how perilous the road ahead, you must fight bloody battles (浴血奋战) just as Huang Jiguang did!"

On both ends of the screen, old and young, the vows of two generations of soldiers about "charging forward" reverberated and echoed.

"Each generation has its own Shangganling to hold, each generation has its own charge mission to shoulder." These words from the leadership of the Airborne Corps unit expressed the firm resolve of this formation to carry forward the heroic bloodline (赓续英雄血脉). Today, generation after generation of "torch-bearers" are advancing in the footsteps of the hero: there are training backbone personnel who take on the heaviest burdens under extreme conditions, and there are first-grade master sergeants (一级军士长) who innovate tactics to enhance the effectiveness of massed parachute assaults (集群伞降)… The hero's figure has never left.

When the video call ended, the two photographs were solemnly placed in the display case of the military history museum. The image of the hero's family, the chisel marks on the icy cliff, the charge vow that defied death… They are silent witnesses to history, and also like a bugle call to battle, inspiring the "torch-bearing" heirs of the hero to overcome every difficulty and press boldly forward.

黄继光生前战友、九旬抗美援朝老兵何易修向空降兵某部捐赠两张老照片—— 英雄的身影一直都在 ■林清集 高 迪 李建青 “黄继光班”第37任班长李鹏超向何易修汇报部队近况。马子安摄 “今天，我终于把老战友黄继光的照片送回‘家’了！”近日，在空降兵某部特级英雄黄继光纪念馆内，一场视频连线，引出跨越时空的牵挂和缅怀。 屏幕那头，91岁的抗美援朝老兵何易修缓缓站起身，努力挺直腰杆。屏幕这头，空降兵某部官兵整齐列队，庄严敬礼——老人把珍藏半个多世纪的两张老照片“送回家”，也把黄继光的故事，再次讲给英雄传人听。 今年5月6日，空降兵某部收到一封来自河南信阳的信函。寄信人是黄继光生前战友、曾服役于该部前身部队的何易修。 “我今年已经91岁了，有一件事一直放心不下。”老人在信中写道，“我想把珍藏的两张英雄黄继光的照片，赠送给我心心念念的老部队。” 上甘岭战场五圣山石壁上的纪念石刻。资料照片 两张老照片，如同打开时光之门的钥匙。一张，是镌刻在上甘岭战场五圣山石壁上的纪念石刻：“中国人民志愿军马特洛索夫式特级战斗英雄黄继光同志以身殉国永垂不朽”；另一张，是黄继光牺牲后，母亲邓芳芝到部队看望儿子战友的留影——照片中，邓妈妈与两名战士并肩而坐，目光坚毅而温柔。 1952年10月，上甘岭战役打响。时任志愿军第15军45师135团2营4连文书何易修，负责战地统计。他清晰记得，黄继光在部队冲锋受阻的危急时刻，拖着已经负伤的身躯，毅然扑向敌人的火力点，用胸膛堵住枪眼。那个壮烈的身影，永远定格在他心中。 刻石为碑，英名不朽。1953年元旦，大雪封山。时任45师文化干事毕开文，奉命带着6名战士，冒着零下数十摄氏度的严寒，一步一滑地登上五圣山崖顶。他们以火烤石壁，在坚硬的山石上一锤一锤凿刻，把英雄的名字深深刻进山体。毕开文心中坚信：“只要春天一来，这里将会盛开火红的金达莱和漫山的野花，来装饰这座英雄的纪念碑。” 黄继光母亲邓芳芝（中）到部队看望儿子战友。资料照片 这两张照片，一张记录着刻石留名壮举，一张定格了英雄母亲影像，被何易修视若生命。归国后，从北大荒的沃野到信阳玻璃厂的车间，装着照片的相册始终伴他同行。岁月流转，老兵心底的夙愿从未改变——让照片回归英雄部队。 收到来信与照片后，空降兵某部第一时间安排专人对接致谢，邀请军史专家考证照片的历史价值与文物意义，并找到当年的亲历者、96岁的毕开文老人，还原那段烽火往事。同时，官兵们考虑到何易修年事已高，决定用“云端归队”的方式，迎接老照片“回家”。 视频接通的那一刻，屏幕内外两代军人的目光交汇，历史与现实瞬间重叠。 上等兵周凯提问：“何老，您最难忘的战斗场景是什么？” 何易修精神陡然一震：“冲锋号一响，所有人拼了命往上冲！枪林弹雨，没一人后退！”讲到动情处，他右臂奋力一挥，嗓音洪亮，三个“冲”字如惊雷炸响：“冲！冲！冲！”70多年前的战斗豪情，瞬间撞进每个年轻官兵的心底。 曾任“黄继光班”第37任班长的李鹏超向何易修汇报：“每次国际比武吃劲的时候，我眼前就会浮现出老班长舍身堵枪眼的身影。作为英雄传人，我们只有冲锋的责任，没有放弃的权利！”何易修连连颔首：“无论前路多险，都要像黄继光那样浴血奋战！” 屏幕两端，一老一少，两代军人关于“冲锋”的誓言激荡回响。 “一代人有一代人的上甘岭要守，一代人有一代人的冲锋使命要扛。”空降兵某部领导的这句话，道出这支部队赓续英雄血脉的坚定决心。如今，一批批“接棒者”正踏着英雄足迹前进：有在极限条件下勇挑重担的训练骨干，有创新战法提升集群伞降效能的一级军士长……英雄的身影一直都在。 视频连线结束，两张照片被郑重地放入军史馆展柜。英雄亲人的留影、冰崖上的刻痕、无惧生死的冲锋誓言……它们是无声的历史见证，也如催征的号角，激励着“接棒”的英雄传人攻坚克难、奋勇前行。
