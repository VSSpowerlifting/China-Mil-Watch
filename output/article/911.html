About this analysis
Summary
On the 24th International Day of UN Peacekeepers (May 29), PLA Daily published a feature-length survey of Chinese troops currently deployed across three mission areas — Lebanon (24th Multifunctional Engineer Detachment), South Sudan Juba (12th Infantry Battalion), South Sudan Wau (engineer and medical detachments), and Abyei (6th Helicopter Detachment and quick reaction force) — profiling named personnel including demining team leader Hu Miaoqi, pilot Wang Chen, and three First-Class Sergeant Majors (一级军士长) who maintain the Abyei helicopter fleet. The piece explicitly frames every operational vignette — mine clearance near the Blue Line, an armed checkpoint standoff in Tombek, an emergency UNISFA evacuation, a life-threatening medical case — through the Global Security Initiative (全球安全倡议) and the community with a shared future for mankind (人类命运共同体), making the framing itself the analytical object: PLA Daily is using named, operationally specific peacekeeping stories to populate those political concepts with concrete human content for a domestic audience. The reference to the peacekeeping formation's appearance at the September 3rd parade extends that messaging into the domain of military prestige, though what remains unknown is whether any of the operational details — sortie rates, checkpoint encounters, patient caseloads — reflect changed mission tempo or are selected purely for narrative effect.

China's Blue Helmets, a Major Power's Responsibility

— A Survey of Our Overseas Peacekeeping Personnel on Mission

■ PLA Daily Reporters Wang Min, Sun Qi

May 29 of this year marks the 24th International Day of United Nations Peacekeepers. In mission areas including Lebanon, South Sudan, and Abyei, Chinese peacekeeping soldiers have forged a shield of peace with the blood of their youth, and through concrete action have upheld the Global Security Initiative (全球安全倡议) and demonstrated the responsibility of a major power. They have earned high praise from the United Nations and local populations, erected monuments of peace in foreign lands, and inscribed the glory of Chinese soldiers into the cause of world peace.

Mission on Their Shoulders, Resolute and Unwavering——

A Peacekeeping Force Worthy of Admiration

The minefields near the "Blue Line" on the Lebanon-Israel border are a genuine battlefield of life and death.

In scorching heat, clad in protective gear weighing tens of jin, Hu Miaoqi — demining team leader of the multifunctional engineer detachment deployed to Lebanon — has long since had his uniform soaked through with sweat. Heavier than the gear itself is the psychological pressure of confronting unknown dangers. The terrain in the minefield is complex; beneath weeds and fallen leaves, unexploded ordnance may lurk anywhere. For a demining operator, every time he bends down to probe the ground is decidedly not a simple repetitive task, but a life-and-death trial in a contest with death itself.

Yet whenever a mission arrives, Hu Miaoqi always leads the charge into the minefield without hesitation. Having grown from a green recruit into a demining backbone, he has remained on the front lines of peacekeeping, never forgetting the trust of his homeland and always preserving his original peacekeeping conviction (维和初心).

In mid-January of this year, the 12th Infantry Battalion deployed to South Sudan (Juba) dispatched personnel who, together with UN military observers and civilian staff, traveled to the Tombek area to carry out a joint long-range patrol mission and provide armed escort for a UN staff safety investigation.

Tombek is located in the northern part of South Sudan's Central Equatorial State. The route requires passing through five armed checkpoints and more than 130 kilometers of communications dead zones, posing extremely high security risks. Shortly after departure, the convoy encountered a checkpoint. Two abandoned pickup trucks had been parked in the middle of the road as barriers, with more than ten armed personnel standing on either side holding weapons, making forceful demands to inspect the convoy; some of the armed individuals deliberately — or as if inadvertently — pointed their gun barrels toward the convoy.

"Convoy halt — establish defensive formation!" Mission commander Liu Keyi issued the order without hesitation. The convoy rapidly spread out, forming a layered defense; machine gunners on vehicle rooftops maintained close surveillance, and escort personnel quickly established a perimeter using the vehicles as cover. Liu Keyi then stepped out of the vehicle, authorization documents in hand, to negotiate. "Under the relevant regulations, United Nations vehicles have right of passage." The tone was calm, yet carried an unmistakable firmness. After a principled, forceful, and measured exchange, the other party ultimately allowed the convoy through. Similar scenes occurred multiple times along the route and were each resolved in turn.

"The outstanding performance of the Chinese infantry battalion is worthy of admiration!" At the conclusion of the mission, Ghanaian military observer Adom offered his heartfelt praise.

The mission of peace weighs heavier than a mountain. At critical moments, Chinese peacekeeping soldiers always step forward: when a civilian mission-area helicopter suffered a sudden malfunction, China's 6th Helicopter Detachment deployed to Abyei immediately shifted to full-time combat readiness, with four crews urgently filling the gap to ensure the aerial corridor remained open; to race against construction deadlines, officers and soldiers of the Chinese engineer detachment deployed to South Sudan (Wau) camped in the field — "dust-covered on clear days, mud-covered on rainy days" — ensuring the unobstructed flow of a critical supply route in the mission area; the "blue barrel" (蓝桶) base to be restored sat at the bottom of a valley, inaccessible to machinery and vehicles, so officers and soldiers of the Chinese construction engineer detachment deployed to Lebanon shouldered and hand-carried building materials and equipment, traversing steep, sandy safety corridors to reach the work site… "An indispensable force in the mission area," "a key factor in UN peacekeeping operations" — these repeated accolades speak to the high regard all parties hold for China's Blue Helmets.

Strong Capabilities, Protecting Lives——

A Peace Partner Worthy of Trust

An unstable situation, a harsh environment, and scarce medical resources constantly threaten the lives of peacekeeping personnel in the mission area. In times of peril, Chinese peacekeeping soldiers, drawing on their solid professional competence and resolute sense of responsibility, have built reliable safety barriers for allied peacekeeping forces.

"Thank you, Chinese military! Thank you, Chinese comrades!" On March 4, after stepping off the helicopter upon returning from a forward mission point, Tamid embraced pilot Wang Chen tightly.

Tamid is an officer in the Bangladeshi peacekeeping infantry battalion of the United Nations Interim Security Force for Abyei (UNISFA). He had studied in China and, after arriving in the mission area, had developed close ties with officers and soldiers of China's peacekeeping helicopter detachment deployed to Abyei due to the demands of their work, forging a deep friendship. Following a sudden deterioration in the security situation, UNISFA decided to urgently withdraw forces from two forward mission points, and the helicopter detachment was ordered to take on the evacuation and transfer mission.

With allied forces in danger, the longer they remained, the greater the risk. Neither mission point had lighting to support night flight operations. To gain more transfer time, Wang Chen coordinated with maintenance squadron comrades to prepare in advance, ensuring they could take off the moment dawn broke. Throughout the operation, Wang Chen and his comrades raced against the clock, fighting continuously, with every sortie and every leg of the route operating at full capacity, ultimately completing the transfer safely and efficiently. "Every time we saw the peacekeeping helicopter bearing the Five-Star Red Flag appear on the horizon, we saw hope." "You are peace partners worthy of our trust." … The praise from Bangladeshi allied forces was filled with gratitude and with the highest recognition of China's peacekeeping soldiers.

On January 15, Ernest, a Kenyan civilian aviation crew member with the United Nations Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS), was urgently brought to the Chinese peacekeeping medical detachment. Ernest had developed symptoms including general fatigue and coughing a week earlier; his condition then deteriorated sharply over the following days, and by the time he was brought in, his difficulty breathing and feelings of chest tightness and suffocation had markedly worsened, and he was unable to move independently.

Examination revealed that the patient's complexion was cyanotic, his breathing was faint, his consciousness was clouded, and his blood oxygen saturation had dropped to 85%. He was diagnosed with severe pneumonia complicated by hypoxemia, hyponatremia, and multiple other complications. His life was in danger — there was not a moment to lose. The detachment immediately activated its critical emergency treatment protocol, key personnel assembled rapidly, and Deputy Detachment Commander Wen Feng took the lead in coordinating the overall treatment effort. Real-time monitoring of vital signs, precise medication, scientifically managed fluid replacement, scheduled oxygen therapy, psychological reassurance… For several consecutive days, medical personnel took turns on watch, using their superb medical skills and sense of responsibility to build a lifeline for the patient. After careful treatment, the patient's critical symptoms were relieved and his various functions steadily recovered; he ultimately pulled through. Before being discharged, Ernest expressed his thanks repeatedly: "It was the Chinese military doctors who gave me a second life. Thank you!"

Conveying Warmth, Kindling Hope——

Friendship Envoys Worthy of Lasting Memory

Not far from the UNISFA headquarters stands the humble Mumu Primary School. Apart from one mud-brick classroom, the remaining seven rooms are built from dry branches gathered on the spot. The school operates on a rotation system: when class is in session, the classroom is packed with students; those not in class wait outside.

On March 11, China's peacekeeping quick reaction force and helicopter detachment deployed to Abyei delivered school bags, footballs, and other cultural and sporting supplies to the school. The arrival of the officers and soldiers immediately brought the campus to life. Interpreter Yang Yuchi improvised as a Chinese-language teacher, teaching the children to read and write words such as "China" and "friendship." The sound of reading rang out, light and rhythmic, like a song of friendship floating on the air. When asked about their aspirations, one boy answered earnestly: "When I grow up, I want to put on a uniform and protect people who need help, just like the Chinese peacekeeping soldiers." Yang Yuchi recalled: "At that moment, his eyes were shining — the light of hope kindled, growing toward the sun."

To touch lives with one's own life, to illuminate the future with hope. While fulfilling all authorized missions, Chinese peacekeeping officers and soldiers across mission areas have also actively carried out humanitarian assistance activities, bringing tangible warmth to people who have suffered under war and poverty.

In educational assistance activities, officers and soldiers not only donated cultural and sporting supplies, but also leveled playgrounds and repaired desks and chairs, doing their utmost to improve the children's learning environment. At free medical clinics, military doctors examined residents, distributed medicine, and spread epidemic prevention knowledge, using their professional expertise to safeguard the people's health. When delivering water to residential areas, officers and soldiers played songs and taught children simple Chinese phrases — "delivering cool relief, and also sowing seeds of hope."

Sincerity earns genuine affection; effort wins respect. During patrols, local residents along the route often proactively greet Chinese peacekeeping officers and soldiers. Whether it is a resounding "China, friend" or a slightly halting "nǐ hǎo," it brings the peacekeeping soldiers a distinctive warmth. "You are friendship envoys worthy of lasting memory!" The words of Kodito, director of the Abyei civilian hospital, give voice to the shared sentiment of the people in the mission area.

Time Leaves Its Mark; Peace Is Priceless

■ Lü Desheng

There is a kind of mission that travels ten thousand li away, crossing mountains and seas, marching forth for peace. There is a kind of steadfast commitment forged in hot blood, boundless in its great love, honoring pledges through conviction. There is a group of officers and soldiers who, like the morning light spreading across many corners of the blue planet, sow vitality in turbulent lands and, with sincere and unwavering conviction (赤诚初心), write China's deep and abiding watch over world peace.

Since first participating in United Nations peacekeeping operations in 1990, successive waves of Chinese peacekeeping soldiers, moving to the rhythm of "China red, peace blue," have traversed the line between life and death with the blood of their youth, kindling the light of hope. Through their professionalism, tenacity, dedication, and sacrifice, they have demonstrated the responsibility of a responsible major power and earned widespread praise from the international community. China's Blue Helmets have become a vivid emblem of upholding world peace and advancing the building of a community with a shared future for mankind (人类命运共同体).

"The sky holds no trace of the wings' passage, but I have flown through it." When speaking of their peacekeeping experience, some soldiers cite this line from Tagore to encourage themselves. Yet how could successive waves of peace envoys from the East have left no trace of their own? Walking along newly repaired roads and bridges, treading on land cleared of mines and unexploded ordnance, using stationery donated to schools — how could local people not feel gratitude toward those Eastern faces wearing the Five-Star Red Flag armband? What China's Blue Helmets have shown them is not only the long-absent peace, but also hope for their lives and their happiness. As one peacekeeping force commander put it: every morning, as long as local residents see peacekeeping vehicles passing by their doors, they know that peace and security are at their side, and they find renewed confidence and courage to face life. Letting people who have endured the ravages of war know that a powerful force is nearby, guarding the peace — this is precisely the meaning of "I have flown through it."

Time leaves its mark; peace is priceless. The world needs peace just as people need air and all living things need sunlight. Through the changing of seasons, as figures come and go, the conviction of China's Blue Helmets in guarding peace has never wavered. With hot blood and a sense of responsibility, they have soothed the wounds left by war, caused barren land to bloom, and helped scattered families reclaim warmth. This protection that transcends national borders is silent yet priceless, enduring and far-reaching. Countless peacekeeping officers and soldiers, during their tours of duty, miss their families, the food, and the peaceful environment of their hometowns; but after returning home, they find themselves thinking of the blue sky, white clouds, and red earth of the mission area, and feeling concern for the simple, honest people and innocent children there. Liu Sen, who has carried out peacekeeping missions in South Sudan three times, often recalls squad leader Yang Shupeng and Li Lei, who gave their lives in the mission area. For him, this concern has become a knot in his heart: "I hope that place can achieve peace. Even if it is not during the time I am there, I hope that day comes soon."

Peace is the common aspiration of the peoples of all nations; development is the eternal theme of human society. At the September 3rd military parade held last year, the peacekeeping forces formation once again appeared in Tiananmen Square, passing in review in a powerful and majestic bearing. Viewed from a distance, the blue berets merged into a vast blue shield of peace, conveying to the world China's resolve to uphold world peace — peace is hard-won, and defending it demands every effort.

Salute, China's Blue Helmets!

Words from the Heart of Peacekeeping

Knowing full well that every step in a minefield is fraught with peril, why do we always press forward without hesitation? The answer is written on the land that has regained vitality after mines have been cleared, and etched in the smiles that have returned to the faces of local people. Clearing mines, healing wounds, turning barren and dangerous ground back into fertile fields and open roads — this is the sincere commitment of Chinese soldiers who guard peace with their lives.

— Hu Miaoqi, Demining Team Leader, 24th Multifunctional Engineer Detachment Deployed to Lebanon

Every time the battle eagle spreads its wings, it is a severe test of the crew's combat capability; every precise takeoff and landing lays a firmer foundation for the stable operation of the mission area; every emergency medical evacuation proves that China's Blue Helmets are peace partners that allied peacekeeping forces can trust.

— Wang Chen, Pilot, 6th Helicopter Detachment Deployed to Abyei

Though the needle is small, it bears the weight of life entrusted to it; though our steps are hurried, they measure the journey of peace. We are not on the front line where artillery fire rages, yet we hold the front line of protecting life. On the red earth of South Sudan, we soothe wounds with a healer's benevolence, fulfill our mission with a soldier's sense of responsibility, and with the sweat of our youth water the flowers of peace and friendship.

— Lin Li, Head Nurse, 16th Medical Detachment Deployed to South Sudan (Wau)

Through small, everyday actions, providing local people with concrete, tangible help — these ordinary small things allow them to feel the deep friendship of the Chinese people, powerfully consolidating the public foundation of friendship between the two countries, and letting the seeds of peace and friendship take root, grow, and bloom in the mission area.

— Lu Jinfang, Commander, 12th Infantry Battalion Deployed to South Sudan (Juba)

The "Iron Triangle" That Lifts the Wings of the Blue Helmets

■ Wang Peng, Zhang Shuai

In Abyei, East Africa, sandstorms swirl and the clouds of war have not yet dispersed. The battle eagles of China's peacekeeping helicopter detachment take wing again and again into the rising sun, cruising along the watch line of peace.

"The battle eagles guard the peace; we guard the battle eagles." Within the maintenance squadron, three First-Class Sergeant Majors (一级军士长) — Zhou Jieqing, Wang Mingzheng, and Sun Honghong — are the "Iron Triangle" in the minds of their comrades, the ones who lift the iron wings of the Blue Helmets. Since deploying to the mission area, they have maintained a high sense of responsibility at all times, using their superb skills to build a solid safety barrier for mission flights.

"The aircraft has a life of its own; we must be the ones who understand it best." More than twenty years of shared military service have led them to uphold the same conviction: absolutely never let a battle eagle take off with a fault; absolutely never leave any hidden danger in the peace mission. The three have distinct but mutually complementary responsibilities: mechanical technician Zhou Jieqing, who has spent years working with engines, transmission systems, and mechanical structures, is the battle eagle's "guardian of sinew and bone," acutely sensitive to abnormal mechanical sounds and temperature changes; avionics technician Wang Mingzheng is the battle eagle's "neural regulator," precisely calibrating all parameters before takeoff and monitoring data feedback in real time during flight, providing precise and stable electronic control support for the aircraft; airborne mechanic Sun Honghong monitors the in-flight control status and equipment condition throughout, able to relay in-flight anomalies at the first moment and provide critical reference for precise ground troubleshooting.

During a pre-flight ground engine run before one mission, at the instant of engine shutdown, Zhou Jieqing keenly caught a faint metallic friction sound from within the engine compartment — the abnormal sound was brief, sharp, and highly irregular, unlike the normal mechanical settling sound of a routine shutdown. A hidden danger is an order! Zhou Jieqing immediately called a halt to the launch and reported the situation. Wang Mingzheng retrieved the complete parameter record from the engine run without delay, replaying the data curves second by second to precisely pinpoint the time node at which the abnormal sound appeared, and by comparing against baseline data from previous normal engine runs, inferred that the fault originated from mechanical jamming or foreign object ingestion, thereby defining a precise scope for the investigation. Sun Honghong, drawing on past in-flight operating experience, judged that if the aircraft took off with this hidden danger, it would very likely cause aggravated vibration and control lag; if the engine blades were damaged, it could even trigger an in-flight engine shutdown, directly endangering the aircraft and personnel. After joint deliberation, the three unanimously determined that the aircraft must be grounded and the hidden danger thoroughly investigated.

On an overseas peacekeeping battlefield, there are no temperature-controlled maintenance hangars, no precision disassembly equipment, and no external support. Yet the mission was at hand and time was pressing. On the flight line, with heat waves rolling and sand blowing in their faces, the three divided the work and cooperated, conducting a section-by-section investigation and component-by-component disassembly, ultimately finding a tiny metal fragment that had been ingested deep inside the compressor air intake. They then thoroughly cleaned the engine and conducted repeated calibration checks, ensuring the helicopter could carry out its mission on schedule.

In recent times, due to the volatile security situation in the mission area, the aerial transport, patrol and surveillance, reconnaissance and assessment, and emergency rescue flight missions undertaken by the detachment have surged sharply. With helicopters being sortied at high frequency and short intervals, the previous practice of conducting maintenance and servicing on a fixed weekly "maintenance day" has become very difficult to sustain. If any one helicopter develops a fault, the remaining helicopters must operate under overload, which can very easily trigger cascading problems. To address this, Zhou Jieqing proposed adopting a new model of "gap maintenance" (插空维护) — conducting maintenance during intervals between flights. Whenever a battle eagle lands, the maintenance crew immediately begins hidden-danger inspection and servicing. This approach increases the workload, but allows mission requirements and hidden-danger inspection to be balanced simultaneously, enabling the battle eagles to fly more steadily and farther.

Individual excellence is a breakthrough at a single point; team excellence is what achieves comprehensive victory. The three systematically summarized typical fault cases in high-temperature, sand-and-dust environments, took the lead in compiling a concise and practical field maintenance manual, and organized a complete set of combat-proven experience covering rotor manual assembly, engine field periodic inspection, equipment protection in sandy and dusty environments, noise and heat reduction under high-temperature conditions, and rapid air-ground coordinated troubleshooting. They also turned the flight line into a live-fire classroom, personally teaching apprentices hands-on. Wang Mingzheng teaches diagram reading, wiring, and troubleshooting; Zhou Jieqing passes on the techniques of listening for faults, feeling for temperature, and identifying vibration; Sun Honghong imparts the key points of in-flight monitoring, anomaly handling, and air-ground coordination. Under their patient instruction, more and more young officers and soldiers have been tested and grown into outstanding "guardians of the battle eagle."

检查发现，患者面色青紫、呼吸微弱、意识模糊，血氧饱和度降至85%，确诊为重症肺炎伴低氧血症、低钠血症等多重并发症。生命垂危，刻不容缓！分队立即启动重症应急救治预案，骨干力量火速集结，由副队长文峰牵头统筹整体救治工作。实时监测生命体征、精准用药、科学补液、定时吸氧、心理安抚……连续数日，医护人员轮番值守，以精湛医术和责任担当，为患者筑起生命防线。经过精心治疗，患者危重症状缓解，各项机能稳步恢复，最终转危为安。出院前，厄尼斯特连声道谢：“是中国军医给了我第二次生命，谢谢你们！” 传递温暖，点燃希望—— 值得铭记的友谊使者 在联阿安全部队司令部不远处，坐落着简陋的木木小学。除一间土坯教室外，其余七间均由就地取材的枯枝搭建而成。学校实行轮换制，上课的时候，教室里密密麻麻挤满学生，不上课的学生就在外面等着。 3月11日，中国赴阿卜耶伊维和快反分队和直升机分队给学校送来了书包、足球等文体用品。官兵的到来，让校园的气氛顿时热闹起来。翻译杨雨驰临时客串中文老师，教孩子们读写“中国”“友谊”等词汇。书声琅琅，节奏轻快，宛如友谊之歌悠扬回荡。当被问及理想，一名男孩认真回答：“长大后我要穿上军装，像中国维和军人一样保护需要帮助的人。”杨雨驰回忆说：“那一刻，他眼中闪着光，那是希望被点燃后向阳而生的光芒。” 以生命影响生命，用希望点亮未来。在完成各项授权任务的同时，各任务区的中国维和官兵还积极开展人道主义援助活动，为饱受战乱与贫困之苦的人们送去实实在在的温暖。 助学活动中，官兵们不仅捐赠文体用品，更平整操场、修缮桌椅，竭力改善孩子们的学习环境；义诊现场，军医们为民众检查身体、发放药品、普及防疫知识，用专业知识为他们守护健康；为居民点送水时，官兵们播放歌曲，教孩子们简单中文，“送去清凉，也播撒希望的种子”。 真心换来真情，付出赢得尊重。巡逻途中，沿途民众常主动向中国维和官兵打招呼。无论是响亮的“China, friend（中国，朋友）”，还是略显生涩的“你好”，都让维和官兵感受到别样的温暖。“你们是值得永远铭记的友谊使者！”阿卜耶伊平民医院院长柯迪托的话语，道出了任务区民众的共同心声。 岁月有痕 和平无价 ■吕德胜 自1990年首次参加联合国维和行动以来，一批批中国维和军人，踏着“中国红，和平蓝”的旋律，以热血青春穿越生死一线、点亮希望之光，用他们的专业、坚韧、奉献和牺牲，展现负责任大国的担当，赢得国际社会广泛赞誉。中国蓝盔，已成为维护世界和平、推动构建人类命运共同体的鲜明标识。 “天空没留下翅膀的痕迹，但我已飞过。”谈到维和经历，有战士用泰戈尔的诗句自勉。然而，一批批来自东方的和平使者，又怎么可能没有留下自己的痕迹？走过修缮一新的道路桥梁、踏上地雷和未爆弹被清排干净的土地、用着赠送给学校的文具，当地民众怎能不感念那群佩戴五星红旗臂章的东方面孔？中国蓝盔让他们看到的，不仅是久违的和平，更是关乎生活、关乎幸福的希望。正如一名维和部队指挥员所说，每天早上，当地民众只要看到维和部队的车辆从门前经过，就知道和平与安全就在他们身边，就会重拾生活的信心和勇气。让饱经战乱的民众知道，他们身边有一支强大的力量在守护和平，这正是“我飞过”的意义所在。 岁月有痕，和平无价。世界需要和平，就像人需要空气、万物生长需要阳光一样。寒暑交替，身影来去，中国蓝盔守护和平的信念从未动摇。他们以热血与担当，抚平战争带来的创伤，让荒芜的大地绽放光彩，让失散的家庭重拾温暖。这份跨越国界的守护，无声亦无价，恒久且绵长。无数维和官兵在任务期间，会想念家乡的亲友、美食与和平的环境，但回国之后，又会想起任务区的蓝天、白云、红土地，牵挂那里朴实的民众、纯真的孩子。曾3次到南苏丹执行维和任务的刘森，常常回忆起在任务区牺牲的班长杨树朋和李磊。对他而言，这种牵挂已化作一个心结：“我希望当地能够实现和平。即使不是我在的这一批，也愿这一天早日到来。” 和平是各国人民的共同愿望，发展是人类社会的永恒主题。在去年举行的九三阅兵中，维和部队方队再次亮相天安门广场，以威武雄壮的姿态接受检阅。从远处眺望，蓝色贝雷帽汇成一面蔚蓝的和平之盾，向全球传递中国维护世界和平的决心——和平来之不易，捍卫尽心竭力。 致敬，中国蓝盔！ 维和心语 明知雷场步步惊心，为何我们始终义无反顾？答案，写在扫除雷患后重焕生机的土地上，刻在当地民众重展的笑颜中。扫清雷患、抚平疮痍，让荒芜险地变回良田通途，这是中国军人用生命守护和平的赤诚担当。 ——第24批赴黎巴嫩维和多功能工兵分队扫雷组长 胡妙奇 每一次战鹰振翅，都是对机组实战能力的严峻考验；每一次精准起降，都在为任务区的平稳运转夯实根基；每一次紧急医疗转运，都证明中国蓝盔是维和友军值得托付的和平伙伴。 ——第6批赴阿卜耶伊维和直升机分队机长 王 琛 针尖虽小，承载的是生命重托；步履匆匆，丈量的是和平征程。我们不在炮火纷飞的一线，却坚守在守护生命的前沿。在南苏丹红土地上，我们以医者仁心抚慰创伤，以军人担当践行使命，用青春汗水浇灌出和平与友谊之花。 ——第16批赴南苏丹（瓦乌）维和医疗分队护士长 林 莉 “战鹰守护和平，我们守护战鹰。”机务中队中，三名一级军士长周洁清、王明征、孙红红是战友们心目中托举蓝盔铁翼的“铁三角”。自部署到任务区以来，他们始终保持高度的责任感，凭借精湛技艺为任务飞行筑起一道坚固的安全屏障。 “战机是有生命的，我们要做最懂它的人。”二十余载共同的军旅生涯，让他们恪守同样的信念：绝不让战鹰带故障升空，绝不让和平使命留有隐患。三人职责各有侧重却又相辅相成：常年与发动机、传动系统、机械结构打交道的机械技师周洁清是战鹰的“筋骨守护者”，对机械异响、温度变化等极其敏感；航电技师王明征则是战鹰的“神经调控师”，起飞前精准校验各项参数、飞行中实时监测数据反馈，为战机提供精准稳定的电控支撑；空中机械师孙红红则全程关注空中操纵状态、装备工况，能在第一时间反馈空中特情，为地面精准排故提供关键参考。 一次飞行任务前的地面试车环节，停车熄火的瞬间，周洁清敏锐地捕捉到发动机舱内一丝金属摩擦音，异响短促尖锐、极不规律，不同于正常停车的机械回落声。隐患就是命令！周洁清当即叫停放飞，上报情况。王明征第一时间调取了本次试车的完整参数记录，逐秒回放数据曲线，精准定位异响出现的时间节点，通过对比历次正常试车基线数据，推断故障源于机械卡滞或异物侵入，为排查划定精确范围。孙红红结合以往的空中操作经验认为，若带着隐患升空，极易引发振动加剧、操纵迟滞，一旦发动机叶片损伤，还可能引发空中停车，直接危及战机和人员安全。三人会商研判后，一致认定，需要执行停飞、彻查隐患。 海外维和战场，没有恒温检修厂房、没有精密拆解设备、没有外援支撑。然而，任务当前，时间紧迫。在热浪滚滚、沙尘扑面的停机坪上，三人分工协作，逐段排查、逐件拆解，最终在压气机进气口深处找到一块被吸入的细小金属碎屑。之后，他们又对发动机进行彻底清理和反复校验，确保直升机能够按时执行任务。 近段时间以来，由于任务区安全局势动荡，分队承担的空中运输、巡逻警戒、勘察评估、应急救援等飞行任务陡增，直升机出动频次高、间隔短，以往每周固定在“机械日”进行维护保养的做法，已经很难满足任务需要。任何一架直升机出现故障，其余的直升机就要超负荷运转，极易引发连锁问题。为此，周洁清建议采取利用飞行间隙进行“插空维护”的新模式。只要战机落地，机务班组就立刻展开隐患排查和检修保养。这种做法增加了工作量，却能够兼顾任务需求和隐患排查，让战鹰飞得更稳、更远。 自身过硬是单点突破，团队过硬才能全面制胜。三人系统总结了高温沙尘环境下典型故障案例，牵头编写简易实用的野外维护手册，并梳理出旋翼人力装配、发动机野外定检、沙尘环境装备防护、高温条件下降噪降温、空地协同快速排故等成套实战经验。他们还把停机坪当作实战课堂，手把手带徒施教。王明征教识图、接线、排故，周洁清传听诊、摸温、辨振技巧，孙红红授空中监测、特情处置、空地联动要点。在他们的悉心带教下，越来越多的年轻官兵经过考验，成长为优秀的“战鹰守护者”。
