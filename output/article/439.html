That Military Salute Spanning 91 Years Moved Everyone Present

那个跨越了91年的军礼，感染了现场的每一个人

About this analysis
Summary
Representatives of the 'Red First Company' (红一连) — a unit tracing its lineage to the 1927 Autumn Harvest Uprising and the Sanwan Reorganization (三湾改编), where the practice of 'building the Party branch at the company level' (支部建在连上) originated — traveled more than 2,000 kilometers to visit 105-year-old Tibetan female Red Army veteran Wang Quanying at her home in Dujiangyan, Sichuan. The article is standard political work content: its value is as a record of how the PLA uses living Long March veterans and founding-era unit lineages to reinforce Party-branch loyalty and the 'red bloodline' (红色血脉) narrative for company-grade officers, not as evidence of any operational or organizational development. The brief mention that the company has 'transformed into an informatized armored infantry unit' and holds the 'Model of the Era' (时代楷模) designation is incidental framing, not the article's analytical weight.

Representatives of the "Red First Company" of an Army brigade visit 105-year-old female Red Army veteran Wang Quanying——

A Military Salute Spanning 91 Years

■ Yan Xuhong, Li Shaoqing

105-year-old female Red Army veteran Wang Quanying renders a salute toward the camera. Photo by Qiao Zifeng.

"Old comrade, we've come to see you…"

On an afternoon in early summer, 105-year-old female Red Army veteran Wang Quanying, of Dujiangyan City, Sichuan Province, welcomed a special group of visitors to her home — representatives of the "Red First Company" (红一连) of an Army brigade.

The "Red First Company" is a Red Army company born out of the Autumn Harvest Uprising. During the Sanwan Reorganization (三湾改编), Comrade Mao Zedong personally developed six Party members within the "Red First Company" and established the first company-level Party branch in our army, pioneering the practice of "building the Party branch at the company level" (支部建在连上). In June 1935, the First Front Army, in which this company served, and the Fourth Front Army achieved a victorious rendezvous at Xiaojin (present-day Xiaojin County), Sichuan. At that time, Wang Quanying was a medic in the Fourth Front Army's Women's Independent Regiment. Ninety-one years later, representatives of the company's officers and soldiers traveled more than 2,000 kilometers from their garrison to pay a special visit to this revolutionary forerunner.

Wang Quanying is of Tibetan ethnicity. Originally named "Guixiang," she was born in May 1921 in a small Tibetan mountain village. Orphaned in early childhood, she was sold into serfdom at age five and later resold, living a life of hunger, inadequate clothing, and constant abuse.

In 1935, the revolutionary spark illuminated Wang Quanying's life. "That night, I saw the Red Army's torches on a distant hillside and quietly followed them," the elderly Wang Quanying recalled with deep feeling. "At first I was frightened, but through contact with the Red Army officers and soldiers, I found a sense of being respected…" Watching the Red Army's wholehearted dedication to the common people, she resolved to join the revolutionary ranks.

After enlisting, Wang Quanying was assigned to the Fourth Front Army's Women's Independent Regiment as a medic. She studied nursing knowledge with an insatiable eagerness, attentively cared for the wounded, and actively served the unit as a guide and carried out propaganda and agitation work.

On one occasion, while Wang Quanying was moving with her unit toward Aba in western Sichuan, they were surrounded and attacked by a large enemy force, and the fighting was exceptionally fierce. "Many comrades were killed. While I was rescuing the wounded, bullets were whistling past my ears…" Wang Quanying recalled. In the intense battle, she and her comrades lost contact with the main force and were compelled to withdraw toward Dasang in Danba County, dispersing and breaking out in small groups.

Holding fast to her firm convictions, Wang Quanying and several comrades took a roundabout route through Xiaojin and Balang Mountain, following in the Red Army's footsteps and crossing two snow-capped mountains — Balang Mountain and Niutou Mountain — in succession. In May 1936, after enduring untold hardships and brushing with death countless times, they made their way to Sanjiang Town in Wenchuan to recuperate from their wounds. That year, Wang Quanying was 15 years old.

To evade enemy searches, Wang Quanying concealed her identity and settled in Sanjiang Town. After the founding of the People's Republic of China, she changed her name from the old name "Guixiang" — which symbolized her suffering — to "Wang Quanying." Wang Quanying never disclosed her experiences to her neighbors. It was not until 1984, when her identity was verified and confirmed by the Aba Prefecture People's Government, that the relevant authorities issued Wang Quanying a "Certificate of a Straggled Red Army Member" (流落红军证明书). She subsequently received commemorative medals marking the 80th anniversary of the victory of the Chinese Workers' and Peasants' Red Army's Long March and the 70th anniversary of the founding of the People's Republic of China.

"Truly worthy heirs of the Red Army…" Upon learning that the "Red First Company" officers and soldiers had carried forward the red bloodline (红色血脉), trained hard to master the skills needed to win, had successfully transformed into an informatized armored infantry unit, and that the company had received honors including the Military Training Model Company, the Model Company for Party Branch Development, and "Model of the Era" (时代楷模), Wang Quanying nodded repeatedly with gratification and gave a thumbs-up.

"Old comrade, this is our company's flag." Just before the farewell, Political Instructor Wu took out the neatly folded "Red First Company" pennant from his bag and slowly unfurled it. Gazing at the flag suffused with glory, Wang Quanying's eyes grew moist; her fingers gently stroked the surface of the flag, as if touching those years of war and fire.

Then, Wang Quanying slowly raised her right hand and rendered a solemn military salute to the company flag. In that moment, this salute spanning 91 years seemed to silently speak of a loyalty and faith that will never fade, moving deeply every person present.

陆军某旅“红一连”官兵代表看望105岁女红军王全英—— 跨越91年的军礼 ■颜旭洪 李少卿 105岁女红军王全英面向镜头敬礼。乔子丰摄 “老前辈，我们来看您了……” 初夏的一个午后，四川省都江堰市105岁女红军王全英，在家中喜迎一批特殊客人——陆军某旅“红一连”官兵代表。 “红一连”是诞生于秋收起义的红军连队，“三湾改编”时，毛泽东同志亲自在“红一连”发展了6名党员，建立了我军第一个连队党支部，开创了我军“支部建在连上”的先河。1935年6月，该连所在的红一方面军和红四方面军在四川小金（今小金县）胜利会师。当时，王全英是红四方面军妇女独立团的一名卫生员。91年后，该连官兵代表从驻地跨越2000多公里，专程来探望这位革命前辈。 王全英，藏族，原名“桂香”，1921年5月出生在一个藏族小山村。她幼年父母双亡，5岁被卖为农奴，后来又被转卖，过着食不果腹、衣不蔽体、备受欺凌的生活。 1935年，革命星火照亮了王全英的人生。“那晚，看见远处山坡上红军的火把，我悄悄跟了过去。”王全英老人深情回忆，“起初心里害怕，可在与红军官兵的接触过程中，让我找到了受尊重的感觉……”看着一心为老百姓着想的红军，她决心投身革命队伍。 入伍后，王全英被编入红四方面军妇女独立团，成为一名卫生员。她如饥似渴地学习护理知识，悉心照料伤员并积极为部队当向导、做宣传鼓动工作。 一次，王全英随部队向川西阿坝转移途中，遭到敌军重兵围攻，战斗异常激烈。“牺牲了好多同志，我抢救伤员时，子弹就在耳边呼啸……”王全英老人回忆道，激战中，她和战友们同大部队失去联系，被迫向丹巴县大桑方向转移，并化整为零、分散突围。 怀着坚定信念，王全英和几名战友绕道小金、巴朗山，追寻红军足迹，连续翻越巴朗山、牛头山两座雪山。1936年5月，她们历经千难万险、九死一生，辗转至汶川三江镇养伤。这一年，王全英15岁。 为躲避敌人搜捕，王全英隐姓埋名，定居三江镇。新中国成立后，她为告别象征苦难的旧名“桂香”，更名为“王全英”。王全英从未向邻里透露自己的经历，直至1984年经阿坝州人民政府核实确认，有关部门为王全英颁发了“流落红军证明书”。她先后获得中国工农红军长征胜利80周年、庆祝中华人民共和国成立70周年纪念章。 “真不愧为红军传人……”当得知“红一连”官兵赓续红色血脉，苦练打赢本领，已成功转型为信息化装甲步兵，连队获得军事训练标兵连、党支部建设模范连及“时代楷模”等荣誉时，王全英欣慰地连连点头，并竖起了大拇指。 “老前辈，这是我们连队的旗帜。”即将告别时，吴指导员从行囊中取出叠放整齐的“红一连”连旗，并缓缓展开。凝视着浸染荣光的旗帜，王全英老人眼眶湿润，手指轻抚旗面，仿佛在触摸那段烽火岁月。 紧接着，王全英老人缓缓抬起右手向连旗敬了一个庄严的军礼。那一刻，这个跨越了91年的军礼，似乎在无声诉说着永不褪色的忠诚与信仰，深深地感染着现场的每一个人。
