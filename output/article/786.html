About this analysis
Summary
A political work essay in PLA media profiles Sun Jixian, a battalion commander who led the 17-man assault crossing of the Dadu River in May 1935 and later founded China's first comprehensive missile test range—now Dongfeng Space City (东风航天城)—in the Gobi Desert, pegged to the May 24 launch of the Long March-2F Yao-23 rocket carrying Shenzhou-23. This is standard political education content; its value is as a record of how PLA media links revolutionary martyrdom narratives to the defense-industrial mission, using the missile test range's founding generation to frame austerity, sacrifice, and institutional loyalty as models for current personnel.
Translation

One Man's Two "Long Marches"

■ Zhao Leixiang

At 23:08 on May 24, the Long March-2F Yao-23 carrier rocket carrying the Shenzhou-23 crewed spacecraft roared into the sky. Crimson flames tore through the night, illuminating the letters "CZ"—standing for "Long March" (长征)—emblazoned on the milky-white rocket body, making them gleam brilliantly.

That China's aerospace community inscribed "Long March," this iconic cultural totem, onto their rockets carries profound meaning. Gazing at the rocket body as it flew ever farther away, people could not help but think of a man who undertook the "Long March" twice—Sun Jixian.

The first time was May 1935, when Sun Jixian, then serving as a battalion commander, followed the Central Red Army on the Long March to the banks of the Dadu River. The Dadu River was one of the most treacherous passes along the Red Army's Long March route, known since ancient times as a "natural barrier." Chiang Kai-shek massed heavy forces, attempting to use this natural obstacle to encircle and annihilate the Red Army, boasting that he would make the Red Army "a second Shi Dakai."

The Red Army's only hope at that moment was to force a crossing. When advance force commander Liu Bocheng and political commissar Nie Rongzhen entrusted Sun Jixian with this mission—one that bore on the Red Army's very survival—he replied without hesitation: "Never mind ten Shi Dakais or nine Shi Dakais—we will cross that river!" Sun Jixian personally selected 17 warriors to form a river-crossing assault team.

What followed was a heart-stopping charge into the jaws of death. The wooden boat was like a lone leaf, pushing forward through whirlpools under a hail of enemy fire. Red Army soldiers did their utmost to stay concealed while returning fire toward the opposite bank. When the Red Army rushed onto the riverbank, the enemy frantically hurled grenades and rolling mines down from above. Sheltered in the dead angles of the steep, towering embankment steps, the Red Army charged upward and ultimately routed the defending enemy.

Sun Jixian led the 17 warriors and, with a fighting spirit of heroic tenacity and fearlessness in the face of sacrifice, tore open a breach with their own flesh and blood, opening a path northward for the Red Army.

Twenty-two years later, Sun Jixian—by then Deputy Commander of the 20th Army Corps of the Chinese People's Volunteer Army—returned to China to receive a new assignment. This was a new "Long March." What Sun Jixian faced was no longer guns and a river, but the vast desert stretching to the horizon in the heartland of the motherland: starting from scratch, to establish China's first comprehensive missile test range.

Nie Rongzhen once again assigned him the mission: "We must now walk the Long March road of scientific and technological development—we must climb the 'snow mountains' of cutting-edge science and cross the 'Dadu River' of the new era. This is a matter of great consequence for the future of the military and the nation… It is something that can only be scaled through hard and painstaking effort." Sun Jixian responded on the spot: "Die on the Gobi, be buried on the green hills." In the early days of establishing the test range, he called on everyone at the base to economize on food and reduce the burden on the state. On one occasion, he learned that in addition to their regular standard wages, personnel working at the base also received a certain supplementary allowance from the state. He made his position unequivocal: he would draw only the local standard wage—not a single extra fen of any other allowance.

Developing the missile program was not a matter of days or months; to sustain the work over the long term, it was necessary to put down roots in the Gobi. To that end, Sun Jixian worked alongside officers and soldiers, wielding shovels and pickaxes, and built in the Gobi desert a reservoir covering 9.25 square kilometers with a water storage capacity of 17 million cubic meters.

During that period, he and the officers and soldiers carried forward the outstanding tradition of fearing neither bloodshed nor sacrifice, and of willingly enduring great hardship and heavy toil. On a stretch of wasteland where not a blade of grass grew, they built China's first comprehensive missile test range. Later, this place acquired a name more widely known—Dongfeng Space City (东风航天城).

In 1990, Sun Jixian died of illness. He was buried at the Dongfeng Revolutionary Martyrs' Cemetery, and a portion of his ashes was scattered into the Dadu River, so that he might rest forever in the place where he had fought during his "Long March."

Beneath the same canopy of stars, the Long March of our forebears and our own journey of struggle have never been separated. When the sky is once again streaked with the trail of exhaust flames, and the thunderous roar shakes the heavens like ten thousand catties of wind and thunder, it seems to echo across more than 90 years: "Feeling the hardship? Think of the Long March's 25,000 li."

Today, people have grown accustomed to calling those long and arduous tasks "Long Marches," and to calling difficult challenges "Dadu Rivers," "Loushan Passes," and "Lazikou Passes." These place names, once cold and bare, have gradually become spiritual symbols through the struggle and sacrifice of generation after generation—the source of backbone that allows this country and this nation to stand tall even in the most difficult of times.

The waters of the Dadu River still surge onward, and the vast starry sky still awaits our exploration. Sun Jixian's two "Long Marches" serve as a clear mirror, allowing us to see plainly what loyalty is, what courage is, what sacrifice is, what nobility is, what excellence is… These are precisely what we need to walk the Long March road of the new era well, and they will surely inspire us to achieve new accomplishments on the new journey!

Original Chinese
一个人的两次“长征” ■赵镭饷 5月24日23时08分，搭载神舟二十三号载人飞船的长征二号F遥二十三运载火箭腾空而起。赤红烈焰划破夜空，将乳白色箭体上代表“长征”的“CZ”字样映射得熠熠生辉。 中国航天人把“长征”这个标志性文化图腾写在火箭上，颇具深意。遥望越飞越远的箭体，人们不由想起曾两次进行“长征”的人——孙继先。 第一次是1935年5月，时任营长的孙继先随中央红军长征，来到大渡河边。大渡河，红军长征途中最为凶险的关口之一，自古有“天险”之称。蒋介石调集重兵，企图凭借这道天然屏障围歼红军，叫嚣着要让红军成为“石达开第二”。 当时红军唯一的希望，就是强渡过河。当先遣队司令员刘伯承、政委聂荣臻把这项关乎红军生死的任务交给孙继先时，他干脆利落地说：“管他十达开九达开，我们一定能过河！”孙继先亲自挑选17名勇士组成渡河突击队。 这是一场惊心动魄的生死冲锋。木船就像一片孤叶，在敌人密集的火力中顶着漩涡向前移动。红军战士一面尽量隐蔽，一面向对岸还击。红军冲上江滩时，敌人慌忙往下扔手榴弹、滚雷。在又高又陡的台阶死角掩护下，红军向上冲杀，最终击溃守敌。 孙继先带领17名勇士，以英勇顽强、不怕牺牲的作风，用血肉之躯硬生生地撕开了一道缺口，为红军北上开辟了通路。 22年后的一天，已是中国人民志愿军第20兵团副司令员的孙继先，回国受领任务。这是一次新的“长征”，孙继先面对的不再是枪炮与河流，而是祖国腹地那片一眼望不到边的荒漠——从零开始，组建我国第一个导弹综合试验靶场。 聂荣臻又一次向他部署任务：“我们现在要走科技发展的长征之路，要爬尖端科学的‘雪山’，渡新时期的‘大渡河’，这是关系军队和国家未来前途的大事……是要下一番苦功才能攀登上去的。”孙继先当即表示：“死在戈壁滩，埋在青山头。”靶场创建初期，他号召基地上下节约粮食，减轻国家负担。一次，他得知在基地工作除按正常标准领取工资外，国家还发一定比例的补贴。对此，他明确表示，只按当地标准拿工资，其他的补贴一分也不多拿！ 发展导弹事业不是朝夕之事，要长久地干事业，就得在戈壁滩扎下根。为此，孙继先与官兵一起挥锹抡镐，硬是在戈壁大漠中建起一座面积9.25平方公里、蓄水容量达1700万立方米的水库。 那段时间，他和官兵发扬不怕流血、不怕牺牲，勇于吃大苦、耐大劳的优良作风，在寸草不生的荒滩上建起我国第一个导弹综合试验靶场。后来，这里有了一个更广为人知的名字——东风航天城。 1990年，孙继先因病去世，被安葬在东风革命烈士陵园，部分骨灰被撒进大渡河，长眠在自己“长征”战斗过的地方。 同一片星空下，先辈的长征与我们的征途从来没有分开过。当苍穹再次留下尾焰痕迹，如万钧风雷的隆隆巨响，仿佛是跨越90多年的回响——“苦不苦，想想长征两万五”。 今天，人们已习惯于将那些长期而艰巨的任务称为“长征”，习惯于把困难挑战称为“大渡河”“娄山关”“腊子口”。这些原本冰冷的名词，在一代代人的奋斗与牺牲中，渐渐变成了精神符号，成为我们这个国家、这个民族在艰难时刻依旧挺直脊梁的底气。 大渡河的河水还在奔腾，浩瀚的星空仍在等待着我们探索。孙继先的两次“长征”如一面明镜，让我们清清楚楚地看到，什么是忠诚，什么是勇敢，什么是牺牲，什么是崇高，什么是卓越……这些正是我们走好新时代长征路所必需的，也必将激励我们在新征程上创造新的成绩！
