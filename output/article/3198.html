They Inscribe Loyalty and Responsibility at Their Respective Posts, Becoming "Waves" That Flow into the Torrent of Army-Building

他们在各自战位镌刻忠诚与担当，化作“浪花”汇入强军洪流
PLA Daily (解放军报) 13 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
An Air Force unit's tactical training exercise and a digitized self-propelled artillery live-fire drill serve as the concrete backdrop for a PLA political-work feature profiling four junior enlisted soldiers and NCOs — Private Yuan Zhenkai, an unnamed artilleryman, a soldier named Abai, and Private Li Guorong — framed around the viral 后浪 ('Younger Wave') concept to celebrate generational continuity in military service. The piece documents how PLA political organs adapt civilian cultural touchstones for internal messaging aimed at junior personnel, here linking personal family military lineage, equipment modernization anxiety, and disaster-relief mythology to reinforce commitment to the 强军洪流 ('torrent of army-building'). Its value is as a baseline record of how the institution packages loyalty narratives for the rank and file, not as evidence of a capability or posture development.
Translation

In recent years, a video called "Hòu Làng" (后浪, "The Younger Wave") went viral online, cheering on youth and celebrating the spirit of young people, reshaping public perceptions of the younger generation in the new era. In the heat of military barracks, there is also such a group of young officers and soldiers. Full of vitality and passion, they use youth as their paper and hot blood as their ink, inscribing loyalty and responsibility at their respective posts, becoming surging "waves" that flow into the torrent of army-building (强军洪流). Today, let us get to know these "younger waves" (后浪) of the barracks!

Recently, a unit of the Air Force organized tactical training. Pictured is Private Yuan Zhenkai crawling forward with full effort. Photo by Zhu Haitao.

My uncle is a veteran. Growing up, I listened to his stories of military life — hunkering down in mountain gullies eating steamed buns, hauling sandbags on flood-control levees for three days without sleep. When he told these stories, his eyes shone bright, as if he were recounting the proudest moments of his life.

My uncle dug out an old combat uniform. On the back was a tear the size of a fingernail, with visible traces of having been mended and then split open again. "It got torn during the flood-control operation," he said with a smile. "This uniform went through a lot of hardship with me."

Later, I joined the military too. The first time I took part in tactical training, I charged out in front. The barbed wire caught my back, and when I crossed the finish line and looked, my combat uniform had been ripped open to the size of a coin. I thought of my uncle's old combat uniform.

After being assigned to my unit, I buried myself in hard training, charging ahead in everything. At the year-end assessment, my overall ranking was third in the entire company. I sent the commendation notice home, and my uncle called: "Good lad — you've surpassed me in my day!"

On the artillery position, we bent our backs to carry shells, our knuckles worn into blisters, our backs soaking through our combat uniforms with sweat. The direction operator turned the wheel calling out commands, the gunner adjusted again and again, five people working in seamless coordination.

Later, a new type of digitized self-propelled artillery was fielded. Faced with automatic loading, I gripped the manual with sweaty palms: "Leaving it all to electronic components — will that really work?" Despite my doubts, my comrades and I still riveted ourselves to the training ground every day, diligently studying and practicing the operating techniques for the new equipment.

During the first live-fire exercise, the fire-control system auto-calibrated; the time from halt to firing was cut in half, and after firing we immediately displaced to a new position. In the wind and sand, with visibility under one hundred meters, the vehicle-mounted system updated firing data in real time. I operated the firing handle, and over the intercom came the words: "First round, on target."

The old equipment taught me to forge hard skills; the new equipment forced me to update my entire knowledge system. As equipment iterates, we who operate it must also be forward-surging "waves."

"Abai, the squad leader is waiting for you to come back." During my four years at the military academy, whenever I was in contact with my squad leader, she would often say this to me.

Seven years ago, when I enlisted, my squad leader had three years of service and was top-notch in both physical fitness and professional skills. I was the first batch of new recruits she trained; she was the benchmark on my military journey.

After graduation, when I returned to the unit, my first "clash" with my squad leader came at the unit's "Three Meetings, One Good" (三会一好) skills competition.

Late at night the barracks were completely still. Warm light filtered through the windows of the study room, and faint sounds of recitation drifted out. I pushed open the door, clutching a speech draft I had revised over and over, and saw that my squad leader's draft was covered in annotations.

When the competition ended, my squad leader and I stood together on the award platform. The soldier who had once chased along behind her squad leader could, at that moment, finally "stand shoulder to shoulder" with her.

The regiment organized a "Stories of Army-Building" (强军故事会) storytelling competition. Among the contestants who took the stage were political instructors, platoon leaders, and veteran squad leaders — Private Li Guorong was the only private.

Standing on the stage, Li Guorong began: "When I was eight years old, floodwaters ravaged my hometown. My grandmother and I were trapped on a rooftop when an assault boat cut through the curtain of rain and stopped in front of me. A soldier in uniform lifted me onto the boat, then turned around to carry my grandmother on his back. I never saw him again after that, and I never learned his name…"

"Today, I stand here wearing that same uniform, and I too want to become that kind of person," Li Guorong said. "Before, I was someone who was protected. Now I want to become someone who protects others."

When the competition ended and the judges revealed their scores, Li Guorong ranked first. A leader commented: "Li Guorong spoke well. The younger generation should have exactly this kind of resolve — to walk further along the path blazed by their predecessors. Today he is the first private to step up onto this stage. I hope that in the future there will be a second, and a third."

Original Chinese
近些年，一则《后浪》的视频火爆网络，为青年加油、为青春喝彩，刷新了人们对新时代青年的看法。在火热的军营中也有这样一群青年官兵。他们富有朝气、激情洋溢，以青春为纸、热血为墨，在各自战位镌刻忠诚与担当，化作奔涌的“浪花”汇入强军洪流。今天，就让我们一起来了解下这些军营里的“后浪”吧！ 近日，空军某部组织战术训练。图为列兵袁振凯正在奋力匍匐向前。朱海涛摄 叔叔是老兵。我从小听他讲当兵的故事，蹲山沟啃馒头，抗洪堤坝上扛沙袋3天没合眼……讲这些故事时，叔叔的眼睛亮晶晶的，像在讲这辈子最得意的事。 叔叔翻出一件旧作训服，后背上有个指甲盖大的破口，能看到补过又裂开的痕迹。“抗洪的时候被刮破的。”他笑着说，“这身衣服，跟着我吃了不少苦。” 后来我也当了兵。第一次参加战术训练，我冲在最前面。铁丝网挂住后背，冲过终点一瞧，作训服被撕出个硬币大的口子。我想起了叔叔那件旧作训服。 下连后，我埋头苦练，样样往前冲。年底考核，综合排名我排在全连第3名。我把喜报寄回去，叔叔打来电话：“好小子，比我当年强！” 火炮阵地上，我们弓腰扛炮弹，指节磨出血泡，后背汗透作训服。方向手摇转轮喊着口令，瞄准手一遍遍调校，5个人默契配合。 后来，新型数字化自行火炮列装。面对自动装填，我攥着手册手心冒汗：“全交给电子元件，行吗？”虽然心存疑虑，我和战友们依然每天铆在训练场，勤学苦练新型装备操作技术。 第一次实弹演练，火控自动校准，停车到击发时间缩短一半，打完随即转移阵地。风沙中，能见度不足百米，车载系统实时更新诸元，我操纵击发手柄，对讲机传来“首发命中”。 老装备教会我练硬功，新装备逼我更新知识体系。装备迭代，驾驭它的我们，也得是向前奔涌的“浪花”。 “阿白，班长等你回来。”军校4年，和班长联系时，她常这样对我说。 7年前，我入伍时，班长兵龄3年，体能、专业样样拔尖。我是她带的第一批新兵，她是我军旅路上的标杆。 毕业回到单位，我与班长第一次“交锋”，是在单位“三会一好”比武赛场上。 深夜营区一片静谧，学习室的窗户透出暖光，细碎的诵读声隐隐传来。我攥着反复修改的讲稿推门而入，看到班长手中的讲稿满是批注。 比武结束，我跟班长一同站上领奖台。那个曾经在班长身后追赶的兵，此刻终于能和班长“并肩而立”。 团里组织强军故事会，走上讲台的参赛选手有指导员、排长、老班长，列兵只有李国荣一人。 站在台上，李国荣开口说：“8岁那年，家乡洪水肆虐，我和奶奶被困于屋顶，一艘冲锋舟穿过雨幕停在我面前。一位穿军装的叔叔把我抱上船，又转身去背奶奶。后来我再没见过他，不知道他的名字……” “如今，我穿上同样的军装站在这里，也要成为那样的人。”李国荣说，“以前我是被保护的人，现在我想成为保护别人的人。” 比赛结束，评委亮分，李国荣排名第一。领导点评：“李国荣讲得好。年轻一代就该有这样的志气，沿着前辈的足迹走得更远。今天他是第一个站上来的列兵，我希望以后有第二个、第三个。”
