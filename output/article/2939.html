About this analysis
Summary
During the 三伏 (dog-days) heat peak, a PLA Daily reporter visited a garrison battalion of an Army 'Red Army' regiment stationed near Turpan's Flaming Mountain, where outdoor temperatures exceeded 43°C, and found that the unit had implemented a 全员上哨制度 (all-hands sentry rotation) requiring officers, including the battalion instructor, to man midday posts alongside enlisted soldiers. The practice documents a grassroots officer-soldier relations initiative driven by a specific institutional problem: officers making decisions about sentry conditions based on reports rather than direct experience, which the rotation corrected by generating officer-sourced proposals that the regiment Party committee then approved — shorter midday shifts, upgraded cooling equipment, shade canopies, and expanded heat-prevention supplies. The article is standard political-work content in PLA Daily's 'work style' genre; its value is as a record of how the institution frames 身先士卒 (leading from the front) to junior officers, not as evidence of a new policy or changed operational posture.
Translation

A Reporter Visits an Army "Red Army" Regiment's Turpan-Garrison Unit —

Beneath Flaming Mountain: Officers Stand Watch to Gauge Soldiers' Conditions

■ Li Xiaolong, PLA Daily Reporter Wang Yue | Illustration: Fang Han

During the dog days of summer (三伏时节), a reporter traveled to an Army "Red Army" regiment's Turpan-garrison unit, stationed beneath Flaming Mountain. Stepping off the vehicle, a wall of heat hit immediately.

It was midday rest period, and the cicadas were growing louder by the moment. The reporter noticed that the soldier standing post at the camp gate was not an enlisted man but a major. Zhang Zhilin, a staff officer accompanying the reporter, explained that the man was Instructor Lü, the officer in charge of the garrison battalion.

In the shimmering heat, Instructor Lü stood gripping his rifle in full duty gear, eyes alert, sweat streaming steadily down his face.

"Does the instructor have to stand watch too?" the reporter asked, puzzled. "Turpan is ringed by mountains on all sides, and the dog days are especially brutal here," Zhang Zhilin said. "They implement an all-hands sentry rotation (全员上哨制度) — all officers are assigned to duty squads and platoons and share the same posts and shifts as the soldiers." He pulled up the weather forecast on his phone to show the reporter: the outdoor temperature had already exceeded 43 degrees Celsius.

Twenty minutes later, the shift change arrived. Instructor Lü completed the handoff with the relieving sentry and stepped down from the post, drenched in sweat. The reporter handed him a bottle of water, and the two fell into conversation.

"The soldiers train through the scorching heat all morning — midday is a precious window for rest and recovery," Instructor Lü explained. To give everyone more rest time, Party member officers had voluntarily stepped up to take on sentry duty during the peak-heat noon hours. Reviewing the sentry logbook, the reporter found that during the midday rest period each day, posts were mostly manned by officers, with the instructor's name appearing repeatedly.

After stepping down from his post, Instructor Lü did not return to the battalion headquarters to rest. Instead, he took his notebook and set off to inspect each sentry post — an invariable routine he followed after every shift. Along the way, the reporter noticed that every post was stocked with supplies including Huoxiang Zhengqi Water and ice packs.

"We not only equipped every post with heat-prevention and cooling supplies, but also made appropriate adjustments to the length of individual midday sentry shifts," Instructor Lü said. On the subject of that change, he recounted his experience standing the noon watch for the first time.

That day, the outdoor temperature was exceptionally high, and the old air conditioner was nearly useless for cooling. In the second half of his shift, Instructor Lü noticed his concentration flagging and his head growing heavy and dull.

"Sitting in the office, we look at data and listen to reports; standing at the post, we feel the heat and understand the hardship firsthand," he said. After the all-hands sentry rotation was implemented, several officers came to him to discuss their experience standing the noon watch and put forward specific suggestions: "shorten midday sentry shifts," "increase heat-prevention supplies," "replace the cooling equipment," "build shade canopies." They then submitted a post-facility improvement plan to the regiment's functional departments, which the regiment Party committee approved without delay. "Having officers rotate through the midday watch lets everyone genuinely feel the weight of sentry duty and better understand what the soldiers are hoping for," Instructor Lü said.

At the ammunition depot post, company officer Wang Chenhao was on duty. Instructor Lü told the reporter that Wang Chenhao had just stood the midday watch the day before, yet had come again today to relieve Sergeant Yalikku Yasen of his post. It turned out that Yalikku had accidentally sprained his ankle during training a few days earlier but was still insisting on standing his own watch. Upon learning this, Wang Chenhao went to Yalikku directly: "Standing watch in this heat is bad for your ankle's recovery. You rest easy — I'll take this shift for you!"

"Turpan is not only a place of high temperatures; it is also a place of high spirit," Instructor Lü said. Ten kilometers from the garrison, a themed sculpture commemorating the spirit of military land reclamation (军垦精神) stands as a monument to a chapter of history worth remembering.

In early 1949, Comrade Wang Zhen volunteered: "I ask to go to the most arduous places, to every frontier that needs us, to Xinjiang!" After the founding of New China, he led his troops into Xinjiang, where officers and soldiers held a rifle in one hand and a pickaxe in the other, garrisoning the frontier while opening wasteland for cultivation (屯垦戍边、开荒造田). In the first lunar month of 1950, a snow road stretching more than 20 kilometers was crowded with people loading stones onto sleds and running along the snow-packed track. Running at the very front was Wang Zhen himself.

To advance frontier infrastructure construction, he personally led army commanders, division commanders, and soldiers at every level to haul sleds and transport stone. In the bitter cold, Wang Zhen's padded jacket was soaked through with sweat, then frozen stiff by the wind — pressed against his body, ice-cold to the bone — yet he worked with undiminished vigor. Inspired by Wang Zhen's example, officers, soldiers, and people of all ethnic groups threw themselves into the work of reclamation and construction.

"Today, officers rotating through the midday watch and voluntarily relieving injured or ill soldiers of their duty is a continuation and practice of the tradition of 'leading from the front, setting an example' (身先士卒、模范带头)," Instructor Lü told the reporter. "In the near term, we plan to organize all battalion officers to conduct in-situ instruction (现地教学) using Comrade Wang Zhen's story of garrisoning the frontier through land reclamation as the teaching material, to conduct in-depth study on 'how officer cadres should take the lead,' and to guide Party member officers to carry forward fine traditions and shoulder their mission and responsibilities."

Returning to the camp entrance, the reporter observed an intelligent license-plate recognition system operating efficiently, automatically screening and verifying incoming vehicles, with sentries needing only to monitor from inside the guardhouse. A recently replaced air conditioner blew a steady stream of cool air. Beside the post, a refrigerator labeled "Convenience Service Point" (便民服务点) held a large container of lightly salted water; the reporter took a drink and felt an immediate wave of refreshment.

"This container of lightly salted water serves both the sentries on duty and passersby," Instructor Lü explained. In past dog-day summers, sentries frequently encountered pedestrians suffering from heat exhaustion; the lightly salted water could help them rehydrate and cool down in a timely manner. "This service initiative was also a suggestion put forward by an officer based on his own sentry experience," he added.

Lan Zhongli at the sentry post. Photo by Yi Zhiming

As the interview concluded and the reporter prepared to leave, the soldiers of the battalion's First Company happened to return from training. Their camouflage uniforms were soaked through, their faces flushed red from the sun. As they reached the sentry post, the officer in charge, Lan Zhongli, directed the unit's backbone cadres to lead the men back to the barracks to rehydrate and rest, then walked straight toward the post himself. It turned out that the soldier scheduled to relieve the sentry had shown mild heat-exhaustion symptoms during training, and Lan Zhongli had volunteered to stand the shift in his place.

At the post, a sweat-drenched Lan Zhongli completed the handoff with the sentry and took the rifle with practiced ease. Not far away, on the battle-flag plaza, two battle flags — "Courageous and Tenacious" (勇猛顽强) and "Truly Tenacious" (真正顽强) — flew in the breeze.

A Cool Breeze Over the High-Temperature Sentry Post

■ Wang Yue

The three-foot sentry post (三尺哨位) reflects one's original aspiration; work style is revealed in the smallest details. Standing watch beneath Turpan's summer sun, in temperatures exceeding 40 degrees Celsius, is the genuine daily reality for frontier officers and soldiers. That the garrison battalion's Party member officers voluntarily take the midday watch may look like nothing more than standing a single shift — but in practice it is a vivid exercise in transforming work style (转作风), warming soldiers' hearts (暖兵心), and rallying morale (聚士气).

Nothing at the grassroots level is trivial; every leaf and branch touches the heart. The true state of soldiers' hearts and minds is not found in the data reports on office desks — it is found on the sun-scorched sentry post, in the sweat flowing down soldiers' faces. One genuine experience of role reversal is worth more than a hundred paper-based investigations. By stepping onto the high-temperature sentry post, the battalion's officers not only felt firsthand what soldiers endure in the blazing heat, but also identified with greater precision the pain points and difficulties that exist at the grassroots level.

From replacing cooling equipment and building shade canopies, to stocking heat-prevention supplies and adjusting duty shift lengths — a series of targeted, practical measures not only solved concrete problems but also put the fine tradition of "officer-soldier camaraderie and unity between superiors and subordinates" (官兵友爱、上下团结) into tangible practice. The story of the garrison battalion's officers rotating through the midday watch is a reminder to leaders at every level: only by exchanging sincerity for soldiers' trust and rallying morale through concrete action can one forge a battle-hardened force that is united in purpose and dares to fight and is determined to win.

记者探访陆军某红军团驻吐鲁番部队—— 火焰山下：干部站哨察兵情 ■李小龙 解放军报记者 王 越 插图：方 汉 三伏时节，记者来到火焰山下的陆军某红军团驻吐鲁番部队。下车后，热浪扑面而来。 正值午休，耳边的蝉鸣一声高过一声。记者注意到，站在营门哨位上的不是战士，而是一名少校军官。同行的机关干部张智林告诉记者，他是留守营的吕教导员。 热浪蒸腾中，穿戴全套执勤装具的吕教导员手握钢枪、目光警惕，汗水顺着脸颊不断滑落。 “教导员也得上哨吗？”记者有些疑惑。“吐鲁番四面环山，三伏天格外热，他们实行全员上哨制度，所有干部编入执勤班排，与战士同岗同勤。”张智林打开手机天气预报页面向记者展示，此时的室外气温已超过43摄氏度。 20分钟后，下哨时间到了，吕教导员与接岗的哨兵交接完毕，满头大汗地走下哨位。记者递过一瓶水，与他攀谈起来。 “战士们上午冒着酷暑训练，午间是宝贵的休整窗口。”吕教导员介绍，为了让大家有更多休息时间，党员干部主动顶上，承担起正午高温时段的执勤站哨任务。记者翻看岗哨登记本看到，该营每天午休时段大多由干部站哨，教导员的名字多次出现。 走下哨位，吕教导员没有返回营部休息，而是带着笔记本前往各哨位查勤。这是他每次下哨后雷打不动的例行工作。同行途中，记者发现每个哨位都配备了藿香正气水、冰袋等物资。 “我们不仅给各哨位配齐了防暑降温物资，还将午间站哨单次时长进行了适当调整。”谈起这项改变，吕教导员向记者讲起他第一次站午间哨的经历。 那天，室外气温特别高，老旧空调几乎起不到制冷作用。站哨后半段，吕教导员明显感觉注意力难以集中，脑子昏昏沉沉。 “坐在办公室，我们看的是数据、听的是汇报；站上哨位，我们感受的是热浪、体会的是不易。”吕教导员说，实行全员上哨后，多名干部找他讨论站午间哨的感受，并提出“缩短午间站哨时间”“增配防暑物资”“更换制冷设备”“搭建遮阳棚”等具体建议。随后，他们向团机关业务部门上报岗亭整改计划，很快得到团党委批准。“干部轮站午间哨，让大家真切体会到岗哨的职责，也更加读懂了战士们的期盼。”吕教导员说。 弹药库哨位上，某连干部王晨浩正在值守。吕教导员告诉记者，王晨浩昨天刚站完午间哨，今天又来替连队中士亚力库・亚森站哨。原来，亚力库前几天在训练中不慎扭伤脚踝，却仍坚持自己站哨。得知情况，王晨浩找到亚力库：“高温天气站哨，不利于脚踝恢复。你先安心休息，这班哨，我替你！” “吐鲁番不仅气温高，也是一座精神高地。”吕教导员介绍，营区10公里外，矗立着一座军垦精神主题雕像，镌刻了一段值得铭记的历史岁月。 1949年初，王震同志主动请缨：“我要求到最艰苦的地方去，到一切需要去的边疆去，到新疆去！”新中国成立后，他率部挺进新疆，官兵一手持枪、一手拿镐，屯垦戍边、开荒造田。1950年正月，一条20多公里的雪路上人头攒动，大家不断往爬犁上装石头，在雪路上奔跑。跑在最前面的就是王震。 为推进边疆基建，他亲自带着军长、师长等各级指挥员和战士一同拉爬犁运石料。寒冬里，王震的棉衣被汗水浸透后经风一吹，冻得硬邦邦的，贴在身上，冰凉入骨，但他依旧干劲十足。在王震的示范带动下，干部战士、各族群众纷纷主动投身屯垦建设。 “如今，干部轮站午间哨、主动替伤病战士执勤，是对‘身先士卒、模范带头’传统的赓续和实践。”吕教导员对记者说，“近期，我们准备组织全营干部以王震同志屯垦戍边的故事为教材，深入开展‘干部骨干如何带头’现地教学，引导党员干部发扬优良传统、扛起使命担当。” 回到营区门口，记者看到，智能车牌识别系统正高效运转，自动对外来车辆进行甄别核验，哨兵只需要在岗亭内监督。新换不久的空调不断吹送着凉风，哨位旁一个标注着“便民服务点”字样的冰箱里，存放了一大桶淡盐水，记者取水品尝，顿感一阵清凉。 “这桶淡盐水，既保障执勤哨兵，也服务过往行人。”吕教导员介绍，往年三伏时节，哨兵们常遇到过路行人中暑不适的情况，淡盐水能及时帮助他们补水降温。“这项服务举措，也是一名干部结合站哨经历提出的建议。”他说。 兰钟栗在哨位上。易志明 摄 采访结束，记者准备离开，正巧碰到该营一连官兵结束训练归来。此时，官兵的迷彩服湿淋淋的，脸庞被晒得发红。走到岗哨处时，带队干部兰钟栗安排骨干带领大家回宿舍补水休整，自己径直走向哨位。原来，本该接哨的战士训练时出现轻微中暑症状，兰钟栗主动提出替他站哨。 哨位上，大汗淋漓的兰钟栗与哨兵完成交接，利落地接过钢枪。不远处的战旗广场，“勇猛顽强”与“真正顽强”两面战旗正迎风飘扬。 高温哨位清风徐来 ■王 越 三尺哨位映初心，细微之处见作风。顶着吐鲁番的夏日酷暑，在40多摄氏度的高温天气下站哨，是边防官兵的真实日常。留守营党员干部主动值守午间哨，看似只是站一班岗，实则是转作风、暖兵心、聚士气的生动实践。 基层无小事，枝叶总关情。真正的兵心兵情，不在办公室的数据报表里，而在烈日炙烤的哨位上，在战士流淌的汗水中。一次真实的换位体验，胜过百次纸面调研。走上高温哨位，该营干部不仅在热浪熏蒸中亲身体会战士感受，也更精准地发现了基层存在的痛点难点。 从更换制冷设备、搭建遮阳棚，到配齐防暑物资、调整执勤时长……一系列靶向施治的务实举措，不仅解决了问题，更把“官兵友爱、上下团结”的优良作风落到了实处。留守营干部轮站午间哨的故事提醒各级，唯有以真心换兵心、以实干聚士气，才能锻造出勠力同心、敢打必胜的过硬队伍。
