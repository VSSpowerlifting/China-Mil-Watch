From Huangyaidong to Huangyanjie: A Roots-Seeking Team Visits Revolutionary Sites of Its Predecessor Units

从黄崖洞到黄洋界，寻根小队一路寻访前身部队革命旧址
PLA Daily (解放军报) 24 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A brigade of the 71st Group Army organized a 寻根 (roots-seeking) team to visit the Huangyaidong Arsenal site in Shanxi and the Huangyanjie battlefield at Jinggangshan, tracing predecessor units back to the Special Service Company of the 1st Regiment, 1st Division, 1st Army of the Workers' and Peasants' Revolutionary Army. The article explicitly ties the trip to 政治整训 (political rectification training), framing battlefield heritage as the mechanism for purging 'fearing hardship and fearing loss' attitudes among current officers and soldiers. This is standard political work content; its value is as a record of how the brigade-level institution is operationalizing political rectification training through historical narrative, not as evidence of a capability or posture change.
Translation

A Roots-Seeking Team from a Brigade of the 71st Group Army Visits Revolutionary Sites of Its Predecessor Units Along the Way—

From Huangyaidong to Huangyanjie

■ Huang Zihan

Soldiers at the Huangyaidong Arsenal use hand tools to machine weapon components. (Archival photo) Provided by the Huangyaidong Revolutionary Memorial Site

Passing over hills and advancing northward along a winding road, the author rounds a bend to find a stretch of red cliff face coming into view—this is the former site of the Huangyaidong Arsenal, acclaimed as "the cradle of the people's defense industry."

Eighty-five years ago, dark clouds pressed upon the city as Japanese forces massed heavy troops in an attempt to destroy the Huangyaidong Arsenal. Eighth Route Army regimental commander Ou Zhifu led his soldiers in a desperate resistance, fighting a bloody battle for eight days and nights against a Japanese force several times their size, achieving the brilliant record of a 6-to-1 enemy-to-friendly casualty ratio. The regiment was awarded the honorary battle flag "Hero Regiment of the Huangyaidong Defense Battle."

Eighty-five years later, amid lush vegetation, the stone walls of the former arsenal site are pocked with bullet scars, silently bearing witness to those smoke-filled years. As the unit that has inherited the battle flag of its forebears, a brigade of the 71st Group Army organized a roots-seeking team to come here. Their hearts were filled with reverence, and also with a question: in the face of the enemy's surging offensive, how were their predecessors able to achieve the feat of defeating the strong with the weak, and the many with the few?

"Beyond that mountain is the Huangyaidong Arsenal, within Licheng territory—that's the direction the Japanese came from…" Standing atop the ridge at Guanjianaocun, looking in the direction pointed out by the team's leading officer, Gu Yuanqiang, the map comes to life amid a landscape of gullies and ravines; place names overlap with hilltops, and the sound of guns and artillery seems to carry from deep within history.

Why did the Japanese forces covet the Huangyaidong Arsenal? A set of figures provides the answer: by 1940, the arsenal employed nearly 1,000 workers, with peak monthly output of 430 rifles and more than 2,000 artillery shells; its annual weapons production could equip 16 regiments, making it the largest arsenal in the anti-Japanese base areas behind enemy lines.

Deep in the mountains, machines turned. Without precision instruments, chive leaves were used as measuring tools; lacking specialized machine tools, files were used to manufacture firearms. Everyone believed that as long as the arsenal stood, the Eighth Route Army would have weapons and could win battles.

Proceeding along the mountain plank road, underfoot lies the Wenggelang Gorge—a place where "one man holds the pass against ten thousand"—which was both the battlefield of the Huangyaidong Defense Battle and the last barrier on the road to the arsenal.

On November 11, 1941, Japanese forces exploited heavy fog in an attempt to launch a surprise attack on the southern entrance of Huangyaidong. One by one, the comrades around him fell to enemy fire, until only bugler Cui Zhenfang remained to guard the sole passage into Huangyaidong.

For seven full days and nights, Cui Zhenfang fought alone, exploiting the advantageous terrain of the cliff face, hurling more than a hundred grenades and annihilating dozens of Japanese soldiers. When his ammunition and rations were exhausted, reinforcements finally arrived. But at that moment, an artillery shell exploded beside him; shrapnel struck his throat, and Cui Zhenfang died a heroic death.

"We should all carry forward this conviction of holding on to the end for the sake of victory," said Corporal Guo Ming with deep feeling. Last year, he transferred to the chemical defense specialty and went with his unit to a certain area in the northwest to conduct toxic agent detection operations.

At the work site, fierce winds and sand flew together; dryness and scorching heat combined. On top of that, the operating conditions were completely unfamiliar, the types of contamination complex and dangerous, and a single careless move could cause serious consequences…

"During the battle at Huangyaidong, the enemy launched a poison gas attack. Cui Zhenfang detected it with keen awareness and, heedless of personal danger, immediately raised his bugle to sound the alarm, preventing the unit from suffering major losses." Just as Guo Ming was beginning to waver, his political instructor led everyone in reviewing the deeds of their predecessors, rekindling Guo Ming's fighting spirit.

After successfully completing the mission and returning to the garrison, Guo Ming voluntarily signed up to join the roots-seeking team. Now, standing before the statue of Cui Zhenfang and gazing down at the precipitous valley below, Guo Ming understood ever more deeply the heroic spirit of his predecessors—their fearlessness before a powerful enemy and their determination to fight to the last—and his eyes took on a new measure of resolve.

"Once you truly understand what conviction means, your heart opens wide," said Gu Yuanqiang. Since the launch of political rectification training (政治整训), an increasing number of officers and soldiers in the brigade have been actively holding themselves up against the deeds of their predecessors, examining and reflecting on themselves, and driving thoughts of "fearing hardship and fearing loss" from their minds.

Leaving the former site of the Huangyaidong Arsenal, the team found answers to the questions in their hearts, only for another question to arise: in conditions of extreme material scarcity, with danger ahead and pursuers behind, why were the revolutionary forebears still able to maintain an unshakeable conviction in victory? They set out again, heading for the Jinggangshan revolutionary base area to seek the spiritual origins of their brigade.

Five hundred li of Jinggangshan, range upon range of mountains. Tracing further back into history, more than 90 years ago, the brigade's predecessor unit was once the Special Service Company of the 1st Regiment, 1st Division, 1st Army of the Workers' and Peasants' Revolutionary Army. It endured the arduous struggles of multiple counter-"encirclement and suppression" campaigns and traversed the long road of the 25,000-li Long March…

At the site of the Huangyanjie Defense Battle, crumbling trenches and scorched stone walls pulled the officers and soldiers back to that breathtaking battlefield: in August 1928, the Red Army's main force had gone far to southern Hunan, leaving fewer than one battalion of troops to garrison Jinggangshan. The enemy, exploiting the thinly held defenses of Jinggangshan, advanced with four regiments in two columns against Huangyanjie.

The weapons in the hands of the garrison soldiers were mostly crude muskets and spears; some even carried hoes and woodcutting knives. Facing an enemy force far superior in both equipment and numbers, they piled rolling logs and boulders at the pass, buried bamboo spikes in the trenches, and, relying on the commanding terrain, held fast to the key routes into the mountains.

At the height of the fighting, the enemy concentrated its firepower in a fierce assault on the sentry post, shells pouring down like rain. Even so, the soldiers holding the position did not retreat a single step, repelling the enemy's charges again and again.

Just as the enemy was massing strength to launch another attack, the soldiers brought up the only mortar they had and, with three shells, scored a direct hit on the enemy's command post. The enemy mistakenly believed that the Red Army's main force had arrived and fled under cover of night.

In the exhibition hall, a passage of text caught the attention of Political Instructor Chai of the roots-seeking team: 31st Regiment Commander Zhu Yunqing and Party Representative He Tingying, bearing in mind Comrade Mao Zedong's instructions to hold Jinggangshan, constructed defensive works at key terrain features, strictly implemented the operational deployment, and neutralized the enemy's advantages in firepower and manpower, creating the miracle of defeating the many with the few. Upon hearing the news, Mao Zedong took up his brush and wrote in high praise: "The enemy surrounds us ten thousand strong, yet I stand unmoved. Long have our walls been strong; now our resolve is one."

"Weapons must be gripped firmly in our own hands; conviction must be planted deep in the hearts of officers and soldiers," Political Instructor Chai said with heartfelt emotion. "It is precisely this conviction—infused into the blood, unwavering and steadfast—that allowed our predecessors to remain unyielding even when trapped in desperate straits."

Before bidding farewell to Jinggangshan, the team members held a video call with Cheng Peiran, a veteran of the "Hero Regiment of the Huangyaidong Defense Battle." "Who among us is not flesh and blood—what person has no fear of death?" Recalling that iron-and-blood chapter of history, the old veteran remained impassioned and stirring: "But everything for victory, everything for the front!" On their end of the video call, the young officers and soldiers rose solemnly to their feet and rendered a formal salute to the veteran.

Original Chinese
第七十一集团军某旅寻根小队一路寻访前身部队革命旧址—— 从黄崖洞到黄洋界 ■黄子晗 黄崖洞兵工厂官兵使用手工工具加工军械零部件。（资料图片）黄崖洞革命纪念地提供 车过山丘，沿蜿蜒公路一路北进，拐过弯，一片赤色的崖壁映入笔者眼帘——这里便是被誉为“人民军工的摇篮”的黄崖洞兵工厂旧址。 85年前，黑云压城，日军集结重兵企图摧毁黄崖洞兵工厂。八路军团长欧致富带领官兵拼死抵抗，与数倍于己的日军血战8昼夜，创造了敌我伤亡6比1的辉煌战绩，该团被授予“黄崖洞保卫战英雄团”荣誉战旗。 85年后，草木葱茏，兵工厂旧址的石壁上布满弹痕，无声诉说着那段硝烟弥漫的岁月。作为接过先辈战旗的部队，第71集团军某旅组织寻根小队来到这里，他们心中充满敬意，也带着疑问：在敌寇汹涌的攻势下，先辈们何以创造以弱胜强、以少胜多的壮举？ “山那边是黄崖洞兵工厂，属黎城地界，日军就是从那边过来的……”站在关家垴村垴顶，顺着带队干部谷远强指的方向看去，沟壑纵横间，地图变为实景，地名与山头重叠，枪炮声仿佛从历史深处传来。 日军为何对黄崖洞兵工厂虎视眈眈？一组数据可以解答：到1940年，黄崖洞兵工厂有工人近1000名，最高月产步枪430支，炮弹2000余发，年产武器可装备16个团，是敌后抗日根据地最大的兵工厂。 大山深处机器运转，没有精密仪器，就拿韭菜叶当量具；缺少专业化机床设备，就用锉刀制造枪械。大家相信，只要兵工厂在，八路军就有武器，就能打胜仗。 顺着山间栈道前行，脚下便是“一夫当关，万夫莫开”的瓮圪廊峡谷——这里是当年黄崖洞保卫战的战场，也是通往兵工厂的最后一道屏障。 1941年11月11日，日军趁大雾企图偷袭黄崖洞南口。身边战友一个个中弹倒下，只剩司号员崔振芳一人把守进入黄崖洞的唯一通道。 整整7天7夜，崔振芳依托崖壁有利地形孤身作战，投掷出手榴弹百余枚，歼灭了日军数十人。弹尽粮绝之际，增援部队终于赶来。但此时，一发炮弹在他身旁炸响，弹片击中了喉咙，崔振芳壮烈牺牲。 “我们都应传承这种为了胜利坚守到底的信念。”中士郭铭动情地说。去年，他转岗至防化专业，随队奔赴西北某地域开展侦毒作业。 作业现场，狂风和沙砾齐飞，干燥和酷热交加。再加上完全陌生的作业条件，复杂危险的染毒类型，操作稍有不慎就可能造成严重后果…… “在黄崖洞的战斗中，敌人使用毒气进行袭击。崔振芳敏锐察觉，不顾个人安危，立即拿起军号发出警示，避免了部队遭受大的损失。”正当郭铭打起“退堂鼓”时，指导员带大家回顾前辈壮举，激起了郭铭的斗志。 圆满完成任务返回营区后，郭铭主动报名加入寻根小队。此刻，站在崔振芳雕像前，望着脚下险峻山谷，郭铭愈发读懂了先辈不畏强敌、血战到底的英雄气魄，眼神中多了几分笃定。 “读懂了什么是真正的信念，心中就会豁然开朗。”谷远强说，开展政治整训以来，该旅越来越多官兵主动对照先辈事迹查摆反思，把“怕吃苦、怕吃亏”的想法赶出头脑。 离开黄崖洞兵工厂旧址，大家心中的问题有了答案，另一问又随之升起：革命先辈在物质极度匮乏，前有险境后有追兵的情况下，为何仍能保持必胜的信念？他们继续出发，前往井冈山革命老区探寻旅队的精神源头。 五百里井冈，层峦叠嶂。向历史更深处追溯，90多年前，该旅前身曾是工农革命军第一军第一师第一团特务连，历经数次反“围剿”的艰苦斗争，走过两万五千里长征的漫漫征途…… 在黄洋界保卫战遗址，残破的战壕、焦黑的石壁，把官兵们拉回当年惊心动魄的战场：1928年8月，红军主力远赴湘南，仅留下不足一个营的兵力驻守井冈山。敌军趁着井冈山兵力空虚，以4个团的兵力，兵分两路进犯黄洋界。 留守的官兵手中武器多是土枪、梭镖，甚至还有人拿着锄头、柴刀。面对装备、人数远胜于己的敌军，他们把滚木礌石堆在隘口，在战壕里埋下竹钉，凭借险要地形，死死守住进山的要道。 战斗最激烈的时候，敌人集中火力猛攻哨口，炮弹如雨水般倾泻而下。即便如此，坚守阵地的战士一步也没有退缩，打退了敌人一次又一次冲锋。 正当敌人积蓄力量再次发起进攻时，战士们搬来仅有的一门迫击炮，用3发炮弹打中了敌军指挥部。敌人误以为红军主力到来，连夜逃之夭夭。 在展馆中，一段文字引起了寻根小队柴教导员的注意——第31团团长朱云卿、党代表何挺颖牢记毛泽东同志关于坚守井冈山的嘱托，据险构筑防御工事，严格执行作战部署，瓦解了敌军的火力和兵力优势，创造了以少胜多的奇迹。毛泽东闻讯，挥毫盛赞：“敌军围困万千重，我自岿然不动。早已森严壁垒，更加众志成城。” “武器，要紧紧握在自己手里；信念，要牢牢扎在官兵心间。”柴教导员由衷感慨，“正是这份融入血液、矢志不渝的信念，让先辈们即使身处绝境，依然顽强不屈。” 告别井冈山前，队员们和“黄崖洞保卫战英雄团”老兵程培然视频通话。“谁不是血肉之躯，哪有不怕死的道理？”回忆起那段铁血战史，程老依然慷慨激昂，“但一切为了胜利一切为了前线！”视频这头，年轻官兵们肃然起立，向老兵庄严敬礼。
