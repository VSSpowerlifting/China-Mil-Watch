Looking Back on the People's Army's Struggle Through History's "Telescope"

用历史的“望远镜”回望人民军队奋斗历程
PLA Daily (解放军报) 28 July 2026

About this analysis
Summary
A political education essay published in PLA media traces the People's Army's founding principles — Party command of the gun and serving the people — from the 1927 Nanchang Uprising and the 1929 Gutian Conference through the 2015 military reforms and a January 2025 earthquake-response operation in Dingri County, Tibet, closing with the J-20 and carrier battle groups as proof that those principles persist into the modernization era. The piece is standard ideological content aimed at junior officers and soldiers; its analytical value lies in documenting how the PLA currently frames the Gutian Conference legacy and the CMC Chairman responsibility system as the institutional spine of Party control, and in the explicit claim that artificial intelligence and cybersecurity professionals will be folded into wartime mobilization under a people's war framework — a framing that extends the traditional concept to justify civil-military integration in high-technology sectors.
Translation

Why We Have Always Been Able to Press Forward Without Hesitation — Looking Back on the People's Army's Struggle Through History's "Telescope"

■ Sun Xiaoqing

History is a mirror, and also a telescope through which to observe the present.

Mao Zedong — great leader of the Chinese Communist Party. Zhang Side — an ordinary soldier of the Eighth Route Army.

On September 8, 1944, at a memorial service held in Yan'an for Zhang Side, who had died in the line of duty, Mao Zedong delivered his famous speech "Serve the People," stating at the outset the one and only purpose of the Party and the army: "Our Communist Party and the Eighth Route Army and New Fourth Army led by our Communist Party are revolutionary troops. Our entire army is absolutely at the service of liberating the people and works entirely in the people's interests."

"Serve the People" — five concise characters that are both a precise summation of the twenty-three years of Party-building and seventeen years of army-building that had come before, and a fundamental requirement for the entire Party and the entire army. Looking across the People's Army's nearly one hundred years of struggle, holding fast to the nature of the army as defined by the word "people," and upholding the principle of the Party commanding the gun and the purpose of serving the people — this is the soul and the root that has kept our army forever undefeated.

(I)

Looking back through history's "telescope," old China more than one hundred years ago presented a scene of shattered mountains and rivers and untold suffering.

"The long night darkens the land of the red county; for a hundred years monsters and demons have danced; five hundred million people are not reunited." Patriotic men of vision who sought to save the nation either pursued reform and self-strengthening or rose in armed revolt, yet all ended in failure — including the Revolution of 1911, which overthrew the imperial system, which also suffered severe setbacks. In those turbulent times, who could seek happiness for the people and rejuvenation for the nation?

In the early 1920s, the Chinese Communist Party was born amid the nation's internal troubles and external threats, carrying a burning founding aspiration, and began the arduous struggle to unite and lead the Chinese people in winning national independence and the liberation of the people. During the First Revolutionary Civil War, the still-young Communist Party first engaged with military affairs through its cooperation with the Kuomintang — including participating in the founding of the Whampoa Military Academy, establishing and leading political work within the National Revolutionary Army, cultivating its own military cadres, controlling a portion of the revolutionary armed forces, and at the same time actively organizing worker-peasant armed forces and participating in the Northern Expedition, where it effectively led political work.

Then the situation changed dramatically. In 1927, Chiang Kai-shek launched the April 12 counter-revolutionary coup, massacring Communists on a massive scale; within barely a year, more than 310,000 Communist Party members and revolutionary masses had been killed, and the surging Great Revolution was cut short. The bloody lesson taught Communists a truth: "Political power grows out of the barrel of a gun."

From August 1927 to the first half of 1928, the Chinese Communist Party organized and led nearly one hundred armed uprisings across the country. Among these, the Nanchang Uprising fired the first shot of armed resistance against the Kuomintang reactionaries, marking the beginning of the Chinese Communist Party's independent leadership of revolutionary war, the founding of the People's Army, and the seizure of political power through armed struggle. "The army is called the Workers' and Peasants' Revolutionary Army, its banner the sickle and the axe" — the Autumn Harvest Uprising was the first time the banner of the Chinese Communist Party was openly raised in armed struggle. The Guangzhou Uprising directly proclaimed the establishment of the Guangzhou Soviet Government.

The founding aspiration of the Chinese Communist Party, projected onto military affairs, was to build a revolutionary army and fight for the people's world. It was like a ray of light in the dark night, piercing through the fog, illuminating the path ahead, and awakening in countless people their longing for the future.

The leaders of the Nanchang Uprising were mostly senior commanders from the old armies. Dissatisfied with the darkness of society and unattached to high office and generous salaries, they wholeheartedly yearned for revolution; Zhu De had even traveled to Europe in pursuit of the Communist Party. After the Nanchang Uprising failed, the troops split at Sanheба, with Zhu De leading his forces as the rear guard to cover the main force's southward withdrawal. In the fierce fighting, the troops suffered more than fifty percent casualties, and then came news that the main force had been defeated at Chaoshan. The military situation was critical, and morale had collapsed. Zhu De stepped forward, steadied the troops, and declared with iron resolve: the darkness is temporary; those who want to make revolution, follow me — final victory will surely be ours. He reorganized the remaining eight hundred or so men and led them up to Jinggang Mountain, achieving the great juncture with the Autumn Harvest Uprising forces led by Mao Zedong.

The surviving forces of the Nanchang Uprising became the backbone of the People's Army's founding; the revolutionary spark ignited by the Party's founding aspiration became the loyalty inscribed in the bones of this army: loyal to the revolution and loyal to the Party, loyal to the people is our true character.

(II)

Looking through history's "telescope," Gutian ninety-seven years ago was the critical turning point at which the People's Army remade itself.

"Looking back, the Gutian Conference enabled this army of ours to be reborn through fire, like a phoenix rising from the ashes." On October 31, 2014, Chairman Xi delivered an important speech at the All-Army Political Work Conference. The conference was held at the site of the Gutian Conference — reflecting and looking forward from the place where the new-type People's Army took its definitive shape made the occasion all the more thought-provoking. How to firmly establish ideals and convictions throughout the entire army, how to firmly establish the principle of Party character throughout the entire army, how to firmly establish the standard of combat effectiveness throughout the entire army, and how to firmly establish the authority of political work throughout the entire army — these became the major subjects of army-building in the new era.

Surveying Chinese military history from the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods onward, wars for supremacy over the Central Plains spanned every dynasty, producing no shortage of classic battles and legendary commanders. In terms of combat power, the tiger-and-wolf army forged over several generations by the state of Qin helped achieve the unification of China under "the King of Qin sweeping the six states"; the iron cavalry of the Western Han, commanded by Wei Qing and Huo Qubing, drove deep into the desert and struck directly at the Xiongnu royal court. In terms of military governance, the Han-dynasty general Zhou Yafu encamped at Xiliu with strict military discipline — even the emperor had to observe military regulations when entering the camp — and the Tang poem "the Willow Camp towers over the Han palace in spring" borrowed this allusion to praise Zhuge Liang's skillful command. In terms of loyalty and valor, the Yang family generals of the Northern Song, the Yue family army of the Southern Song, and the Qi family army of the Ming dynasty all bravely resisted foreign aggression and pledged their lives to defend their homeland, earning lasting admiration. Yet no matter how mighty and powerful these military forces were, even those that left their names in the historical record ultimately could not avoid leaving the stage of history.

The People's Army burst onto the scene, and its entirely new concepts of army-building and methods of military governance constituted a complete overturning of the traditional army.

On September 29, 1927, when Mao Zedong led the Autumn Harvest Uprising forces to Sanwan Village in Yongxin County, Jiangxi Province, he reorganized the force of fewer than one thousand men: Party organizations at every level and the Party representative system were established, ensuring that every company had a Party branch, every squad and platoon had a Party cell, every company and above had a Party representative, and every battalion and regiment had a Party committee; at the same time, soldiers' committees were established at every level, democratic systems were implemented, and equality between officers and soldiers was realized — this became known historically as the "Sanwan Reorganization."

Two years later, the Gutian Conference, held in Shanghang, Fujian, in December 1929, was another milestone in the history of our Party and our army. At that time, the newly founded Red Army was quite weak, its composition was complex, its thinking was diverse, and facing encirclement by reactionary forces, it urgently needed to correct and overcome various erroneous ideas. The resolution passed at the conference — the Resolution of the Ninth Congress of the Communist Party of China Representatives of the Fourth Army of the Red Army — solemnly declared: the Red Army of China is an armed body for carrying out the political tasks of the revolution. "The Red Army fights not merely for the sake of fighting but in order to conduct propaganda among the masses, organize them, arm them, and help them to establish revolutionary political power."

The series of basic principles for building the People's Army established by the Sanwan Reorganization and the Gutian Conference laid the foundation for building the Party through ideology and building the army through politics, forming the political character and fundamental advantages that distinguish the People's Army from all old-type armies.

In terms of political nature, the Red Army was not a private armed force serving any individual, but an armed body that obeyed Party command and carried out revolutionary political tasks. In terms of value orientation, the officers and soldiers who joined this army no longer used "soldiering for a meal" as a means of livelihood, but took serving the people as their purpose and were willing to sacrifice their own lives for the people's interests. In terms of internal management, the Red Army maintained strict discipline — not taking so much as a needle or thread from the people — while internally practicing political democracy, economic democracy, and military democracy, abolishing corporal punishment, standing in complete contrast to the image of the old armies, of whom it was said "bandits passing through are like a comb, soldiers passing through are like a fine-tooth comb."

Take the Long March as an example. As a great military expedition and a heroic epic of the human spirit, the Long March has moved countless people to admiration. Zhang Xueliang also touched on this point in his "oral history" in his later years: "Tens of thousands of li of Long March, surrounded, nothing to eat, such hardship — yet they stayed together; when scattered, they came back. Of course, their leadership was the Communist Party." Zhang Xueliang had himself been a commander of troops, and he often said to his subordinates: "This Long March of ten thousand li — who among you could lead it? Who could lead an army to stay with you no matter the circumstances? If it had been us, wouldn't we have lost all our soldiers long ago?"

The secret of how the Red Army led its troops was, in fact, what Mao Zedong said: "One reason the Red Army has been able to carry on in spite of such poor material conditions and such frequent engagements is that the Party branch is organized on the company level." During the Long March, the Red Army's General Political Department issued multiple directives emphasizing that company-level Party branches must be fully functioning, and that Party members must play an exemplary and leading role at moments of crisis. When the 3rd Company of the 28th Regiment of the 10th Division of the Fourth Front Army of the Red Army was crossing the grasslands, twenty-nine wounded and sick soldiers fell behind and found themselves in desperate straits. Deputy Company Commander Li Yusheng gathered the men and established a temporary Party branch by a campfire, serving as its secretary. He led everyone in mutual aid — digging for wild vegetables while marching by day, lighting campfires and huddling together for warmth at night — and in the end the entire group walked out of the grasslands and rejoined the main force.

The Party's leadership took root and flourished at the grassroots level of the army, transforming the Party's founding aspiration into the ideals and convictions of all officers and soldiers.

With lofty ideals and convictions came a life's purpose — knowing for whom one soldiers and for whom one fights. Looking back through military history, at every critical juncture of the revolution, the Party placed special emphasis on the army maintaining a firm and correct political orientation — from the Yan'an Rectification Movement's purification of thinking and unification of will, to the New-Style Army Consolidation Movement during the War of Liberation. Concretely implemented through educating and transforming people with correct thinking, the People's Army was like a great furnace, transforming countless peasants, soldiers from old armies, and captured soldiers into courageous revolutionary fighters. The hundreds of thousands of Kuomintang officers and soldiers captured during the War of Liberation were quickly transformed through education and joined the fight to win the world for the people. Why? Because the two armies were worlds apart, and their life's purposes were different.

With lofty ideals and convictions came a high degree of conscious resolve to resolutely carry out revolutionary discipline. The three rules of discipline that Mao Zedong announced while standing on "Thunder Rock" on Jinggang Mountain were gradually refined into the "Three Main Rules of Discipline and the Eight Points for Attention," becoming the iron law of military governance that continues to this day. When troops marched past an orchard during the assault on Jinzhou in the War of Liberation, though desperately hungry and thirsty, not a single person picked a piece of fruit. In the soldiers' view, eating the common people's fruit without permission was contemptible; not eating it was noble. On Wusheng Mountain during the War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea, the young soldier Qiu Shaoyun was carrying out a concealment mission when surrounding grass was ignited by enemy incendiary bombs; to avoid exposing the unit's position, he endured the agony of the flames consuming his body without moving, becoming a steel warrior who lives forever in the fire.

With lofty ideals and convictions came the fighting spirit of fearing neither hardship nor death. The glorious battle history of the People's Army is woven together by the blood and lives of countless heroes and models. In the War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea alone, the Chinese People's Volunteer Army produced 2 special-class heroes, 236 special-merit recipients, 51 first-class heroes, 270 second-class heroes, 4 first-class models, and 83 second-class models. The battle history written in blood resounds with a heroic symphony of struggle, sacrifice, further struggle, and ultimate victory. Those heroes and models — named and unnamed, living and fallen — are like stars in the sky: visible, but too numerous to count. It is precisely their existence that made the heavy night sky of the war years blaze with brilliant starlight.

(III)

Looking carefully through history's "telescope," we see a long-enduring founding aspiration hidden within a single artifact.

On a white handkerchief, a few lines of writing bring instant tears to the eyes — words written in June 1950 by a certain regiment to comrades being demobilized: "We hope that you will henceforth actively participate in production and construction and serve the people to the end." Savoring this seemingly unremarkable artifact, we can weigh its profound historical significance: education in the purpose of serving the people ran through the entire military careers of officers and soldiers, influencing them for their whole lives.

Yes, the People's Army loves the people and serves the people — not only sincerely relying on the people and wholeheartedly serving the people, but also holding fast to the concept that the people create history, implementing a series of people's war strategies and tactics, and allowing the mighty power of people's war to be fully released.

This mighty power was generated by correct guiding principles. As early as the founding period, the Red Army put forward "dispersing forces to mobilize the masses, concentrating forces to deal with the enemy," establishing and applying the guerrilla-style strategic and tactical principles of "the enemy advances, we retreat; the enemy camps, we harass; the enemy tires, we attack; the enemy retreats, we pursue" — starting from the reality of the enemy's strength and our weakness, relying on the masses to contend with the enemy. During the War of Resistance Against Japan and the War of Liberation, our army brought the strategies and tactics of people's war to the fullest expression. For the sake of a protracted war of resistance, our Party implemented the "Ten Major Policies," among which streamlining the army and simplifying administration, supporting the government and cherishing the people, developing production, the "three-thirds system" (三三制), and reducing rent and interest were all focused on the people's livelihood, formulated to reduce the burden on the people and win popular support. As Mao Zedong revealed in "On Protracted War": "The richest source of power to wage war lies in the masses of the people" and "Mobilize the entire people, and you will create a vast sea in which the enemy will be drowned."

This mighty power drew from the selfless dedication of the people. Who are the people? Countless heartbreaking stories tell us: the people are that wife in the Soviet area whose husband followed the Red Army on its long expedition, who waited and waited, making one pair of military shoes every year, and who at her death left behind seventy-five pairs of military shoes. The people are those "Yimeng Red Sisters" (沂蒙红嫂) and "Taihang Nursing Mothers" (太行奶娘) who nourished wounded Eighth Route Army soldiers with their own breast milk and used their own children to ransom Eighth Route Army descendants from enemy blades. The people are those mountain villagers who, to smash Japanese "mopping-up" operations, cleared the fields and hid their stores, leaving the enemy with nothing to eat or drink until their spirits broke. The people are also those ordinary common folk who sent their last flesh and blood to the battlefield, handed over their last bowl of rice as military rations, used their last foot of cloth to make military uniforms, and spread their last old cotton-padded jacket over a stretcher... In a word, the tens of millions of people who ruined their families to aid the nation's cause, gave everything they had, and were willing to make great sacrifices for the revolution — they wrote two towering characters: the People!

This mighty power created a spectacle of war without precedent in history. In the War of Resistance Against Japan, our Party was able to serve as the mainstay of resistance because its foundation was the people.

When the sea of the people surged in fury, the military and civilian population behind enemy lines created tunnel warfare, mine warfare, sparrow warfare (麻雀战), aquatic guerrilla warfare, and transportation sabotage warfare, among others, bringing people's war to a consummate art. During the War of Liberation, the people marched alongside the army — from rear-area production to transporting supplies, from repairing roads to treating the wounded, from clearing battlefields to guarding prisoners — wherever the People's Liberation Army fought, the people were there in support. In the three great campaigns of Liaoshen, Huaihai, and Pingjin alone, the PLA mobilized a total of more than 8.8 million civilian laborers; the people deployed 1.41 million vehicles of all sizes, more than 360,000 stretchers, more than 2.6 million draft animals, and contributed 425 million kilograms of grain. One Shandong civilian worker led a small cart team from Laiyang County, following the army through battle after battle across the Huaihai battlefield, covering more than five thousand li; at each place he arrived, he carved the place name into a small bamboo stick he carried with him, and in the end that bamboo stick bore the names of eighty-eight cities, towns, and villages across three provinces — Shandong, Jiangsu, and Anhui. No wonder Chen Yi exclaimed: the victory of the Huaihai Campaign was pushed out by the people's wheelbarrows.

The Jilubian (冀鲁边) Anti-Japanese Base Area in the old revolutionary region was an open plain with no natural defenses, yet it relied on the vast green sorghum fields of summer and the warm kangs in villagers' homes in winter to nurture generation after generation of the people's sons and daughters in arms. In response to the needs of the developing War of Liberation, many units were reorganized, large numbers of young men enlisted, and successively some one hundred thousand or more people were incorporated into the Northeast, East China, Central Plains, Northwest, and other field army units, bidding farewell to their homeland and maneuvering across regions, fighting within and beyond the Great Wall, battling north and south of the great rivers. They were like great trees that had taken root and grown in the fertile soil of the Jilubian region's deep mass foundation — weathering wind and rain, enduring cold and heat, flourishing and spreading their branches, their dense shade extending to the Changbai Mountains and the Heilong River, to island coconut groves, to the eastern seaboard, to the snow-covered western frontier.

From the founding of the People's Army to the birth of New China, the Chinese Communist Party waged armed struggle for twenty-two years, fighting to the death against reactionary armies, and ultimately won the hearts of the people, the will of the people, and the strength of the people, founding a republic in which the people are masters of their own country.

Today, although the form of warfare is evolving toward informatization and intelligentization (信息化、智能化), the essential nature of the army — that its "foundation lies in the people" — has not changed, and people's war will manifest in new forms. For example, practitioners in fields such as artificial intelligence and cybersecurity will be rapidly incorporated into the wartime mobilization system, driving deep coupling between military requirements and social development, and transforming the extraordinary creativity of the people into an important factor for victory in modern warfare.

(IV)

Looking far ahead through history's "telescope," on the road of advance, the answer of victory is clear and resolute.

After the founding of New China, tempered by the War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea, the People's Liberation Army began its march toward modernization. In peacetime, everything in army-building — the internal and external environment, education and training, logistical support, equipment development — has been changing; yet two cornerstones have never been loosened by the passage of time: the Party commanding the gun, rooted in fundamental institutions, and serving the people, flowing in the blood of officers and soldiers.

Facing the hard requirements of modern warfare for efficient command, the Regulations on Political Work of the Chinese People's Liberation Army (Draft), promulgated in 1954, established in regulatory form the principle of the Party's absolute leadership over the army. Since the 18th National Congress of the Party, the Party Central Committee, the Central Military Commission, and Chairman Xi have, through a series of institutional designs and arrangements, driven the implementation of the system of CMC Chairman responsibility to become strict and substantive, making this leadership system the fundamental form for upholding the Party's absolute leadership over the People's Army — occupying the highest level and holding the commanding position within the entire institutional system of Party leadership over the army. When the curtain rose on military reform in 2015 — restructuring the command system, transferring and repositioning units, with unprecedented operational difficulty — because the top-level design and efficient leadership of "the CMC exercises overall management, theater commands focus on warfighting, and the services focus on building" was upheld, the People's Army not only "slimmed down" but also "strengthened its bones": new-type combat forces emerged as the times required, the joint operations system took full shape, and the People's Army achieved a complete renewal of its institutional structure, organizational structure, overall configuration, and overall appearance.

The grassroots frontline is also the main battleground for transmitting the army's soul. The "Red First Company" of a certain Army brigade has undergone dozens of organizational adjustments, equipment upgrades, and relocations, yet has consistently emphasized the role of the Party branch as a fighting fortress. Facing the difficulties of mastering new equipment, integrating new tactics, and fitting into new systems, the Party branch took the lead in tackling hard problems, Party member cadres charged to the front, and led the entire company's officers and soldiers to rapidly master informatized combat skills; the company was the first to complete the transition from mechanization to informatization.

All the transformation and strengthening on the road to a strong army ultimately serves to benefit the people. From the era of mule-and-horse transport — defending the borders — to the information age — disaster relief and rescue — the more advanced the People's Army's equipment and the stronger its capabilities, the greater its confidence in protecting the people. During the 2020 flood-fighting and rescue operations, the Eastern Theater Command Army's "Linfen Brigade," which had just completed its reform and reshaping, moved immediately upon receiving orders, rushing from the training ground to the front lines of flood fighting along the Huai and Yangtze Rivers; modern rescue equipment, rapid mobility and delivery capabilities, and scientific coordinated operational modes dramatically improved rescue efficiency. In January 2025, a magnitude 6.8 earthquake struck Dingri County, Tibet; a certain regiment's rescue force closest to the epicenter arrived at the disaster area in just thirty-five minutes. Such rapid response not only validated the efficiency of the modern command system but also tested the officers' and soldiers' conscious fulfillment of their duties. Whenever the people are in distress, the sons and daughters of the army will charge to the front.

The founding aspiration of Communists is the founding aspiration of the People's Army. The prolonged peaceful environment and the temptations of abundant material goods have caused a small number of people to become corrupt and degenerate through their failure to hold fast to the founding aspiration. To ensure that the Party's founding aspiration is carried through to the end within the army, since the 18th National Congress of the Party, the Party Central Committee, the Central Military Commission, and Chairman Xi have vigorously pursued comprehensive and strict governance of the Party and comprehensive and strict governance of the army, steadfastly advancing political rectification; the People's Army has undergone revolutionary tempering in the struggle against corruption and has won the historical initiative in building a strong army and rejuvenating the army.

Surveying the vast course of our army's modernization and reform-driven strengthening, the iteration of equipment and the leap in combat power are the outward transformation; obeying Party command and serving the people are the eternal inner constants — ensuring that no matter how equipment is upgraded or how the battlefield changes, the army always knows clearly where it came from and for whom it fights.

When the J-20 fighter jet streaks across the sky, when carrier battle groups sail toward the deep blue, when soldiers of the Lei Feng Squad finish training and immediately send tuition money to children in mountain areas, when the words of the martyred Chen Xiangrong — "pure love, only for China" — become the heartfelt cry of countless young people, we are all the more convinced: the army's soul of obeying Party command, and the roots sunk deep in the people, are the underlying logic by which our army can fight and win battles and comprehensively build a world-class military.

上世纪20年代初，怀揣滚烫初心的中国共产党在国家内忧外患中诞生，开始了团结带领中国人民争取民族独立和人民解放的艰苦斗争。第一次国内革命战争时期，尚处幼年的共产党在同国民党合作中初涉军事问题，包括参与创办黄埔军校，在国民革命军中创设和领导政治工作，培养自己的军事干部，掌握部分革命武装，同时积极组建工农武装，参加北伐战争实际领导政治工作等。 风云突变。1927年，蒋介石发动四一二反革命政变，大肆屠杀共产党人，短短一年间被杀害的共产党员和革命群众达31万余人，轰轰烈烈的大革命被断送。血的教训让共产党人认识了一个真理——“政权是由枪杆子中取得的”。 从1927年8月到1928年上半年，中国共产党在全国范围内组织领导了近百次武装起义。其中，南昌起义打响武装反抗国民党反动派的第一枪，标志着中国共产党独立领导革命战争、创建人民军队和武装夺取政权的开端；“军叫工农革命，旗号镰刀斧头”，秋收起义第一次在武装斗争中公开打出中国共产党的旗帜；广州起义则直接宣告成立广州苏维埃政府。 中国共产党的初心投射在军事问题上，就是创建革命军队，为人民打天下。它像暗夜里的一道光，穿透迷雾，照亮前程，唤醒无数人对未来的憧憬。 南昌起义的领导者多为旧军队中的高级将领，他们不满社会黑暗，不恋高官厚禄，一心向往革命，朱德甚至远赴欧洲追寻共产党。南昌起义失败后，部队在三河坝分兵，朱德率部担任后卫，掩护主力南下。激战中部队伤亡过半，又传来主力在潮汕失利的消息。军情危急，军心涣散。朱德挺身而出，稳住军心，斩钉截铁地说，黑暗是暂时的，要革命的跟我走，最后胜利一定是我们的。他将仅剩的800余人重新整编带上井冈山，实现了与毛泽东领导的秋收起义部队的伟大会师。 南昌起义残存的兵力，成为人民军队建军的中流砥柱；被党的初心点燃的革命火种，化作这支军队刻进骨子里的忠诚：忠于革命忠于党，忠于人民是本色。 （二） 拿起历史的“望远镜”观察，97年前的古田，是人民军队自我重塑的关键拐点。 “回过头来看，古田会议使我们这支军队实现了浴火重生、凤凰涅槃。”2014年10月31日，习主席在全军政治工作会议上发表了重要讲话。会议在古田会议旧址召开——站在新型人民军队定型的地方回顾与前瞻，更加引人深思。如何把理想信念在全军牢固立起来，把党性原则在全军牢固立起来，把战斗力标准在全军牢固立起来，把政治工作威信在全军牢固立起来，成为新时代军队建设的重大课题。 浏览中国军事史，自春秋战国以降，逐鹿中原的战争遍及历朝历代，经典战例、传奇战将层出不穷。论战力，秦国几代打造的虎狼之师，助力实现了“秦王扫六合”的华夏一统，卫青、霍去病统领的西汉铁骑，深入大漠直捣匈奴王庭；论治军，汉代周亚夫屯军细柳，军纪严明，皇帝入营也要遵守军规，唐诗“柳营高压汉宫春”，又借此典赞赏诸葛亮治军有方；论忠勇，北宋的杨家将，南宋的岳家军，明代的戚家军，勇敢抵御外侮，誓死保家卫国，令人敬重。然而，多少威武强大的军旅，纵然青史留名，终不免退出历史舞台。 人民军队横空出世，其全新的建军理念和治军方式是对传统军队的彻底颠覆。 1927年9月29日，毛泽东率领秋收起义部队到达江西省永新县三湾村时，对不足千人的部队进行改编：建立党的各级组织和党代表制度，做到连有党支部，班排有党小组，连以上设党代表，营、团设党委；同时成立各级士兵委员会，实行民主制度，实现官兵平等，史称“三湾改编”。 两年后，即1929年12月在福建上杭召开的古田会议，是我党我军发展史上又一座里程碑。那时，创建不久的红军相当弱小，成分复杂，思想纷纭，面对反动势力的包围，急需纠正和克服各种错误思想。会议通过的《中国共产党红军第四军第九次代表大会决议案》郑重宣告：中国的红军是一个执行革命的政治任务的武装集团。“红军的打仗，不是单纯地为了打仗而打仗，而是为了宣传群众、组织群众、武装群众，并帮助群众建设革命政权才去打仗的”。 三湾改编和古田会议确立的人民军队建设的一系列基本原则，为思想建党和政治建军奠定了基础，形成人民军队区别于一切旧军队的政治特质和根本优势。 就政治属性而言，红军不是维护任何个人的私人武装，而是听党指挥执行革命政治任务的武装集团；就价值取向而言，加入这支军队的官兵，不再以“当兵吃粮”为谋生手段，而是以服务人民为宗旨，为了人民利益不惜牺牲自己的生命；就内部管理而言，红军以严格的纪律约束对外秋毫无犯，对内实行政治民主、经济民主和军事民主，废除打骂体罚，与旧军队那种“匪过如梳，兵过如篦”的形象判若云泥。 以长征为例。作为一次伟大的军事远征和人类精神的英雄史诗，长征令无数人感佩。张学良晚年在其“口述历史”中也谈到这一点，“几万里长征，被包围，没有吃的，那么苦哇，可是他（们）还在一块，被打散了，他（们）还回来。当然了，他（们）的领导是共产党啊”。张学良曾是带兵人，他常对部下说：“这万里长征，你们谁能带，谁能把军队带成这个样子？带得什么情况下都跟你走？如果换成我们，还不早就把兵都带没了？” 红军带兵的密码，其实就是毛泽东说的：“红军之所以艰难奋战而不溃散，‘支部建在连上’是一个重要原因。”长征途中，红军总政治部多次发文，强调连队必须健全党支部，党员在危难关头发挥模范带头作用。红四方面军红10师第28团3连过草地时，有29名伤病员掉队陷入绝境。副连长李玉胜收拢人员，在篝火旁成立临时党支部，任支部书记。他带领大家开展互助，白天赶路挖野菜，夜晚燃起篝火抱团取暖，最终全员走出草地，赶上大部队。 党的领导在军队基层落地开花，使党的初心化为全体官兵的理想信念。 有了崇高的理想信念，就有了为谁当兵为谁打仗的人生目标。回顾军史，每到革命的重要关头，党特别强调军队要把握坚定正确的政治方向。从延安整风纯洁思想统一意志，到解放战争中开展新式整军运动，莫不如此。具体落实到以正确思想教育人改造人上，人民军队就像大熔炉，把无数农民、旧军人、俘虏兵转化成英勇的革命战士。解放战争中被俘的成百万国民党军官兵，经过教育很快实现转变，加入为人民打天下的战斗。为啥？新旧军队两重天，人生目标不一样。 有了崇高的理想信念，就有了坚决执行革命纪律的高度自觉。当年毛泽东站在井冈山“雷打石”上宣布的三项纪律，逐渐完善成“三大纪律八项注意”，成为延续至今的治军铁律。解放战争打锦州，部队行军路过果园，虽饥渴难耐却没有一人摘果子。在战士们看来，擅自吃老百姓的果子是卑鄙的，不吃才是高尚的。抗美援朝五圣山，年轻战士邱少云执行潜伏任务时，周边杂草被敌燃烧弹引燃，为了不暴露部队目标，他强忍烈焰焚身痛苦岿然不动，成为在烈火中永生的钢铁战士。 有了崇高的理想信念，就有了一不怕苦二不怕死的战斗精神。人民军队的光荣战史，是无数英模用鲜血和生命连缀起来的。仅抗美援朝战争中，志愿军就涌现出特级英雄2名，特等功臣236名，一级英雄51名，二级英雄270名，一级模范4名，二级模范83名。血写的战史回荡着奋斗、牺牲、再奋斗直至胜利的英雄交响。那些有名的、无名的、活着的、牺牲的英模，像天上的星星，看得见，数不清。正是他们的存在，战争年代的沉沉夜空才有了星光璀璨的壮丽。 （三） 拿起历史的“望远镜”细寻，我们看见一件文物里藏着的绵长初心。 一方白色的手帕上，几行字迹让人瞬间泪目——那是1950年6月某团赠给复员战友的一句话：“希望你们今后积极参加生产建设为人民服务到底”。细品这看似不起眼的文物，我们可以掂量出它沉甸甸的历史分量：为人民服务的宗旨教育贯穿官兵的军旅生涯，影响着他们的一生一世。 是的，人民军队爱人民、为人民，不仅真心实意依靠人民、全心全意服务人民，而且坚守人民创造历史的理念，实行了一系列人民战争的战略战术，使人民战争的伟力得以充分释放。 这伟力产生于正确的方针指引。早在初创时期，红军就提出“分兵以发动群众，集中以应付敌人”，确立与运用带游击性的“敌进我退，敌驻我扰，敌疲我打，敌退我追”战略战术，从敌强我弱的实际出发，依靠群众与敌周旋。抗日战争和解放战争时期，我军更是将人民战争的战略战术运用到极致。为了持久抗战，我党实行“十大政策”，其中精兵简政、拥政爱民、发展生产、“三三制”、减租减息等，都是着眼民生，为减轻人民负担、赢得民众支持而制定的。正如毛泽东在《论持久战》中揭示的：“战争的伟力之最深厚的根源，存在于民众之中”“动员了全国的老百姓，就造成了陷敌于灭顶之灾的汪洋大海”。 这伟力来源于人民的无私奉献。人民是谁？无数催人泪下的故事告诉我们，人民是那个在丈夫随红军远征后苦苦等待，并且每年做一双军鞋，辞世时留下75双军鞋的苏区军嫂；人民是那些用自己的乳汁滋养八路军伤员、用自己的孩子从敌人刀下换回八路军后代的“沂蒙红嫂”和“太行奶娘”；人民是那些为粉碎日寇“扫荡”而坚壁清野，让敌人没吃没喝精神崩溃的山野村民；人民还是那些把最后的亲骨肉送去上战场，把最后的一碗米交出充军粮，把最后的一尺布用来做军装，把最后的老棉袄盖在担架上的普通百姓……一句话，千千万万毁家纾难、倾其所有，为了革命不惜做出重大牺牲的人们，写出顶天立地的两个字：人民！ 这伟力创造出亘古的战争奇观。抗日战争中，我党之所以成为中流砥柱，因为基座是人民。 当人民之海怒涛翻卷时，敌后军民创造的地道战、地雷战、麻雀战、水上游击战、交通破袭战等等，将人民战争演绎得出神入化。解放战争中，人民与军队结伴，从后方生产到运输物资，从抢修道路到救治伤员，从打扫战场到看押俘虏，解放军打到哪里，人民群众就支援到哪里。仅以辽沈、淮海、平津三大战役为例，解放军总计动员民工880余万人次，人民群众出动大小车辆141万辆，担架36万余副，牲畜260余万头，贡献粮食4.25亿公斤。一位山东民工带领小车队从莱阳县出发，随军转战，跑遍淮海战场，行程5000多里，每到一地，他就把地名刻在随身携带的小竹竿上，最终，那竹竿留下山东、江苏、安徽3个省88个城镇和村庄的名称。难怪陈毅感叹：淮海战役的胜利是人民群众用小车推出来的。 革命老区冀鲁边抗日根据地，本是无险可守的大平原，却凭着夏季广袤的青纱帐和冬季老乡家的热炕头，养育了一批又一批人民子弟兵。因应解放战争形势发展需要，许多部队整编，大批青年参军，陆续有十几万人被编入东北、华东、中原、西北各野战军等部队，辞别故土跨区机动，转战于长城内外，战斗在大江南北。他们就像在冀鲁边区这片群众基础深厚的大地上扎根生长的大树，栉风沐雨，历经寒暑，蓬蓬勃勃地开枝散叶，浓郁的绿荫向白山黑水、海岛椰林、东部沿海、西陲雪域铺展。 从创建人民军队到新中国诞生，中国共产党开展武装斗争22年，与反动军队殊死较量，最终赢得民心所向、民意所归、民力所聚，开创了人民当家作主的共和国。 今天，虽然战争形态正向信息化、智能化演进，但军队“根基在人民”的本质没有变，人民战争将会以新的形态呈现。比如，人工智能、网络安全等领域的从业者会快速纳入战时动员体系，推动军事需求与社会发展的深度耦合，把人民群众的非凡创造力转化为打赢现代战争的重要制胜因素。 （四） 拿起历史的“望远镜”远眺，前进征途上，胜利的答案清晰而坚定。 新中国成立后，经历抗美援朝战争的洗礼，人民解放军开始迈向现代化。和平年代，军队建设的内外环境、教育训练、后勤保障、装备发展等，一切都在变，然而有两块基石从未因岁月冲刷而松动，即根植于基本制度的党指挥枪和流淌于官兵血脉的为人民服务。 面对现代战争对高效指挥的硬性要求，1954年颁布的《中国人民解放军政治工作条例（草案）》以法规形式确立了党对军队绝对领导的原则。党的十八大以来，党中央、中央军委和习主席通过一系列体制设计和制度安排，推动贯彻军委主席负责制严起来、实起来，使这一领导体制成为坚持党对人民军队绝对领导的根本实现形式，在党领导军队的一整套制度体系中处于最高层次、居于统领地位。2015年军队改革大幕开启，指挥体系重构，部队转隶移防，工作难度前所未有。然而，由于坚持“军委管总、战区主战、军种主建”的顶层设计和高效领导，人民军队不但“瘦了身”，更加“强了骨”，新型作战力量应运而生，联合作战体系全面成型，人民军队体制一新、结构一新、格局一新、面貌一新。 基层一线更是军魂传承的主阵地。陆军某旅“红一连”，历经数十次编制调整、装备更新、移防换址，始终注重发挥党支部的战斗堡垒作用。面对新装备操作难、新战法磨合难、新体系融入难的困境，党支部带头攻坚，党员干部冲锋在前，带领全连官兵快速掌握信息化作战技能，连队率先完成机械化向信息化转型。 强军征程上的一切变革与强大，最终都是为了造福人民。从骡马化时代的卫国戍边，到信息化时代的抢险救灾，人民军队装备越先进、能力越强大，守护人民的底气就越充足。2020年抗洪抢险中，刚刚完成改革重塑的东部战区陆军“临汾旅”闻令即动，从训练场紧急奔赴江淮抗洪一线，现代化的抢险装备，迅捷的机动投送能力，科学的协同作战模式，让救援效率大幅提升。2025年1月，西藏定日县发生6.8级地震，距震中最近的某团救援力量仅用35分钟就抵达灾区。如此快反不仅验证了现代指挥系统的高效，更检验了官兵履职尽责的自觉。只要人民有难，子弟兵必冲锋在前。 共产党人的初心就是人民军队的初心。长期的和平环境和纷繁的物质诱惑，让少数人因守不住初心而腐化变质。为确保党的初心在军队中贯彻到底，党的十八大以来，党中央、中央军委和习主席狠抓全面从严治党、全面从严治军，坚定不移推进政治整训，人民军队在反腐败斗争中经受革命性锻造，赢得了强军兴军历史主动。 纵观我军现代化建设与改革强军的壮阔历程，装备迭代、战力跃升是外在嬗变，听党指挥、服务人民是内在永恒，从而确保无论装备如何升级、战场如何变换，都清楚自己从何而来、为谁而战。 当歼-20战机掠过天际，当航母编队驶向深蓝，当雷锋班的战士结束训练又马上给山区的孩子寄去学费，当陈祥榕烈士“清澈的爱，只为中国”成为无数青年的心声时，我们更加坚信，听党指挥的军魂，扎根人民的根脉，是我军能打仗、打胜仗，全面建成世界一流军队的底层逻辑。
