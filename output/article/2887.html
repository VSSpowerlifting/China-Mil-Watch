An "Off-Topic" Professional Tutoring Session Inspires Young Cadets to Press Forward on the Road to Strengthening the Military

一堂“偏题”的专业辅导课，激励青年学员在强军路上奋勇前行
PLA Daily (解放军报) 4 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Two veteran squad leaders, Zhang Jianzhou and Yu Xingwang, conducted a communications equipment tutoring session for cadets at the People's Armed Police Coast Guard Academy, using personal accounts from operational service—including a far-sea fault-repair mission in heavy weather—to address low motivation among cadets assigned to communications specialties. The article documents a recurring institutional problem the PAP frames as a political-work challenge: technical trainees who see communications billets as unglamorous and lose "inner motivation" (内在动力 is the implied concern, though the article uses plain language rather than the term). The framing points to the PAP's preference for embedding ideological cultivation inside professional instruction rather than delivering it as separate political education, using the 千里眼、顺风耳 formulation to dignify the communications role rather than mandate enthusiasm for it.
Translation

Not long ago, a professional tutoring session on the theme of "Communications Equipment Hands-On Operation and Fault Diagnosis" was successfully held in the study room of a certain battalion at the People's Armed Police Coast Guard Academy. The two veteran squad leaders invited to instruct, Zhang Jianzhou and Yu Xingwang, are regarded by officers and soldiers as living encyclopedias of communications.

In the classroom, the two squad leaders followed the established plan and imparted to the cadets the valuable experience accumulated over years of practical operations, covering topics such as hands-on techniques for communications equipment, handling of common faults, and professional skills development. But as the instruction progressed, the topics shared gradually drifted off the preset "track."

"Everyone, look—this is my 'old comrade-in-arms' from when I first enlisted." Squad Leader Zhang Jianzhou held up an equipment model bearing the marks of years of use, his fingertips gently tracing the worn casing, his words full of warmth. In the past, communications equipment was heavy and cumbersome; a minor circuit contact failure could cause an information blackout. But he and his comrades overcame every difficulty, devoted themselves to studying the equipment, and successfully completed every support mission. Squad Leader Zhang, while marveling at the pace of equipment development, carefully introduced to the cadets the circuit principles, parameter calibration, and operating techniques of new-type equipment.

Professional knowledge woven into real stories brought dry principles to life. As the discussion deepened, Zhang Jianzhou spoke about his feelings after more than 30 years of military service. "When I first enlisted, I also had thoughts of 'switching positions,'" Zhang Jianzhou admitted frankly. At first, he felt that the communications post meant spending every day with circuits, keys, and code—no surging blood of charging into battle, no heart-pounding real combat confrontations—and over time, he lacked inner motivation.

It was not until an emergency support mission that Zhang Jianzhou, drawing on his proficient professional skills, cleared a communications fault at a critical moment and won precious time for the mission's successful completion. In that instant, he suddenly understood the mission of the communications soldier as the "eyes that see a thousand li, ears that hear with the wind" (千里眼、顺风耳)—on a battlefield without gunsmoke, communications is the lifeline. "Doing one's job, loving one's job, mastering one's job—this is not a slogan but a day-after-day perseverance, a pursuit of continuous improvement." The veteran squad leader's words were plain and unadorned, yet thought-provoking.

"In communications work, a hair's breadth of error leads to a thousand li of divergence." The other veteran squad leader, Yu Xingwang, picked up the thread, pointing to the communications circuit diagram on the screen and speaking in a grave tone: "A single keystroke misoperation, a deviation in one set of data, damage to one circuit line—any of these could affect the entire mission, and may even bear on the safety of comrades."

Yu Xingwang recalled that one year, while executing a far-sea support mission, the communications equipment suffered a sudden fault and the signal was cut off. He and his comrades, braving howling winds and enormous waves, traced the fault in a pitching ship's cabin—fingers cut by circuits, foreheads bruised by equipment—and never gave up, ultimately restoring communications within the allotted time. "At that moment I understood that love for one's post and loyalty to one's mission can sustain us through adversity and enable us to break through in the face of challenges."

The atmosphere in the classroom quietly shifted. The cadets put down their pens and looked up to listen intently. Their eyes moved from initial traces of puzzlement, slowly to focused attention and being moved, and then to resolve. There was no hollow preaching, no forced indoctrination. The veteran squad leaders used their own personal experiences of growing from ordinary soldiers into professional backbone personnel to guide the young cadets toward a correct understanding of their specialty, their posts, and their own growth.

Time passed without anyone noticing. This "off-track" professional session not only never fell flat but left the cadets wanting more. After the bell rang to end class, everyone continued to crowd around the two veteran squad leaders with unending questions.

"The veteran squad leaders' instruction goes deeper into the heart and is more satisfying than pure technical explanation—it grasps the very essence of educating people," the battalion leadership stated frankly. Professional instruction must not only impart solid skills but must also cultivate firm conviction. Without firm conviction and love for one's post, even the most refined technique is difficult to bring into full effect.

As the sun set in the west, its afterglow spread across the garrison. The cadets walked out of the study room and strode with firm steps toward the training ground. The veteran squad leaders' words had planted in the young cadets' hearts the seeds of steadfast commitment and a sense of responsibility, inspiring them to press forward with courage on the road to strengthening the military.

Original Chinese
前不久，一堂主题为“通信装备实操与故障排查”的专业辅导课，在武警海警学院某大队学习室内顺利进行。这次受邀前来授课的张建洲、俞兴旺两位老班长，是官兵心中响当当的“通信活字典”。 课堂上，两位班长按照既定计划，围绕通信装备实操技巧、常见故障处置、专业技能提升等内容，把多年实战积累的宝贵经验传授给学员们。可随着授课推进，分享的主题逐渐偏离了预设的“轨道”。 “大家看，这是我刚入伍时的‘老战友’。”张建洲班长手持一台布满岁月痕迹的装备模型，指尖轻轻摩挲着斑驳的外壳，话语里满是温情。过去通信设备笨重繁琐，一个微小的线路接触不良，都可能导致信息中断，但他和战友们克服各种困难潜心研究装备，顺利完成每一次保障任务。张班长一边感慨装备发展速度，一边向学员认真介绍新型装备的线路原理、参数调试和使用技巧。 专业知识穿插在真实故事里，令枯燥的原理生动起来。随着话题愈发深入，张建洲谈起他30余年军旅生涯的感受。“刚入伍时，我也有过‘换岗位’的念头。”张建洲坦言，起初他觉得通信岗位整日与线路、按键、代码为伴，没有冲锋陷阵的热血澎湃，没有惊心动魄的实战对决，久而久之，内心缺乏动力。 直到一次紧急保障任务，张建洲凭借熟练的专业技能，在关键时刻排除通信故障，为任务圆满完成赢得宝贵时间。那一刻，他忽然理解了通信兵“千里眼、顺风耳”的使命——在没有硝烟的战场上，通信就是生命线。“干一行、爱一行、精一行，不是一句口号，而是日复一日的坚守，是精益求精的追求。”老班长的话语朴实无华，却发人深思。 “通信工作，差之毫厘，谬以千里。”另一位老班长俞兴旺接过话茬，指着屏幕上的通信线路图语气严肃地说，“一个按键的误操作、一组数据的偏差、一条线路的破损，都可能影响任务全局，甚至关乎战友安危。” 俞兴旺回忆，有一年执行远海保障任务，通信设备突发故障，信号中断。他和战友们顶着狂风巨浪，在颠簸的船舱里排查故障，手指被线路划破、额头被设备撞出淤青也不放弃，最终在规定时间内恢复了通信。“那时我明白了，对岗位的热爱、对使命的忠诚，能让我们在困境中坚守，在挑战中突破。” 课堂氛围悄然转变，学员们放下手中的笔，抬头凝神倾听。他们的眼神从最初的些许疑惑，慢慢变为专注、动容，再到坚定。没有空洞的说教，没有生硬的灌输，老班长们用自己从普通一兵成长为专业骨干的亲身经历，引导年轻学员正确看待专业、看待岗位、看待成长。 时间在不知不觉中过去，这堂“跑偏”了的专业课，非但没有出现冷场，反而让学员们意犹未尽。下课铃响起后，大家依然围着两位老班长追问不停。 “老班长的授课，比单纯的技术讲解更入心、更解渴，恰恰抓住了育人的根本。”该大队领导坦言，专业教学不仅要传授过硬技能，更要培育坚定信念。没有坚定的信念和对岗位的热爱，再精湛的技术也难以发挥效能。 夕阳西下，余晖洒满营区。学员们走出学习室，步履坚定地走向训练场。老班长的话语，在青年学员心中种下了坚守与担当的种子，激励他们在强军路上奋勇前行。
