Correct Outlook on Achievements, Practiced in the Barracks | The "Pneumatic Drill" Passed Down to the Training Ground

正确政绩观 践行在军营丨“风钻”传到训练场

View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A company of the 4th Detachment, PAP Chongqing General Corps, has spent six consecutive years building combat casualty care proficiency, progressing from a zero score at a 'Devil Week' assessment in late 2020 to a General Corps–level first-place finish in 2024 using new rapid first-aid and vital-signs monitoring equipment. The article documents the company's use of a successive-commander sign-off system on a running 'capability advancement chart' (能力进阶图) to institutionalize training continuity across leadership rotations—a concrete mechanism for the problem of short-term achievement-seeking (显功 over 潜功) that PAP political work has been targeting. The framing, published under a campaign on 'correct outlook on achievements' (正确政绩观), points to persistent institutional pressure on grassroots commanders to show quick visible results rather than sustain unglamorous long-cycle training.

"The Pneumatic Drill" Passed Down to the Training Ground

■ Cao Wei, PLA Daily Special Correspondent Zhang Yongqing

Illustration: Fang Han

The Wujiang River roars; the Wuling Mountains stand tall. Deep in the mountains, a massive cave lies hidden among lush greenery.

In the 1960s, the state launched the Third Front Construction (三线建设), laying out a nuclear industrial system in the deep mountains of the southwest. It was decided that at a concealed location in Fuling, Chongqing—backed by mountains and adjacent to the Wujiang River—a strategic defense project would be built, codenamed "816." At a single order, tens of thousands of builders converged from every corner of the country, concealed their identities, and toiled for 18 years, carving into the deep rock strata a spiritual landmark of "doing only unseen work, seeking no fame."

Today, the place where those pioneers gave their youth has become the "816" Project ruins site, a tourist destination that visitors flock to; a pneumatic drill once used by the builders stands quietly in a display case, silently recounting to visitors that era of burning passion.

Sixty years have passed in the blink of an eye. Atop the mountain, officers and soldiers of a certain company of the 4th Detachment, China People's Armed Police Force Chongqing General Corps, have taken up the invisible "pneumatic drill" and continue writing the story of hard-fought breakthroughs on the training ground.

The "hard bone" (硬骨头) that this company has been gnawing at for six consecutive years stems from a course that seems unremarkable at first glance: combat casualty care. At the end of 2020, then-Deputy Company Commander Chen Shouren led a team to participate in "Devil Week" extreme training and was scored zero in the combat casualty care event for non-standard operations.

This failure cut deep into the participating officers and soldiers. At the after-action review, Chen Shouren produced a blank sheet of paper and analyzed the causes person by person and item by item against the outline standards, formulating corrective measures. That densely written sheet of paper became the starting point for the company's combat casualty care "capability advancement chart" (能力进阶图).

At just that time, the detachment planned to roll out standardized combat casualty care training across grassroots companies. After the notice was issued, Chen Shouren, drawing on the lessons of the competition defeat, set standards for the officers and soldiers that far exceeded the outline requirements. Word spread, and the officers and soldiers erupted. Some felt, "We're not medical personnel—training to a rough standard is good enough." Others thought, "The outline doesn't even demand this much; why make things hard for ourselves?" Still others questioned, "In peacetime, what use is there for combat casualty care skills?"…

Faced with these differing voices, Chen Shouren was resolute: "Train well and train hard—that's the only way to save lives when it counts." He led the company's officers and soldiers into the "816" Project ruins, pointed to the dense drill marks on the rock walls, and told everyone with feeling: "Faced with hard granite and dangerous rockfall zones, the builders of that era set no conditions, waited on no one, and hollowed out an entire mountain, in exchange for national security and the people's peace. On the battlefield, failing to save a wounded comrade because training was cut short in peacetime is the greatest betrayal of the mission."

Those heartfelt words, like that pneumatic drill, bored deep into the hearts of the officers and soldiers. In the training that followed, everyone's enthusiasm surged, and training scores rose accordingly. By the end of 2021, all of the company's combat casualty care scores had reached "Good" or above.

"Our secret is just two words: hard work (刻苦)." Then-Platoon Leader Chen Shunpeng said that during the sprint period before the assessment, for the single action of hemorrhage control and bandaging alone, everyone practiced more than a hundred times a day, finally achieving the ability to "precisely locate in darkness and operate to standard in low light."

When the results were announced, the company ranked at the top in the detachment assessment. The outstanding results were a vivid lesson in education on the correct outlook on achievements (政绩观)—outline standards are the floor, winning is the high bar of combat effectiveness, and training military skills to the utmost is genuine achievement.

Deep inside the "816" Project tunnel, every meter the pneumatic drill advanced was an accumulation of quantitative change breaking through to qualitative change (量变向质变突破). The company's path of refinement on the training ground is likewise a quiet perseverance in doing unseen work (潜功).

In 2023, when Chen Shouren was transferred away from the company, the company's combat casualty care training scores had already been firmly at the top of the detachment. Many officers and soldiers felt that "this subject can be 'closed out'" and "as long as we maintain our current state, other units will find it hard to surpass us"…

Noticing that the officers and soldiers' thinking was beginning to slide, then-Company Political Instructor Wu Yanbin led everyone into the detachment history museum to revisit the "unfinished" stories of the "816" Project: the project ultimately never went into production, and the tens of thousands of builders who fought hard for 18 years left behind not a single "meritorious service stele." "Those 18 years of construction were not wasted, because the mountain had been bored through and the strategic foundation had been built up. This is like our approach to training—reaching the standard on one subject does not mean the hard fighting is over. Combat effectiveness building has only a present continuous tense, never a completed tense." Wu Yanbin said.

Not long after, the company's "capability advancement chart" received an upgraded version: two sub-courses were added—"rescue in confined spaces" and "rescue under multiple overlapping injuries"—with standards higher and more demanding than the foundational courses.

The previously self-confident officers and soldiers were quickly doused with cold water. During one training session, a rescue team worked for more than 20 minutes without being able to extract the "casualty"—in the confusion, the soldiers had installed the splint backwards. During the after-action review, the company cadres reached a consensus through discussion: "Training cannot remain at the level of 'getting by'; it must be trained to the point of 'holding up under any conditions.'"

For a period after that, the company's officers and soldiers noticed that Chen Shunpeng often shut himself in the study room. Before long, he produced a set of "rapid first-aid procedures under low-light conditions." It turned out that Chen Shunpeng had used his spare time to systematically sort through the various variables that might be encountered under extreme conditions, studying solutions one by one, and refining the procedures down to every single step.

A year later, the company achieved an "all-excellent" score in the confined-space rescue exercise.

Last year, higher authorities issued new-generation rapid first-aid equipment and intelligent monitoring devices, and the company seized the opportunity to propose a new training subject: rapid first-aid and decision-making based on vital signs monitoring. Through repeated simulation training, the officers and soldiers worked out a set of rescue procedures that are fast to respond and quick to operate, and they won first place in the training assessment organized by the General Corps.

"Combat effectiveness building is not shouted into existence—it is 'chiseled' out blow by blow, like the '816' Project." Speaking of the hard-won results, the company's current Political Instructor Liu Qianlong showed this reporter the combat casualty care "capability advancement chart," which not only records in detail the achievements the company has made along the way, but also bears the signatures of successive company commanders.

Over six years, the name of the subject has changed, the training conditions have changed, and the assessment standards have changed—but their determination to overcome difficulties has not changed, and their goal of striving to be first has not changed. Over those six years, company commanders have been rotated one after another; at each handover, the outgoing and incoming commanders jointly sign the "capability advancement chart," completing the relay of the mission. On this "capability advancement chart," there are both subjects marked "completed" and "hard bones" still being worked on. At each handover, detachment leaders say the same thing to the new company commander: "A new official making new achievements is a form of achievement; a new official settling old accounts is also a form of achievement."

In the study and education campaign on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on achievements (正确政绩观), the detachment organized officers and soldiers to once again revisit the story of the "816" Project. Everyone firmly declared: the "pneumatic drill" has been passed down to our generation, and we have only one thing to do—keep our eyes fixed on the "hard bones" of combat effectiveness building and keep drilling, without stopping.

At the end of the interview, as this reporter passed the company training ground, several soldiers were seen sitting together reviewing that day's training. The pneumatic drill of the "816" Project stopped long ago, but the continued struggle of these young soldiers is clearly another kind of charge forward—courageous and resolute, never pausing.

Unseen Work as Foundation, Real Results Reaching Far

■ Zhou Junbing

An old pneumatic drill displayed in a case, a "capability advancement chart" soaked in sweat—these bear witness to the unseen work (潜功) of Third Front Construction builders who concealed their identities, and to the responsibility of officers and soldiers of the new era who have carried on the hard fighting, vividly interpreting for us what a correct outlook on achievements means.

To establish and practice a correct outlook on achievements, one must recognize at a deep ideological level the dialectical relationship between visible achievements (显功) and unseen work (潜功). A towering building, magnificent and imposing—that is visible achievement; a solid foundation, buried deep underground—that is unseen work. If one is impatient for quick results and eager for instant success, prioritizing visible achievement over unseen work and short-term gains over long-term development, it will be impossible to lay a solid foundation, and the so-called towering building can only be a castle in the air with an unstable base.

In those years, tens of thousands of builders took root in the deep mountains for eighteen years, chiseling through mountains to forge a shield without seeking fame, solely to consolidate the strategic barrier of national security and build up the foundation of national development. Today, successive commanders of a certain company of the 4th Detachment, China People's Armed Police Force Chongqing General Corps, have carried on the hard fighting in relay, leading officers and soldiers in deep and meticulous study, continuously achieving improvements in quality and upgrades in capability. Their practice of not seeking visible results and instead cultivating unseen work has set the example of what it looks like to practice a correct outlook on achievements.

True achievement is often the unseen work that accumulates day by day without anyone knowing, the hard skill (硬功夫) that cannot be seen for a time but holds up when it counts. Combat effectiveness building is a systematic project—absolutely not the work of a single morning or evening. We should be like the officers and soldiers of that company: take up the invisible "pneumatic drill" passed down by our predecessors, cast aside the restless and utilitarian mindset, endure the hardship of cultivating unseen work, and keep our eyes fixed on the "hard bones" of combat effectiveness building, drilling and chiseling without stop. Only in this way can the edifice of the cause of strengthening the military (强军事业) have a permanently solid foundation.

“风钻”传到训练场 ■曹 伟 解放军报特约记者 张永清 插图：方 汉 乌江滔滔，武陵巍巍。群山深处，一个巨大的山洞隐没在苍翠之间。 上世纪60年代，国家启动三线建设，在西南深山布局核工业体系，决定在重庆涪陵一处背靠大山、紧邻乌江的隐蔽之地，建设一项战备工程，代号“816”。一声令下，数万名建设者从天南海北汇聚于此，隐姓埋名一干就是18年，在岩层深处刻下“只做潜功、不求扬名”的精神坐标。 如今，当年创业者奉献青春的地方，已变成“816”工程遗址，成为游客争相打卡的旅游胜地；当年建设者使用的一台风钻，静静地陈列在展柜里，向参观者无声诉说着那段激情燃烧的岁月。 一甲子光阴如梭。山顶之上，武警重庆总队执勤第四支队某中队官兵接过无形的“风钻”，在训练场上续写攻坚故事。 该中队持续“啃”了6年的“硬骨头”，源自一个看似不起眼的课目：战伤救护。2020年底，时任副中队长陈首任带队参加“魔鬼周”极限训练，在战伤救护课目中因操作不规范被判零分。 这次失利，深深刺痛了参训官兵。复盘分析会上，陈首任拿出一张白纸，对照大纲标准逐人逐项分析原因，制订改进措施。这张写得密密麻麻的纸，成为该中队战伤救护课目“能力进阶图”的起点。 恰逢此时，支队计划在基层中队推开战伤救护规范化训练。通知下发后，陈首任结合比武失利的教训，给官兵定下了远超大纲要求的标准。消息传出，官兵炸了锅。有人觉得，“我们不是卫勤人员，练个大概就行了”；有人认为，“大纲都没这么高要求，何必跟自己过不去”；还有人质疑，“和平时期，战伤救护技能哪有用武之地”…… 面对不同声音，陈首任态度坚决：“练得好练得硬，关键时刻才能救命。”他带领中队官兵走进“816”工程遗址，指着岩壁上密密麻麻的钻痕，动情地告诉大家：“面对坚硬的花岗岩、危险的落石层，当年的建设者没有讲条件、没有等靠要，把整座山都掏空了，换来了国家的安全、人民的安宁。上了战场，因平时训练打折扣而救不下负伤战友，是对使命最大的辜负。” 一番推心置腹的话语，如同那台风钻，深深扎进官兵心里。此后的训练中，大家热情高涨，训练成绩也随之水涨船高。2021年底，该中队战伤救护课目成绩全部达到“良好”以上。 “我们的秘诀就两个字：刻苦。”时任排长陈顺鹏说，考核冲刺那段时间，仅止血包扎一个动作，大家每天要练上百遍，终于做到了“黑暗中能精准定位、微光下能规范操作”。 成绩公布，该中队在支队考核中名列前茅。亮眼的成绩，是一堂生动的政绩观教育课——大纲标准是底线，打赢才是战斗力的高线，把军事技能练到极致才是真政绩。 “816”工程洞体深处，风钻每钻进一米，都是量变向质变突破的积累。该中队训练场上的精进之路，也是做潜功的默默坚守。 2023年，陈首任调离中队时，该中队战伤救护课目训练成绩已稳居支队前列。不少官兵觉得“这个课题可以‘销号’了”“保持好目前的状态，别的单位很难超越”…… 发现官兵思想出现滑坡的情况，时任中队指导员武彦斌带着大家走进支队史馆，重温“816”工程那些“未完成”的故事：该工程最终没有投产，数万名建设者苦战18年，没留下一块“功臣碑”。“18年的建设并没有白费，因为大山已经凿穿，战略底气已经蓄足。这就像我们抓训练，一个课目达标了，不代表攻坚结束了。战斗力建设只有进行时，没有完成时。”武彦斌说。 不久后，该中队的“能力进阶图”迎来升级版：增加“狭小空间救援”和“多重伤害叠加救援”两个子课目，标准比基础课目更高、更难。 原本自信满满的中队官兵，很快被泼了一盆冷水。一次训练，救援组忙活20多分钟也没能把“伤员”救出来——慌乱中，官兵把固定夹板装反了。复盘时，中队干部经过研讨达成共识：“训练不能停留在‘过得去’层面，而要练到任何条件下都‘过得硬’。” 此后一段时间，中队官兵发现，陈顺鹏经常把自己关在学习室里。很快，他拿出一套“微光条件下快速急救流程”。原来，陈顺鹏利用业余时间，系统梳理了极端条件下可能遇到的各种变量，逐条对应研究解决方案，将流程细化到每一步。 一年后，中队在狭小空间救援演练中取得“全优”成绩。 去年，上级配发了新一代快速急救器材和智能监测装备，该中队顺势提出一项新的训练课题——基于生命体征监测的快速急救与决策。一次次模拟训练，官兵探索出一套响应迅速、操作快捷的救护流程，并在总队组织的训练考核中夺得第一名。 “战斗力建设不是喊出来的，而是像‘816’工程那样，一锤一锤‘凿’出来的。”谈及来之不易的成绩，中队现任指导员刘黔龙给记者展示了那张战伤救护课目“能力进阶图”，上面不仅详细记录了中队一路走来取得的成绩，还有历任中队主官的签名。 6年间，课目名称变了，训练条件变了，考核标准也变了，但他们攻坚克难的决心没变、勇争第一的目标没变。6年来，中队主官先后调整，每一次交接，新老主官都要共同在这张“能力进阶图”上签名，完成使命的接力。这张“能力进阶图”上，既有标注“已完成”的课题，也有还在攻坚的“硬骨头”。每次交接，支队领导都会对新任中队主官说同一句话：“新官新作为是政绩，新官理旧账也是政绩。” 在树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育中，该支队组织官兵再次重温“816”工程的故事。大家坚定表示，“风钻”传到我们这一代人手中，我们要做的只有一件事——紧盯战斗力建设的“硬骨头”，持之以恒“钻”下去。 采访结束，记者路过中队训练场看到，几名战士围坐在一起，复盘当天的训练。“816”工程的风钻早已停转，但这些年轻士兵的接续奋斗，分明是另一种冲锋——勇毅向前，从未停步。 潜功为基 实绩致远 ■周俊冰 一台陈列在展柜里的老旧风钻，一张浸透汗水的“能力进阶图”，见证着三线建设者隐姓埋名的潜功与新时代官兵接续攻坚的担当，为我们生动诠释了何为正确政绩观。 树立和践行正确政绩观，必须在思想深处认清显功与潜功的辩证关系。大厦矗立，雄伟壮观，这是显功；地基牢固，深埋地下，这是潜功。如果急于求成、急功近利，重显功轻潜功、重短期效益轻长远发展，就难以打牢地基，所谓的高楼大厦就只能是根基不牢的空中楼阁。 当年，数万名建设者扎根深山十八载，凿山铸盾不求功名，只为筑牢国家安全战略屏障，蓄足国家发展底气。如今，武警重庆总队执勤第四支队某中队几任主官接力攻坚，带领官兵深钻细研，不断实现素质提升和能力升级。他们不求显绩、深耕潜功的实践，立起了践行正确政绩观应有的样子。 真正的政绩，往往是那些不为人知却日积月累的潜功，是那些一时看不出、关键时刻顶得上的硬功夫。战斗力建设是一项系统工程，绝非一朝一夕之功。我们应像该中队官兵那样，接过前辈无形的“风钻”，摒弃浮躁功利之心，耐住深耕潜功之苦，紧盯战斗力建设的“硬骨头”不停钻、持续凿。唯有如此，强军事业的大厦才能根基永固。
