Fan Didi, Range Survey Soldier and Staff Sergeant Third Class of a Rocket Force Unit — The Party's Order Weighs a Thousand Jun

■ Li Xupeng, PLA Daily Special Correspondent Li Yinghong

On the Gobi Desert in midsummer, surface temperatures approach 60 degrees Celsius. A thunderclap tears open the sky, a missile strikes the target, and smoke and yellow sand surge skyward. Before the shockwave has even dissipated, a line of figures advances rapidly toward the point of impact. At the front of the formation is Fan Didi, range survey soldier and Staff Sergeant Third Class of a Rocket Force unit.

"No birds fly overhead, no grass grows underfoot; a hundred-li no-man's-land where the wind sends stones rolling." This is the desert range where Fan Didi has stood his post for 18 years. As one of the people who works closest to missile impact points, he has long shouldered dangerous and difficult missions including target-point surveying, strike-precision measurement, and dud-missile disposal and neutralization. Deep in the gunsmoke, every advance is a test between life and death.

What has driven him to charge forward again and again? The answer is hidden in an old artillery panoramic telescope Fan Didi has treasured for many years.

Gray-green body, crisscrossing scratches of varying depths — this was the primary piece of equipment Fan Didi used when he first arrived at the range. Over 18 years, the observation of every missile's impact and the assessment of every strike's damage were accomplished through this panoramic telescope. The mottled marks covering it — some left by rock fragments flung from impact points, some worn into the surface by years of Gobi wind and sand — all bear witness to his 18 years of charging toward danger.

Qiu Feiyang, a post-2000 recruit fresh to the unit, has asked Fan Didi more than once: "Sergeant, when you're charging toward the impact point, aren't you really afraid?" Every time he hears this question, Fan Didi smiles and looks toward that panoramic telescope in the storage cabinet. He remembers that when he first took up his post, the blast shockwave left his eardrums numb and his palms soaked with sweat; on nights after disposing of dud missiles, he would be jolted awake by dreams of flying shrapnel, drenched in cold sweat.

"Afraid? How could I not be afraid? The more afraid you are, the more you have to press forward." What transformed timidity into courage was the example set by Senior Sergeant Sheng Dehua. On every mission, Sergeant Sheng always kept the observation position closest to the impact point — the highest-risk position — for himself, and even when flying rock fragments left mark after mark on the telescope body, he never retreated a single step. "Glory lies in hardship. Every step further we push forward, the more precision our measurements gain, and the more confidence we have in winning."

These words quietly took root in Fan Didi's heart. He gradually came to understand that holding the line on the Gobi is never a pointless ordeal; advancing toward danger requires forging a courageous heart, because they are the ones who sustain the sharpness of the great nation's long sword and bear on their shoulders the mission of Communist Party members.

"One point of inaccuracy in the data means one more hidden danger on the future battlefield." In order to precisely capture the moment of impact amid swirling sand and dust, Fan Didi trained his eyesight under the blazing sun and honed his steadiness against the wind and sand, mastering the hard skill of "one look, one accurate reading; one measurement, one definitive result."

During one test-launch mission, electronic imagery remotely assessed the point of impact as within acceptable parameters — but Fan Didi measured a 1.3-meter deviation on the ground. To ensure data accuracy, Fan Didi led his team through three rounds of re-measurement, ultimately overturning the imagery-analysis conclusion and filling a critical gap in equipment performance optimization. Since enlisting, he has mastered the skills of eight different positions, achieved zero errors across more than a hundred mission measurements, and earned recognition among his comrades as the "precision anchor."

This loyal commitment to advancing toward danger requires leading by example. Since becoming a squad leader, Fan Didi has never hoarded his techniques as a "personal trade secret"; he has always sought to pass on every skill he possesses to younger comrades, so that survey soldiers can put down roots on the Gobi.

During one high-altitude survey mission, extreme cold put the equipment at risk of losing power. Squad soldier Fu Yanhui, without a word, took off his cold-weather coat and wrapped it around the instrument, then stood watch himself in a culvert wearing only a thin field uniform. Fan Didi went over to check on him, and Fu Yanhui said: "Sergeant, you always said the data must be absolutely foolproof. Leave this to me — Sergeant, rest easy!" In that moment, Fan Didi knew: the seed of loyalty had taken root and begun to grow in the hearts of the younger generation.

Today, the range has long since been equipped with high-precision laser surveying instruments, and that old artillery panoramic telescope has been relegated to secondary status — yet Fan Didi still polishes it until it gleams and keeps it in the most prominent place in the storage cabinet. In his heart, equipment will iterate and be updated, and the scratches will settle into memory, but the true character of generation after generation of survey soldiers advancing toward danger will never change: the moment the Party gives the order, charge toward the impact point at any time!

Original Chinese
火箭军某部靶场测量兵、三级军士长范迪迪—— 党的号令重千钧 ■李旭鹏 解放军报特约记者 李映虹 盛夏的戈壁滩，地表温度直逼60摄氏度。一声惊雷撕裂长空，导弹命中靶心，烟云裹挟黄沙冲天而起。冲击波尚未散尽，一行身影便朝着爆心迅速挺进。冲在队伍最前面的，是火箭军某部靶场测量兵、三级军士长范迪迪。 “天上无飞鸟，地上不长草；百里无人区，风吹石头跑。”这，就是范迪迪坚守了18年的大漠靶场。作为离导弹爆心最近的人之一，他常年担负目标点位测定、打击精度测量、哑弹销毁排除等险难任务。硝烟深处，每一次挺进都是生与死的考验。 是什么支撑他一次次冲锋在前？答案，藏在范迪迪珍藏多年的一台老式炮队镜中。 灰绿色镜身、深浅交错的划痕，这是范迪迪初入靶场时所用的主战装备，18年来，一枚枚导弹的着靶观测、毁伤研判，都经由这件炮队镜完成。上面一道道斑驳痕迹，有的是爆心飞溅的碎石擦过的印记，有的是戈壁风沙常年打磨的纹路，都是他18年向险而行的冲锋见证。 刚下连的“00后”新兵邱飞扬不止一次问范迪迪：“班长，往爆心冲的时候，您真的不怕吗？”每次听到这个问题，范迪迪总笑着望向内务柜里那台炮队镜。他记得，自己初上战位时，爆炸冲击波震得耳膜发麻，他的手心浸满汗水；处置完哑弹的深夜，他也曾在夜间被弹片飞溅的梦境惊醒，一身冷汗。 “怕！怎么不怕？越怕越要向前。”让胆怯化作果敢的，是老班长盛德华的言传身教。每一次任务，盛班长总把距爆心最近、风险最高的观测位留给自己，哪怕镜身被飞溅的碎石砸出道道痕迹，也不退半步。“光荣在于艰巨，我们多往前闯一步，测量就多一分精度，打赢就多一分底气。” 这番话，悄悄在范迪迪心底扎根。他慢慢明白，戈壁的坚守从不是无谓的苦熬，向险而行需要练就一颗勇敢的心，因为他们托举着大国长剑的锋芒，肩扛着共产党员的使命。 “数据有一点失准，未来战场就多一分隐患。”为了在漫天沙尘中精准捕捉着靶瞬间，范迪迪迎着烈日练眼力、顶着风沙练定力，修成了“一眼准、一测定”的过硬本领。 一次试射任务，电子影像远程研判落点合格，他却实地测出1.3米偏差。为了确保数据精准，范迪迪带队开展三轮复测，最终推翻影像判读结论，为装备性能优化补齐了关键短板。入伍至今，他熟练掌握8个岗位技能，百余次任务测量零差错，成为战友公认的“精度定盘星”。 这种向险而行的忠诚担当，需要以身作则的示范身教。担任班长以来，范迪迪从不把技术攥成“独门绝技”，总想着把一身本事悉数教给年轻战友，让测量兵在戈壁滩扎根。 一次高原测量任务，极寒天气导致设备面临断电风险，班组战士付彦辉二话不说，脱下防寒大衣裹住仪器，自己穿着单薄作训服在涵洞里坚守。范迪迪上前关切，付彦辉说：“班长你说过，数据必须万无一失。这里交给我，班长请放心！”那一刻范迪迪知道，忠诚的种子已在年轻一代心中生根发芽。 如今，靶场早已列装高精度激光测量设备，那台老式炮队镜虽退居二线，却仍被范迪迪擦得锃亮，珍藏在内务柜最显眼的位置。在他心里，装备会迭代更新，划痕会沉淀成记忆，但一代代测量兵向险而行的本色永不改：只要党一声令下，随时向爆心冲锋！
