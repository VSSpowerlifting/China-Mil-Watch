Summary
Private First Class Wei Bangyao, a conscript in a border defense company of an unspecified Army unit stationed in Yunnan's deep mountains, captained a three-person team—including two platoon leaders—to the grand prize (特等奖) in the Army-wide Military Modeling Competition (军事建模竞赛), after Political Instructor Li Jiang overrode rank-convention objections to appoint him team captain. The article documents a specific institutional friction the PLA is actively managing: the tension between hierarchical seniority norms (军衔倒挂, 'inverted rank') and merit-based assignment of technical leadership roles, with Li Jiang's decision framed as a deliberate break from convention rather than standard practice. Wei Bangyao has since been appointed head of his battalion's war gaming (兵棋) group, placing the question of how far this talent-utilization model extends beyond a single political instructor's discretion beyond what the article can answer.

A Private First Class Leads His Team to a Grand Prize

■ PLA Daily Reporter Chen Xiaojie

Wei Bangyao, a private first class in an Army unit, has become a sensation.

The reason: this private first class served as team captain, led his team in the Army-wide Military Modeling Competition, and took home the grand prize (特等奖).

In this border defense company stationed at the edge of Yunnan's deep mountains, mention the name of Private First Class Wei Bangyao and every officer and soldier will sing his praises. Two years ago, Wei Bangyao had just graduated from university. He entered the vibrant military camp with a longing for the armed forces and a resolve to achieve something in the practice of strengthening the military. On the training ground he threw himself into the dirt, drilling hard to master the skills needed to win; in his spare time, the reading room became his "second battlefield."

During his school years, Wei Bangyao had a natural sensitivity to mathematical logic. He proactively broke free from the confines of the classroom, independently exploring mathematics and the field of modeling, and regularly exchanged ideas online with like-minded "modeling enthusiasts." At the time, he never imagined that this passion and accumulated knowledge would one day shine with a distinctive brilliance along the border defense line.

Opportunity always favors the prepared. Last year, the unit received a registration notice for the Army-wide Military Modeling Competition. Political Instructor Li Jiang, having learned of Wei Bangyao's particular skill, contacted him at the first opportunity and told him that if he was willing to compete, he would personally help coordinate the team and build a platform for him.

Immediately, a competition team captained by a private first class was rapidly assembled. During the preparation period, Wei Bangyao and his comrades buried themselves in the company study room—consulting vast amounts of reference material, analyzing professional videos, carefully reading relevant books, and dissecting the difficult points of past competition problems. This steady, step-by-step, solid work style laid a firm foundation for the final victory.

Five days and four nights of fierce competition: a battle of intellect without the smoke of gunfire. In the formal competition, Wei Bangyao led his team in a race against time and a contest against difficult problems, throwing themselves in wholeheartedly, and ultimately completed the modeling task to a high standard, capturing the grand prize in a single stroke.

"Let every officer and soldier have the opportunity to shine in life" — this is a vivid embodiment of this deep-mountain company's philosophy of cultivating talent. The organization built a broad stage for Wei Bangyao to display his special skills and carefully guided him in realizing his youthful dreams; Wei Bangyao, in turn, lived up to expectations, forging himself into steel in the course of the journey to strengthen the military, and growing into a modeling sharpshooter (建模尖兵) who commands the attention of the entire army.

The mountains stand silent, bearing witness to growth. In this deep-mountain garrison, many young soldiers like Wei Bangyao are growing vigorously, carrying their passions and sense of responsibility, transforming through steadfast duty and achieving success through hard work, writing with ordinary perseverance the youthful chapter of border defense officers and soldiers in the new era.

From a Deep-Mountain Sentry Post to the Army-Wide Awards Podium

■ Guan Lei, Wei Xiaolong, PLA Daily Reporter Chen Xiaojie

Soldiers of an Army unit on patrol. Photo by Wu Haoyuan

"I Want to Be a Good Soldier and Do Something Meaningful"

When new recruits were assigned to their companies, Wei Bangyao was placed in the squad of Staff Sergeant First Class (一级上士) Cai Shaolin of an Army unit.

When asked about his first impression of Wei Bangyao, Cai Shaolin said bluntly that he was "a bit worried" —

Gentle eyes, a soft-spoken manner — at first glance, he was far removed from the tough, resolute image of a border defense soldier.

What worried Cai Shaolin even more was that Wei Bangyao's physical fitness baseline was weak. He struggled especially with apparatus training such as the horizontal bar, and his early progress was very slow.

But after spending time together, Cai Shaolin quickly changed his view of Wei Bangyao. During one heart-to-heart talk, Cai Shaolin asked him about his original motivation for enlisting. Wei Bangyao's gaze was resolute: "I want to be a good soldier and do something meaningful."

After graduating from university, Wei Bangyao had for a time fallen into confusion about his life's direction. His father had worked for many years in the construction engineering industry and hoped his son would carry on the family business. But the young Wei Bangyao yearned to set out on a path of passionate striving, to give his youth more weight and more value. Carrying this sincere original aspiration, he resolutely enlisted and headed to the front line of border defense.

In the course of daily training and life, Cai Shaolin also genuinely saw the valuable potential in Wei Bangyao. Although his foundation was weak and his starting point was not high, he was grounded and self-disciplined, willing to endure hardship and able to bear pressure. When he fell behind in training, he proactively used his free time to add extra load and practice; when his movements were not up to standard, he repeatedly refined the details; when he encountered shortcomings or made mistakes, he never shirked or complained, but simply reflected quietly, corrected course, and steadily caught up.

In Wei Bangyao's mind, solidly completing daily training and holding fast to his border defense post were the duty and obligation of every border defense soldier — the foundation of taking root in the military camp and fulfilling one's responsibilities, with an irreplaceable value. Yet on the basis of fulfilling his duties and doing his job well, he always harbored a higher pursuit: to stand on the foundation of the military camp, give full play to his special skills, and ensure that his youth would not be limited to steadfast duty alone, but would truly shine and generate heat, not failing his military service.

This plain yet burning pursuit was something that Political Instructor Li Jiang had also heard about in his conversations with Wei Bangyao.

Last year, Li Jiang was stationed at the company for an extended period, conducting one-on-one heart-to-heart talks with new recruits, surveying their individual special skills, and understanding their aspirations for growth. When the topic of professional skills came up, Wei Bangyao mentioned that he had participated in modeling competitions three times, winning second place in a provincial-level competition.

Having said this, Wei Bangyao felt uneasy inside. The core mission of a border defense company is combat readiness and garrison duty. In his view, special skills like mathematical modeling seemed far removed from the front-line border defense mission and difficult to put into practice. He even privately regretted it, thinking it might have been better to say his major was engineering cost estimation — at least that could contribute something to garrison construction. Wei Bangyao, who was single-mindedly determined to "be a good soldier," was afraid his answer had failed to make a good impression on the political instructor. But what he did not expect was that Li Jiang not only appreciated him greatly, but quietly kept him in mind.

Last year, the unit received notice that an Army-wide Military Modeling Competition would soon be held. Li Jiang contacted the company at the first opportunity and specifically sought out Wei Bangyao's willingness to compete.

In the company office, seeing the familiar characters "modeling" (建模) on the notice, Wei Bangyao's eyes lit up. He felt that the passion he had set aside for so long, the special skill that had lain dormant for so long, had finally found an opportunity to bloom in this deep-mountain garrison.

"It's finally my turn to contribute to the unit using my own special skills." In that moment, Wei Bangyao was absolutely certain.

Wei Bangyao (far left) exchanges insights on military modeling with his comrades. Photo by Wu Haoyuan

"In This Team, Ability Does the Talking"

After receiving Wei Bangyao's enthusiastic response, Li Jiang quickly coordinated and integrated the backbone forces, assembling a competition team within the battalion and designating Wei Bangyao as team captain to lead the team in preparing for the Army-wide Military Modeling Competition.

The team had two other members: Platoon Leader Deng Wenju and Zhang Qinghua.

The move caused a stir. This competition team — led by a conscript (义务兵) commanding two officers — sparked considerable discussion within the battalion. In the eyes of many, the two platoon leaders had the advantage in both age and experience, and many people watched with a cautious, wait-and-see attitude.

On this matter, Li Jiang had a more pragmatic and far-sighted calculation. Although Deng Wenju and Zhang Qinghua had rich positional experience and a solid professional foundation, neither had participated in a modeling competition before and both lacked practical competitive experience. Wei Bangyao, though a conscript, had participated in multiple high-level competitions and had accumulated more mature practical experience.

"In this team, ability does the talking — rank doesn't determine seniority, and military grade doesn't determine who leads." With that, the team established a scientifically clear division of labor to maximize the team's combat effectiveness: Wei Bangyao was responsible for building the overall problem-solving framework and finalizing the core modeling approach; Deng Wenju applied his professional strengths, focusing on program writing, data computation, and technical implementation; Zhang Qinghua, with his meticulous thinking and solid writing skills, was primarily responsible for organizing results, reviewing logic, and drafting the paper.

From the very beginning, Wei Bangyao placed team cohesion at the top of the preparation agenda. With his rich competition experience, he knew well that going it alone rarely succeeds — unified thinking and seamless coordination are the keys to victory on the competition floor.

So, after everyone had completed their initial individual groundwork, Wei Bangyao proposed conducting concentrated joint exercises modeled on the themes of mature civilian modeling competitions, using combat-realistic (实战化) simulations to refine the rhythm of collaboration, unify problem-solving approaches, and address capability shortfalls.

In this process, clashes of ideas and disagreements arose frequently. The three members each spoke from their own professional perspectives, debated with one another, and often reviewed and simulated until the early hours of the morning. Whenever thinking could not be unified, Wei Bangyao would proactively pause the argument, take the lead in sorting out the core logic, break down the key issues, and verify step by step, driving the team to build consensus and set direction.

To ensure that the problem-solving approach was rigorous and correct and that the core formulas were scientifically sound and effective, Wei Bangyao also proactively connected online with experts at civilian universities to seek answers and verification, eliminating cognitive biases and dispelling the team's doubts, so that every step of reasoning and every conclusion could withstand scrutiny.

Through day after day of grinding and hard work, the coordination among the three grew increasingly smooth and tacit, and their mutual trust deepened. Wei Bangyao also proved the capability of this "conscript team captain" through his steady, pragmatic style and his solid, outstanding professional ability.

The day of the competition arrived quickly. The competition problems were closely tied to the realities of training and combat readiness, covering military professional knowledge across multiple domains, with strong comprehensiveness and significant difficulty. The three coordinated closely and divided labor, simultaneously working to quickly grasp the core of the problems and absorb key military theory points, while precisely deriving formulas and constructing the model architecture — the entire process was intense and efficient.

In the middle of the competition, the team hit a bottleneck on a core difficult problem. At the critical moment, Wei Bangyao broke down the logic layer by layer and argued through each point step by step, successfully clearing the blockage in the problem-solving process, unifying the team's direction of attack, and leading the team out of the impasse.

On the afternoon of the final day of the competition, they successfully completed all research on the competition problems, model optimization, and content refinement, and submitted their competition paper and modeling results to a high standard.

The subsequent three-month review and waiting period felt especially long to Wei Bangyao. He was terrified of letting down the organization's trust, letting down his teammates' efforts, and letting down the days and nights of hard striving.

When the good news arrived that they had won the grand prize, everyone was elated. Li Jiang patted Wei Bangyao on the shoulder, full of satisfaction: "I knew it. I hadn't misjudged you."

"Here, We All Became Better Versions of Ourselves"

At the beginning of this year, the unit held an awards ceremony for its "Top Ten Border Defense Exemplars" (十大戍边标兵). Among the outstanding officers and soldiers draped in honor, Wei Bangyao was the only conscript.

On the large screen at the awards venue, a short growth film played slowly, unfolding scene after scene of everyday border defense life and moments of striving. The camera recorded the trajectory of Wei Bangyao's transformation: the once slight and slender figure had become upright and sturdy through day after day of border defense training and tempering in wind and rain; the formerly green and uncertain eyes had shed the timidity and restlessness of youth, settling into the steadfastness, composure, and certainty unique to a border defense soldier. The fertile soil of the military camp and the wind and rain of the frontier had reshaped his appearance and his character.

"Here, we all became better versions of ourselves." Looking back on the path of growth and transformation, Wei Bangyao spoke from the heart.

In fact, Wei Bangyao's "accelerated" growth is not an isolated case in this unit. In this border defense force rooted in the deep mountains, generation after generation of young officers and soldiers have quietly transformed in this fertile military soil, blooming with a distinctive brilliance —

Some had won second prize in a CNC machine tool competition before enlisting; after enlisting, they precisely matched their skills to the needs of their posts, specializing in precision equipment calibration and fault diagnosis, becoming indispensable technical experts in their companies.

Some are proficient in new media creation, skilled in the full workflow of video planning, filming, and editing, using the camera to capture the everyday life of border defense and bringing stories of military strengthening from the deep mountains out to the wider world. Others have studied traditional Chinese medicine therapy techniques, are skilled in acupuncture and massage, and quietly relieve their comrades' pain and injuries in their spare time after training, using warmth to safeguard their comrades' physical and mental health.

"Every young officer and soldier who comes to the border defense carries a passion. What we need to do is understand them and build a stage for every officer and soldier to realize their dreams." In Li Jiang's view, the deep-mountain sentry post needs soldiers who will hold fast, but it needs even more innovative talent suited to the demands of training and combat readiness in the new era.

To this end, they persist in teaching in accordance with individual aptitude (因材施教) and cultivating talent with precision, proactively tapping the potential of every soldier, precisely matching individual special skills to the practical combat needs of border defense readiness, sentry management and control, and positional innovation, injecting continuous momentum into the cause of border defense.

Not long ago, Wei Bangyao was recommended to participate in a higher-level specialized training course on war gaming (兵棋). After completing the training and returning to his unit, he was appointed head of the battalion's war gaming group, taking the lead in guiding his comrades in deepening war gaming practical training and concentrating efforts on technical breakthroughs, targeting the shortfalls of the existing war gaming system and optimizing its effectiveness in practical application.

In response to the practical characteristics of the border defense area — complex terrain, dispersed sentry positions, and significant management and control difficulty — they devoted themselves to studying the operational logic of the war gaming system, repeatedly simulating, testing, and working through optimizations, completing iterative upgrades and functional improvements to multiple core modules of the system.

As the morning light breaks, awakening the deep mountains, the long sound of the bugle pierces the stillness of the mountain forest.

Through layer upon layer of forest, Wei Bangyao and his comrades assemble on time, adjust their gear, check their supplies, fall into formation, and set out on the patrol route into the rising sun.

Let Every Officer and Soldier Find "Something Meaningful"

■ PLA Daily Reporter Chen Xiaojie

How does a private first class earn the right to lead a team to the Army-wide grand prize?

Before entering this border defense company, that question kept circling in this reporter's mind. After the interviews concluded, the answer gradually became clear — he had the ability, and there were people willing to believe in his ability and build the ladder and pave the road for him.

During the interviews, Political Instructor Li Jiang mentioned a detail. When assembling the competition team, he overrode objections and made Wei Bangyao, a private first class, the team captain. When the news broke, discussion spread throughout the battalion. Some worried that a conscript leading the team wouldn't be able to hold things together; others felt that "inverted rank" (军衔倒挂) was contrary to convention. Li Jiang responded with a single sentence: "Competition is about ability. Whoever can do it, goes."

"The way to strengthen the military lies in obtaining the right people (强军之道，要在得人)." Obtaining the right people means not only selecting them, but also putting them to use — placing them in the right positions. The military emphasizes clear hierarchy and strict obedience to orders; that is a function of the mission. But when it comes to combat effectiveness, daring to break convention and placing the right person in the right position requires not only discernment, but also a sense of responsibility.

I later asked the other two team members what they thought about "a conscript serving as team captain." They admitted that at first they did have some doubts, but those were completely dispelled after the first joint exercise. Platoon Leader Zhang Qinghua said something that left a deep impression on me: "When Wei Bangyao explained his understanding of modeling, we knew — on this matter, we had to listen to him." Letting ability do the talking, winning respect through demonstrated competence — this is probably the most plain and yet most powerful logic in the military camp.

On the evening of the day I left the company, Wei Bangyao brought up the original aspiration he had when he enlisted: "I want to be a good soldier and do something meaningful." He said he now has a new understanding of those words — "Meaningful things don't come from waiting. They come from the day you discover that what you are capable of doing is exactly what the unit needs."

From "what can I do" to "what does the unit need me to do" — the distance between those two questions is sometimes bridged only by a pair of eyes skilled at noticing, and one decisive decision.

As dusk fell, several soldiers walked out of the study room, talking about something as they went, their voices low but their expressions extraordinarily focused. In that moment I suddenly felt that this is probably the finest sight in a military camp — every spark of flame is seen, and every passion has a place to burn.

A force capable of winning battles has never been solely a contest of weapons and equipment; it is equally a contest of talent and vitality. Discovering a soldier's strengths, fulfilling a soldier's dreams, letting every officer and soldier find that "meaningful thing" — this in itself is building the future of a force.

Military Modeling Competition

Military modeling (军事建模) is a quantitative research method that takes real-world military problems as its orientation and integrates mathematics, computer technology, and military science. It originates from the system of military operations research (军事运筹学). Through scientific assumptions about combat scenarios, model construction, data solving, review and retrospection, and assessment of results, it equips traditional military research with a precise "digital ruler," making vague combat analysis quantifiable, simulable, and verifiable. It is an important supporting means for the scientization and refinement of modern military research.

The scientific application of military modeling has fundamentally changed the traditional mode of military research. Around the time of the First World War, British engineer F. W. Lanchester used systems of ordinary differential equations to quantify combat force attrition, constructing a classic mathematical model of combat, laying the mathematical and physical research foundation for the application of firepower, and introducing quantitative analytical thinking into modern military research. During the Second World War, Allied forces used mathematical methods to solve a series of operational problems, achieving significant results.

Today, military modeling has become the "temple calculation instrument (庙算利器)" and "intelligent brain" of modern warfare, capable of precisely characterizing various military elements such as operational command, battlefield actions, and weapons and equipment operation, providing scientific support for training and combat readiness and for operational decision-making.

Conducting military modeling competitions is of significant importance for driving the transformation of the military's operational theory development toward engineering-based, precise, and standardized approaches; for promoting the use of quantitative analytical methods by forces to resolve difficult and key problems in operational training and organizational management; and for improving the thinking level and decision-making capability of commanders at all levels.

After years of exploration and practice, our military's military modeling competition has built a competition operation and management system structured around the principle of "competition problems sourced from the front line, problem-setting relying on experts, competition open to the entire army, and results serving the force." It takes the real bottlenecks faced by forces as the living source of competition problems, has identified and cultivated large numbers of data analysis talents for the force, has provided effective solution approaches for resolving real-world difficulties, and has bridged the "last mile" from competition results to the generation of combat effectiveness in the force.

一石激起千层浪。这支由义务兵带领两名军官的参赛队伍，在营里引发不少讨论。在大家看来，两名排长无论年龄、阅历都更占优势，很多人都带着审慎的心态观望。 对此，李江有着更为务实长远的考量。虽说邓文钜、张清华岗位经验丰富、专业基础扎实，但都没有参加过建模竞赛，缺乏实战历练。魏邦耀虽是义务兵，但多次参与高水平竞赛，积累了更为成熟的实际经验。 “在这支队伍里，靠的是实力说话，不靠资历论高低、不靠军衔定主次。”就这样，队伍确定了科学清晰的分工，最大化释放团队战力：魏邦耀负责搭建整体解题框架、敲定核心建模思路；邓文钜发挥专业所长，专注程序编写、数据演算与技术落地；思维缜密、文字扎实的张清华，主要负责成果梳理、逻辑复盘与论文撰写。 组建之初，魏邦耀便把团队磨合放在备赛首位。有着丰富参赛经验的他深知，单打独斗难成气候，思路统一、配合默契才是赛场决胜的关键。 于是，在大家各自完成初期基础积累后，魏邦耀提议参照地方成熟的建模赛事主题开展集中合练，在实战化推演中打磨协作节奏、统一解题思路、补齐能力短板。 在这个过程中，思维的碰撞与分歧时常出现。3人立足各自专业视角各抒己见、相互研讨，常常复盘推演至凌晨。每当思路难以统一时，魏邦耀都会主动暂停争论，牵头梳理核心逻辑、拆解问题关键、分步推演验证，带动团队凝聚共识、确定方向。 为确保解题思路严谨无误、核心公式科学有效，魏邦耀还主动连线地方高校的专家答疑求证，破除认知偏差、打消团队疑虑，让每一步推演、每一个结论都经得起检验。 日复一日的磨合攻坚中，3人的配合愈发娴熟默契，彼此之间也愈发信任。魏邦耀也以沉稳务实的作风、过硬扎实的专业能力，证明了自己这名“义务兵队长”的实力。 比赛的日子很快到来。赛题紧贴练兵备战实际，涵盖多领域军事专业知识，综合性强、攻坚难度大。3人紧密配合、分工协作，一边快速吃透题目内核、消化军事理论要点，一边精准推演公式、搭建模型架构，全程紧锣密鼓、高效攻坚。 赛程中段，团队在一道核心难题上遇到瓶颈。关键时刻，魏邦耀层层拆解逻辑、逐条推演论证，成功打通解题堵点，统一了团队攻坚方向，带领队伍走出困境。 比赛进入最后一天下午，他们顺利完成全部赛题研究、模型优化与内容打磨，高质量提交参赛论文与建模成果。 随后的3个月评审等待期，对魏邦耀而言格外漫长。他生怕辜负组织的信任、辜负队友的付出、辜负这段日夜兼程的奋斗时光。 当获得特等奖的喜讯传来，所有人为之振奋。李江拍着魏邦耀的肩膀，满是欣慰：“我就知道，没有看错你。” “在这里，我们都成为了更好的自己” 今年初，该部举办“十大戍边标兵”颁奖典礼。一众身披荣光的优秀官兵中，魏邦耀是唯一一名义务兵。 颁奖现场的大屏幕上，一段成长短片缓缓播放，一幕幕戍边日常、奋斗瞬间铺展开来。镜头记录着魏邦耀的蜕变轨迹：曾经清瘦单薄的身姿，在日复一日的边防训练与风雨淬炼中变得挺拔壮实；从前青涩迷茫的眼眸，褪去了少年的怯懦浮躁，沉淀出边防军人独有的坚毅、沉稳与笃定。军营沃土、边关风雨，重塑了他的模样与心性。 “在这里，我们都成为了更好的自己。”回望一路成长蜕变，魏邦耀由衷感慨。 事实上，魏邦耀的成长“加速度”在该部并非孤例。在这支扎根深山的戍边队伍里，一茬茬年轻的官兵在这片军营沃土中悄然蜕变，绽放别样光彩—— 有的入伍前曾获得过数控机床竞赛二等奖，入伍后精准对接岗位需求，专攻装备精密调试、故障排查，成为连队不可或缺的技术能手； 有的精通新媒体创作，熟练掌握视频策划、拍摄剪辑全流程，用镜头定格戍边日常，让深山里的强军故事走出大山；还有的学过中医理疗技艺，擅长针灸推拿，训练之余默默为战友缓解伤痛，用温情守护战友身心健康…… “每一名奔赴边防的青年官兵，都怀揣着热爱，我们要做的，就是读懂他们，为每一名官兵搭建实现梦想的舞台。”在李江看来，深山哨位需要坚守的战士，更需要适配新时代练兵备战需求的创新人才。 为此，他们坚持因材施教、精准育才，主动挖掘每名战士的潜能，让个人专长精准对接边防战备、执勤管控、岗位创新等实战需求，为戍边事业注入源源不断的动力。 前不久，魏邦耀又被推荐参加上级兵棋专项集训。学成归队后，他被任命为营兵棋小组负责人，牵头带领战友深耕兵棋实训、聚力技术攻关，针对性补齐现有兵棋系统短板、优化实战运用效能。 针对边防地形复杂、执勤点位分散、管控难度较大的现实特点，他们潜心钻研兵棋系统运作逻辑，反复推演测试、攻坚优化，完成该系统多项核心模块迭代与功能升级。 晨光破晓，唤醒深山，悠长的哨声划破山林静谧。 层层林海间，魏邦耀和战友们准时集结，整理装具、检查物资、整装列队，迎着朝阳走上了巡逻路。 让每一名官兵都找到“有意义的事” ■解放军报记者 陈晓杰 一个上等兵，凭什么带队拿下全军特等奖？ 走进这支边防连队前，这个问题一直盘旋在记者心里。采访结束后，答案渐渐清晰——他有这个本事，也有人愿意相信他的本事，为他搭梯铺路。 采访中，教导员李江说起一个细节。当初组建参赛队伍，他力排众议，让魏邦耀这个上等兵当队长。消息一出，营里议论纷纷。有人担心义务兵带队压不住阵，有人觉得“军衔倒挂”不合常理。李江只用一句话回应：“比赛拼的是本事，谁能干，谁就上。” “强军之道，要在得人。”得人，不只是把人选出来，更是把人用起来、放在对的地方。部队讲究层级分明、令行禁止，这是使命使然。但在战斗力面前，敢于打破常规、把合适的人放在合适的位置上，需要的不只是眼光，更是一份担当。 后来我问另外两名队员，对“义务兵当队长”怎么看。他们坦言，一开始确实有些疑虑，但第一次合练之后就全打消了。排长张清华说了一句话，我印象很深：“当魏邦耀讲出自己对建模的理解时，我们就知道，这事得听他的。”以能力说话，用实力服人——这大概是军营里最朴素也最有力量的逻辑。 离开连队那天傍晚，魏邦耀跟我提起入伍时的初心：“我想当一个好兵，做有意义的事。”他说如今对这句话有了新的理解——“有意义的事不是等来的，是有一天你发现自己会做的事，正好是部队需要的。” 从“我能做什么”到“部队需要我做什么”，这中间的距离，有时只隔着一双善于发现的眼睛、一个敢拍板的决定。 天色渐暗，几个战士从学习室走出来，边走边讨论着什么，声音不大，神情却格外专注。那一刻我忽然觉得，这大概就是军营里最好的样子——每一簇火苗都被看见，每一份热爱都有地方燃烧。 一支能打胜仗的部队，从来不只是武器装备的较量，更是人才活力的较量。发现一名战士的长处，成全一名战士的梦想，让每一名官兵都找到那件“有意义的事”，本身就是在成就一支部队的未来。 军事建模竞赛 军事建模，是以实战军事问题为导向，融合数学、计算机技术与军事科学的量化研究方法，源自军事运筹学体系。它通过对作战场景科学假设、模型构建、数据求解、检验复盘与结果研判，为传统军事研究装上精准的“数字标尺”，让模糊的作战分析变得可量化、可推演、可验证，是现代军事研究科学化、精细化的重要支撑手段。 军事建模的科学应用，彻底改变了传统军事研究模式。一战前后，英国工程师兰彻斯特通过常微分方程组量化作战兵力损耗，构建经典战斗数学模型，奠定了火力运用的数理研究基础，为现代军事研究引入定量分析思维；第二次世界大战期间，盟国军队运用数学方法解决了一系列作战难题，取得了显著的战果。 如今，军事建模已成为现代战争的“庙算利器”与“智能大脑”，能够精准刻画作战指挥、战场行动、武器装备运行等各类军事要素，为练兵备战、作战决策提供科学支撑。 开展军事建模竞赛对推动军队作战理论建设向工程化、精确化、标准化转变，促进部队运用定量分析方法解决作战训练和建设管理中的重难点问题，提高各级指挥员思维水平和决策能力具有重要意义。 经过多年探索实践，我军军事建模竞赛通过构建“赛题来自一线、命题依靠专家、竞赛面向全军、成果服务部队”的竞赛运行管理体系，把部队现实瓶颈作为赛题的源头活水，为部队发掘培养了大批数据分析人才，为破解现实难题提供有效解决思路，打通了竞赛成果到部队战斗力生成的“最后一公里”。
