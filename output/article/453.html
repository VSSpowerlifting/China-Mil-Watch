Forum on Establishing and Practicing a Correct Outlook on Political Achievement: How to Better Do Real Work, Seek Real Results, and Produce Real Performance

树立和践行正确政绩观大家谈丨如何更好干实事、求实效、出实绩
PLA Daily (解放军报) 19 May 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A forum in what appears to be a PLA political education outlet publishes five opinion pieces elaborating the Central Committee's 2025 campaign against distorted political achievement metrics, framing the "five heavies and five lights" (五重五轻) — prioritizing beginnings over endings, short-term over long-term, visible over hidden, easy over difficult, and local over overall — as live problems requiring correction across Party and military organizations. Two of the five pieces address the armed forces directly, invoking the "centenary goal of army building" and calling on leading cadres at all levels to take on hard tasks rather than manage appearances. This is standard political work content; its value is as a record of how the institution is framing the campaign's application to the military bureaucracy specifically, not as evidence of a new policy line or changed command behavior.
Translation

Editor's Note: During his tenure working in Zhejiang, Chairman Xi identified a prominent problem among some Party members and cadres in their outlook on political achievement — the "five heavies and five lights" (五重五轻): prioritizing beginnings over endings, short-term over long-term, visible over hidden, easy over difficult, and the local over the overall. These problems remain relevant today. Earlier this year, the General Office of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China issued the "Notice on Carrying Out Study and Education on Establishing and Practicing a Correct Outlook on Political Achievement Throughout the Party," requiring that "Party organizations at all levels and Party members and cadres be educated and guided to uphold seeking truth from facts and a pragmatic work style, to produce political achievements for the people and through hard work, and to effectively prevent and correct deviations in the outlook on political achievement." In this issue's forum, we have selected five opinion pieces to explore with readers how to better do real work, seek real results, and produce real performance.

Do Things Well from Start to Finish — Resolutely Guard Against "Prioritizing Beginnings Over Endings"

■ Yao Guoyu

Drums and gongs at the start, silence in the middle, a hasty retreat at the end — this phenomenon of "prioritizing beginnings over endings" refers to the state of individual Party members and cadres at different stages of their work. Everything begins with soaring enthusiasm and pledges of commitment, but as time goes on the drive slackens and the course drifts, leaving behind "half-finished projects" and "unresolved debts." Such situations must be guarded against.

Confucius once lamented: there are seedlings that do not flower, and flowers that do not bear fruit. The meaning is that some crops sprout but never put forth ears, and some put forth ears but never set grain. Similar phenomena exist in actual work today. Some people treat launching a project as success and treat ceremonies as results; their work is performed for superiors and leaders to see, and their outcomes are written in documents, spoken aloud, and "implemented" in meetings — all beginning but no end, strong start but weak finish, grasping without sincerity, working without substance. All such conduct wastes opportunities and yields little.

"Be as cautious at the end as at the beginning, and there will be no failure." For any piece of work or any project, the true test lies both in the opening and in the conclusion. The reason the poverty alleviation battle was able to achieve complete victory was precisely the tenacity of fighting one battle after another, striking one blow after another, and absolutely refusing to stand down before total victory. Chairman Xi has incisively pointed out: "Faced with new situations and new tasks, Party members and cadres must work with real diligence — do real work, use real methods, seek real results, do things well from start to finish, and resolutely eliminate the practice of sloganeering, posturing, and packaging as implementation." These words cut to the heart of the matter and give one much to reflect upon.

Empty posturing cannot build a lasting enterprise; only genuine hard work can produce results from start to finish. Resolutely guarding against "prioritizing beginnings over endings" requires every Party member and cadre to have a "process consciousness" and an "outcomes consciousness," and to reflect the drive for implementation in work measures, work processes, and work results. In their work, they should establish a closed-loop awareness: every task must have deployment, inspection, feedback, and results, forming a chain of responsibility from beginning to end. At the same time, more refined management and less rough-and-ready targeting, more down-to-earth effort and less reaching beyond one's grasp, more hard and honest work and less opportunism — only then can one separate the wheat from the chaff and ensure that what is undertaken is accomplished.

Persevere Over the Long Term — Resolutely Guard Against "Prioritizing the Short-Term Over the Long-Term"

■ Liu Weijiao

At present, the great majority of leading cadres are able in their actual work to correctly handle the dialectical relationship between immediate interests and long-term interests, and pay attention to doing things that lay foundations and build momentum for the future. But there are individual leading cadres whose eyes see only "within the term," "at assessment time," and "on the report form." Driven by vanity and a hunger for quick success, they inflate figures, build "bonsai projects," and launch "short, flat, fast" initiatives. They are not truly "bringing prosperity to one side" but are focused on "creating a momentary impression," manufacturing chips to advance their own interests. "One who does not plan for all time is not fit to plan for the moment." If one only fixes one's gaze on immediate results, one will kill the goose for the golden egg and eat tomorrow's grain today, very likely turning "a moment's political achievement" into "a long-term burden."

A goose leaves its cry, a person leaves their name. There is nothing wrong with wanting to accomplish something within one's term, but one must absolutely never violate objective laws or deviate from the fundamental purpose of serving the people wholeheartedly — otherwise it is "putting on a show" rather than "doing the work." Three generations of people at Saihanba planted trees for over 60 years; Yuan Longping spent decades walking the fields of hope day after day; Huang Danian spent his entire life igniting the torch of deep-earth exploration... None of them ever pursued "immediate results," yet they created enterprises that benefit generations to come. This enlightens us: long and short are not absolute values on a timeline but a watershed in value orientation. Those "impressive figures" bought by mortgaging the future are ultimately a flash in the pan; but those who are willing to lay the groundwork and carry on unfinished work will ultimately be remembered by the people.

Looking further ahead, planting one's feet more firmly, overcoming some of the restlessness of "putting speed first," and maintaining some of the steadiness of "careful, unhurried work" — this is the correct outlook on political achievement that is responsible to history and responsible to the people. As Chairman Xi has pointed out, for long-term tasks, one must maintain strategic resolve and patience, persist in seeing a single blueprint through to the end, and work with the perseverance of water wearing through stone, persevering over the long term.

Be Willing to Serve as a Cornerstone — Resolutely Guard Against "Prioritizing the Visible Over the Hidden"

■ Han Wei

Visible achievement (显功) is the most direct manifestation of Party members and cadres fulfilling their duties and responsibilities; it is mostly seen and felt, easy to stand out, and convenient to quantify. Hidden achievement (潜功) is the deep foundation of an enterprise's development; it mostly consumes time and effort, does not show on the surface, and is difficult to see results from in a short time. But Party members and cadres must clearly recognize that in many cases, hidden achievement is the foundation of visible achievement, and visible achievement is the result of hidden achievement. Without a foundation buried deep in the ground, how can a tall building rise from the earth? Without the quiet support of the "hidden," how can the "visible" shine brilliantly?

Chairman Xi has pointed out: "We must firmly establish a correct outlook on political achievement — we must do practical things that the common people can see, feel, and benefit from, and we must also do good things that lay the groundwork for those who come after, build foundations, and serve long-term interests; we must produce both visible achievements and hidden achievements." The "talent strategy" of Zhengding, the "3820" Strategic Project of Fuzhou, the "Eight-Eight Strategy" of Zhejiang... When working at the local level, Chairman Xi placed great emphasis on focusing on the present while looking toward the future, both grasping immediate concrete work and planning long-term strategic layouts, reserving broad space for local development, accumulating rich resources, and gathering lasting momentum.

"One who seeks the growth of a tree must first consolidate its roots; one who desires a stream to flow far must first dredge its source." The current and coming period is a critical period for the modernization of national defense and the armed forces, and fighting the decisive battle to achieve the centenary goal of army building is increasingly urgent and pressing. Leading cadres at all levels of the armed forces must recognize from the depths of their thinking the dialectical relationship between visible and hidden achievement, and consciously cultivate the spirit of "success need not come in my time" and "success must certainly have my contribution." On one hand, with the urgency of seizing every moment and the sense of responsibility that time waits for no one, they must act proactively and correctly. At the same time, they should run the "relay race" well — one generation working after another, not coveting momentary merit or momentary fame — producing visible results through hard work and dedication, and consolidating the hidden foundation through far-sighted planning, truly creating real performance that can withstand the tests of practice, the people, and history.

Have the Courage to Take on Responsibility — Resolutely Guard Against "Prioritizing the Easy Over the Difficult"

■ Liu Dongcai

Digging only in loose soil, avoiding the hard bones; only doing things that go with the current, never daring to enter turbulent rapids and dangerous shallows. This selective conduct of "prioritizing the easy over the difficult" still finds a market among individual Party members and cadres. They rush to be first for work that is easy to shine at and simple to complete, while avoiding tasks that concentrate contradictions and require hard fighting, playing the role of "yes-men" and "enlightened gentlemen." Little do they know that this is precisely a departure from the Party member and cadre's true character of shouldering responsibility and waging struggle.

In all matters under heaven, if effort is applied only where things are easy, the difficult places will forever remain obstacles. Admittedly, in leadership decision-making and work deployment, it is sometimes necessary within a given time and space to weigh pros and cons or to tackle easy things before difficult ones. But if the guiding ideology is not correct and one persistently chooses the easy and abandons the difficult, problems will only accumulate more and more, and risks will only grow greater and greater. Those cadres who only add "flowers to brocade" and refuse to "send charcoal in the snow" may seem clever but in reality delay affairs.

As the ancient saying goes: "Confront the most difficult and plan for the most distant." True political achievement has always been forged in the place of "difficulty." Faced with sandstorms, Jiao Yulu did not take a detour but led the masses to plant paulownia trees one by one; in the poverty alleviation battle, millions of cadres dug into impoverished mountain villages and gnawed through the hardest bones. It was precisely because they chose "difficulty" that they achieved extraordinary "results."

Those who prioritize the easy over the difficult are, at their root, afraid of taking risks and afraid of bearing responsibility. The negative mentality of "do more, err more; do less, err less; do nothing, err nothing" is an irresponsible attitude toward the cause. One must understand: the more thorny the matter, the more one must step forward to meet it; the hotter the potato, the more one must take it in hand. Trampling the word "difficult" underfoot builds the ability and confidence to get things done.

Those who fear difficulty will face difficulty for a lifetime; those who overcome difficulty will face difficulty only for a time. At present, achieving the centenary goal of army building is in its critical assault phase, and the "Loushan Pass" and "Lazikou" that cannot be avoided or circumvented are right before us. Party members and cadres should have the courage to take on responsibility — in their positions, plan their affairs, fulfill their duties, and proactively shoulder heavy and difficult burdens within the scope of their responsibilities. They should face difficulties, contradictions, risks, and hidden dangers squarely, and advance in the face of difficulty to overcome it. They should uphold Party principles, be clear about right and wrong, dare to struggle, and take an unambiguous stand on major issues and matters of principle. Only in this way can they break new ground and produce genuine results.

Plan Systematically — Resolutely Guard Against "Prioritizing the Local Over the Overall"

■ Wang Haigang

Chairman Xi once gave a precise "portrait" of those who "prioritize the local over the overall": some cadres have weak political awareness and overall perspective; when they encounter matters, they start from personal interests or the small-group interests of their locality or unit, calculating personal interests and disregarding the interests of the people; they look only to local interests and ignore overall interests, and even sacrifice overall interests to seek small-group interests; they adopt a utilitarian attitude toward higher-level policies — acting swiftly on what benefits them, shelving what does not benefit them, paying lip service to what harms them while acting otherwise, and even engaging in "policies from above, countermeasures from below," not hesitating to sacrifice overall interests for local interests. This kind of parochialism (本位主义) often damages the overall situation and ultimately harms the local as well.

"One who does not plan for the whole is not fit to plan for a part." This saying has long made clear the dialectical relationship between the local and the overall. The local and the overall are mutually dependent and mutually reinforcing. When thinking about problems and doing work, if one does not consider the overall picture and the whole, it is easy to "see only the trees and not the forest" and to attend to one thing while losing sight of another. Equally, the overall cannot be separated from the local and requires the local to play its role. One should proceed from the standpoint of serving the overall with one's own domain, using key breakthroughs in the local to drive overall improvement across the board, thereby achieving "bringing honor to one's own domain while adding luster to the overall."

One who grasps the greater can also encompass the lesser. In those years, when the Liu-Deng Army advanced into the Dabie Mountains, Comrade Deng Xiaoping said: "As long as we annihilate the main enemy forces on the southern front, even if the Central Plains Field Army is wiped out entirely, the liberation armies on all fronts across the country can still achieve national victory — this price is worth paying." Looking back at history, it was precisely because the broad ranks of commanders and fighters correctly handled the relationship between the local and the overall, sacrificing the lesser self to fulfill the greater self, that our army won one hard battle after another and achieved one victory after another.

To establish and practice a correct outlook on political achievement, Party members and cadres must keep in mind "the great affairs of the nation" and the great affairs of the military, think about problems and handle matters from the height of the overall situation, shoulder responsibility and wage struggle within the larger context of the Party, the state, and the military, and frequently ask themselves: Does this decision affect those upstream and downstream, those to the left and right? Is this "bright spot" built upon a "dark spot" elsewhere? Is the small local ledger consistent with the large overall ledger, the general ledger, and the long-term ledger?

Original Chinese
编者按在浙江工作期间，习主席就提出一些党员干部在政绩观上存在“五重五轻”的突出问题，即重始轻终、重短轻长、重显轻隐、重易轻难、重局部轻全局，至今仍有现实针对性。今年初，中共中央办公厅印发的《关于在全党开展树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育的通知》，要求“教育引导各级党组织和党员、干部坚持实事求是、求真务实，为人民出政绩、以实干出政绩，有效防范和纠治政绩观偏差”。本期大家谈，我们选编5篇言论，与读者一起探讨，如何更好干实事、求实效、出实绩。 善作善成，力戒“重始轻终” ■姚国玉 开局锣鼓喧天，中场悄无声息，结尾草草收兵——这种“重始轻终”的现象，指的是个别党员干部在工作不同阶段的状态。凡事开头热情高涨、誓师立愿，可时间一长便松了劲、走了样，最后留下“半拉子工程”“烂尾账”。这样的情况，当戒。 孔子曾感慨，苗而不秀者有矣夫，秀而不实者有矣夫。意思是说，庄稼出了苗不吐穗扬花的是有的，吐穗扬花不结果的是有的。当前实际工作中也存在类似现象。有人把启动当成功，把仪式当成果，工作是做给上级和领导看的，成效是写在文件上、挂在口头上、落实在会议上的，有始无终、虎头蛇尾，抓而不真、干而不实。凡此种种，贻误时机、难有成果。 “慎终如始，则无败事。”一项工作、一个项目，真正的考验既在开局，也在收尾。脱贫攻坚之所以能取得全面胜利，靠的就是一仗接着一仗打、一锤接着一锤敲，不获全胜决不收兵的韧劲。习主席深刻指出，“面对新形势新任务，党员干部一定要真抓实干，务实功、出实招、求实效，善作善成，坚决杜绝口号式、表态式、包装式落实的做法。”可谓一针见血，发人深省。 凌空蹈虚，难成千秋之业；真抓实干，方能善作善成。力戒“重始轻终”，要求每名党员干部有“过程意识”“效果意识”，把抓落实体现在工作举措、工作流程、工作实效上。工作中，应树立闭环意识，每一项任务都要有部署、有检查、有反馈、有结果，形成从始到终的责任链条。同时，多一些精细管理、少一些概略瞄准，多一些脚踏实地、少一些好高骛远，多一些苦干实干、少一些投机取巧，方能吹糠见米、做则必成。 久久为功，力戒“重短轻长” ■刘维姣 目前，绝大多数领导干部在实际工作中能够正确处理当前利益和长远利益的辩证关系，注重干好打基础、增后劲的事。但也有个别领导干部眼中只有“任期内”“考核时”“报表上”，好大喜功，于是搞“数字注水”、做“盆景工程”、上“短平快”项目。他们不是真正“造福一方”，而是着力“造势一时”，为自己谋利制造筹码。“不谋万世者，不足谋一时”。若只盯着眼前的成绩，就会杀鸡取卵、寅吃卯粮，很可能把“一时的政绩”变成为“长期的包袱”。 雁过留声，人过留名。想在任期内干出一番成绩无可厚非，但决不能违背客观规律或者背离全心全意为人民服务的根本宗旨，否则就是“做秀”而非“做事”了。塞罕坝三代人60余年植绿，袁隆平数十年如一日奔走在希望的田野上，黄大年用毕生奋斗点燃地球探测的灯火……他们从来没有追求“立竿见影”，却创造了泽被后世的伟业。这启示我们：长与短，不是时间刻度上的绝对值，而是价值取向上的分水岭。那些靠透支未来换来的“漂亮数据”，终究是昙花一现；而那些甘做铺垫之事、甘续未竟之业的人，终将被人民铭记。 把眼光放远，把脚步踩实，克服一些“快字当头”的浮躁，保持一些“慢工细活”的定力，才是对历史负责、对人民负责的正确政绩观。正如习主席所指出的，对长期任务，要保持战略定力和耐心，坚持一张蓝图绘到底，滴水穿石，久久为功。 甘当基石，力戒“重显轻隐” ■韩 炜 显功是党员干部履职尽责最直观的体现，大多看得见、摸得着，容易出彩、便于量化。潜功是事业发展深厚的底气，大多耗时费力、不显山露水，短时间内难以见效。但党员干部必须清醒认识到，很多时候，潜功是显功的基础，显功是潜功的结果，没有深埋地下的地基，何来拔地而起的高楼？没有“隐”的默默支撑，哪来“显”的熠熠生辉？ 习主席指出：“要牢固树立正确政绩观，既要做让老百姓看得见、摸得着、得实惠的实事，也要做为后人作铺垫、打基础、利长远的好事，既要做显功，也要做潜功”。正定的“人才经”、福州的“3820”战略工程、浙江的“八八战略”……在地方工作时，习主席就高度重视聚焦当下、着眼未来，既抓实眼前的具体工作，又谋划长远的战略布局，为当地发展预留了广阔空间、储备了丰厚资源、汇聚起持久动能。 “求木之长者，必固其根本；欲流之远者，必浚其泉源。”当前和今后一个时期是国防和军队现代化的关键时期，打好实现建军一百年奋斗目标攻坚战更加现实紧迫。军队各级领导干部必须从思想深处认清显功与潜功的辩证关系，自觉涵养“功成不必在我”和“功成必定有我”的精神。一方面，以只争朝夕的紧迫感、时不我待的责任心，积极作为、正确作为。同时应跑好“接力赛”，一茬接着一茬干，不贪一时之功、不图一时之名，以实干担当做出显绩，以远见谋划夯实潜基，真正创造出经得起实践、人民、历史检验的实绩。 勇于担当，力戒“重易轻难” ■刘东才 专挑松土挖，避开硬骨头；只做顺水推舟的事，不敢涉激流险滩。这种“重易轻难”的选择性作为，在个别党员干部中仍有市场。他们对容易出彩、轻松完成的工作争先恐后，对矛盾集中、攻坚克难的任务避之不及，当“好好先生”“开明绅士”。殊不知，这恰恰背离了党员干部担当斗争的本色。 天下事，在易处用力，难处便永远成坎。诚然，领导决策和工作部署，在一定时空范围内权衡利弊或先易后难是必要的，但如果指导思想不端正，长期择易弃难，难题只会越积越多、风险只会越积越大。那些只做“锦上添花”、不肯“雪中送炭”的干部，看似精明，实则误事。 古语云：“犯其至难而图其至远。”真正的政绩，从来都是在“难”处铸就。焦裕禄面对风沙，没有绕道走，而是带着群众一棵一棵种下泡桐；脱贫攻坚战中，数百万干部扎进贫困山村，啃下了难啃的硬骨头。正因为他们选择了“难”，才成就了非凡的“绩”。 重易轻难者，根子是怕担风险、怕负责任。“多做多错、少做少错、不做不错”的消极心态，是对事业的不负责任。须知，越是棘手的事越要迎上去，越是烫手的山芋越要接过来。把“难”字踩在脚下，就长了干事的本事底气。 怕难者难一辈子，克难者难一阵子。当前，实现建军一百年奋斗目标正处在攻坚期，躲不开、绕不过的“娄山关”“腊子口”就在眼前。党员干部应勇于担当，在其位、谋其政、尽其责，在职责范围内主动担重、担难。应正视困难矛盾、风险隐患，迎难而上、攻坚克难。应坚持党性原则，是非分明、敢于斗争，在重大问题、原则问题上旗帜鲜明。如此，才能闯出新天地、干出真业绩。 系统谋划，力戒“重局部轻全局” ■王海港 习主席曾给“重局部轻全局”者精准“画像”：有的干部政治意识和全局观念不强，遇事从个人利益或本地本单位小团体利益出发，计较个人利益，漠视人民利益；只顾局部利益，不顾全局利益，甚至以牺牲全局利益来谋取小团体利益；对上级政策采取实用主义态度，对己有利的雷厉风行，对己无利的束之高阁，对己不利的阳奉阴违，甚至搞“上有政策、下有对策”，为了局部利益不惜牺牲全局利益。这种本位主义，往往损害大局，最终也贻害局部。 “不谋全局者，不足谋一域。”这句话早已点明局部与全局的辩证关系。局部和全局互相依存、互相促进。想问题、干工作，如果不从全局、整体去考虑，就容易“只见树木不见森林”、顾此失彼。同样，全局也不能脱离局部，需要局部作用的发挥。应立足以一域服务全局，通过局部的重点突破推动全局的整体提升，从而实现“既为一域争光、更为全局添彩”。 得其大者可以兼其小。当年，刘邓大军挺进大别山，邓小平同志说：“只要歼灭南线敌人主力，中野就是打光了，全国各路解放军还可以取得全国胜利，这个代价是值得的。”回顾历史，正是广大指战员正确处理局部与全局的关系，牺牲小我、成全大我，我军才打赢一场场硬仗，赢得一个个胜利。 树立和践行正确政绩观，党员干部必须心怀“国之大者”、军之大事，站在全局的高度想问题、办事情，站在党、国家和军队大局中担当和斗争，常问问自己：这个决策是否影响上下游、左右邻？这个“亮点”是否建立在别处的“暗点”之上？局部的小账与全局的大账、总账、长远账是否一致？
