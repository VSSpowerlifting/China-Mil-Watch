Correct Outlook on Political Achievement, Practiced in the Barracks | Looking Up at the Jiao Tong Tree, Bowing Down to Answer the Test

正确政绩观 践行在军营丨仰望焦桐 躬身答卷
PLA Daily (解放军报) 15 June 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
The PAP Kaifeng Contingent's Lankao Detachment has made Jiao Yulu—the model Communist cadre who governed Lankao County until his death in 1964—the organizing frame for its ongoing campaign on the 'correct outlook on political achievement' (正确政绩观), using the paulownia tree Jiao planted near the garrison as a recurring ritual site for recruit indoctrination and veteran farewell ceremonies. The article documents how the detachment's political work apparatus is deploying a locally rooted revolutionary martyr narrative to answer internal skepticism about whether cadre-led physical training and mass-line service work remain necessary given advances in surveillance technology and simulation equipment. The framing points to a broader institutional effort to use the 正确政绩观 campaign to preempt the kind of metric-gaming and risk-aversion among junior leaders that PLA and PAP political work organs have repeatedly identified as a problem in recent years.
Translation

**Editor's Note:** In order to thoroughly implement the deployment and arrangements for comprehensively carrying out study and education on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on political achievement (正确政绩观) throughout the entire Party and the entire military, and to drive units at all levels to deeply grasp the core meaning of "for whom political achievements are built, what kind of political achievements to build, and what to rely on to build political achievements," the PLA Daily is today launching the column "Correct Outlook on Political Achievement, Practiced in the Barracks: Reporter's Record."

The column focuses on Chairman Xi's important statements on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on political achievement, and, in conjunction with the vivid explorations and fresh cases from units at all levels in rectifying their outlook on political achievement and advancing the comprehensive development of the force, analyzes typical practices, summarizes useful experience, and reflects genuine results. It aims to guide the broad ranks of officers and soldiers—especially leading cadres—to establish a correct view of power (权力观), outlook on political achievement (政绩观), and view of their undertaking (事业观), and to consolidate the strength to strive with full force to realize the centenary goal of army-building. We invite your attention.

**Looking Up at the Jiao Tong Tree, Bowing Down to Answer the Test**

*■ PLA Daily Reporters Zhang Tiannan, Wang Zhenjiang, Gao Liang*

In Lankao in summer, the paulownia blossoms have quietly fallen, and new leaves are unfurling one after another.

More than 60 years ago, Jiao Yulu planted a paulownia sapling here, and with it planted the hope of "a thousand acres of clear blue" (千顷澄碧). Today, that paulownia stands straight and tall, its canopy spreading wide enough to require three people to encircle it, and the people call it with deep affection the "Jiao Tong" (焦桐—Jiao's Paulownia).

Five kilometers from the "Jiao Tong" stands the garrison of the Lankao Detachment of the People's Armed Police Kaifeng Contingent. Inside the garrison, a statue of Jiao Yulu stands quietly. Sunlight falls upon him—coat draped over his shoulders, hands on his hips, gaze fixed on the distance—his eyes resolute and warm. Behind him, a grove of paulownias flourishes, branches full and leaves dense, casting a deep shade.

After each new cohort of recruits joins the detachment, their first class is held beneath the "Jiao Tong" tree. Before each cohort of veterans retires, they bring the new recruits to adopt a paulownia. The veterans often say: "Secretary Jiao once shielded the people from wind and sand; we are stationed here to guard the peace—it is the same thing."

When the reporters arrived at the detachment for interviews, it was nearly noon. The garrison was quiet, with only the rustling of wind through paulownia leaves. Several soldiers who had just finished training sat beneath the paulownia trees and told the reporters stories about Jiao Yulu, as though speaking of an "old acquaintance."

"If the cadres don't lead, the water buffalo falls into the well." (干部不领，水牛掉井。) Jiao Yulu left more than 60 years ago, but these words are kept in the hearts of the detachment's cadres and soldiers.

When Jiao Yulu said these words, he was leading Lankao cadres to survey wind gaps and probe water routes. He knew that the masses possess boundless creativity, but without leadership that strength cannot be brought to bear—like a water buffalo that has fallen into a well, with strength but nowhere to use it. In the eyes of those who lead soldiers in the detachment, this principle means "watch me" and "follow me up."

That year, a serious criminal case occurred in the garrison area, and the suspect was putting up desperate resistance. When the arrest order was issued, the then-Detachment Commander Qu was running a high fever. His comrades urged him to rest, but he said with iron resolve: "The arrest mission is urgent—move out immediately!"

In the darkness of night, Detachment Commander Qu led his team into the residential compound where the suspect was hiding. After locking onto the target, he kicked open the door with one blow and charged in first. The suspect fought back desperately with a dagger; in the struggle, Detachment Commander Qu's right thumb was broken. Enduring the excruciating pain, he wrested away the dagger and pinned the suspect down.

Some called him a tough man, but Detachment Commander Qu said: "When the cadre charges forward, the soldiers follow. If I had fallen back, who would have gone forward?"

Wasn't it the same principle when Jiao Yulu, enduring liver pain, always worked at the front? When leaders take the lead, nothing is too difficult to handle. "Leading" means daring to be the first—which inevitably entails risk and challenge; "leading" means guiding through action, unafraid of bleeding and sweating.

During the study and education on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on political achievement, someone once asked: Now that technology is advanced and duty patrols have electronic eyes and intelligent sentries, do cadres still need to be at the front in everything?

When the reporters visited the detachment's operations duty room, every corner of the jurisdiction was clearly visible on the large screen. But Detachment Political Instructor Wang told the reporters: "No matter how advanced technology becomes, it cannot replace cadres standing in front of soldiers as an example; no matter how sophisticated the equipment, it cannot convey the warmth of cadres fighting shoulder to shoulder with soldiers. 'If the cadres don't lead, the water buffalo falls into the well'—this principle never goes out of date."

When Jiao Yulu rolled up his trouser legs and leaned on a wooden staff, braving wind and rain to wade through mud, survey wind gaps, conduct on-the-ground investigations, and visit the poor and suffering—today in the Lankao Detachment, it is the cadres who stand at the head of the line on the training ground; it is the cadres who are the first to charge forward when a mission arises; it is the cadres who call out "follow me" in moments of danger.

Over the years, the detachment has been awarded a collective first-class merit citation once, second-class merit citations six times, and third-class merit citations seventeen times; it has been rated an "Advanced Detachment in Grassroots Development" by the contingent for 26 consecutive years; and its detachment Party branch has been commended by the Central Military Commission as an "Advanced Grassroots Party Organization of the Entire Military." In the honors room, plaques and trophies are displayed in quiet rows. "All of this was achieved step by step by the detachment's cadres leading us," one soldier said with pride.

"Revolutionaries must be heroes in the face of difficulty." (革命者要在困难面前逞英雄。) These words of Jiao Yulu are likewise carved into the bones of the detachment's officers and soldiers. During the interview, Political Instructor Wang recounted a past event.

That year, the contingent organized a record-breaking military skills competition. Squad Leader Huang Ke signed up for the individual all-around event—high in points, high in difficulty; everyone knew it was a "tough bone to gnaw."

More than a month before the competition, he began training every morning at 5 a.m. Where others ran 5 kilometers, he ran 8; where others carried 10 kilograms, he added to 15. A single combatives move he would drill until his muscles trembled, then grit his teeth and do another set.

On the day of the competition, Huang Ke ranked near the top in the first four events. In the final event—combatives sparring—he accidentally sprained his ankle. The coach asked if he could continue; he gritted his teeth and said: "I can!" In the end, enduring piercing pain, he limped through the competition and took second place overall.

Afterward, someone asked how he could push so hard while injured. Huang Ke said: "Secretary Jiao kept working even when his liver pain was so bad he couldn't straighten up—what is this compared to that? A soldier cannot be a deserter in the face of difficulty."

During the study and education on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on political achievement, some raised doubts: Now that we emphasize training with science and technology, with simulation equipment and data analysis all in place, is it still necessary to train this hard?

The reporters saw on the detachment's training schedule that, in addition to standard events, there were extreme physical conditioning and adversarial training sessions. Detachment Commander Li explained: "Equipment has improved and conditions have gotten better, but the spirit (精气神) of 'being heroes in the face of difficulty' must not be lost. In his day, Secretary Jiao rejected the mentality of waiting, relying, and demanding, and went deep into the front lines to explore and summarize methods for combating desertification; today, we cannot let the possession of advanced equipment cause us to lose the fighting spirit (血性) to press forward in the face of adversity."

Over the past five years, in military skills competitions organized by higher authorities, the detachment has taken first place in individual events 41 times, and more than 230 personnel have received commendations from higher authorities. As the reporters leafed through the record books, behind each name lay a story full of sweat and fighting spirit.

Jiao Yulu often said that Communist Party members should appear before the masses at the moment when the masses are in their greatest difficulty. These words have long since transformed into the conscious action of the detachment's officers and soldiers.

That winter, a factory near the detachment's garrison suddenly caught fire. Thick smoke billowed and flames shot into the sky. Squad Leader Wang Jintao was the first to spot the fire through video surveillance and report it, and the detachment immediately deployed. Inside the fire scene, waves of heat scorched the skin and visibility was nearly zero. Wang Jintao bent low, calling out and searching through the thick smoke again and again. When the rescue was over, his arms had been burned by the high temperature and his face was blackened by smoke. Afterward, someone asked if it hurt. He said: "At the time, there was only one thought in my mind—save people!"

Apprehending criminals, neutralizing dangerous situations, rescuing the masses… Over the years, the detachment's officers and soldiers have performed their duties wholeheartedly with the people in their hearts, and the local people affectionately call them "Guardians of Tong Township" (桐乡卫士).

The reporters followed the detachment's officers and soldiers to Zhangzhuang Village in Lankao County. This was once the county's largest wind gap, and also the place where Jiao Yulu found the method for combating desertification. Since 2014, the detachment has formed a "kinship" (亲戚) relationship with Zhangzhuang Village. Officers and soldiers frequently enter the village with shovels and pickaxes in hand, helping villagers build greenhouses, repair roads, and plant saplings. Today, rustic-decorated shops open their doors to welcome customers, shelves are filled with a wide variety of local products, and beneath the Jiao Tong trees there is a scene of abundant harvest (好"丰"景).

Old Wang at the Chengguan Township nursing home once witnessed Jiao Yulu leading cadres and the masses in combating the "three harms" (三害—wind, sand, and waterlogging), and he will never forget that "Secretary Jiao was someone who, even if he had only one steamed bun, would share it with us." Over the years, the detachment has established a co-development relationship with the nursing home, visiting the elderly every weekend or holiday to clean, tidy beds, cut hair, and chat. "In his day, Secretary Jiao treated the people as family; now you are as close to me as my own sons!" Old Wang said, clasping the hands of the officers and soldiers.

During the study and education on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on political achievement, someone once asked: The detachment's primary mission is duty and training—won't helping and supporting the masses divert energy?

Political Instructor Wang told the reporters that they organize everyone to study Jiao Yulu's deeds, continuously correcting deviations and purifying thinking. Officers and soldiers have come to deeply understand that training soldiers and loving the people not only do not conflict—they complement each other. When Jiao Yulu combated the "three harms" in his day, wasn't it precisely to let the people live a good life? The foundation of the people's military is the people; supporting the masses is not a "sideline" but a "duty." Duty and training are to protect the people; supporting the masses is to serve the people.

"Heart for heart, measure for measure." (人心换人心，八两换半斤。) Because they have always kept the people in their hearts, creating real achievements for the people through dedication and commitment, and guarding the people's happiness with wholehearted sincerity, the detachment has been rated an "Advanced Unit in Supporting the Government and Loving the People" (拥政爱民先进单位) by the garrison area for 25 consecutive years.

In those days, when heavy snow blocked the doors, Jiao Yulu endured severe liver pain to go from village to village visiting the poor and suffering. Facing an elderly person bedridden with illness who asked "who are you," he spoke from the depths of his heart: "I am your son…" These words have always been kept in the hearts of the people of Lankao. "Whatever political achievements there may be, only those recognized by the masses are good political achievements. Being close to and loving the people is not about giving money or goods—it is about us treating the masses as family, so that the masses treat us as family in return." Detachment Commander Li said.

Before the interview concluded, the reporters came once more to stand beneath the paulownia trees in the garrison. A gentle breeze passed through, the paulownia leaves rustled, and scenes of the profound bond between the military and the people (鱼水情深) surfaced one after another before their eyes: the person rescued from the fire who afterward clutched Wang Jintao's hand and wept; the elderly woman at the flood-control front line who wiped sweat from the soldiers' brows, her eyes full of tenderness; the children who spontaneously stood at attention and saluted as the military vehicles withdrew, a sight that moved people to tears…

The Jiao Tong tree speaks no words, yet a path forms beneath it of its own accord. (焦桐无语，下自成蹊。) The tree Jiao Yulu planted in those days has now become a forest; the spirit he left behind has also taken root in the hearts of officers and soldiers.

**The Jiao Tong Tree as Mirror: Solid Work and Steadfast Action**

*■ Tian Zhe*

The Jiao Tong tree, rooted in sandy soil, blooms full in spring, casts dense shade in summer, sheds its leaves in silence in autumn, and retains its upright character through winter. Comrade Jiao Yulu worked in Lankao for 475 days, fulfilling his mission with his life and tempering his original aspiration (初心) through solid work, setting the benchmark for serving the Party for the public and governing for the people.

Chairman Xi has emphasized: "The Jiao Yulu Spirit (焦裕禄精神)—of being close to and loving the people, of arduous struggle, of seeking truth from facts, of pressing forward in the face of adversity, and of selfless dedication—was, is, and will remain a precious spiritual treasure of our Party, and will never become outdated." Over the years, the officers and soldiers of the PAP Lankao Detachment have transformed Jiao Yulu's original aspiration of "sharing the Party's worries and adding luster to the Party" and his sentiment of "keeping all the people in his heart while leaving no room for himself" into the image of those who bravely stand at the head of the line on the training ground, the charge of those who bravely advance in the opposite direction at critical moments, and the actions of those who are close to and love the people with wholehearted dedication. Each weighty honor and each commendation from the masses is their powerful answer to the questions of "for whom political achievements are built, what kind of political achievements to build, and what to rely on to build political achievements."

In the study and education on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on political achievement, all Party member cadres should hold up the Jiao Yulu Spirit as a mirror, a ruler, and a benchmark—frequently reflecting on their original aspiration, measuring their sense of responsibility, and correcting their direction—always maintaining the drive to get things done, the tenacity to carry out work, and the closeness to the masses. In doing so, the achievements we produce will stand the test of time, and the "trunk" of our striving will, like the Jiao Tong tree, take deep root and grow with full branches and dense leaves.

60多年前，焦裕禄在这里种下一株泡桐幼苗，也种下“千顷澄碧”的希望。如今，这棵泡桐笔直挺立，亭亭如盖，需3人合抱，老百姓深情地唤它“焦桐”。 距“焦桐”5公里处，就是武警开封支队兰考中队营区。营区内，焦裕禄的雕像静静伫立。阳光洒下，他肩披外套、双手叉腰、目视远方，目光坚毅而温暖。身后那片泡桐，枝繁叶茂，遮起一片浓荫。 中队每一茬新兵下队后，第一堂课就设在“焦桐”树下；每一批老兵退伍前，都会带着新兵认养泡桐。老兵们常说：“焦书记当年给老百姓挡风沙，我们驻守一方守护平安，干的是一件事。” 记者来到中队采访时，临近中午。营区安静，只有风吹桐叶的沙沙声。几名刚结束训练的战士坐在泡桐树下，与记者讲起焦裕禄的故事，像是聊一位“老熟人”。 “干部不领，水牛掉井。”焦裕禄离开60多年了，但这句话，中队的干部战士都记在心里。 当年焦裕禄说这话的时候，正带领兰考干部查风口、探水路。他知道，群众有无限的创造力，但如果没有领导，这力量就发挥不出来，如同一头水牛掉进井里，有劲无处使。这个道理，在中队带兵人眼里，就是“看我的”“跟我上”。 那年，驻地发生一起恶性案件，犯罪嫌疑人负隅顽抗。抓捕任务下达时，时任屈中队长正发着高烧。战友们劝他休息，他却斩钉截铁地说：“抓捕任务要紧，立即出发！” 夜色中，屈中队长带队摸进犯罪嫌疑人居住的小区。锁定目标后，他一脚踹开房门，第一个冲进去。犯罪嫌疑人手持匕首拼死反抗，搏斗中，屈中队长的右手大拇指被折断。他忍着剧痛夺下匕首，将对方死死摁住。 有人说他是硬汉，屈中队长却说：“干部冲在前，战士跟着干。我要是退了，谁还往前上？” 焦裕禄当年忍着肝痛处处干在前，不也是这个理吗？领导带头，万事不愁。“领”就要敢为人先，注定有风险挑战；“领”就要率之以行，不怕流血流汗。 在树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育中，曾有人问：现在科技发达了，执勤巡逻有了电子眼、智能哨兵，干部还要不要事事冲在前？ 记者在中队作战勤务值班室看到，大屏幕上辖区每个角落都看得清清楚楚。但中队王指导员告诉记者：“科技再发达，也代替不了干部站在战士前面作表率；设备再先进，也无法传递干部与战士并肩战斗的温情。‘干部不领，水牛掉井’，这个道理什么时候都不过时。” 焦裕禄当年卷起裤腿、拄着木棍，冒着风雨蹚泥水、查风口，实地调研、访贫问苦。如今在兰考中队，训练场上站排头的，是干部；任务面前第一个冲上去的，是干部；危险时刻叫响“跟我上”的，还是干部。 这些年，中队荣立集体一等功1次、二等功6次、三等功17次，连续26年被总队评为“基层建设先进中队”，中队党支部被中央军委表彰为“全军先进基层党组织”。荣誉室里，一块块奖牌、一个个奖杯静静陈列。“这些都是中队干部带着我们一步一个脚印干出来的。”一名战士自豪地说。 “革命者要在困难面前逞英雄。”焦裕禄说的这句话，同样刻在了中队官兵的骨子里。采访中，王指导员向记者讲起一件往事。 那年，总队组织创破纪录比武竞赛，班长黄柯报名参加个人全能项目——分值高、难度大，谁都知道这是块“硬骨头”。 赛前一个多月，他每天清晨5点开始训练。别人跑5公里，他跑8公里；别人负重10公斤，他加到15公斤。一个擒敌动作，练到肌肉发抖，咬咬牙再来一组。 比武当天，前四个课目黄柯名列前茅。最后一项擒敌对抗，他不慎扭伤了脚踝。教练员问他能不能坚持，他咬着牙说：“能！”最终，他忍着钻心疼痛，一瘸一拐地完成了比赛，夺得总分第二名。 赛后有人问，受了伤咋还这么拼。黄柯说：“焦书记当年肝痛得直不起腰还坚持工作，我这算啥？当兵的不能在困难面前当逃兵。” 在树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育中，曾有人产生疑惑：现在讲求科技练兵，模拟器材、数据分析样样俱全，训练还有必要这样拼命吗？ 记者在中队训练计划表上看到，除了常规课目，还有极限体能和对抗训练。栗中队长解释说：“装备好了，条件改善了，但‘困难面前逞英雄’的精气神不能丢。当年焦书记摒弃等靠要思想，深入一线摸索总结治沙办法；今天，我们也不能因为有了先进装备，就丢了迎难而上的血性。” 近5年来，在上级组织的比武竞赛中，中队41次获单项第一，230多人受到上级表彰。记者翻阅成绩册，一个个名字背后，满是汗水和血性的故事。 焦裕禄常说，共产党员应该在群众最困难的时候，出现在群众的面前。这句话，早已化作中队官兵的行动自觉。 那年冬天，中队营区附近一家工厂突发大火。浓烟滚滚，火光冲天。班长王金涛第一个从视频监控中发现火情并报告，中队立即出动。火场里，热浪灼人，能见度几乎为零。王金涛俯身弯腰，在浓烟中一遍遍呼喊搜寻。救援结束后，他的手臂被高温灼伤，脸上被浓烟熏黑。事后有人问他疼不疼，他说：“当时心里只有一个念头——救人！” 抓歹徒、排险情、救群众……这些年，中队官兵尽心履职、心系群众，当地老百姓亲切地称他们“桐乡卫士”。 记者跟随中队官兵来到兰考县张庄村。这里曾是全县最大的风口，也是焦裕禄当年找到治沙方法的地方。2014年起，中队和张庄村结了“亲戚”。官兵经常拿着锹、扛起镐进村，帮群众建大棚、修道路、种树苗。如今，装饰古朴的店铺开门迎客，品类繁多的特产摆满货架，焦桐树下一派好“丰”景。 城关乡敬老院的王大爷，当年曾目睹过焦裕禄带领干部群众治“三害”，永远记着“焦书记是有一个馍，也要分给我们的人”。这些年，中队与敬老院建立起共建关系，每逢周末或节假日就去看望老人，打扫卫生、整理床铺、理发聊天。“当年焦书记把老百姓当亲人，现在你们就像我的儿子一样亲！”王大爷拉着官兵的手说。 在树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育中，曾有人问：中队的主要任务是执勤训练，帮扶群众会不会分散精力？ 王指导员对记者说，他们组织大家学习焦裕禄事迹，不断校正偏差、提纯思想。官兵深刻认识到，练兵和爱民不但互不冲突，而且相得益彰。焦裕禄当年治“三害”，不就是为了让老百姓过上好日子吗？人民军队的根基在人民，帮扶不是“副业”，而是“本分”。执勤训练是为保护人民，帮扶群众是为服务人民。 “人心换人心，八两换半斤。”因为始终把人民放在心中，以担当奉献创造为民实绩，以赤诚之心守护人民幸福，中队连续25年被驻地评为“拥政爱民先进单位”。 当年，大雪封门，焦裕禄强忍肝痛走村入户，访贫问苦。面对卧病在床的老人问“你是谁”，他言出肺腑：“我是您的儿子……”这句话，兰考群众一直记在心坎上。“这政绩，那政绩，群众认可才是好政绩。亲民爱民，不是帮钱帮物，而是我们把群众当亲人，群众才把我们当家人。”栗中队长说。 采访结束前，记者再次来到营区的泡桐树下。微风吹过，桐叶沙沙，那些鱼水情深的画面，一幕幕浮现在眼前：那位被从火场里救出的群众，事后拉着王金涛的手直掉眼泪；抗洪一线，为官兵擦汗的老大娘眼里满是疼爱；军车撤离，那些自发敬礼的娃娃令人泪目…… 焦桐无语，下自成蹊。焦裕禄当年种下的那棵树，如今已是一片林；他留下的精神，也在官兵心中扎了根。 插图：方 汉 焦桐为镜，实干笃行 ■田 哲 扎根沙土的焦桐，春来花开满枝，夏至浓荫蔽日，秋去落叶无声，冬临风骨犹存。焦裕禄同志在兰考工作475天，以生命践行使命、用实干砥砺初心，树立了立党为公、执政为民的标杆。 习主席强调：“亲民爱民、艰苦奋斗、科学求实、迎难而上、无私奉献的焦裕禄精神，过去是、现在是、将来仍然是我们党的宝贵精神财富，永远不会过时。”多年来，武警兰考中队官兵把焦裕禄“为党分忧、为党添彩”的初心、“心中装着全体人民、唯独没有他自己”的情怀，化作训练场上勇立排头的身影、危急关头勇敢逆行的冲锋、亲民爱民倾情奉献的行动。一个个沉甸甸的荣誉、一次次群众的褒奖，正是他们对“政绩为谁而树、树什么样的政绩、靠什么树政绩”的有力回答。 在树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育中，广大党员干部应把焦裕禄精神当作一面镜子、一把尺子、一根标杆，时常照一照初心、量一量担当、正一正方向，始终保持干事业的拼劲、抓工作的韧劲、对群众的亲劲。如此，我们做出的业绩就会经得起时间检验，奋斗的“树干”就会像焦桐一般，深深扎根、枝繁叶茂。
