Cultivate Martial Spirit and Courage (血性胆气): Continuously Accumulate the Confidence to Dare to Fight and Win Through Grueling Training (摸爬滚打)

培育血性胆气，在摸爬滚打中不断积蓄敢打必胜的底气
PLA Daily (解放军报) 5 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A PLA political education article, likely from official military media, uses three Long March engagements—the Dapojie Gorge ambush (1934, 6th Red Army Corps), the Songjia Ridge deception action (Red Army Northern Expedition Vanguard), and the Dushu Town bayonet fight (25th Red Army)—to define 血性胆气 (martial spirit and courage) and argue it must be cultivated through 实战化训练 (combat-realistic training) and 摸爬滚打 (grueling physical training). This is standard political work content; its value is as a record of how the PLA currently frames courage—distinguishing reckless 匹夫之勇 from disciplined 有勇有谋—for junior officers and soldiers, not as evidence of a new training policy or changed operational posture.
Translation

● Cultivating martial spirit and courage (血性胆气) is an important component of improving soldiers' character and an important guarantee for the ability to win battles.

"War is a matter of courage." Cultivating martial spirit and courage (血性胆气) is an important component of improving soldiers' character and an important guarantee for the ability to win battles. In China's outstanding traditional culture, the reckless bravery (匹夫之勇) of acting on momentary impulse was never held in esteem; instead, full affirmation was given to the value commitment expressed in "to see what is right and not do it is cowardice" and to the sense of mission embodied in "face hardship and never seek easy escape." This fearless courage flowing through the nation's bloodline was forged into the blood of revolutionary soldiers in the fires of the 25,000-li Long March, and cast into the indelible martial spirit and courage of the People's Army.

In the autumn of 1934, the 6th Red Army Corps, serving as the Long March vanguard, advanced toward the Hunan-Guizhou border. While passing through the Dapojie Gorge at Xiaoshui Village in Tongdao County, it was suddenly ambushed by enemy forces. The enemy occupied high ground on both sides and fired down from above, placing the Red Army in a desperate situation that risked total annihilation. At this critical juncture, a vanguard platoon of more than 30 men from the 18th Red Division was ordered to hold off the enemy. They launched a frontal assault on the flanking high ground to draw enemy fire and tear open a gap for the main force to break out. The soldiers tasked with the blocking action carried heavy machine guns and charged bravely up the hillside through a hail of bullets. After seizing the high ground, they attacked the enemy's flank with fierce fire, causing the enemy to mistakenly believe that Red Army main-force reinforcements had arrived, whereupon the enemy concentrated its strength to assault the hilltop. Over the course of more than three hours, five enemy charges were pinned down hard at the foot of the slope. By the end of the battle, only eight wounded soldiers remained on the position, all ammunition exhausted, and encircling enemy troops had stormed the summit and driven them to the edge of a cliff. The eight soldiers smashed their weapons, straightened their uniforms, shouted "Long live the Red Army," and leapt from the cliff. A fight to the death that embraced death in order to live bought survival for the entire unit. Though small in scale, this battle gave full expression to the soldier's martial spirit (血性) of "where duty calls, one does not shrink even from death."

Also involving a covering mission, the battle at Songjia Ridge on the Anhui-Zhejiang-Jiangxi border displayed another dimension of martial spirit and courage (血性胆气). In September 1934, the Red Army's Northern Expedition Vanguard for Resisting Japan was passing through the area with Nationalist Army forces in close pursuit. Had the main force been pinned down, the march tempo would have been completely disrupted, with unthinkable consequences. A single squad from the reconnaissance company assigned to the rear guard voluntarily took on the task of holding back the pursuers. Exploiting the terrain of interlocking mountain gullies, they simultaneously raised red flags and opened fire from two positions—Songjia Ridge and Diaojin Mountain. In the darkness, both enemy columns mistook the other for the Red Army main force and engaged in fierce exchanges of fire across the deep ravine. The reconnaissance squad, using the sound of gunfire as cover, silently withdrew from their positions and slipped away along an ancient road to rejoin the main force. The two enemy columns fought each other until midnight before realizing they had been deceived, by which time the Red Army main force had long since moved on. In this engagement, the Red Army annihilated more than a hundred enemy troops at minimal cost, using a single squad to tie down several regiments of pursuers. This demonstrates that combining courage with cunning (有勇有谋) is the mature form of a soldier's martial spirit and courage (血性胆气) and the supreme realm to be pursued in winning future battles.

A soldier's martial spirit and courage (血性胆气) must ultimately prove itself in real combat. In November 1934, the advance units of the 25th Red Army reached the vicinity of Dushu Town in Fangcheng County and were suddenly ambushed by a large Nationalist force. At the time, a cold wave had struck suddenly, bringing rain and snow; temperatures plummeted below freezing. Red Army soldiers, clad only in thin clothing after a long march, had frozen hands and feet, and their rifle bolts were iced over and could not fire normally. The enemy pressed forward step by step with superior firepower, and the unit was temporarily thrown on the defensive. At this critical moment, Red Army commanders took the lead in charging to the front line. Soldiers fixed bayonets and swung rifle butts, rushing into the enemy ranks through dense fire and engaging in close-quarters combat. Frozen hands could not grip knife handles, so they clamped them under their arms and pressed with their bodies; when comrades in front fell, those behind immediately filled the gap. This hand-to-hand struggle lasted several hours. Red Army soldiers, driven by a spirit of fearlessness in the face of death and refusal to accept defeat, tore open the enemy's encirclement by sheer force and opened the way to continue northward. This battle was won entirely through the soldiers' courage that feared no sacrifice (无惧牺牲的胆气)—this is the most plain and most authentic expression of a soldier's martial spirit and courage (血性胆气).

Looking back at these battles of the Long March, the tactics differed and the scenes varied, yet the spiritual core was consistent: place the revolutionary overall situation first, place the mission and task foremost; dare to step forward at moments of life and death, dare to charge ahead in the face of a powerful enemy, and hold on through adversity. Seek nothing but victory; spare nothing for the sake of victory. This is the precious spiritual wealth the Long March has left us, and the combat gene passed down from generation to generation in the People's Army.

Martial spirit and courage (血性胆气) are not innate; they must be cultivated through long-term character development and tempering. Today, as we look back on the history of the Long March and revisit the stories of martyrs and heroes, we must ultimately bring this to bear on the cultivation and practice of martial spirit and courage (血性胆气). As revolutionary soldiers of the new era, we must consolidate the foundation of faith, study the Party's innovative theories deeply and carefully, draw spiritual nourishment from the history of the Party and the military, clearly understand the fundamental question of "for whom we carry the rifle and for whom we fight," and ensure that martial spirit (血性) has a soul and courage (胆气) has direction. We must temper our will and train our capabilities through combat-realistic training (实战化训练), bring forth the fighting spirit of "fearing neither hardship nor death (一不怕苦、二不怕死)," treat every exercise and training event and every mission as a battlefield test, forge a tough work style through grueling training (摸爬滚打), and continuously accumulate the confidence to dare to fight and win. We must steel our sense of responsibility through major missions, boldly take on the most arduous tasks and confront the most severe challenges, dare to advance in the face of difficulty and overcome obstacles, and through shouldering heavy burdens and working hard and practically, let the spirit stand tall and let courage burst forth—winning new victories and creating new glory on the new Long March.

Original Chinese
●培育血性胆气，是提升军人修养的重要内容，也是能打胜仗的重要保证 “夫战，勇气也。”培育血性胆气，是提升军人修养的重要内容，也是能打胜仗的重要保证。在中华优秀传统文化中，从来不推崇逞一时之快的匹夫之勇，而是充分肯定“见义不为，无勇也”的价值坚守和“临难毋苟免”的使命担当。这种流淌在民族血脉里的无畏勇气，在二万五千里长征的烽火淬炼中注入革命军人的血脉，熔铸成人民军队永不磨灭的血性胆气。 1934年秋，红6军团作为长征先遣队挺进湘黔边界，行至通道县小水村大坡界峡谷时，突遭敌军伏击。敌军占据两侧高地居高临下射击，红军陷入可能全军覆没的险境。危急关头，红18师一支30余人的前卫排奉命阻击，强攻侧翼高地吸引敌军火力，为大部队突围撕开缺口。负责阻击的官兵们抬着重机枪、迎着弹雨，向山腰勇猛冲锋，夺占高地后以火力猛烈攻击敌侧翼，让敌军误判为红军主力增援，转而集中兵力猛攻山头。在3个多小时的时间里，敌军5次冲锋都被死死压在坡下。战至最后，阵地上仅剩8名负伤战士，弹药全部耗尽，迂回之敌冲上山顶将他们逼至悬崖边缘。8名战士砸毁枪支、整理军装，高呼“红军万岁”纵身跃下悬崖。一场向死而生的殊死阻击，换来了整支部队的生机。这场战斗虽规模不大，却把“义之所在，虽死不辞”的军人血性展现得淋漓尽致。 同样是担负掩护任务，发生在皖浙赣交界处的送驾岭战斗，展现了血性胆气的另一面。1934年9月，红军北上抗日先遣队途经当地，身后国民党军紧追不舍，一旦主力被咬住，行军节奏被彻底打乱，后果不堪设想。负责断后的侦察连一个班主动接下了牵制追兵的任务。他们利用山地沟壑交错的地形，在送驾岭与钓金山两处高地同时竖起红旗、分头开火。夜色里，两路敌军都把对方当成了红军主力，隔着深沟展开激烈对射。侦察班则借着枪声掩护，悄无声息撤出阵地，沿古道追上了大部队。两路敌军混战到半夜才发现中计，此时红军主力早已走远。此战，红军以极小代价歼敌百余人，用一个班的兵力拖住了几个团的追兵。由此可见，有勇有谋，是军人血性胆气的成熟形态，也是制胜未来应当追求的至高境界。 军人的血性胆气，最终要在真刀真枪的较量中见真章。1934年11月，红25军先头部队到达方城独树镇附近，突遭国民党重兵伏击。当时寒潮突至，雨雪交加，气温骤降到冰点以下，红军将士身着单衣长途跋涉，手脚冻僵，枪栓被冰霜冻住，无法正常击发。敌人凭借优势火力步步紧逼，部队一度陷入被动。危急时刻，红军指挥员带头冲上火线，将士们端起刺刀、抡起枪托，迎着密集火力扑向敌群，与敌展开白刃格斗。冻僵的手握不住刀柄，就用胳膊夹、用身体顶；前面的战友倒下了，后面的人立刻补位。这场血肉相搏的厮杀持续了数小时，红军将士凭着一股不怕死、不服输的劲头，硬是撕开了敌人的包围圈，为继续北上打开了通道。这场战斗，全靠将士们以一股无惧牺牲的胆气拼出胜利，这就是军人血性胆气最朴素、最真实的反映。 回望长征中的这些战斗，打法不同、场景各异，精神内核却一致：以革命大局为重、以使命任务为要，生死关头敢站出来、强敌面前敢冲上去、困境之中能扛下来，除了胜利一无所求，为了胜利一无所惜。这是长征留给我们的宝贵精神财富，也是人民军队代代相传的战斗基因。 血性胆气并非天生拥有，要靠长期的修养和磨砺才能养成。今天我们回望长征历史、重温英烈故事，最终要落到对血性胆气的培育践行上。作为新时代革命军人，要筑牢信仰之基，深学细悟党的创新理论，从党史军史中汲取精神养分，认清“为谁扛枪、为谁打仗”的根本问题，让血性有灵魂、胆气有方向。要在实战化训练中磨意志练本领，拿出“一不怕苦、二不怕死”的战斗精神，把每一次演训、每一项任务都当成战场考验，在摸爬滚打中锤炼过硬作风，不断积蓄敢打必胜的底气。要在重大任务中砥砺担当，勇于承担最艰巨的任务、应对最严峻的挑战，敢于迎难而上、攻坚克难，在知重负重、苦干实干中让精神挺立、让胆气迸发，在新的长征路上不断取得新胜利、创造新辉煌。
