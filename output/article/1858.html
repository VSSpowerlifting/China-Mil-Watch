About this analysis
Summary
The 7th Duty Detachment, Haixi Branch, People's Armed Police Qinghai General Corps guards the two permanent posts flanking the 1,686-meter Kunlun Mountain Tunnel at 4,868 meters elevation — the highest permanent duty post on the Qinghai-Tibet Railway — where Senior Sergeant First Class Wang Shang has served nearly 18 years and the unit has logged 210,000 kilometers of patrols and protected more than 200,000 train passages since 2006. This is standard PAP political-work feature content, published under a Xinhua series framing loyalty and hardship endurance as institutional virtues; its value is as a record of how the PAP presents long-term retention and mission commitment to a broad audience, not as evidence of changed posture or capability at this post. The framing around Party membership applications, stone-map rituals, and handwritten training notebooks points to the specific messaging problem the institution is managing: sustaining volunteer re-enlistment and morale at a remote, physically punishing post with no rotation incentive beyond ideological commitment.

Loyalty Inscribed at 4,868 Meters Above Sea Level

Xinhua News Agency reporters Sun Luming and Zhang Ruijie

Heading west from Golmud, snow-capped mountains roll one after another and the Gobi stretches without end.

"That year I came to the foot of the Kunlun Mountains, a soldier ascending to the sentry post in the clouds; the romance of youth before the edelweiss, the soldier's pride beneath the mountain-pass moon; let the cold wind blow through memories of green, let the snow mountains bear witness to the altitude of faith…"

The officers and soldiers of the 7th Detachment who accompanied us up the mountain hummed their unit song, "Sentry Post in the Clouds," in the vehicle. After nearly four hours of jolting along a "washboard road," our reporter team finally arrived at the northern foot of the Kunlun Mountains.

Here stands the "sentry post in the clouds" at 4,868 meters above sea level — the highest permanent duty post on the Qinghai-Tibet Railway — where the oxygen content is only half that at sea level.

At the tunnel entrance, a large slogan blazes in vivid red: "A place of hardship, a place far away, a fine place to render meritorious service and build a career."

This is the oath of loyalty of the officers and soldiers of the 7th Duty Detachment, Haixi Branch, People's Armed Police Qinghai General Corps.

"A Soldier Ascending to the Sentry Post in the Clouds"

On May 1, 2026, detachment officers and soldiers patrol at the entrance to the Kunlun Mountain Tunnel. Photo by Yang Hao.

The sentry tower is not large — only two stories.

But to reach the "clouds," one must climb a further 110 steps.

Ascending the steep stone steps, accompanied by the chest tightness, shortness of breath, and intensifying headaches that come with the rising altitude, our every step was enveloped by the heavy sound of breathing and the rapid pounding of our hearts.

Gripping the railing, we climbed step by step. On the steep slope, at intervals, stone blocks are carved with inscriptions: one-third of the way up reads "retreat in the face of difficulty, give up halfway"; at the midpoint, "halfway through the course, take a brief rest"; and when "mission accomplished, victory achieved" finally came into view, we had reached the top.

Looking out from the "sentry post in the clouds," the view is an unchanging expanse of wilderness and mountains. Since the Qinghai-Tibet Railway opened along its full length in 2006, the officers and soldiers of the 7th Duty Detachment have stood guard here for nearly 20 years. Day after day, young officers and soldiers keep watch at the post, protecting the "road to heaven" beneath their feet.

The 1,686-meter-long Kunlun Mountain Tunnel is the number-one control project on the entire Qinghai-Tibet Railway line, with treacherous terrain and complex geological conditions. The two ends of this tunnel — the world's longest tunnel in a high-altitude permafrost zone — are the 7th Detachment's two permanent duty posts.

On June 10, 2021, officers and soldiers of the 7th Duty Detachment patrol during a thunderstorm. Photo by Yang Hao.

Senior Sergeant First Class Wang Shang, who extended his service, has stood guard here for nearly 18 years and is the oldest soldier at the post. "The first time I stood watch, after just 20 minutes I started feeling dizzy, my vision went dark, and I had to lean against the wall to stay upright." The scene of his first arrival at the post remains vivid in his memory to this day.

The officers and soldiers joke that this is the plateau's exclusive "welcome gift" to them.

The most recent recipient of this "gift" was the post's youngest soldier, Li Shujie. In September 2024, he traveled thousands of li from a small coastal city in Guangdong to the "sentry post in the clouds." "On the first night, I wanted to sleep but couldn't — difficulty breathing, headache, eyes swollen and aching," said Li Shujie.

In the Kunlun Mountains, loyalty and dedication are embodied in every day and every night, in every minute of eating, sleeping, and walking.

On the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, with an average elevation exceeding 4,000 meters, threats to bodily function and to life itself lurk everywhere. "Even a minor cold can trigger high-altitude illnesses such as pulmonary edema or cerebral edema," said Political Instructor Jin Shenglin.

Yet over 20 years, not a single officer or soldier in the detachment has voluntarily requested a transfer.

In just over a year, Li Shujie has tasted every flavor the "plateau" has to offer. Having just celebrated his 20th birthday, he has come to understand more deeply the poetry and distant horizons of youth, and has solemnly submitted his application to join the Party to the organization.

This soldier from the south has completed the leap from the sea to the "clouds."

On New Year's Eve 2024, officers and soldiers stand duty at the entrance to the Kunlun Mountain Tunnel on the Qinghai-Tibet Railway. Photo by Yang Hao.

"The Soldier's Pride Beneath the Mountain-Pass Moon"

In June, a cold wind still blows through the heart of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau.

The soldiers describe it this way: there are only two seasons here — winter, and "approximately winter."

Here, the Kunlun Range temporarily forecloses the possibility of seasonal change. Snow mountains and wilderness are the year-round, unchanging scenery.

Because of this, every officer and soldier harbors a stubborn attachment to the color green: they use foam boxes as seedling trays, planting cold-resistant flower and grass seeds, quietly tucking all their hopes for life and vitality inside.

When officers and soldiers return from duty and resupply runs in Golmud, their bags never lack for potted plants of various colors and green seedling shoots. Everyone carries a fixation in their heart — a desire to bring green up to the post — yet the vast majority of plants cannot survive more than half a month.

Political Instructor Jin Shenglin is the most stubborn of all. Before one return to the post, he carefully selected a pot of asparagus fern and a pot of chrysanthemums, carried them all the way up to the sentry post through a roundabout journey, and tended to them with great care. In the end, they stubbornly stayed green in the Kunlun Mountains for ten days.

Just ten days — yet no one felt any regret.

Everyone often says: on the plateau, seeing a vivid patch of green is the same as seeing life itself. The green grass and trees that are everywhere on the plains are the most extravagant scenery here.

For Private First Class Zhang Lianzong, something even more extravagant is playing a full basketball game.

He has been looking forward to it ever since he arrived at the detachment over a year ago. But in reality, even in warm weather, they can play at most a half-court game, and a doctor and oxygen tanks must be on standby throughout.

Night deepened, and a train whistle cut through the sleeping valley. Private First Class Li Gaoyu knew: this was a passenger train, approximately 10 to 12 carriages.

The railway tracks of the Qinghai-Tibet Railway run just four or five meters from the sentry post; the roar of trains hurtling past and the silence of the mountains face off at the soldiers' bedsides.

It would be hard for anyone to imagine that the young soldiers have found amusement in this — by listening to the sounds, they can determine the approximate arrival time of a train, whether it is a freight or passenger train, whether it is heading up the mountain or down.

Opening veteran Wang Shang's "sentry post diary," the first several pages are densely filled with tally marks (正字). After arriving at the post, he recorded every train he stood guard over.

1:00 a.m., 4:00 a.m.… Every officer and soldier carries a familiar train timetable in their heart. Whenever the thin air makes sleep impossible, they lie in the darkness waiting for and confirming each whistle blast. The train passes; they wait for the bed boards to stop trembling, and then everything goes quiet again.

When Li Gaoyu stepped up to the sentry post, the full moon happened to be rising just above the towering Kunlun Mountains. On this night, he and his comrades were the Chinese People's Armed Police closest to the sky.

"Let the Snow Mountains Bear Witness to the Altitude of Faith"

Officers and soldiers conduct tactical training in the snow. Photo by Yang Hao.

On the hillside in front of the detachment compound, there is a giant map of China assembled from stones. A base of blue-gray stones, outlined in red bricks, with the six large characters "I Stand Guard for the Motherland" (我为祖国站岗) laid out in the center.

Every stone on the map was selected one by one by officers and soldiers from the Gobi wilderness, hammered into the hillside, and secured with wire. They have steadied their longing for home and country — stretching across ten thousand li — in a place very close to the sky.

Twenty years ago, the first group of officers and soldiers came here and left behind a map of China carved into the Kunlun Mountains. Since then, every year when new recruits join the detachment, the unit organizes them to repair the map, using it to inspire generation after generation of officers and soldiers to take root on the plateau.

This stone-built map, assembled from more than 40,000 Kunlun Mountain stones, is the eternal landscape of rivers and mountains in their hearts. One section of stones on the map is arranged most neatly and densely — the position everyone in their hearts loves most and holds most dear: Beijing.

Most of them have never been to Beijing. Yet the longing they carry in their hearts is deeper than anyone else's.

People often ask: what can this small sentry post do, besides guarding the tunnel?

National Highway 109 makes a sharp bend just below the post, with a steep grade and a tight curve — a stretch prone to traffic accidents. Detachment officers and soldiers have erected 15 People's Armed Police assistance point signs along more than 100 kilometers of hazardous road, with contact numbers posted publicly and available to respond around the clock.

"South of the sentry post is the Hoh Xil uninhabited zone, where wildlife is abundant and animals frequently stray onto the railway. Last year, we also rescued a railway worker who had been struck and injured by a wild yak," said Political Instructor Jin Shenglin.

One year, a sudden heavy snowfall at the Kunlun Mountain pass stranded more than 300 vehicles. The soldiers braved wind and snow, brought all the plateau medicines, supplies, and combat rations down from the mountain, and delivered water, food, and medicine to each vehicle one by one.

One year, soldiers discovered an overturned sedan during a patrol, with three people injured. On the way to the hospital, Wang Shang cradled the head of a seriously injured girl on his arm for more than three hours. At the hospital, he donated 400 milliliters of blood and advanced more than 20,000 yuan in medical expenses out of his own pocket.

"Keeping soldiers for a thousand days is really for the sake of the possible one-in-ten-thousand emergency — that is the meaning of our existence," said Jin Shenglin.

Over 20 years, generation after generation of officers and soldiers have stood guard at the "sentry post in the clouds," measuring out 210,000 kilometers of patrol routes on foot, neutralizing more than 500 dangerous situations, and protecting more than 200,000 trains in safe passage.

In 2023, the 7th Duty Detachment drilled its first water well; officers and soldiers celebrate with cheers. Photo by Yang Hao.

As his service term nears its end, veteran Wang Shang has handwritten a thick stack of "soldier-training lesson plans," covering everything from "prediction and prevention of high-altitude pulmonary edema" to "frostbite prevention during snow patrols," filling several notebooks with dense writing. Having been cooped up in the mountains for nearly two decades, the earthy smell of the plateau has bored into his bones and never left.

Trains roar past, and the sound of the wind fades into the distance. Generation after generation of young people have inscribed here their most radiant coordinates of loyalty.

(Xinhua News Agency, Xining, June 28)

忠诚镌刻在海拔4868米 新华社记者孙鲁明、张瑞杰 从格尔木一路西行，雪山起伏，戈壁无垠。 “那年我来到昆仑山下，当兵走上云端的哨卡；青春的浪漫在雪绒花前，士兵的风流在关山月下；任寒风吹过绿色的记忆，让雪山见证信仰的海拔……” 陪同我们上山的七中队官兵，在车上哼唱起他们的队歌《云端哨卡》，经过近4小时“搓板路”的颠簸，记者一行终于来到昆仑山北麓。 这里坐落着海拔4868米的“云端哨所”，青藏铁路海拔最高的固定执勤哨所，空气含氧量仅有海平面的一半。 隧道口的大字标语鲜红夺目，“苦地方，远地方，建功立业的好地方”。 这，是武警青海总队海西支队执勤七中队官兵的忠诚誓言。 “当兵走上云端的哨卡” 2026年5月1日，中队官兵在昆仑山隧道口巡逻。杨浩 摄 哨楼不大，只有两层。 但想走上“云端”，还要再攀上110级台阶。 沿着陡峭的石阶往上攀登，伴随着海拔升高带来的胸闷气喘、头痛加剧，我们的每一步都被沉重的呼吸声和急促的心跳声包围。 扶着栏杆，拾级而上。陡坡上，每隔一段都有一块刻着字句的石块：攀过三分之一，是“知难而退，半途而废”；行路中半，是“赛程过半，稍事休息”；等到“大功告成，旗开得胜”映入眼帘，我们终于登顶。 从“云端哨所”望出去，视野里是一成不变的荒原大山。自2006年青藏铁路全线开通以来，执勤七中队的官兵们就坚守在这里，已近20年。日复一日，年轻的官兵守着哨卡，守护着脚下的“天路”。 1686米长的昆仑山隧道是青藏铁路全线头号控制工程，地势艰险、地质条件复杂。这条世界高原多年冻土区第一长隧的两端，便是七中队的两个固定执勤哨位。 2021年6月10日，执勤七中队官兵在雷暴天气下巡逻。杨浩 摄 延期服役的一级上士王赏已经坚守了近18个年头，是哨所最老的兵。“第一次站哨，才20分钟就开始头晕目眩，眼前发黑，得扶着墙才能站稳。”初到哨所的情景，他至今记忆深刻。 官兵们戏称，这是高原送给他们专属的“欢迎礼”。 上一个收到这份“礼物”的，是哨所最年轻的战士李树杰。2024年9月，他从广东沿海小城千里迢迢奔来了“云端哨所”。“第一天夜里，想睡也睡不着，呼吸困难、头痛、两眼发胀。”李树杰说。 在昆仑山，忠诚与奉献体现在每一个白天和夜晚，体现在吃饭、睡觉、走路的每一分钟。 在平均海拔超4000米的青藏高原，处处都潜藏着对人体机能的损害和生命的威胁。“即使是小感冒，也有可能引发肺水肿、脑水肿等高原性疾病。”指导员靳盛林说。 但20年来，中队官兵无一人主动要求调离。 一年多的时间，李树杰尝尽了“高原的滋味”。刚刚度过20岁生日的他，更加读懂了青春的诗与远方，郑重地向组织递交了入党申请书。 这个来自南方的战士，完成了从大海到“云端”的跨越。 2024年除夕夜，官兵在青藏铁路昆仑山隧道口执勤。杨浩 摄 “士兵的风流在关山月下” 六月的青藏高原腹地，依旧吹着寒风。 战士们形容，这里只有两个季节：冬季和“大约在冬季”。 在这里，昆仑山脉暂时阻隔了季节变化的可能性。雪山和荒原，是常年不变的景象。 为此，每一名官兵都有着一份对绿色的执着：他们用泡沫箱作育苗盆，栽种下耐寒花种、草种，把所有对生机的期盼，都悄悄藏进去。 官兵们从格尔木执勤、补给归来，行囊里总少不了各色花草盆栽、绿植幼苗。人人心里都揣着一份执念，想把绿色搬上来，可绝大多数花草都熬不过半月。 指导员靳盛林最执拗。一次归队前，他精心挑选了一盆文竹和一盆菊花，一路辗转带上哨所，精心娇养。最终，它们硬生生地在昆仑山绿了十天。 短短十天，却没人觉得遗憾。 大家常说，在高原，能看见一抹鲜活的绿，就等于看见了生机。在平原随处可见的青草绿树，在这里却是最奢侈的风景。 对上等兵张廉忠来说，更奢侈的是，打个整场篮球赛。 来到中队一年多，他一直盼望着。但实际上，即使是天气暖和的时候，最多也只能打个半场，还需要医生和氧气瓶全程守候。 夜沉了，一声列车鸣笛划过沉睡的山谷。上等兵李高禹知道，这是一趟客车，大约10到12节车厢。 哨所四五米外就是青藏铁路的铁轨，飞驰而过的喧嚣和大山的沉默在士兵的床头对峙。 很难有人会想到，年轻的战士竟然从中找到了乐趣——听着声音，就能判断出火车到达的概略时间，货车还是客车，上山还是下山。 打开老兵王赏的“哨位日记”，前几页密密麻麻写满了“正”字。来到哨所后，他把自己守护的每趟列车都记录了下来。 凌晨1点、凌晨4点……每名官兵心中，都有一张熟悉的列车时刻表。每当氧气稀薄难以入眠时，他们都会在黑暗中等候并印证那一声声汽笛的响起。列车驶过，等床板停止颤动，一切又安静下来。 当李高禹站上哨位时，圆月恰好贴着巍巍昆仑升起。这个夜晚，他和战友们是离天最近的中国武警。 “让雪山见证信仰的海拔” 官兵进行雪地战术训练。杨浩 摄 中队院前的山坡上，有一幅用石头拼成的巨幅中国地图。青色的石块打底、红色的砖块勾边，中间铺写着“我为祖国站岗”六个大字。 地图上的每块石头，都是官兵们从戈壁荒原中一遍遍筛选出来，用锤子敲入山坡，再用铁丝一道道固定的。他们把遥遥万里的家国牵挂，稳稳安放在了离天很近的地方。 20年前，第一批官兵来到这里，留下了刻在昆仑山的中国地图。此后，每年新兵下连，中队都会组织新兵修缮地图，以此激励一代代官兵扎根高原。 这块由4万多块昆仑山石拼成的石筑版图，是他们心中永恒的山河。地图上有一方石块，排布得最为整齐紧密，那是大家心里最偏爱、也最珍重的位置——北京。 他们大多数人，都没去过北京。可他们心里的惦念，却比任何人都深沉。 常有人问，这方小小的哨位，除了守隧道，还能做些什么？ 109国道在哨所下方急急地拐了个弯，坡陡弯急，交通事故易发。中队官兵在100多公里长的危险路段旁，设置了15块武警救助点指示牌，公开联系电话，24小时有求必应。 “哨所再往南就是可可西里无人区，野生动物多，常有动物误闯铁路。去年，我们还救助了一名被野牦牛撞伤的铁路工作人员。”指导员靳盛林说。 那年，昆仑山口突降大雪，300多辆车被困。战士们顶风冒雪，将山上的高原药品、补给和战备食品全部拿下去，挨个车送水、送粮、送药。 那年，战士们在巡逻途中发现一辆轿车侧翻，有3人受伤。送医途中，王赏用胳膊垫着重伤女孩的头部3个多小时。医院里，他献血400毫升，垫付医药费2万多元。 “养兵千日，其实就是为了可能的万一，这就是我们存在的意义。”靳盛林说。 20年来，一茬茬官兵坚守“云端哨位”，用脚步丈量21万公里巡逻路，排除险情500余起，守护20多万趟列车平安穿行。 2023年执勤七中队打通第一口水井，官兵们欢呼庆祝。杨浩 摄 临近服役期满，老兵王赏手写了厚厚一沓“带兵教案”，从“高原肺水肿预判”到“雪地巡逻防冻伤”，密密麻麻写满了几个本子。在山上窝了小二十年，高原的土腥味钻进他的骨头里，再也没出来。
