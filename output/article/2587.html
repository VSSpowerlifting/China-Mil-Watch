Those Who Lead Troops Should Genuinely Care for Their Soldiers, Deepening Mutual Understanding Through Measured Flexibility and a Balance of Leniency and Strictness

带兵人应当真心爱兵，在张弛有度、宽严相济中增进彼此理解
PLA Daily (解放军报) 26 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A company political instructor writing in PLA official media recounts how rigidly applying 严管厚爱 (strict management with deep care) without emotional attentiveness caused a sergeant to conceal a family emergency rather than report it, and describes the corrective shift toward what the article calls 人文关怀 (humanistic care) and rule-of-law discipline. This is standard political work content aimed at junior officers; its value is as a record of how the institution currently frames the tension between authority and soldier welfare for company-grade cadres, particularly in units now composed predominantly of post-1995 and post-2000 conscripts. The repeated warnings against 压而不服 (suppression without genuine compliance) and substituting personal authority for law document an institutional concern that overly rigid grassroots leadership is generating passive resistance rather than cohesion.
Translation

When I first became a company political instructor, how to quickly establish authority and manage the company well became a practical challenge before me. Because I believed that "strictness is the hard truth," my standards grew ever higher and my demands ever stricter. Watching the soldiers fall into complete compliance under my management, I thought I had found the way of leading troops—until a later experience made me realize my problem.

One day, Sergeant Xiao Zhang was sent away for centralized training. His father then suffered a sudden illness and urgently needed him to return home to care for him. What puzzled me was that I learned of this only by chance, overhearing Xiao Zhang on the phone with the company clerk—he had not reported it to me on his own initiative. I had once been Xiao Zhang's platoon leader; he used to greet me with a smile, and the two of us had talked about everything. Now he was deliberately avoiding me. The magnitude of this change was something I had not anticipated. After repeated questioning on my part, Xiao Zhang spoke his mind: "Instructor, last time I didn't shout the slogan loudly enough, you criticized me in front of the whole company, and it chilled my heart. On top of that, whenever you were with me your expression was always grave and heavy. I thought you had a serious problem with me, so when I ran into difficulties I didn't dare to just speak up…" After a heart-to-heart conversation, we cleared away the estrangement between us. To resolve Xiao Zhang's urgent situation, I immediately reported it to my superiors, and the next day he was granted permission to return home and take his father to see a doctor. Xiao Zhang's family circumstances were poor; the company applied for the "Compassion Fund" (爱心基金) on his behalf in accordance with regulations and reminded him to seek medical care at a military hospital whenever possible.

After this incident, I reflected seriously and concluded that my understanding of "strict management with deep care" (严管厚爱) contained a fundamental error. Establishing authority through strictness must also be accompanied by building trust through genuine feeling. Those who lead troops should genuinely care for their soldiers, truly enter the inner world of officers and soldiers, and deepen mutual understanding and support through measured flexibility and a balance of leniency and strictness. From that point on, I no longer managed purely in a "cold tone," but instead took the initiative to integrate myself with everyone. The atmosphere in the company became considerably more harmonious as a result.

Manage strictly, but do not arbitrarily raise the bar. At present, military barracks are composed primarily of officers and soldiers born after 1995 and 2000. Young officers and soldiers think actively and hold diverse views. If those who lead troops do nothing but put on a stern face—this is not allowed, that is not permitted—it is easy to provoke a psychology of resistance. There is nothing wrong with holding officers and soldiers to strict standards and strict management in itself, but if regulations and rules are applied mechanically without accounting for the actual circumstances of officers and soldiers, and if any minor problem is immediately elevated into a matter of principle, it may look imposing on the surface while in reality planting the hidden danger of "suppression without genuine compliance" (压而不服). Manage within reason, be strict within bounds, and hold love in the heart. Strictness does not mean piling on layer after layer; it must be strictness within the law and strictness grounded in evidence. Depart from this premise and the phenomena of substituting personal words for law and substituting personal authority for law will emerge. Only when those who lead troops establish rule-of-law thinking and carry out their work in strict accordance with laws and regulations can they find the scientifically calibrated measure for managing troops and drive continuous new achievements in unit building.

Have both "hardness" and "warmth." "Set love first, set authority afterward" (爱设于先，威设于后) is a principle of troop governance put forward by ancient military strategists. It means first caring for soldiers and drawing them close, and only then establishing authority and making punishments clear—this is the only way to forge a truly capable force. Deep care is the key to leading troops, but it is not a loosening of discipline or a slackening of conduct; it is encouragement in work and concern in life. Those who lead troops must genuinely care for officers and soldiers, respect and trust them with an attitude of equality, think more from their perspective, understand more of their difficulties and hardships, and bring wholehearted enthusiasm to helping them resolve their worries and pressing concerns. One must do the work of ideological education, psychological counseling, and the like, combining strict management with effective communication and the building of consensus, and in practice emphasizing the embodiment of humanistic care (人文关怀) and management wisdom—only then can the unit, from top to bottom, be filled with warmth and with vigorous fighting spirit.

Original Chinese
刚任连队指导员时，如何快速树立威信、管好连队，成为摆在我面前的现实课题。因为信奉“严才是硬道理”，我的标准越来越高、要求越来越严。眼见战士们被我管得“服服帖帖”，我自认为找到了带兵之道，直到后来的一次经历，让我意识到自己的问题。 某天，中士小张被外派集训。可其父亲突发疾病，急需他回家照顾。让我不解的是，这件事情我是偶然听到小张和连队文书电话沟通才得知的，并非他主动报告。我曾是小张的排长，他以前见我总是笑眯眯的，我俩过去无话不谈，现在他却有意避开我，前后变化之大，让我始料未及。在我再三询问下，小张说出了心里话：“指导员，上次我口号没喊响，您当着全连的面批评了我，噎得我心凉了半截。再加上您和我在一起时总是面色凝重，我以为您对我有很大意见，所以遇到困难也不敢随便开口……”经过促膝长谈，我们消除了隔阂。为了解决小张的急难事，我立刻向上级报告，第二天他就获准回家带父亲看病。小张家庭条件不好，连队按照规定为其申请“爱心基金”，并叮嘱他尽量在军队医院就医。这件事过后，我认真反思，觉得自己对“严管厚爱”的理解存在误区。以严立威，更要以情增信。带兵人应当真心爱兵，真正走进官兵内心，在张弛有度、宽严相济中增进彼此理解、支持。于是，我不再一味以“冷色调”抓管理，而是主动和大家打成一片，连队氛围因此和谐了许多。 从严抓管，但不可随意加码。目前军营以95后、00后官兵为主，青年官兵思维活跃、观念多元，带兵人倘若一味板起面孔，这也不行，那也不准，容易引发其逆反心理。对官兵严格要求、严格管理本身没有错，但如果对制度规定机械执行，不结合官兵实际，出了一点小问题就上纲上线，表面看“有声势”，实则埋下了“压而不服”的隐患。管在“理”上，严在“格”内，爱在心中。从严不是层层加码，必须严在法内、严之有据。离开这个前提，就会出现以言代法、以权代法的现象。带兵人只有树立法治思维，严格按照法规制度开展工作，才能找到管理部队的科学尺度，推动部队建设不断取得新成效。 既有“硬度”，也有“温度”。“爱设于先，威设于后”，是古代军事家提出的治军原则，意思是先关爱士兵，使其亲近，然后立威明罚，这样才能带出一支过硬的部队。厚爱是带兵的关键，但它不是纪律松绑、作风松懈，而是工作上激励、生活上关怀。带兵人要真心关爱官兵，以平等的态度尊重信任官兵，多站在他们的角度想问题，多理解他们的难处和苦处，拿出满腔热情设身处地帮他们解决烦心事、揪心事。要做好思想教育、心理疏导等工作，把严格管理与有效沟通、凝聚共识结合起来，在实践中注重体现人文关怀与管理智慧，才能使部队上下充盈融融暖气、虎虎生气。
