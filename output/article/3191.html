A Yellowed Order Document — Why Did It Stop Everyone in Their Tracks?

一封泛黄的指令文件，为何让所有人停下了脚步
PLA Daily (解放军报) 13 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Officers and soldiers of the Quanzhou Detachment of the People's Armed Police Guilin Contingent visited the Red Army Long March Xiangjiang Battle Memorial Museum in northern Guangxi, where a December 1, 1934 emergency directive — jointly signed by the CCP Central Committee, the Central Revolutionary Military Commission, and the Red Army General Political Department — anchored a political education session led by Detachment Political Instructor Mo Jiaquan. The article documents how the detachment uses the Xiangjiang Battle's 34th Division as its founding lineage narrative, tying the directive's phrase 不是胜利就是失败 ('either we are the victors, or we are the vanquished') to a self-reported training reform that replaced evaluation-driven drill overload with foundational physical and professional development. The framing points to a standard PAP political-work genre — heritage site visit as unit cohesion exercise — and its value is as a record of how the institution links revolutionary martyrdom to present-day guard duty and training discipline, not as evidence of changed posture or capability.
Translation

"Either we are the victors, or we are the vanquished."

■ Shi Chenzhou, Chen Dengke

A directive document on display at the Red Army Long March Xiangjiang Battle Memorial Museum. Photo by Zhang Chenfei.

In July, in northern Guangxi, the Xiang River flows quietly. Officers and soldiers of the Quanzhou Detachment of the People's Armed Police Guilin Contingent walked into the Red Army Long March Xiangjiang Battle Memorial Museum, where a yellowed directive document on display in a glass case brought everyone to a halt.

"Either we are the victors, or we are the vanquished." These twelve characters are intimately familiar to every officer and soldier in the detachment. The directive contained in this document has always been the spiritual benchmark passed down from generation to generation in this detachment, which is garrisoned on the land where the Long March's bloody battle was fought.

The directive was issued in the early morning hours of December 1, 1934. That was the most brutal day of the Xiangjiang Battle, and the final pass that would determine the life or death of the Red Army.

At that moment, after breaking through three lines of encirclement, the Central Red Army had reached the Xiang River, with nearly 300,000 enemy troops closing in from all sides. The Red Army had been compressed into a narrow strip of land east of the Xiang River, and a portion of the Military Commission column and the main force had yet to cross. If the blocking line were breached, the entire Central Red Army would face the danger of being split apart and annihilated.

The situation had reached a critical juncture. In the early morning hours, this emergency directive, jointly signed by the Central Committee of the Chinese Communist Party, the Central Revolutionary Military Commission, and the Red Army General Political Department, was transmitted to every position: "One day of battle concerns our entire field army. Victory in the westward advance can open the path of development going forward; retreat will result in our field army being cut off layer by layer… Either we are the victors, or we are the vanquished. Victory or defeat concerns the overall situation. Every person must summon the highest courage in battle, disregard all sacrifice, overcome fatigue, and with resolute assault, carry out the mission of attacking and annihilating the enemy…"

There was no room to maneuver, no retreat to speak of. Victory and defeat, life and death, were compressed into a single-choice question that could not have been clearer.

When the directive reached the positions of the 34th Division of the Red Army, the enemy forces facing them had already multiplied several times over. Division Commander Chen Shuxiang led his troops in a lone, fight-to-the-death battle in the Guanyang area, buying time for the main force to cross the river. After days of bloody fighting, with ammunition and food exhausted, Chen Shuxiang was shot in the abdomen and captured. During the escort, he refused to yield, tore out his own intestines to show his resolve, and died a heroic death at only 29 years of age. The entire division paid the price of near-total annihilation, pinning the enemy forces firmly in their wake.

"The moment the order reached the position, it meant that one had to answer with one's life." Detachment Political Instructor Mo Jiaquan told the officers and soldiers: "Every single character of this directive is soaked in the resolve to burn one's boats (破釜沉舟) in a desperate situation."

These words do not feel distant to the officers and soldiers. In the Quanzhou Detachment, "either we are the victors, or we are the vanquished" has never been a slogan hung on a wall — it is a battle cry that can be roared out at a critical moment.

Not long ago, the garrison area experienced days of torrential rain and sudden flooding. In the dead of night, an emergency assembly call sounded abruptly. Officers and soldiers moved the instant the order came, racing against the floodwaters to evacuate civilians and reinforce embankments. That night, no one asked "how long will this take," and no one said "I can't hold on."

On another occasion, a wildfire broke out suddenly in the local area. The detachment assembled on an emergency basis, and then-Detachment Commander Zhou Jiafeng stepped in front of the formation and, without a lengthy mobilization speech, said only one sentence: "Comrades, either we are the victors, or we are the vanquished. Before us is the fire; behind us are the people."

"The benchmark our forebears erected in a life-or-death desperate situation has been passed into our hands — and that means not cutting a single corner when faced with a mission," said Mo Jiaquan. This consciousness of "no victory means defeat" has also quietly reshaped the detachment's philosophy of training soldiers.

The detachment once went through a misguided period of seeking quick results: blindly adding time and drills to prepare for evaluations, without differentiating by skill level or accounting for the pressure of guard duties. It looked like increased training loads, but in reality the difficult and key points were not studied in depth, the foundation was not laid solidly, and it even wore away the officers' and soldiers' enthusiasm for independent study and training. "Deviating from the standard of winning in battle means failure. On the training ground there is no middle state — only two answers: victory and defeat," Mo Jiaquan said at a reflection meeting. After that, the detachment resolutely abandoned the approach of brute-force effort, starting from strengthening foundational physical capacity, making up for professional shortcomings, and doing every guard shift well.

The Xiang River surges on, flowing without cease. The detachment has ranked near the top in the contingent's duty-training rotation for four consecutive years, with more than 50 officers and soldiers winning gold and silver medals in competitions at various levels.

"In peacetime, the guard post is the combat position, and standing guard is combat." The words of soldier Huang Lingzhi express the understanding shared by the detachment's officers and soldiers — the test of vowing to fight for victory and never conceding defeat is embodied in every guard shift and every mission. Keeping one side safe and secure, protecting the lights of ten thousand homes — this is the finest consolation to the martyrs, and the victory that our generation must win.

Original Chinese
“不为胜利者，即为战败者” ■施晨洲 陈登科 红军长征湘江战役纪念馆展出的指令文件。张辰飞 摄 7月桂北，湘江静流。武警桂林支队全州中队官兵走进红军长征湘江战役纪念馆，展柜里展出的一封泛黄的指令文件，让所有人停下了脚步。 “我们不为胜利者，即为战败者。”这短短12个字，对该中队官兵来说，再熟悉不过，这份文件中的指示，一直是这支驻守在长征血战之地的中队代代相传的精神标尺。 指令发于1934年12月1日凌晨。那是湘江战役最惨烈的一天，也是决定红军生死存亡的最后关口。 彼时，中央红军突破三道封锁线后抵达湘江，敌军近30万兵力合围而来。红军被压缩在湘江以东狭长地带，军委纵队和主力部队仍有一部分尚未过江。一旦阻击线被突破，整个中央红军将面临被分割围歼的危险。 局势已到千钧一发之际。凌晨时分，这份由中共中央、中革军委、红军总政治部联署的紧急指令，传遍每一个阵地：“一日战斗，关系我野战军全部。西进胜利，可开辟今后的发展前途，退则我野战军将被层层切断……我们不为胜利者，即为战败者。胜负关系全局，人人要奋起作战的最高勇气，不顾一切牺牲，克服疲惫现象，以坚决的突击，执行进攻与消灭敌人的任务……” 没有回旋余地，没有退路可言。胜与败，生与死，被压缩为一道再清晰不过的单选题。 指令飞传至红34师阵地时，当面敌军已增至数倍，师长陈树湘率部在灌阳一带孤军死战，为主力抢渡争取时间。数日血战后，弹尽粮绝，陈树湘腹部中弹被俘。在押解途中，他宁死不屈，断肠明志，壮烈牺牲，年仅29岁。全师以近乎全员阵亡的代价，把敌军死死钉在身后。 “命令抵达阵地的那一刻，就意味着必须用生命来作答。”中队指导员莫家权对官兵们说，“这封指令的每一个字，都浸透着绝境之下破釜沉舟的决心。” 这句话，官兵们并不觉得遥远。在全州中队，“不为胜利者，即为战败者”从来不是挂在墙上的标语，而是关键时刻能吼出来的号令。 此前，驻地连日暴雨，洪涝突发。深夜，紧急集合哨骤然响起。官兵们闻令即动，与洪水抢时间，转移群众、加固堤坝。那一夜，没有人问“要干多久”，没有人说“撑不住”。 还有一次，当地突发山火。中队紧急集合，时任中队长周嘉锋站到队伍前，没有长篇动员，只讲了一句话：“同志们，我们不为胜利者，即为战败者。面前是火场，身后是百姓。” “先辈们在生死绝境中立起的标尺，传到我们手里，就是任务面前不打一丝折扣。”莫家权说。这种“不胜即败”的意识，也悄然重塑着中队的练兵理念。 该中队曾走过一段急功近利的弯路：为迎考盲目加时加练，不区分基础、不顾执勤压力，看似训练加码，实则重难点钻研不深、根基打得不牢，甚至消磨了官兵自主研训的热情。“偏离胜战标准就意味着失败。练兵场上没有中间状态，只有胜与败两种答案。”莫家权在反思会上说。此后，中队毅然摒弃蛮干做法，从加练基础力量、补足专业短板做起，从站好每一班哨抓起。 湘水滔滔，奔流不息。该中队连续4年在支队勤训轮换中排名靠前，50余名官兵在各级比武中摘金夺银。 “和平年代，哨位就是战位，执勤就是战斗。”战士黄凌志的话，道出了中队官兵的理解——誓争胜利、永不言败的考验，其实就体现在每一班岗、每一次任务里。守好一方平安，护好万家灯火，就是对先烈最好的告慰，就是我们这代人必须赢下的胜利。
