About this analysis
Summary
The 99th PLA founding anniversary issue of PLA Daily runs a themed feature pairing three historical army-people bond narratives—the 1934 Long March "half a quilt" story from Shazhou Village, Rucheng County; the 1939 Yan'an "Happiness Canal" built by the Shaanxi-Gansu-Ningxia Border Region government; and civilian labor support during the 1948–49 Huaihai Campaign—with current civil-military cooperation vignettes involving the Chenzhou Military Sub-district, the Yan'an Military Sub-district, and an unnamed Eastern Theater Command Army brigade garrisoned in Xuzhou. The framing documents how official PLA media uses the 双拥运动 (Mutual Support Movement) tradition to anchor contemporary military-local relations—greenhouse funding, land-swap negotiations for training grounds, red tourism development—within a continuous revolutionary legitimacy narrative. The article is standard anniversary political work content; its value is as a record of how the institution presents the army-people bond to active-duty personnel and local officials, not as evidence of any policy or posture change.

Special Feature for the 99th Anniversary of the Founding of the Chinese People's Liberation Army

Walking with the People, Walking with Victory

Ninety-nine years ago, the sound of gunfire over Nanchang announced the beginning of the Chinese Communist Party's independent leadership of armed struggle and marked the moment the Chinese people came to possess an army of their own. This people's armed force, born amid the flames of war, planted its roots deep in the fertile soil of the people from its very inception: during the Land Revolution War, the army and the people forged ahead through hardship and depended on each other for survival; during the War of Resistance Against Japan, the army and the people shared the same hatred for the enemy and together resisted foreign aggression; during the War of Liberation, the army and the people joined hands and hearts, united as one. By taking deep root in the people and relying firmly on the people, the People's Army has advanced from victory to victory without cease.

On the occasion of the 99th anniversary of the founding of the People's Army, as we look back on the magnificent journey traveled together by the army and the people, one indestructible truth shines bright: the roots of the People's Army are sunk deep in the vast earth of the people. When the army and the people are united as one, who in the world can stand against them?

—The Editors

Rucheng County, Hunan Province

"Half a Quilt," Sharing Warmth and Cold

■ Deng Shengxiang; Li Liangliang, Contributing Reporter, PLA Daily

In the height of summer, all things flourish. On Baizhang Ridge at the southern foot of the Luoxiao Mountains, a mountain path winds and twists through towering peaks. Setting out from Yanshou Yao Ethnic Township in Rucheng County, Hunan Province, and traveling more than 20 kilometers to Wenming Yao Ethnic Township, the road strings together multiple red villages—Guanheng, Tongcheng, Dongshan, Xiushui, Shazhou, and Wenshi. As the summer holiday begins, cadres and ordinary people from across the country arrive in an unbroken stream to retrace the Long March route.

The first stop on the Long March route is Guanheng Village, the site of the Battle of Qingshizhai—the landmark battle in which the Central Red Army broke through the second blockade line during the Long March.

In November 1934, the Red Army's advance forces arrived at Guanheng Village. Though short of food and medicine, the officers and soldiers maintained strict discipline and did not take so much as a needle and thread from the people. At the suggestion of local notable Hu Side, villagers pooled together 105 dan of rice, 3 pigs, and 12 chickens, which young and able-bodied men carried and drove to the Red Army command post. Ye Zuling, the quartermaster of the 3rd Army Corps, wrote an IOU, promising to honor the debt after the revolution's victory. Hu Side hid the IOU in the wall of his kitchen and never mentioned it to his family. It was not until 1996, when his grandson discovered it by chance, that this moving story came to light.

That same month, in Shazhou Village of Wenming Yao Ethnic Township at the other end of the mountain road, three female Red Army soldiers took lodging in the home of an elderly woman named Xu Jiexiu. Seeing that the old woman's home was bare and that she did not even have a decent quilt, they cut their only quilt in half and left one half for her when they departed.

In 1984, a journalist retracing the Long March heard this story from the elderly Xu Jiexiu. From that point on, "half a quilt" became a symbol of the Party and the People's Army sharing the same joys and hardships, the same life and death, as the common people—What is the Communist Party? The Communist Party is people who, even when they have only one quilt, will cut it in half and give it to the common folk.

More than 90 years later, revisiting the old place, the old face has given way to a new one. Both sides of the mountain road are lined with specialty fruits planted by local residents; the hills and fields are filled with the fragrance of fruit, and laughter rings out one after another.

In recent years, with support from higher authorities, Rucheng County has developed the Red Shazhou 4A-level scenic area and built the Qingshizhai core exhibition zone of the National Long March Cultural Park. Riding the tailwind of military and local joint support for the development of red tourism, 56-year-old Yanshou Yao Ethnic Township resident Song Huisheng opened a specialty guesthouse centered on red cultural elements.

"When I was a child, I often heard the villagers tell stories about the Red Army. Later, the soldiers came to pair up with us and help us, and our lives got better and better. This kindness must never be forgotten," said Song Huisheng.

"Seeing cadres and ordinary people come here to trace the history of the Red Army, we are greatly inspired," said Zhang Xusheng, a staff officer in the Political Work Office of the Chenzhou Military Sub-district, Hunan Province. In recent years, the military sub-district and the Rucheng County People's Armed Forces Department have selected personnel to be permanently stationed in paired-assistance villages and matched with households under monitoring. "It's not just about looking at the pot in the kitchen and the water at home—you also have to ask about the expenses for the children's schooling, clothing, and food," Zhang Xusheng said. "Through times that hard, the people walked through it all with us. Now we must ensure that the people of the old revolutionary base areas share in the fruits of the nation's economic development and social progress."

At the entrance to Song Huisheng's guesthouse, he has posted a set of military-support terms he printed himself: active-duty servicemembers and veterans receive discounts upon check-in. Walking into rooms named after red scenic sites, the reporter found the interiors simply and neatly decorated, with clean and warm bedding.

"Back then, the Red Army was willing to split a quilt in half for the villagers. Now we are willing to keep a few rooms for servicemembers," said Song Huisheng.

Yan'an, Shaanxi Province

The "Happiness Canal" Flows On

■ Chen Kerui; Guo Wei

In July, Yan'an's gullies and ridges are deep in green, and visitors crowd shoulder to shoulder.

Inside the auditorium of the Secretariat of the CPC Central Committee at the Zaoyuan Revolutionary Site, guide Luo Xiaoyu stands before two plain round tables, recounting a story to visitors: "On the Lantern Festival of 1943, Mao Zedong and other central leaders hosted a banquet here for 24 elderly residents of Zaoyuan Township aged 60 and above. Before the meal began, Mao Zedong not only personally went to the door to welcome them, but also toasted each elder in turn during the banquet, wishing them long life."

On the auditorium wall, Mao Zedong's 1943 inscription "Serve the Masses" (为群众服务) shines bright. On February 1, 1943, the Eighth Route Army Garrison Corps' "Covenant on Supporting the Government and Cherishing the People" (拥政爱民公约) was officially promulgated—the first such covenant in our army's history. Beginning from this point, the Mutual Support Movement (双拥运动), centered on Yan'an, flourished vigorously across the Shaanxi-Gansu-Ningxia Border Region and the anti-Japanese base areas behind enemy lines.

To the right inside the courtyard of the Zaoyuan Revolutionary Site, near the hillside, a canal stretching a total of 6 kilometers winds its way through. In 1939, the Construction Department of the Shaanxi-Gansu-Ningxia Border Region, despite extreme financial difficulty, still allocated a special appropriation of 4,000 yuan to dig a canal for the local people. In April of the following year, the canal was completed. Its waters irrigated more than 1,400 mu of land across five villages surrounding Zaoyuan, completely relieving the local predicament of drought, water scarcity, and dependence on the heavens for food.

To thank the Party Central Committee and the Border Region government, the villagers named the canal the "Happiness Canal" (幸福渠). After its completion, whenever the slack winter season came for dredging, the flood season for flood prevention, or spring plowing for water distribution, the soldiers would proactively help the people manage and maintain the canal and ensure irrigation. From then on, a folk song (信天游) spread across the Loess Plateau: "The waters of the Happiness Canal flow without end, flowing without end is the fish-and-water bond between the army and the people; the waters of the Happiness Canal flow without cease, and the kindness of the Communist Party is beyond all telling…"

More than 80 years have passed. Today the old revolutionary base area has a new "Happiness Canal"—the Yan'an Yellow River Water Diversion Project. Access to drinking water has long ceased to be a problem, but the "Happiness Canal" still nourishes this land. Today, new stories of the fish-and-water bond between the army and the people are unfolding along its banks.

Entering Longshetou Village, Zhaoan Town, Ansai District, 62-year-old farmer Song Manxiong holds up his phone, its camera sweeping across the vegetable greenhouses behind him. Ever since the greenhouses built with assistance from the Yan'an Military Sub-district brought him a stable income, he has often picked up his phone to tell online followers about the local customs and the army-people bond, while also taking the opportunity to promote the vegetables and fruits he grows. "I've become an internet celebrity now," Song Manxiong said with a laugh.

In 2020, Longshetou Village's industries were still very weak. "At that time, the village wanted to develop greenhouse cultivation and was thinking about how to solve the funding problem, when the PLA proactively came to us. In less than a month, the funding issue was all sorted out. Once that problem was solved, we could roll up our sleeves and get to work," recalled Song Dongwei, Secretary of the Longshetou Village Party Branch.

"Now the total number of greenhouses in the village has grown to 128, and we also cultivate mountain apples, cherries, and other orchard cash crops. The village's annual output value is approximately 2 million yuan," said Song Dongwei, brimming with excitement as he spoke of the village's transformation. "Seeing the village develop so well, quite a few young people who had gone out to work have come back."

"We are stationed in Yan'an, and the red stories—Zhang Side's wholehearted service to the people, the 359th Brigade Supplementary Regiment opening up Nanniwan—have long been etched in our hearts," said Li Zhaofei, a Yan'an Military Sub-district cadre assigned to provide targeted assistance to the village. "From the revolutionary era when the army and the people joined forces to build canals for irrigation, to modern water conservancy projects moistening the countryside, the army and people of the old base area share the same heart and the same direction. The waters of the Happiness Canal flow on without end."

Xuzhou, Jiangsu Province

Speaking of Original Aspirations Before the "Wheelbarrow"

■ Sun Jinji; Hu Shaowu, Contributing Correspondent, PLA Daily

"Visiting the Huaihai Campaign Martyrs' Memorial Tower—it's actually most fitting to come in winter," Fan Jianwen, a staff officer of an Army unit, told this reporter. "Because the Huaihai Campaign took place in winter. Reading these artifacts then, you can better appreciate the resolve of the people to support the front lines despite the bitter cold, and their deep affection for the People's Army."

Brushing aside the frost of time, we can still find those emotional imprints in the memorial hall today—

A small bamboo stick carved with place names, from Laiyang, Shandong. During the Huaihai Campaign, Laiyang villager Tang He'en heard that civilian laborers were being organized to deliver military grain to the front lines. Having just received his allotment of land, he signed up immediately. This small bamboo stick accompanied him over mountains and ridges, through snow and across rivers. At each place he reached, he carved the name into the stick. Over more than half a year, 88 place names accumulated on the stick, forming a support-the-front map stretching more than 3,000 kilometers.

A chipped porcelain bowl, from Shangshui, Henan. When the Huaihai Campaign began, people in the liberated areas responded to the call en masse and joined the army of front-line supporters. Shangshui villager Xu Naizhen set out with a stretcher team. Besides a tattered padded cotton jacket, he brought only one old porcelain bowl. Under frost and moonlight, the stretcher team often marched dozens of kilometers through the night at a rapid pace; the porcelain bowl jolted along with Xu Naizhen the whole way, serving food and water to the wounded. Not long after the Huaihai Campaign ended, Xu Naizhen died of his wounds, and the porcelain bowl that had accompanied him to the front was later donated by his family, becoming a witness to that era.

"The wheelbarrows came from all directions because the people of the liberated areas understood clearly in their hearts: only if the PLA won its battles could they hold onto their land and live good lives. Everything for the people—only then can you win the people's support. This is something we dare not forget for a single moment," said Zhang Jiahui, a First Sergeant of an Army unit.

"Our unit has been stationed in Xuzhou for more than 70 years," Zhang Jiahui said. Since the Huaihai Campaign Martyrs' Memorial Tower was completed in 1965, conducting educational activities at the tower and memorial hall every year has become an unshakeable tradition for the unit.

Supporting the military and the front lines is a history carried by artifacts, and also a legacy that cadres and ordinary people embody in their actions.

Liu Kui, an assistant in the unit's Barracks Management Section, recounted the following story: part of the unit's garrison area is located within the urban district, where training grounds are small and scattered, making it difficult to conduct realistic combat training subjects, and leaving no room to maneuver for hands-on training with heavy equipment. In August 2025, they submitted a request to the Xuzhou municipal government for training land, hoping to swap some garrison area land.

"The military and local sides sat down together and held several coordination meetings," Liu Kui said. "We put forward our needs, and the local side laid out its old-city renovation plans. When the two sides compared notes, they found a win-win solution was achievable."

In April of this year, the project entered the assessment phase, with all procedures advancing in an orderly manner. "Serving the enhancement of the troops' combat effectiveness is our inescapable responsibility, and this is entirely consistent with our tradition of supporting the military and the front lines," said the local project official involved in the coordination.

"This is consistent with what we feel day to day," Zhang Jiahui said. Officers and soldiers can feel the people's respect for servicemembers in their daily travel and duty. Having served in Xuzhou for more than ten years, he has a personal sense of the military-support atmosphere there: "The people always ask us what we need and spare no effort in providing us with the best policies and services. Whenever the people come to pay their respects to us, I think of the wheelbarrows in the memorial hall. Right now, this is our most genuine feeling—the support of the people is the most solid backing for our struggle to build a strong military."

1934年11月，红军长征先遣部队抵达官亨村，虽然缺粮少药，但官兵纪律严明、秋毫无犯。在乡贤胡四德提议下，村民匀出105担稻谷、3头生猪、12只鸡，由青壮年挑着赶着，送到红军指挥部。红三军团司务长叶祖令打下借条，承诺待革命胜利后兑现。胡四德将借条藏在自家厨房的墙壁里，不曾向家人提起。直到1996年，其孙子偶然发现这张借据，才揭开了这段动人往事。 同一个月，在山路另一端的文明瑶族乡沙洲村，3名女红军借宿徐解秀老人家中。她们见老人家徒四壁，连条像样的被子也没有，便在临走时将仅有的一条被子剪下一半留给老人。 1984年，一位重走长征路的记者从徐解秀老人那里听说这个故事。从那以后，“半条被子”就成为党和人民军队与人民群众同冷暖、共生死的象征——什么是共产党？共产党就是自己有一条被子，也要剪下半条给老百姓的人。 “我小时候，就常听村里人讲红军的故事。后来子弟兵又来结对帮扶，我们的日子越过越好。这份恩情什么时候也不能忘。”宋会生说。 ■陈珂锐 郭 伟 7月的延安，山峁沟壑间绿意正浓，参观的人群摩肩接踵。 枣园革命旧址中共中央书记处礼堂内，讲解员罗晓宇正对着两张古朴的圆桌，向游客讲述一段往事：“1943年元宵节，毛泽东等中央领导同志曾在这里宴请枣园乡24位60岁以上的老人。开席前，毛泽东不仅亲自到门口迎接，席间还一一给老人敬酒，祝他们延年益寿。” 礼堂墙上，1943年毛泽东题写的“为群众服务”题词熠熠生辉。1943年2月1日，八路军留守兵团《拥政爱民公约》正式公布，这是我军历史上首个拥政爱民公约。以此为起点，以延安为中心的双拥运动在陕甘宁边区和敌后根据地蓬勃开展。 枣园革命旧址院内右侧近山坡处，一条总长6公里的水渠蜿蜒穿行。1939年，陕甘宁边区建设厅在财政极度困难的情况下，仍专项拨款4000元，为当地群众开凿水渠。次年4月，水渠建成，渠水灌溉枣园周边5个村落1400余亩地，彻底缓解了当地干旱缺水、靠天吃饭的困境。 乡亲们为感谢党中央和边区政府，把水渠称为“幸福渠”。“幸福渠”建成后，每逢冬闲清淤、汛期防汛、春耕分水等节点，子弟兵都会主动帮助群众管护渠道、保障灌溉。从此，一首信天游在黄土高原上传唱：“幸福渠水流不尽，流不尽军民鱼水情；幸福渠水流不断，共产党的恩情说不完……” 80多年过去，如今老区有了新的“幸福渠”——延安黄河引水工程。吃水早已不成问题，但“幸福渠”仍滋养着一方热土。如今，“幸福渠”畔又上演着军民鱼水情的新故事。 走进安塞区招安镇龙石头村，62岁的种植户宋满雄正举着手机，镜头扫过身后的蔬菜大棚。自从延安军分区援建的大棚给他带来稳定收入后，他就经常拿起手机给网友讲乡村的风土人情和军民鱼水故事，还趁机推销地里种的蔬菜水果。“我现在也成网红了。”宋满雄笑着说。 2020年的龙石头村，产业还很薄弱。“那时候，村里想发展大棚种植，正思谋着资金的问题，解放军就主动找上门来，不到一个月就把资金的事情都办妥了。这个问题一解决，接下来就是甩开膀子加油干。”龙石头村党支部书记宋东伟回忆道。 “现在，全村大棚增加至128座，还种植有山地苹果、樱桃等林果经济作物，全村年产值约200万元。”宋东伟说起村里的变化，兴奋不已，“看着村里发展得不错，不少外出务工的年轻人也回来了。” “我们驻扎在延安，张思德全心全意为人民服务、八路军359旅补充团开垦南泥湾等红色故事早就刻在我们心里。”定点帮扶该村的延安军分区干部李赵斐说，“从革命年代军民合力修渠灌溉，到现代水利工程润泽乡野，老区军民同心同向，幸福渠水流也流不尽。” 淮海战役烈士纪念塔。卢明凯摄 八一建军节来临之际，东部战区陆军某旅步兵分队官兵在演训场向军旗宣誓。王伟博摄 江苏省徐州市 “小推车”前话初心 ■孙金亿 解放军报特约通讯员 胡绍武 “参观淮海战役烈士纪念塔，其实还是冬天来最合适。”陆军某部干事范建文告诉笔者，“因为淮海战役发生在冬季，那时来读这些文物，更能体会到群众冒着严寒也要支前的决心和对人民军队的深厚感情。” 拂去风霜，我们至今依然能在纪念馆里找到那些情感印记—— 一根刻满地名的小竹竿，来自山东莱阳。淮海战役期间，莱阳村民唐和恩听说要组织民工向前线送军粮，刚分到土地的他立刻报了名。这根小竹竿陪着他翻山越岭、踏雪渡河，每到一个地方，他就在竹竿上刻下地名。大半年时间里，小竹竿上留下了88个地名，串联成了一幅绵延3000多公里的支前地图。 一只缺口的瓷碗，来自河南商水。淮海战役打响时，解放区群众纷纷响应号召加入支前大军。商水村民徐乃祯跟着担架队出发了，除了一身破棉袄，他仅带了一只旧瓷碗。星月霜寒，担架队经常一夜急行几十公里，瓷碗跟着徐乃祯一路颠簸，为伤员盛饭端水。淮海战役结束后不久，徐乃祯因伤去世，而伴随他支前的瓷碗后来由家人捐献，成为那段岁月的见证。 “小推车从四面八方而来，是因为解放区的群众心里明白，只有解放军打了胜仗，他们才能守住自己的土地过上好日子。一切为了人民，才能获得人民的支持，这点我们一刻也不敢忘。”陆军某部一级上士张佳辉说。 “我们这支部队，70多年前就在徐州驻扎了。”张佳辉说，从1965年淮海战役烈士纪念塔建成，每年到纪念塔和纪念馆开展教育活动就成了部队雷打不动的传统。 拥军支前，是文物承载的历史，也是干部群众躬身笃行的传承。 该部营房科助理刘奎讲述了这样一个故事：该部部分营区位于市区，训练场地狭小分散，实战化课目难以展开，重型装备实操演练更是“伸不开拳脚”。2025年8月，他们向徐州市提出训练用地需求，希望置换部分营区土地。 “军地双方坐在一起，开了好几次协调会。”刘奎说，“我们提出诉求，地方上也把旧城改造的规划摆出来，两边一碰，发现可以实现双赢。” 今年4月，项目进入评估阶段，各项流程正有序推进。“为部队战斗力提升服务，本就是我们义不容辞的责任，这与我们拥军支前的传统一脉相承。”参与对接的地方项目负责人说。 “这与我们平常的感受一致。”张佳辉说，官兵平日出行、执勤，都能感受到人民群众对军人的尊崇。在徐州服役十多年，他对这里的拥军氛围有着切身体会：“群众总问我们需要什么，不吝把最好的政策、服务提供给我们。每当群众来慰问我们，我都会想起纪念馆里的小推车。当下，这就是我们最真切的感受——人民群众的拥护，是我们奋斗强军最坚实的后盾。” 近日，第81集团军某旅组织官兵赴李大钊纪念馆参观，激励官兵传承红色基因，担当强军重任。姜正旺摄 近日，武警贵州总队黔西南支队组织官兵走进贞丰红军长征陈列馆，现地重温长征精神。宋文明摄 八一建军节来临之际，安徽省砀山县举办“八一”军民联欢暨欢送军校新生入伍主题活动，以真挚情怀致敬人民军队，激励热血青年投身国防事业。李金春摄 版式设计：扈 硕
