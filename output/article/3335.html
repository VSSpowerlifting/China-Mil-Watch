From "Problem-Solvers" to "Problem-Setters": Army Engineering University Forges Cadets' Ability to Organize Training and Conduct Instruction

从“解题人”变身“出题人”，陆军工程大学锤炼学员组训施教能力
PLA Daily (解放军报) 17 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Army Engineering University (陆军工程大学) in Nanjing has restructured cadet training around a 'learning as the center, capability as the foundation' (学为中心、能力为本) philosophy, handing full organizational control of multi-university competitions — including the 'Starting Point Cup' combat team challenge, which drew 31 teams from six institutions — to cadets rather than instructors. The reform addresses a documented gap between individual training performance and unit-level competence: graduate feedback showed cadets who excelled at school repeatedly struggled to organize training and conduct instruction after assignment to grassroots commands. The article documents an institutional response to that feedback loop, not a capability shift; its value is as a record of how Army Engineering University is adjusting pre-commission officer education to close the gap between individual skill and small-unit leadership.
Translation

Once "Solving Problems" at the Front — Now "Setting Problems" Behind the Scenes: An Account of Army Engineering University's Efforts to Forge Cadets' Ability to Organize Training and Conduct Instruction

■ Wang Zi, Qiao Qiao

Midsummer in Jinling, waves of heat rolling in. On the training grounds of Army Engineering University, a "Starting Point Cup" combat team challenge competition is in full swing. Thirty-one competing teams from six military and civilian universities complete seven combat-realistic events in succession over five hours, the scene thick with tension.

"For this competition, we handed all event-organization duties entirely over to the cadets," explained Luo Wenxin, an instructor in the university's Military Fundamentals Department. From start-line registration and event officiating to score tallying and logistics resupply, from event design and rule-setting to on-site coordination and emergency response — all of it was led and executed by cadets. Instructors were responsible only for guidance on key technical points; they did not intervene in the progression of the competition or involve themselves in handling unexpected situations throughout.

Cadets shifting from "problem-solvers" competing at the front to "problem-setters" coordinating behind the scenes reflects a profound transformation in the Party committee's philosophy of cultivating talent at this university.

In recent years, the university has firmly established a talent-cultivation philosophy of "learning as the center, capability as the foundation" (学为中心、能力为本). Relying on four major categories of cadet clubs — including competition and science and technology clubs — it has built a platform for independent learning and practice, given cadets free rein to step up to the front lines of organizing training, and developed self-run competition brands such as "Starting Point," "Standard-Bearer," and "Knapsack," extending the forging of training-organization capability from the classroom lectern to the competition arena. The "Starting Point Cup" combat team challenge is one of the most representative of these competitions.

"In the past, when we focused on teaching, we cared more about how fast cadets could run and how accurately they could shoot. But for grassroots commanders, the fundamental capabilities are organizing training, conducting instruction, and exercising overall command," Luo Wenxin explained. Reviewing feedback from graduates of recent years, they found that many cadets who had been outstanding training performers while at school lacked experience in organizing training and conducting instruction after arriving at their units. "In the previous curriculum, content on organizing training mostly remained at the theoretical level, leaving cadets without hands-on experience. Now, by encouraging cadets to independently run competitions, we aim to help them address this capability gap at the source," Luo Wenxin said.

Yu Wenxu, the cadet who led the planning of this competition, felt this deeply. In the two months before the event, he led a planning team of more than twenty people, plunging into the training grounds to repeatedly rehearse procedures and refine details — from the pacing and sequencing of the competition schedule down to the placement of signage. Every single link was held to strict standards.

"When you're a competitor, crossing the finish line means the mission is complete. When you're running the competition, you have to keep your eyes on every team and every station. The challenge and pressure are easy to imagine," Yu Wenxu said. The shift from individual-combatant thinking to an awareness of overall coordination — the training and growth that this role change brings — is something theoretical instruction cannot replace.

On one occasion, team members disagreed over the judging criteria for the "Priority Crisis" event: some argued for preset standard answers to make judging efficient and consistent; others insisted that "the battlefield has no standard answers" and that judging should focus primarily on the decision-making logic of the commander. After multiple on-site rehearsals and rigorous deliberation, they ultimately settled on a judging standard of "emphasizing logic."

"The process of debate was itself a form of tempering — it made us think through why we were testing what we were testing and how to train for it," Yu Wenxu said. Many competing team members, watching their fellow soldiers organize the competition with scientific rigor and precision, broke out of the habitual mindset of "focusing only on individual scores" and began proactively studying methods of organizing training and learning from the experience of running competitions.

Original Chinese
过去台前“解题” 如今幕后“出题” ——陆军工程大学锤炼学员组训施教能力的一段经历 ■王 子 乔 侨 盛夏金陵，热浪滚滚。陆军工程大学训练场上，一场“起点杯”战斗小组挑战赛激战正酣。来自6所军地高校的31支参赛队伍，5小时内连贯完成7个实战化课目比拼，现场弥漫紧张气氛。 “这场比武，我们把赛事组织事务完全交给学员。”该校军事基础系教员罗文鑫介绍，从起点检录、课目执裁到成绩统计、物资补给，从课目设计、规则制订到现场调度、应急处置，全部由学员主导完成，教员仅负责关键技术环节指导，全程不干预赛程推进、不插手特情处置。 学员从台前竞技的“解题人”变身幕后统筹的“出题人”，折射出该校党委育人理念的深刻转型。 近年来，该校牢固树立“学为中心、能力为本”育人理念，依托竞技、科技等4大类学员俱乐部，搭建自主学习实践平台，放手让学员走上组训一线，打造“起点”“擎旗”“锦囊”等自主赛事品牌，将组训能力锤炼从课堂讲台延伸到比武赛场。“起点杯”战斗小组挑战赛，就是最具代表性的赛事之一。 “过去抓教学，我们更关心学员跑得多快、打得多准。但对基层指挥员来说，组训施教、统筹指挥能力才是根本。”罗文鑫介绍，他们梳理近年毕业学员反馈意见发现，不少在校期间表现突出的训练尖子，到部队后组训施教却经验不足。“以往课程体系中的组训内容，大多停留在理论层面，导致学员缺乏实操经验。如今，我们鼓励学员自主办赛，就是要从源头上帮助学员补齐这块能力短板。”罗文鑫说。 牵头筹办此次赛事的学员于文旭对此感触颇深。赛前两个月，他带着20余人的筹划团队，一头扎进训练场，反复推演流程、打磨细节，大到赛程衔接节奏，小到标识牌摆放位置，每一个环节都从严要求。 “以前参赛，冲过终点线就算完成任务；现在办赛，要盯紧每一支队伍、每一个点位，挑战和压力可想而知。”于文旭说，从单兵作战思维到全局统筹意识，这种角色转变带来的锻炼和成长，是理论教学替代不了的。 一次，团队成员对于“优先级危机”课目的评判规则出现分歧：有人主张预设标准答案，裁评高效统一；有人坚持认为“战场没有标准答案”，评判时应重点关注指挥员的决策逻辑。经过多次实地推演、科学论证，他们最终定下“注重逻辑”的评判规则。 “争论过程本身就是一种历练，让我们想透了为何考、怎么训。”于文旭说，不少参赛队员看着身边战友把赛事组织得科学严密，纷纷跳出“只盯个人成绩”的惯性思维，开始主动琢磨组训方法、学习办赛经验。
