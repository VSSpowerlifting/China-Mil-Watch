Observations and Reflections on a Cadre Training Course at the Nanjing Campus of the Army Branch Arms University

陆军兵种大学南京校区一次干部培训的见闻与思考
PLA Daily (解放军报) 4 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
The Nanjing Campus of the Army Branch Arms University ran a cadre training course—covering artillery battalion field-site tactical instruction and initial-level command education—in which the trainees' unit temporary Party branch enforced strict conduct and study standards through quantified internal affairs inspections, weekly rankings, and a 'hard linkage' mechanism sending transcripts directly back to home units for use in promotion decisions. The article documents a recurring institutional problem: cadres attending off-unit training defaulting to networking, socializing, and minimal effort, a pattern the piece frames as a target of 'political rectification training' (政治整训) and the campaign to normalize conduct-building (作风建设常态化长效化). The framing points to persistent enforcement gaps between stated training standards and actual trainee behavior, with the ranked-results-to-home-unit mechanism presented as the specific lever that changed conduct—though whether this mechanism is new policy or local initiative, and how broadly it is applied across the branch arms university system, the article does not establish.

"The Feeling of Being Tempered in Fire and Steel Has Been Found"

— Observations and Reflections on a Cadre Training Course at the Nanjing Campus of the Army Branch Arms University

■ Liu Xiaotian, Li Qiangyun, PLA Daily Special Correspondent Yang Kaiming

Editor's Note: At present, as the various tasks of the troops continue to expand and deepen, Party member backbone cadres are increasingly going out to participate in all kinds of training. However, individual officers and soldiers attending training still exhibit problems such as a lax mindset, superficial learning, or loose daily conduct and a weakened sense of discipline. Under these circumstances, Party organizations at all levels must deeply grasp the practical requirement in Xi Jinping's Party-building thought of "persisting in advancing the normalization and long-term effectiveness of conduct-building (坚持推进作风建设常态化长效化)," and, in conjunction with carrying out political rectification training (政治整训), make hard efforts to purify study conduct (学风) and work conduct (作风). They must guide Party member backbone cadres to focus all their thoughts and energy on learning to strengthen capabilities, ensuring that every training course becomes a real-combat "tempering in fire (淬火)" that forges Party spirit and elevates ability, thereby injecting a living source of water into the forging of a crack talent force capable of winning battles.

Midsummer in Jinling, with waves of heat bearing down. On a certain field training ground, a field-site tactical instruction exercise for an artillery battalion conducted by the Nanjing Campus of the Army Branch Arms University was reaching a critical juncture. Instructor Gao Wang pointed toward a nearby elevation point and posed a question: "Between the battalion headquarters and the observation posts of each company, who moves first and who moves last when relocating? What is the basis for that?" The trainees vied to speak, and differing views clashed vigorously.

During a training break, the reporter asked several trainees about their impressions of this initial-level command education training course. The words "real," "strict," and "solid" became the most frequently heard. "This kind of 'smell of gunpowder smoke,' this kind of collision of ideas — this is what we truly need." One trainee wiped away sweat and said that this training had taught them genuine skills for winning battles. "The feeling of being tempered in fire and steel has been found!"

I

Watching the trainees still engaged in heated discussion in the distance, Su Yunke, a Party branch secretary from a certain brigade, was filled with deep feeling. He told the reporter that over these several months of training — from the morning formation calls to the evening self-study sessions, from total concentration in the classroom to meticulous attention to internal affairs (内务) — everything carried a sense of being "different": "The most direct change is that the spirit (精气神) of preparing for war and fighting has been lifted!"

This sense of being "different" began the moment they stepped through the school gate.

On the evening of registration, Li, the secretary of the trainees' unit temporary Party branch, convened a full branch Party member meeting. Centering on the requirements of the training mission, and in conjunction with carrying out political rectification training and advancing the normalization and long-term effectiveness of conduct-building, he placed particular emphasis on discipline during the training period: "This training — from daily schedules and classroom discipline to internal affairs standards — will be strictly managed. There will absolutely be no going through the motions (绝不走过场)."

Su Yunke said that the reason the Party branch did this was to require everyone to keep firmly in mind their identity as Party member cadres, to always hold themselves to higher standards and stricter requirements in terms of ideology, study, and conduct, and to treat this place as a "furnace" for tempering Party spirit and elevating capability.

"Unexpectedly, the requirements were strict, and the implementation was even stricter." One trainee was deeply impressed by this. This trainee had participated in similar training before and initially was somewhat dismissive: "Training sometimes has high requirements but low implementation; the unit cadres won't be too serious about it — close enough is good enough." Carrying this "close enough" mentality, he was initially insufficiently engaged in his studies, did not take the assigned pre-study seriously, and dealt with post-class assignments perfunctorily. As a result, during one seminar-style class, he was unprepared when called upon and gave an irrelevant answer; he was seriously criticized and the matter was circulated as a notice to the entire unit. Shortly afterward, his internal affairs inspection also received a "red light" for failing to meet standards.

This left him unable to save face, and it thoroughly awakened him: "So this time they are playing for real. Every one of those requirements is a hard rule (硬杠杠) — not the slightest carelessness is tolerable."

The people and events around them served as a warning to all the trainees. Su Yunke frankly admitted that although he had not been as lax as that trainee, he too had initially harbored the thought that "coming out for training, there's no need to be as serious as in the company." But under the Party branch's strict management — with internal affairs inspections, formation drills, and classroom attendance all quantified and scored, with weekly notifications and monthly rankings — he came to have a genuine understanding of the iron rules and steel regulations, and began to hold himself to strict standards at all times.

"In the unit be strict; when going out for training be even stricter — you must tighten up and learn more genuine skills." Su Yunke's reflection articulated what training ought to look like: by no means a "rest stop," but truly a "refueling station."

II

Late at night, a self-study room in the trainees' unit was still brightly lit.

Following the light, the reporter found trainee Ding Wanpu and several members of a temporary Party small group gathered around a map, repeatedly deducing a scenario assignment. Over the timing of a fire support action, the group was engaged in a heated discussion, sweat sliding down their cheeks unnoticed. "What was taught during the day is all new tactics and new theory close to actual combat. If you don't strike while the iron is hot and get it figured out, you might fall behind the next day!" Ding Wanpu spoke his mind.

This sense of racing against time stemmed from the "hard linkage (硬挂钩)" performance mechanism implemented by the trainees' unit. "At the conclusion of every course, not only is the full unit ranked and the results publicly posted, but the overall transcript is sent directly back to the original unit as an important reference for cadre selection and adjustment." Secretary Li explained to the reporter that establishing this mechanism was intended to set a clear orientation that "good performance and poor performance are not the same."

In the past, influenced by certain bad tendencies and muddled thinking, individual cadres harbored the notion that "it's better to get along well than to study well," believing that going out for training was a chance to "relax." Ding Wanpu recalled that several years ago he had participated in a training course where individual dormitories frequently held "hometown gatherings" and "reminiscing sessions" during off-hours, with individual trainees putting their energy into eating, drinking, and socializing, while the energy and time actually spent on studying was not much.

At the start of this training, similar early warning signs actually appeared. Ding Wanpu said that a trainee in their Party small group received a phone call from an old classmate one weekend, warmly inviting him to "get together and catch up." After hanging up, this trainee was momentarily hesitant: "After all, it's an old classmate I haven't seen in years — it would be a bit awkward not to go."

"We came here this time for training, not to reminisce. Strictly observing discipline and regulations, and putting all our thoughts and energy into studying — that's the only way to be worthy of the organization's cultivation and worthy of our own efforts." Upon learning of the situation, Ding Wanpu, as Party small group leader, promptly offered a quiet word of advice (咬耳扯袖). In the end, this trainee not only politely declined the invitation but also resolved to bid farewell to his past "whatever" attitude and threw himself wholeheartedly into the training and study.

Ding Wanpu said that in conjunction with carrying out political rectification training, the various temporary Party small groups in the trainees' unit held multiple discussions and analyses around some typical cases of violations of regulations and discipline, and a broad consensus was formed: to mutually supervise and remind one another, to proactively discard past bad habits, and to return energy to learning itself.

One weekend morning, the self-study room was already full to capacity. The reporter asked one trainee: "It's finally the weekend — why not go out and relax?"

"This training has a lot of content and a high combat-relevance content (含战量), and measured against the requirements of actual combat, there is far too much I need to study and strengthen. I feel a sense of urgency." He shook his head, indicating that rather than visiting relatives and friends or sightseeing, it was better to spend the time on elevating capability and quality.

III

Inside the campus's "Artillery Passing the Torch (炮兵薪火相传)" lecture hall, Tang Youxuan, a cadre from a certain border defense unit, was drawing on his own experiences to recount stories of strengthening the military by taking root at the frontier and achieving merit on the front lines. The phrase "go to where the motherland needs you most" resonated deeply with the comrades in the audience.

The moment the lecture ended, the young trainees surrounded him. Some sought advice on practical experience in border defense duty; others inquired about methods of day-to-day management. One question followed another, and they were reluctant to disperse for a long time. "After hearing Staff Officer Tang's sharing, I have a clearer picture in my mind — what to do and how to do it when I get to the unit is suddenly clear." Said Zhang Hongsheng, a trainee about to graduate.

Watching the scene before him, Tang Youxuan felt genuine joy. He said that in past training at military academies, everyone basically studied on their own, with relatively little exchange between classes, and the good experiences and practices of different units had difficulty spreading outward to be shared.

What left Staff Officer Tang with a deep impression was an exchange and interaction session among several trainees' units, where it turned out that some comrades' conversations kept circling back to which unit had better conditions and which post offered faster career advancement, while those who actually sat down to talk about fighting and leading troops were not many.

During this training, the campus Party committee listened carefully to everyone's opinions and suggestions and decided to give full play to the functional role of Party organizations at all levels, actively building platforms for mutual learning and mutual reference. Trainees generally felt that activities such as the "Artillery Passing the Torch" lecture hall, book-sharing sessions, and trainee forums were directly aimed at training for war and the practice of leading troops; through in-depth interaction between units and the academy, and between units and units, sparks of thought frequently flew, and everyone gained a great deal and was deeply inspired.

What caught the reporter's eye was that during interactive exchanges, they implemented a "honorary squad leader (名誉班长)" mechanism, selecting outstanding graduates from previous years to establish regular contact with current classes, periodically conducting online exchanges and consultations on difficult problems, so that the mentoring and guiding role of backbone cadres could continue even after training ended.

"Good traditions and experiences cannot remain only at the grassroots level — only by going up to the podium and into the classroom can more comrades benefit." As he noted down the contact information of several trainee comrades, Staff Officer Tang reflected.

From reshaping conduct to reinvigorating study conduct, and then to establishing platforms for experience exchange, the campus Party committee and the trainees' units' temporary Party branches each gave full play to their organizational leadership role, effectively rectifying the chronic problems that had existed in past training. Today, the training course has truly returned to its original purpose of "tempering in fire and steel, cultivating people for war (加钢淬火、为战育人)." The trainees here have not only tempered a solid conduct but have also gained the genuine knowledge and skills to step onto the future battlefield.

This Training "Played for Real"

■ Li Shihang, Trainee, a Certain Trainees' Unit, Nanjing Campus of the Army Branch Arms University

For a period of time in the past, whenever a training notification came down, there were always people saying "training is just a chance to relax" or "go out and make some friends," and some even "tipped me off": "When you get there, what matters isn't how much you learn, but how many people you get to know." Honestly, hearing these things, I was somewhat confused inside.

During this training, when the first rankings came out, the unit posted them directly for public display. At that moment, everyone knew this training was "playing for real." The several people in our Party small group immediately reached a consensus on the spot: any points of confusion from the day's classroom instruction had to be digested that same day — no one could drag the others down. From that point on, no one brought up anything else in the evenings; what was spread out on the desks were all maps and notes, and what everyone discussed and exchanged were all combat details. At first it felt like being driven by pressure, but gradually it became clear that when you truly settle your mind and immerse yourself, learning itself becomes a habit.

What left the deepest impression on me was the command exercise. The topic our small group drew was of relatively high difficulty. We worked overtime for several consecutive days and nights running deductions. Everyone was holding back a surge of energy. No one was single-mindedly trying to find some fellow townsman or leader to pull strings, and no one entertained the thought of "close enough is good enough." Instead, we scrutinized the plan over and over for real. When the final results were announced, not only did I rank near the top, but I was individually rated as an outstanding commander. At that moment I understood: recognition earned through genuine ability is more solid and useful than anything else.

What exactly did political rectification training yield? Judging from this training, it rectified away that "getting by (混)" mentality and that "relying on others (靠)" thinking, and rectified into being the proper appearance of Party member cadres — clean and upright, with solid skills. What I am taking away from this training is not a few more names added to my contact list, but a sense of confidence in training for war and fighting. Wherever I go in the future, I will dare to say with my hand on my chest: stand on your own ability, let your real achievements do the talking.

(Compiled by PLA Daily Special Correspondent Yang Kaiming)

“淬火加钢”的感觉找到了 ——陆军兵种大学南京校区一次干部培训的见闻与思考 ■刘小甜 李强云 解放军报特约记者 杨开明 编者按 当前，随着部队各项任务不断拓展深化，党员骨干外出参加各类培训日益频繁。然而，个别官兵参训时仍存在心态松懈、学习浮于表面，或者日常作风松散、纪律意识淡化等问题。在这种情况下，各级党组织要深刻把握习近平党建思想“坚持推进作风建设常态化长效化”的实践要求，结合开展政治整训，在纯正学风、作风上下硬功夫，引导党员骨干把全部心思精力聚焦到学习强能上，确保每一次培训都成为锤炼党性、提升能力的实战“淬火”，为锻造能打胜仗的过硬人才队伍注入源头活水。 盛夏金陵，热浪袭人。某野外训练场上，陆军兵种大学南京校区一场炮兵营战术现地教学正进行到关键环节。教员高望指向不远处的高程点，抛出一个问题：“营队和各连观察所，转移顺序谁先谁后？依据是什么？”学员们争相发言，不同观点激烈碰撞。 训练间隙，记者问几名学员参加此次初级指挥教育培训的感受，“真”“严”“实”成了高频字。“这样的‘硝烟味’，这样的思想碰撞，是我们真正需要的。”一名学员抹了把汗说，这次培训，学到的都是能打胜仗的真本领，“淬火加钢”的感觉找到了！ 一 望着远处仍在热烈讨论的学员，来自某旅的党支部书记宿云柯感慨良多。他告诉记者，这几个月培训，从清晨的队列呼号到夜晚的自主学习，从课堂的全神贯注到内务的一丝不苟，处处透着“不一样”：“最直观的变化，就是备战打仗的精气神提起来了！” 这份“不一样”，始于踏入校门的那一刻。 报到当晚，学员队临时党支部李书记就召集全体学员开了一次支部党员大会。围绕培训任务要求，他结合开展政治整训、推进作风建设常态化长效化，着重强调了培训期间的纪律：“这次培训，从日常作息、课堂纪律到内务标准，都严格管理，绝不走过场。” 宿云柯说，党支部之所以这么做，就是要求大家牢记党员干部身份，始终在思想、学习、作风等方面有更高标准、更严要求，把这里当成锤炼党性、提升能力的“熔炉”。 “没想到，要求得严，落实得更严。”一名学员对此印象深刻。这名学员此前参加过类似培训，一开始有些不以为意：“培训有时要求高、落实低，队干部不会太较真，差不多就行了。”抱着这种“差不多”心态，他起初在学习上不够投入，布置的预习不够认真，课后作业也敷衍了事。结果，一次研讨式课堂上，他由于准备不足，被抽查到时答非所问，被严肃批评并全队通报。不久后，他的内务检查也因不达标被亮了“红灯”。 这让他脸上挂不住了，也彻底警醒：“原来这次是动真格的，那些要求，条条都是硬杠杠，容不得半点马虎。” 身边人身边事，警醒了所有学员。宿云柯坦言，自己虽未像那名学员一样松懈，但初来时也有过“出来培训，不必像在连队那么较真”的想法。可在党支部严格管理下，内务检查、队列会操、课堂考勤全部量化打分，周周通报、月月排名，让他对钢规铁律有了真切认识，开始时刻严格要求自己。 “在部队严，外出培训更严，必须紧起来，多学点真本领。”宿云柯的感悟，道出了培训该有的样子：绝非“休整点”，实为“加油站”。 二 深夜，学员队一间自习室依然灯火通明。 循光走去，只见学员丁万璞正和临时党小组几名成员围在地图前，对着一份想定作业反复推演。为了一处火力支援的时机问题，几人讨论得热火朝天，汗珠顺着脸颊滑落也浑然不觉。“白天教的都是贴近实战的新战法、新理论，不趁热打铁搞明白，第二天就可能跟不上！”丁万璞说出了心里话。 这种争分夺秒的劲头，源于学员队推行的成绩“硬挂钩”机制。“每门课程结业，不仅全队大排名、张榜公示，总成绩单更要直接寄回原单位，作为干部选用调整的重要参考依据。”李书记向记者介绍，制订这项机制，就是要立起“成绩优劣不一样”的鲜明导向。 以往，受一些不良风气和模糊认识影响，个别干部存在“学得好不如混得好”的思想，认为出来培训就是“放松”。丁万璞回忆，数年前他参加过一次培训，个别宿舍课余时间经常开“老乡会”“叙旧会”，个别学员把精力放在吃吃喝喝、拉拉扯扯上，真正花在学习上的精力和时间并不多。 这次培训一开始，其实也出现过类似问题苗头。丁万璞说，他们党小组一名学员在一个周末接到老同学电话，热情邀约他出来“坐一坐，叙叙旧”。放下电话，这名学员一时有些犹疑：“毕竟是多年未见的老同学，不去的话有点抹不开面子。” “咱们这次来是培训的，可不是叙旧的。严守纪律规定，把全部心思精力都用在学习上，才对得起组织的培养，对得起自己的努力。”了解情况后，身为党小组组长的丁万璞及时咬耳扯袖。最终，这名学员不仅婉拒了邀约，更下定决心与过去的“无所谓”心态告别，全身心投入到培训学习中。 丁万璞说，结合开展政治整训，学员队各临时党小组多次围绕一些违规违纪典型案例展开讨论辨析，大家形成了普遍共识，要相互监督提醒，主动摒弃过去的陋习，将精力回归到学习本身。 一个周末清晨，自习室里已是座无虚席。记者问一名学员：“好不容易到了周末，咋不出去放松放松？” “这次培训内容多、含战量高，对照实战要求，我需要学习加强的内容太多了，有种紧迫感。”他摇摇头表示，与其走亲访友、游山玩水，不如把时间花在提升能力素质上。 三 校区“炮兵薪火相传”大讲堂内，某边防部队干部唐幼珣正结合自身经历，讲述扎根边疆、建功一线的强军故事，一句“到祖国最需要的地方去”，引起了台下战友的共鸣。 讲座一结束，青年学员们便将他团团围住，有的请教边防执勤的实战经验，有的询问日常管理的方式方法，一个接一个发问，久久不愿散去。“听了唐干事的分享，心里有底了，到部队该干啥、怎么干，一下清楚了。”即将毕业的学员张洪生说。 看着眼前的场景，唐幼珣由衷欣喜。他说，以往到院校培训，大家基本是各学各的，班与班之间交流较少，不同部队的好经验好做法很难走出去形成共享。 让唐干事印象深刻的，是一次几个学员队之间的交流互动，结果一些同志的话题总绕不开哪个单位条件好、哪个岗位发展快，真正坐下来聊打仗、谈带兵的不太多。 这次培训，该校区党委充分听取大家的意见建议，决定发挥好各级党组织功能作用，积极搭建互学互鉴平台。学员们普遍感到，“炮兵薪火相传”大讲堂、读书分享会、学员论坛等活动，直指练兵打仗、带兵实践，部队与院校、部队与部队之间通过深度互动，频频碰撞思想火花，大家很有收获、深受启发。 让记者眼前一亮的，是互动交流时，他们实行了“名誉班长”机制，遴选往届优秀毕业学员与在校班次建立常态化联系，定期开展线上交流、难题会诊，让干部骨干的传帮带作用在培训结束后依然延续。 “好的传统和经验不能只停留在基层，走上讲台、走进课堂，才能让更多战友受益。”一边记下几名学员战友的联系方式，唐干事一边感慨。 从重塑作风到提振学风，再到建立经验交流平台，校区党委和学员队临时党支部纷纷发挥好组织引领作用，有力纠治了以往培训存在的顽症。如今，培训班真正回归了“加钢淬火、为战育人”的本源，学员在这里不仅锤炼了过硬作风，更收获了走上未来战场的真才实学。 这次培训“动了真格” ■陆军兵种大学南京校区某学员队学员 李始航 以往有一段时间，每当接到培训通知时，总有人说“培训就是放放松”“出去交交朋友”，甚至有人“点拨”我：“到了那，关键不在学多少，而是认识多少人。”说实话，听到这些，自己心里是有些困惑的。 此次培训，第一次排名出来，队里直接公示，那一刻，所有人都知道这次培训“动了真格”。我们党小组的几人当场就达成共识：白天课堂上的疑点当天消化，谁也不能拖后腿。从那以后，晚上再没人提其他的事，桌上摊开的全是地图和笔记，大家讨论交流的全是战斗细节。起初觉得是压力逼着走，后来慢慢发现，当你真正沉下心、钻进去，学习本身就成了习惯。 我印象最深的是那次指挥演练。我们小组抽到的课题难度较高，连续几天加班熬夜推演，大家都憋着一股劲，没有谁一门心思想找哪个老乡、领导通融一下，也没有谁动过“差不多就行了”的念头，而是实打实一遍遍推敲方案。最终成绩公布，我不仅排名靠前，个人也被评为优秀指挥员。那一刻我明白，凭本事赢来的认可，比什么都踏实管用。 政治整训究竟收获了什么？从这次培训看，就是整掉了那种“混”的心态、“靠”的思想，整出了党员干部该有的样子——干干净净、本领过硬。这次培训，我带走的不是通讯录里多了几个名字，而是一份练兵打仗的底气。今后无论走到哪，我都敢拍着胸脯说：凭本事立身，靠实绩说话。 （解放军报特约记者 杨开明整理）
