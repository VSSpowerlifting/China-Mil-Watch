About this analysis
Summary
Second-Class Staff Sergeant Ma Teng, a fine-arts-trained navigator soldier (导航兵) at an Eastern Theater Command Naval Aviation navigation station on the eastern coast, painted a series of murals on the unit's culture wall (文化墙) depicting carrier strike operations, mountaintop watch posts, and station history, incorporating suggestions from fellow soldiers including First-Class Staff Sergeant Meng Yanwei. This is standard political-work content — its value is as a record of how the PLA Navy frames unit cohesion and retention of technically educated conscripts at remote, unglamorous postings, not as evidence of a new policy or capability development. The framing around the station spirit (站魂) and the culture wall as an onboarding tool for newly assigned soldiers points to an institutional effort to normalize long-term service at isolated navigation stations supporting naval aviation.
Translation

Navigator Soldier Ma Teng of a Certain PLA Navy Unit — Standing on a Mountain Peak to Paint the Sea

■ Shi Yichu, Special Correspondent to the PLA Daily, Wang Ningchuan

Ma Teng applies color to the culture wall.

Along the eastern coast, mountains rise in layers of green. Deep within those mountains, a navigation station of a certain Eastern Theater Command Naval Aviation unit stands in quiet solitude. Day after day, the navigator soldiers (导航兵) stationed here keep watch over radio waves from dawn to dusk, their gaze piercing cloud and sky.

The culture wall (文化墙) in the garrison compound is a striking feature amid this remote and lonely mountain wilderness. The colorful murals on the wall depict the navigator soldiers' vigil: an aircraft carrier cleaving through azure waves, a combat aircraft sweeping out of the cloud swells, a sentinel post on the mountaintop standing straight as a pine — the man who sketched this vast expanse of sea and sky is Ma Teng, a Second-Class Staff Sergeant at the station.

Ma Teng majored in fine arts in college. Coming from a bustling city to this remote station, the sense of displacement left him feeling lost and at a loss. One evening at dusk, the station chief, First-Class Staff Sergeant Meng Yanwei, brought Ma Teng to the freshly whitewashed culture wall and said quietly: "To hold the mountain, you must first hold your heart. This place has the position we stand watch over — it is also the home of the navigator soldiers. You studied painting; use the brush in your hand to paint the landscape in your heart."

Ma Teng did not rush to put brush to wall. He first sketched drafts on paper. His comrades offered their thoughts one by one; he recorded every suggestion, revised the drafts several times, and only then picked up the paint and stood before the wall. Without a long ruler, he soaked a thin cord in paint and snapped it against the wall to mark straight lines. Short on paint, he had his family order supplies online and mail them to the station. Before long, a mural of an aircraft carrier cutting through the waves and sailing into the distance began to take shape.

The day Meng Yanwei returned from duty and looked up to see that sweep of deep blue on the wall, a smile spread across his face. "Ma Teng, Ma Teng — full speed ahead (快马加鞭)!" Encouraged by everyone, Ma Teng could not stop. He brought the everyday work and life of his comrades at the navigation station to the wall, one mural after another.

One painting depicts an old-style mobile navigation vehicle. In those years, flight training often began in the early hours before dawn, and the officers and soldiers had to drive up the mountain in advance. Setting up antennas, calibrating frequencies — whether in torrential rain or in wind as cutting as a blade, they had to hold their post on the mountaintop until the last combat aircraft returned. One deep winter, the antenna was torn apart by a violent gale; several men knelt in the snow making emergency repairs for nearly an hour. The wrenches froze and stuck to their gloves; only after returning to the barracks and soaking their hands in hot water could they pull them free.

Another painting tells the story of a bowl of noodle soup. It was not long after Ma Teng had arrived at the station. One night the temperature plummeted, and he returned from duty chilled to the bone. Just as resentment was welling up inside him, Squad Leader Meng brought him a steaming bowl of noodle soup. "The station may be small, but what we are guarding are the combat aircraft in the sky above." Meng Yanwei's words were like a beam of light that shone into Ma Teng's heart. He gradually came to understand that what he was guarding was not only the distant sea and sky, but the station spirit (站魂) that generation after generation of navigator soldiers had carried forward with their youth.

As the murals grew richer, Ma Teng asked everyone what else could be painted. Corporal Liu Linlin said: one aircraft carrier is too lonely — the sea we stand watch over should have more warships cutting through the waves. Meng Yanwei picked up the thread: "And carrier-based aircraft, bearing our dream of a strong military (强军梦)."

Ma Teng incorporated everyone's hopes into a unified "plan" and kept painting for a long time afterward. Waves surging, combat aircraft with heads held high, a fleet formation like a mountain range — when the final stroke fell, the entire culture wall seemed to come alive.

After finishing the late-night watch, moonlight spilled over the top of the wall and illuminated the figures and scenes in the paintings. Standing before the wall, everyone felt the sea and sky in the murals to be extraordinarily vast. When newly assigned soldiers came to the station fresh from their units, Meng Yanwei would introduce them to the unit's traditions, and the first lesson was always a visit to this culture wall.

One new soldier looked up and asked: will the paintings on the wall fade?

Meng Yanwei smiled and answered with certainty: "If they fade, someone will come to paint them again. When we are gone, there will still be people here to keep the watch."

The mountain wind blows year after year; the grass and trees grow green season after season. The navigator soldiers' steadfast watch is as unyielding as a reef. The murals on the wall remain as vivid as new, and Ma Teng still adds a few strokes from time to time — the newly painted warships are larger, and there are more combat aircraft on the flight decks. The mountains here are so high that the sea cannot be seen, but in the hearts of the navigator soldiers, ten thousand miles of boundless blue waves have long since taken up residence.

Original Chinese
海军某部导航兵马腾—— 站在高山画大海 ■石毅初 解放军报特约记者 王宁川 马腾为文化墙涂色。 华东沿海，群山叠翠。深山之中，东部战区海军航空兵某部一座导航台站静静矗立。日复一日，驻守于此的导航兵在晨曦暮霭间守着电波，望穿云天。 营区的文化墙，是这片孤寂山野中一道风景线。墙上的一幅幅彩绘，描绘着导航兵的守望：航母犁开碧波，战鹰掠出云涛，山巅哨位挺立如松——勾勒这片壮阔海天的人，是台站的二级上士马腾。 马腾大学主修美术。从繁华城市来到偏远台站，内心的落差曾让他感到茫然无措。那天黄昏，台长、一级上士孟彦伟把马腾带到新粉刷的文化墙前，轻声说：“守山先守心。这里有我们守望的阵地，也是导航兵的家。你是学画的，就用你手里的笔把心里的风景画出来吧。” 马腾没有急于动笔。他先在纸上勾画草图，战友们你一言我一语，他记下每个人的建议，几易其稿，才捧起颜料站到墙前。没有长尺，他把细绳浸入颜料，弹线定位；缺少颜料，他让家人从网上购买寄到驻地。不久，一幅航母劈浪远航的彩绘图初显模样。 那天孟彦伟值班归来，抬眼望见墙面上的那抹湛蓝，笑意漫上眉梢。“马腾马腾，快马加鞭！”在大家的鼓励下，马腾一发不可收拾，他把导航台站战友们的工作生活日常，一幅一幅接连绽放在墙上。 有一幅画，画的是一台老式移动导航车。当年飞行训练多在凌晨展开，官兵须提前驱车上山。架天线、校频率……无论暴雨如注，还是寒风似刀，他们都得在山顶守到最后一架战机返航。一年深冬，天线被狂风撕裂，几个人跪在雪地里抢修近一个小时。扳手冻得粘住手套，他们回到宿舍才用热水烫开。 还有一幅画，画的是一碗汤面的故事。那是马腾刚来台站不久，有天晚上气温骤降，执勤归来的他被冻了个透心凉。正当他心中充满怨气的时候，孟班长给他端来了一碗热气腾腾的汤面。“台站虽小，可咱们守的是天上的战鹰。”孟彦伟的这番话像一束光，照进马腾心里。他渐渐明白，自己守护的不只是远方的海天，更是一代代导航兵用青春接力的站魂。 彩绘日渐丰富，马腾问大家还能再画什么。下士刘林林说，一艘航母太孤单，咱们守望的海，应该有更多战舰劈波斩浪。孟彦伟接过话：“还有舰载机，承载着我们的强军梦。” 马腾把大家的期望统一进行了“规划”，又接连画了好久。海浪翻涌、战鹰昂首、舰阵如山……当最后一笔落下，整面文化墙仿佛活了过来。 深夜值完班，月光漫过墙头，照亮了那些画中人、画中景。站在墙前，大家眼中的海天格外壮阔。刚刚下连的新兵来台站，孟彦伟给他们讲传统，第一课就是参观这面文化墙。 一位新兵仰头问：墙上的画，会不会褪色？孟彦伟笑了笑，回答得笃定：“褪了，还有人来画。我们走了，台站还有人接着守。” 山风年年吹，草木岁岁青。导航兵的坚守，如同礁石一般顽强。墙上的彩绘依然鲜亮如新，马腾偶尔还会添上几笔——新画的战舰更大，甲板上的战鹰更多。这里山高不见海，但在导航兵心里，早已装下万里无垠的碧波。
