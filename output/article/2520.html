Military Camp Observations | The "Heat Effect" on the Road to Seeking Roots

军营观察丨寻根路上的“热效应”
PLA Daily (解放军报) 16 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A 'root-seeking detachment' (寻根小分队) from a unit of the Beijing Garrison Command traveled to Tangma Village in Liyang, Jiangsu, to trace the 1941 last stand of the New Fourth Army's 16th Brigade, then returned to conduct report-and-exchange sessions, redesign political education classes around wartime Party membership application forms, and stage a combat-history performance recreating the assault on Taixing City. The article documents the Beijing Garrison Command's current approach to hongse jiaoyu (red education): embedding it in immersive site visits, peer-narrated unit history, and physical performance rather than lecture formats, with the explicit institutional goal of converting historical identification into training motivation and willingness to accept dangerous missions. The framing of the July 2025 Beijing flood-relief operation as proof that the red gene produces combat-ready soldiers extends this political work rationale into contemporary operational contexts, though the article provides no independent measure of whether the approach affects actual unit readiness.

News Sample: "We Have Come Back Bearing the Battle Flag"

■ Huang Weiwei

Liyang, Jiangsu. The fields are lush and green, and the Tangma Battle Martyrs Monument stands tall and imposing. Not long ago, a "root-seeking detachment" (寻根小分队) from a unit of the Beijing Garrison Command entered Tangma Village to trace the combat footprints of revolutionary forebears.

"What did we once rely on to win battles, and what did we rely on to win the support of the people?" "As heirs to heroes, how should we carry on their spirit?" "After returning from the root-seeking trip, what should we tell the soldiers?" — These were the questions the unit's political instructors had been turning over in their minds before setting out. Only by thinking through and truly understanding these questions could they genuinely grasp the weight of being "heirs to heroes" and allow the red gene (红色基因) to flow into the blood of officers and soldiers.

Inside the Tangma Martyrs Memorial Hall, a photograph of New Fourth Army 16th Brigade Commander Luo Zhongyi and Political Commissar Liao Haitao taken on the eve of battle transported everyone back to that tragic and stirring era of struggle.

On a cold morning in November 1941, a bitter wind swept across the fields and smoke rolled over the embankments. More than 500 New Fourth Army soldiers formed up in Tangma Village to meet the enemy, launching a breakout battle of towering courage. Facing an encirclement by more than 3,000 Japanese and puppet troops, the soldiers of the New Fourth Army's 16th Brigade fought vastly outnumbered. When ammunition was exhausted and relief cut off, Brigade Commander Luo Zhongyi raised his arm and cried out: "Communist Party members, Youth League members, step forward — comrades, fight to the death with the enemy!" In the fierce battle that followed, Luo Zhongyi and Liao Haitao ultimately fell on the position, dying heroically for their country.

As the guide's heartfelt narration unfolded, the political instructors seemed to see before them the heroic figures of their forebears charging forward one after another in unyielding combat. Company Political Instructor Zhu Yongchao not only listened with exceptional attentiveness but took meticulous notes. In his view, he had to record every word of the explanation in full and bring it back to the unit intact, so that officers and soldiers could gain a deeper understanding of history.

Word spread that the officers and soldiers of the "root-seeking detachment" were coming, and local villagers gathered early, eager to see what had become of the heroic unit that had once fought so bloodily.

"Fellow villagers, we have come back bearing the battle flag!" the officers and soldiers reported with deep feeling to the locals at the forum. "We are the heirs of the New Fourth Army's 16th Brigade. Returning today to this red land, we want to tell everyone that we have not let down the trust our forebears placed in us. Last year, more than 30 of our members won gold and silver medals in higher-level military skills competitions, and we made an important contribution to flood-relief operations in our garrison area."

Decades have passed, yet the stories connected to the Tangma Battle have never stopped circulating locally. Today, seeing the bright and vigorous faces of the officers and soldiers, many of the village elders had tears glistening in their eyes. "Over the years, the memorial activities in the village have never been interrupted. The story of the New Fourth Army has been passed down from generation to generation here, all to ensure that the red spirit is carried on forever," said villager Liu Zhiqing.

Not only in Tangma Village: at Houzhou Primary School in Liyang, the Young Pioneers established the "Luo Zhongyi Squad" and the "Liao Haitao Squad," and General Wang Zhi — a participant in the battle — was engaged as an off-campus counselor for both squads. For more than 40 years, each cohort of squad members has served as a worthy "heir to heroes," consistently carrying out activities such as the Qingming Festival sweeping of the Tangma Martyrs Cemetery and listening to old heroes recount battle stories.

Red stories are like the growth rings of an old tree, recording the tenacity of a nation rising from suffering, and like seeds full of vitality, taking root and sprouting in the depths of memory across generations.

All along the way, the members of the "root-seeking detachment" found their hearts unable to settle for a long time. This heroic unit had been so glorious in the past; carrying forward the red bloodline is a mission and responsibility that officers and soldiers of the new era must firmly keep in mind.

"We will certainly carry forward the red spirit and, like our heroic forebears, dare to fight and be certain of victory." Before departing, the officers and soldiers solemnly presented a replica of the battle flag to the village Party secretary for display in the memorial hall. This was both a consolation to the fallen martyrs and a solemn pledge to continue passing on the red gene.

Field Investigation: The "Heat Effect" on the Road to Seeking Roots

■ Sun Chengye, Huang Weiwei

[Photo caption: Officers and soldiers of a unit of the Beijing Garrison Command visit and study at the Museum of the Chinese People's War of Resistance Against Japanese Aggression. Photo by Zhou Jiaxing.]

**Ardent Blood (热血) —**

**"What we have taken up from our forebears is not only their rifles, but the faith they guarded with their lives"**

As the lights-out bugle sounded, the bustling barracks gradually fell quiet. Peng Wugang, political instructor of the "Red Fourth Company" of a unit of the Beijing Garrison Command, was bent over his desk organizing the gains from a recent "root-seeking journey."

Old photographs from the revolutionary war era, precious materials from the archives, images of battlefield sites — Peng Wugang needed to convert these materials as quickly as possible into vivid education lessons, so that soldiers, in the process of learning history and reflecting on their founding aspirations (初心), could come to understand the core fighting spirit forged by their forebears in blood.

A few days later, in an education class, Peng Wugang showed officers and soldiers several Party membership application forms preserved in the Maoshan New Fourth Army Memorial Hall.

On the screen, a Party membership application form temporarily mimeographed in 1940 had long since yellowed. Its columns were simple: name, native place, education received, struggles participated in, personal history — no columns for "benefits" or "rank," yet at the top of the form were two resounding pledges: "Obey the organization, strictly guard secrets, never betray the Party" and "Sacrifice the individual, work actively, observe Party discipline."

The officers and soldiers in the audience did not yet fully understand what joining the Party meant in 1940 in the enemy rear areas of southern Jiangsu. In those circumstances, revolutionary forebears faced Japanese and puppet army "mopping-up" operations and betrayal by collaborators at any moment; once captured, torture and death awaited them. The forebears who joined the Party were not seeking promotion or wealth — they were joining the ranks fighting for national liberation. They gave their lives to the cause of the Party and the people.

In 1940 on Maoshan, many comrades who joined the Party may have picked up their rifles the moment they finished filling out their application forms, and stepped onto the battlefield the moment they finished taking their oath. More than 80 years have passed, yet those yellowed application forms still challenge our founding aspirations: today, why exactly do we join the Party?

Private First Class Xiao Wang bowed his head in shame upon hearing this history. Initially, he had felt that joining the Party was a great honor, and so he trained with exceptional diligence, earned recognition through outstanding performance, and was honored to join the Party organization. But measuring himself against the deeds of his forebears, he reflected that he had always harbored a reluctance to face danger when difficult or risky missions arose, and he resolved that in the future he must charge forward and take on responsibilities with courage, putting his founding aspirations into practice through concrete action.

Beyond this class, after the "root-seeking detachment" returned, the unit also arranged for political instructors to give a report-and-exchange session for all officers and soldiers, drawing on everything they had seen, heard, thought, and felt along the way.

Before the root-seeking trip, Company Political Instructor Zhu Yongchao had paused many times in the unit's honor room, holding a silent dialogue across time with revolutionary forebears. But actually setting foot on the ground where history had unfolded left him profoundly shaken. Villagers told him that on the slope where Brigade Commander Luo Zhongyi had fallen, rusted shell casings had been turned up on multiple occasions, and on the trunk of the old locust tree at the village entrance, marks left by shrapnel could still be seen. History was no longer a distant story but the real memory of the land beneath his feet. Moved deeply within, Zhu Yongchao felt the immense weight of the word "inheritance."

"Today, as heirs of the New Fourth Army, we pay you our highest respects. It was you who, at the moment of mortal peril, stepped forward without hesitation, guarded the peace of home and country with your burning blood, and with your lives forged a spiritual monument that will never be erased…" In the video, the detachment's officers and soldiers stood in neat formation facing the monument, paying heartfelt tribute to the martyrs.

At the report-and-exchange session, the officers and soldiers in the audience were also moved by the resounding declaration. "What we have taken up from our forebears is not only their rifles, but the faith they guarded with their lives. As heirs to heroes, only by treating every training session as a charge and every mission as a battle can we honor the wishes of our forebears and pass the red bloodline from generation to generation," said Sergeant Yan Bobo.

**Ardent Passion (热情) —**

**"Restoring and reenacting the heroic deeds of our forebears is to draw closer to them — a little closer, and then a little closer still"**

"Swear to scale the city walls and take Taixing City!" The impassioned cry pierced through the stage smoke and struck hard at the heart of every officer and soldier present.

Not long ago, a cultural performance with the theme of carrying forward the red gene was staged at a unit of the Beijing Garrison Command, vividly recreating the unit's combat history of growth and battles fought across the country through stage plays, situational dramas, and other forms.

On stage, artillery fire struck again and again; the soldiers were repulsed multiple times as they tried to scale the walls, yet still they gritted their teeth and charged forward into danger. When the battle flag bearing its glorious burden was planted atop the city wall, the entire hall erupted in sustained applause.

During the period from scripting to creation and performance, the director's team and the core creative personnel carefully reviewed long-sealed historical battle records, drawing creative nourishment from them. While visiting the history museum, an old table bearing 51 bullet holes moved everyone deeply. It was a witness to the forebears who, under a hail of bullets, carved out the path of assault with their lives.

In order to present well the combat scene of "building a human ladder and scaling the walls to annihilate the enemy," the creative process went through multiple rounds of overhaul and reconstruction. Initially, the script's arrangement emphasized grand narrative, but the work lacked emotional power. To address this, Director Wang Dali spent the night reviewing the forebears' combat accounts and choreographed the "human-ladder assault" dance, depicting the revolutionary forebears' ultimate fighting spirit of "overcoming every difficulty without being overcome by any difficulty."

In order to restore the fearless bearing of forebears who faced death with equanimity, the participating officers and soldiers, wearing performance costumes soaked through with sweat, repeatedly practiced climbing and falling on the hard stage floor — and not one person complained of hardship or fatigue.

Sergeant Zhou Haokun, positioned at the very bottom of the "human ladder," had several deep purple pressure marks on his shoulders and found it difficult even to raise his arms. His comrades urged him to rest before continuing to practice, but he shook his head and said: "Back then, what our forebears carried on their shoulders was the lives of their comrades and the hope of taking the city. Compared to the responsibility they bore — a thousand, ten thousand times greater — what does a little pain like this amount to?"

Passion comes from understanding. Every time Sergeant Chen Xinyang rehearsed, he had to step on a comrade's shoulders and fall from the edge of the "city wall," yet he never once furrowed his brow. He wrote in his diary: "Standing beneath the 'city wall,' I came to understand the courage of our forebears — not that they were unafraid of death, but that knowing the danger, they still charged forward. Restoring and reenacting the heroic deeds of our forebears is to draw closer to them — a little closer, and then a little closer still."

Everyone understood in their hearts: back then, the forebears used flesh and blood to stop real bullets. If one cannot endure a few drops of sweat or a scraped knee, one cannot embody the true soul of a hero.

Understand the heroes. Chase the heroes. Become the heroes. On the competition ground of the all-military bayonet-fighting competition, soldier Wang Weiping's old wrist injury had gone numb from the impact. At the very instant his opponent seized on the opening and thrust forward with his rifle, a flash like lightning crossed Wang Weiping's mind: "Dare to fight and be certain of victory, courageous and tenacious (敢打必胜，勇猛顽强)." He gritted his teeth, abruptly dropped his center of gravity to evade the thrust, then immediately executed a self-invented technique he had drilled more than a hundred times, driving his rifle tip precisely onto the scoring point of his opponent's protective gear.

The whistle blew. Wang Weiping removed his face guard, and the sweat at his temple slid down along his brow. "First place, individual bayonet fighting!" When the result was announced, his gaze passed over the cheering crowd, as if he could see, more than 80 years ago, the figures of his forebears charging across that land.

**Ardent Energy (热能) —**

**"As heirs to heroes, no matter how difficult or dangerous, we must charge to the front and hold the line — we must absolutely not let down the people's expectations"**

In this unit, every batch of new soldiers arriving at their companies is greeted with the "Three Firsts" (三个第一) induction education: the first thing they do is visit the honor room; the first class they attend is a red traditions class; and the first song they sing is a red song.

Private First Class Tang Le still remembers that on the day he set down his pack, the company instructor immediately took him to the company's honor room. "This is the root of our unit, and also your first lesson in military life," the instructor said, pointing to the photograph of martyr Dong Yuxiang on the wall and recounting with deep feeling the heroic story of how the forebear killed and wounded more than 30 enemies on the front line before finally dying a heroic death.

Then a traditional education class formally began. From the fire-and-smoke years when the unit was first formed, to the experiences of officers and soldiers in the new era shouldering heavy responsibilities, one glorious chapter of history after another let Tang Le understand what an "heroic unit" meant. In class, the squad leader taught everyone to sing "March Forward, Loyal Guardians" — a song passed down through generations of the unit's officers and soldiers. In the stirring melody of lines like "Fight bravely, fear no sacrifice, write our glory on the military flag," the "thermal energy" (热能) of the red gene gradually fused into the blood of officers and soldiers.

The young officers and soldiers carrying forward the red gene soon faced a major test. In July 2025, Beijing was struck by extreme heavy rainfall, and the unit's officers and soldiers responded to orders and immediately launched rescue and evacuation operations for the people. Throughout the operation, special situations arose frequently and dangers lurked everywhere, but nothing could stop the officers and soldiers from advancing toward danger in their rescue efforts.

"It's very slippery here — step on the top of my foot to get across!" After being washed by the rain, the mountain rocks were exceptionally slippery. Tang Le used his own foot as a stepping stone to let the people pass safely.

On the riverbank, officers and soldiers were soaked through by the rain; their feet had soaked white and their hands had developed blood blisters, yet they gritted their teeth and persisted. After 15 consecutive hours of combat, they moved and stacked more than 70,000 sandbags and successfully reinforced 4,860 meters of embankment, ensuring the safety of the lives and property of residents along the banks.

If anyone were to ask again whether "this generation of young people can shoulder the future," these soldiers — covered in mud, eyes shining with light — have already answered with their actions.

"From the revolutionary battlefield to the flood-relief front line, what changes is the battlefield environment; what does not change is the founding aspiration of loyal service to the people. As heirs to heroes, no matter how difficult or dangerous, we must charge to the front and hold the line — we must absolutely not (绝不) let down the people's expectations!" said Political Instructor Wu Xuesi.

The river murmured on, and one moving scene after another of unity between the military and the people quietly unfolded. The smoke of battle from more than 80 years ago has long since dissipated, but the stories engraved in the passage of time still carry the power to reach directly into the heart.

The inheritance of the red gene is both deeply planted in the hearts of officers and soldiers and woven into ordinary posts and positions. Not long ago, when higher authorities issued new equipment, Sergeant Liu Xiandong was the first to sign up for training.

However, the transition from sniper to operator of the new equipment was not easy. To close the gap, Liu Xiandong used his rest time to study instructional videos repeatedly, engraving the key points of each movement in his mind. But when it came to actual equipment operation, problems kept arising one after another, which inevitably left Liu Xiandong somewhat discouraged.

Momentary passion cannot sustain a prolonged charge; enduring strength lies in the roots of the spirit. Facing the pressure, Liu Xiandong thought of the heroic deeds of revolutionary forebears and silently resolved: in the revolutionary war era, the forebears held on to the very last moment for the Party's cause — he himself had to grit his teeth and hold on, and win honor for the team.

In the end, through day-after-day of diligent training, Liu Xiandong's accumulated effort bore fruit, and he achieved outstanding results in the assessment.

Eyewitness Account: The Company's History Is Written by Us

■ Chen Zexin

[Photo caption: A unit of the Beijing Garrison Command organizes a live-fire shooting assessment. Photo by Shan Leijie.]

In the year I have served as company instructor, I have come to understand deeply: the depth to which the red gene is transmitted bears directly on the unit's cohesion and combat effectiveness. What we pursue is not to leave the company history sitting in the display cases of the honor room, but to allow the spiritual power contained within it to flow into the blood of officers and soldiers.

At the New Fourth Army Huangqiao Campaign Memorial Hall, the guide's heartfelt narration restored the scene of that time, as if one had personally witnessed every shot fired and every blade of grass in the battle. And the villagers of Tangma Village — the vast majority of them can recount the history of the Tangma Battle fluently. This familiarity with and enthusiasm for history is precisely what our company needs to draw on when transmitting its spirit; only by generating emotional resonance among officers and soldiers can we achieve the leap from "knowing" to "identifying."

"This photograph was taken when I participated in the military parade for the first time in 2019. On the training ground, I looked at the flag I was holding aloft and thought again of the scene of the Huangqiao Battle that the company instructor had described to me when I first enlisted. Our company's forebears fought tenaciously against the enemy for a full day and night — this spirit of fighting to the very end is something we must strive even harder to carry forward and develop." Wang Tao, now the company history narrator, has participated in two military parades; each time he marched with firm and powerful steps past Tiananmen Square, his heart was always stirred by the inspiring force of the company's historical honors.

From listener to practitioner, and then to narrator, Wang Tao's growth trajectory has made me ever more clearly aware: only by allowing officers and soldiers to find a sense of belonging and a sense of value in history, and by making everyone a part of the company's history, will inheritance truly become a conscious act.

Every officer and soldier is a writer of the company's history. Today, after officers and soldiers participate in major tasks and achieve outstanding results, they leave their mark in the company's honor room.

As instructor, I am both a transmitter of history and a builder of the company's development. In the future, I will continue to lead officers and soldiers to deepen their identification with the spirit through its transmission and promotion, and to innovate in practice. When the company song rings out, every officer and soldier's heart surges with passion; when the company flag unfurls, every person's eyes shine with light — these moments contain our inexhaustible driving force to press forward.

Sharp Perspective: Why We Must Frequently "Look Back"

■ Wang Aidong

The red bloodline is not only the bond connecting past, present, and future, but also the spiritual code (精神密码) for forging the unity and soul of officers and soldiers at large. It carries the footprints of a century of struggle and nourishes the growth of generation after generation of officers and soldiers. For the armed forces, the red bloodline is the source of confidence. By carefully tracing the past, cherishing every red memory, and finding paths to cultivating people that are both close to the heart and practically effective, we can allow the light of history to illuminate the present.

The best education often occurs in the resonance of heart with heart. Whether feeling the founding aspiration of joining the Party from yellowed application forms, or reliving the glorious and arduous revolutionary years through situational dramas, or drawing the strength to forge ahead through self-reflection — education is no longer rigid and solemn, but full of the warmth of life and the texture of history. Static text becomes vivid story; abstract spirit becomes vigorous inner drive. Officers and soldiers temper their thinking by looking back at history, improve themselves by measuring themselves against their forebears, enhance their capabilities through realistic training, and transform profound insights into the ability to win battles — allowing red education to truly enter the heart and take effect in action.

And this inheritance is not the work of a single morning or evening; it requires us to cultivate it in a normalized, sustained manner (常态化深耕): making "red root-seeking" a "required course" for officers and soldiers to replenish their spiritual calcium, and through visiting old sites and touching historical artifacts, bringing history "alive" in an immersive experience; making "recounting the stories of heroes and models" a "fixed action" in daily education, so that those vivid heroic figures step out of the display cases of memorial halls and become examples that everyone around can feel and emulate.

When the historical baton is passed into the hands of a new generation of officers and soldiers, this inheritance is no longer merely a ritual, but a soul-stirring relay. Officers and soldiers of the new era must transform the warmth drawn from the depths of history into the courage to hack through thorns and brambles; they must forge the faith that their forebears upheld in desperate circumstances into the unyielding tenacity that refuses to yield in training and war preparation. Every sweat-drenched training session in preparation for war, every instance of volunteering for a mission, is a manifestation of the red gene quietly fusing into the bloodstream. This is the finest consolation to the fallen martyrs, and the firm and resounding footfall of a heroic unit advancing toward the goal of a strong military (强军目标).

几天后的教育课上，彭悟刚向官兵展示了茅山新四军纪念馆中收藏的几张入党志愿表。 屏幕中，一张1940年临时油印的入党志愿表早已泛黄，上面的栏目很简单：姓名、籍贯、受过什么教育、参加过什么斗争、本人经历……没有“待遇”“职级”等栏，却在表头写着两句响亮的承诺：“服从组织、严守秘密、永不叛党”“牺牲个人、积极工作、遵守党纪”。 台下的官兵还不全然了解，在1940年的苏南敌后入党意味着什么。在那样的境遇里，革命先辈随时要面对日伪军的“扫荡”、汉奸的告密，一旦被捕，等待他们的就是酷刑和死亡。先辈们入党，图的不是升官发财，是为了加入为民族解放而战的队伍里。他们把自己的生命，交给了党和人民的事业。 1940年的茅山，很多入党的同志，可能刚填完志愿表就拿起了枪，刚宣誓完就走上了战场。80多年过去了，一张张泛黄的志愿表，依然在叩问我们的初心：今天，我们入党到底是为了什么？ 上等兵小王听到这段历史后惭愧地低下了头。最初，他觉得入党很光荣，于是格外刻苦训练，凭优异表现得到大家的认可，光荣加入了党组织。但对照先辈的事迹，他反思自己在遇到险难任务时总有畏难情绪，立志今后一定冲锋在前、奋勇担当，用实干践行入党初心。 除了这次授课，“寻根小分队”返程后，该部还安排政治教员结合一路所见所闻、所思所悟，为全体官兵进行一场汇报交流。 寻根前，某连政治指导员朱永超曾数次驻足荣誉室，与革命先辈隔空对话。但真正踏足历史发生的现场，他感到震撼不已。村民们告诉他，在罗忠毅旅长倒下的土坡上，曾多次翻出锈迹斑斑的弹壳，村口那棵老槐树的树干上，还能看到当年弹片擦过的痕迹。历史，不再是遥远的故事，而是脚下这片土地真实的记忆。在内心强烈的触动下，朱永超感受到“传承”二字的千钧重量。 “今天，我们以新四军传人的身份，向你们致以最崇高的敬意。是你们，在危亡时刻，挺身而出、义无反顾，用满腔热血守护家国安宁，用生命铸就了永不磨灭的精神丰碑……”视频里，小分队官兵面向纪念碑整齐列队，深情致敬先烈。 汇报交流现场，台下官兵也被掷地有声的告白感染。“我们接过的不只是先辈的钢枪，更是他们用生命守护的信仰。作为英雄传人，唯有把每一次训练都当成冲锋，把每一次任务都当作战斗，才能不辜负先辈遗志，让红色血脉代代相传。”中士闫博博说。 热情—— “还原重现先辈们的壮举，是为了向他们靠近一点，再靠近一点” “誓死登上城头，拿下泰兴城！”激昂呐喊穿透舞台上的硝烟，重重叩击在场每一位官兵的心扉。 前不久，一场以传承红色基因为主题的文艺汇演在北京卫戍区某部上演，通过舞台剧、情景剧等多种形式，生动再现了这支部队发展壮大，转战南北的战斗历程。 舞台上，炮火一次次袭来，官兵们数次攀城被击退，但仍旧咬紧牙关，迎着危险奋勇冲锋。当那面承载荣光的战旗插上城头，全场响起经久不息的掌声。 从编排到创演的那段时间，导演组和主创骨干仔细查阅尘封的战史资料，从中汲取创作养分。参观史馆时，一张留有51个弹孔的旧桌子，令大家深受触动。这是先辈们顶着枪林弹雨，用生命开辟出攻城道路的见证。 为了将“搭设人梯、登城歼敌”的战斗场景呈现好，创作过程经历了多轮推翻与重构。起初，剧本编排侧重于宏大叙事，但作品感染力不足。为此，导演王大利连夜查阅先辈的战斗纪实，编排了“人梯攻城”这段舞蹈，展现革命先辈“压倒一切困难而不被任何困难所压倒”的极致血性。 为了还原先辈们视死如归的无畏姿态，参演官兵穿着被汗水浸透的演出服装，在坚硬的舞台地板上反复进行攀爬与跌落练习，但没有一个人叫苦叫累。 处于“人梯”最下方的中士周昊琨，肩膀上印着几道深紫的压痕，连抬手都有些吃力。战友劝他歇一歇再练，他却摇摇头说：“当年先辈们肩上扛的是战友的生命，是攻城的希望，比起他们扛起的千倍万倍的责任，我这点儿疼算什么？” 热情源于读懂。中士陈鑫阳每次排练时，都要踩着战友的肩膀，从“城墙”边缘摔下，可他从来没有皱过一下眉。他在日记里写道：“站在‘城墙’下，我体会到先辈们的勇敢——不是不怕死，而是明知危险，依然要向前冲。还原重现先辈们的壮举，是为了向他们靠近一点，再靠近一点。” 大家心里都明白：当年先辈们是用血肉去挡真子弹，如果自己流几滴汗、磕破点皮都受不了，就无法展现英雄真正的魂。 读懂英雄，追赶英雄，成为英雄。在全军刺杀比武的赛场上，该部战士王卫平的手腕旧伤被震得发麻。就在对手抓住破绽、举枪刺来的瞬间，王卫平脑海里突然如电光般闪过那句“敢打必胜，勇猛顽强”。他咬紧牙关，猛地沉下重心避开锋芒，紧接着使出苦练了上百次的自创招法，枪尖精准命中对方护具上的得分点。 哨声响起，王卫平摘下护面，额角的汗水顺着眉骨滑下。“单人对刺第一名！”结果公布时，他的目光越过欢呼的人群，仿佛看见了80多年前，先辈们在那片土地上冲锋的身影。 热能—— “作为英雄传人，再难再险也要冲在前、守得住，绝不能辜负人民期望” 在这支部队，每一批新兵下连，迎接他们的是“三个第一”入营教育——第一件事是参观荣誉室，第一堂课是红色传统课，唱的第一首歌曲是红色歌曲。 河岸边，官兵们被雨淋得浑身湿透，双脚泡到发白、双手磨出血泡仍咬牙坚持。经过15个小时接续战斗，他们搬运堆垒沙袋7万余个，成功加固堤坝4860米，确保沿岸居民生命财产安全。 如果有人再问起“这一代年轻人能否扛起未来”，这些满身泥巴、眼中带光的战士已经用行动给出了答案。 “从革命战场到抗洪一线，变的是战场环境，不变的是忠诚为民的初心。我们作为英雄传人，再难再险也要冲在前、守得住，绝不能辜负人民期望！”政治教导员吴学思说。 河水潺潺，一幅幅军民团结的动人画面静静铺展。80多年前的硝烟早已散尽，但那些镌刻在岁月里的故事，依然有着直抵人心的力量。 红色基因传承，既深植官兵内心，又融入平凡战位。前不久，上级配发新装备，中士刘贤东第一时间报名参训。 然而，从狙击手到新装备驭手的转型并不容易。为了弥补差距，刘贤东利用休息时间对着教学视频反复琢磨，把动作要领刻在脑海里。可一到实装操作，问题还是接踵而至，这让刘贤东难免有些泄气。 一时的激情难以支撑长久的冲锋，恒久的力量藏在精神的根脉里。面对压力，刘贤东思及革命先辈的英雄事迹，暗下决心：革命战争年代，先辈们为了党的事业坚守到最后一刻，自己必须咬牙挺住，为团队争光！ 最好的教育，往往发生在心与心的共鸣之中。无论从泛黄的党表中感受入党初心，还是从情景剧中重温革命峥嵘岁月，或是从自我反思中激发奋进力量，教育不再刻板严肃，而是充满了生活的温度与历史的质感。静态的文字变成了鲜活的故事，抽象的精神化作了蓬勃的内在动力。官兵在回望历史中淬炼思想，在与先辈的对照中完善自我，在实战训练中提升能力，让那些深刻的感悟转化为胜战本领，让红色教育真正走进内心里、落在行动上。
