About this analysis
Summary
The PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force, operating in the Gulf of Aden aboard CNS Tangshan, CNS Daqing, and CNS Taihu, has completed over one hundred escort batches since departing in October 2024, participated in the 'Peace Will-2026' and 'Sea Guardian-4' exercises, and institutionalized post-mission after-action reviews that produced revised emergency response procedures down to individual battle-station actions, including a 'graduated warning, tiered response, precision interception' protocol for suspected pirate small-boat encounters. The article documents a deliberate campaign to counter the 临时心态 (temporary-assignment mentality) endemic to multi-unit task forces with finite rotations—a structural problem the Party Committee framed explicitly as a 政绩观 (performance-and-achievement view) failure that breeds formalism and neglect of foundational work. The framing points to institutional awareness that rotating composite task forces systematically underinvest in capability accumulation, and the article's value is as a record of how the PLA Navy is trying to use political education to solve an organizational design problem it cannot easily fix structurally.

On the Gulf of Aden, after that after-action review… ■ Du Jiangfan, Tan Zhenzhen

On a morning over the Gulf of Aden, on the flight deck of the PLA Navy's CNS Tangshan, the roar of engines filled the air as rotor wash sent a mist of water droplets soaking the flight-deck crew's combat uniforms, and the helicopter settled firmly onto the deck.

"This forward reconnaissance and identification run—we confirmed the target almost a full minute faster than last time," pilot Xiao Zhu said, removing his helmet and turning to the aircraft commander beside him. "Looks like those corrective measures we set at the after-action review actually work."

Back in his cabin, Xiao Zhu entered the day's data in his "escort diary." Flipping back through the entries, reading through set after set of flight data, Xiao Zhu understood that the steadily shrinking times traced back to that "unconventional" (不按常理出牌) critique-style after-action review.

Last November, with the safe arrival of the Panamanian cargo vessel Nanyan Gem at a sea area in the western Gulf of Aden, the PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force successfully completed the first escort mission conducted under its independent command.

As the radar return of the Nanyan Gem gradually faded, CNS Tangshan turned to execute follow-on tasks. In the combat operations center, lights blazed as an after-action review originally scheduled for thirty minutes stretched to nearly an hour.

During the escort, the task force had adapted to changing sea-area weather conditions and the actual circumstances of the vessels, employing both accompanying escort and relay escort methods. During key sea areas and key time periods, it had also dispatched helicopters for aerial patrol. The entire mission recorded zero incidents and zero errors. The crew of the Nanyan Gem placed a special call to express their sincere gratitude to the task force.

By convention, the successful completion of a first independent escort mission should have been an occasion to summarize achievements and boost morale. But when the after-action review opened, the task force commander went straight to the point: "The achievements aren't going anywhere—they'll still be there. The problems, if we don't address them, won't go away either. Today, we talk more about problems and less about achievements."

The moment he finished speaking, the operations center fell silent. One by one, participants worked through the detailed shortfalls: coordination and handoff between the helicopter's forward reconnaissance element and the ship was not tight enough; some younger officers and enlisted personnel were not sufficiently proficient in emergency response procedures; there was still room to optimize the special operations detachment's security deployment. Although none of these issues had affected the completion of the current escort mission, they bore directly on the task force's subsequent capability development.

"After the escort mission ends, what will we have left behind?" Even before setting sail, the Party Committee of the PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force had turned this question over repeatedly.

An escort task force that safely delivers merchant vessels to their destinations without incident has fulfilled its mission; a team temporarily assembled and temporarily thrown together just needs to avoid accidents and return safely, and that counts as success—the Party Committee had long sensed these attitudes among the officers and enlisted personnel.

The task force's personnel came from different organic units and would return to their original posts when the escort mission ended. How could a task force assembled from multiple units with a finite mission cycle both hold firm to the escort mission and complete its phased tasks, while also discarding the temporary-assignment mentality (临时心态) and laying a solid foundation for long-term development? The task force commander acknowledged candidly that from the moment the lines were cast off in October 2025, the task force had been facing a major examination (大考) on the question of how to view performance and achievement (政绩观).

"What is temporary is the organizational structure and the tour of duty; what is long-term is the mission and the responsibility." The task force commander pointed to the Gulf of Aden on the nautical chart and looked out at all the officers and enlisted personnel attending the after-action review. "The task force has a finite mission timeline, but there is no rest stop on the road of combat-capability building. Every additional detail we dig into today, every additional shortfall we make up today, makes the foundation of far-seas escort one degree more solid."

In response to the identified problems, the task force established corrective measures on the spot. In the days that followed, Xiao Zhu and his comrades stood their watches by day and worked overtime at night to review procedures, optimize response plans, and conduct targeted supplementary training. What had originally been a single quick escort after-action review became a real-world assault on hard problems (实战攻坚) under the banner of "a temporary task force planning for the long term."

The helicopter crew, drawing on escort realities, refined their plans for covert reconnaissance and ship-helicopter joint security operations. The special operations detachment, focusing on escort security details, optimized position deployment procedures and improved emergency response speed. Veteran senior enlisted leaders shared without reservation the experience and techniques accumulated over multiple long-range voyages, personally coaching younger comrades in hands-on operational skills. There were no grand gestures, no flashy highlights—officers and enlisted personnel settled in and worked each detail into place, shored up each shortfall until it was solid.

The test came without warning. Shortly afterward, the task force received an escort request from the Chinese-flagged cargo vessel Linhai Hope. Learning that the vessel had a slow speed and low freeboard, the task force proactively proposed providing escort through a combination of safety monitoring and accompanying escort.

During the transit, Xiao Zhu once again flew the helicopter forward on patrol and security. When the optimized procedures were put into use, the mission was again completed successfully—but he was pleasantly surprised to find that ship-aircraft communications were smoother and that documenting suspicious targets was more efficient.

The task force escorted the Linhai Hope safely to a sea area in the Gulf of Aden, and at the moment of parting, the captain and all crew members put pen to paper and wrote a letter of thanks titled "Though the Paper Is Short, the Sentiment Runs Deep—An Expression of Respect": "This escort experience has given us the most genuine and vivid understanding of 'a strong motherland.' It is not the numbers in the news, not the language in the reports—it is the 'Chinese coordinate' we could find in an instant when we encountered danger in foreign waters; it is the confidence that lets us tell foreign sailors with pride, 'We have the Chinese Navy escorting us'…"

Radar operator Zhang Miaosen also wrote in his escort diary: "I used to think a temporary task force only needed to hold the line for the present and complete the mission. Now I understand: a temporary task force has a temporary mission, but it also carries a long-term responsibility."

In the space between breaking down and building up, the measuring stick of the performance-and-achievement view (政绩观) grew ever clearer. "Even a temporary organization must plan for the long term and must do substantive work that lays foundations, benefits the future, and serves those who come after." This conviction has become the shared understanding of every officer and enlisted member of the 48th Escort Task Force.

The "Analytical Answer Sheet" Written on Deep-Blue Wake Lines

—A News Investigation into the PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force's Cultivation and Practice of a Correct View of Performance and Achievement (政绩观)

■ Sun Gangzheng, Du Jiangfan

[Photo caption: The PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force escorts the cargo vessel Nanyan Gem through the Gulf of Aden. Photo by Du Jiangfan.]

Not "My" Achievement, but "Our" Relay

How does an escort task force discard short-term thinking? How does an escort mission leave behind a lasting record of achievement? This is the examination question that the PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force—assembled from multiple vessels and multiple units—had to confront from the moment it set sail.

To establish the right measuring stick, misconceptions first had to be broken down. Some officers and enlisted personnel raised the point: "We've been doing escort missions for so many years—how much more achievement can we create?" Others admitted frankly: "Even the most standout achievement fades once we're back."

The Party Committee put these attitudes on the table: "Let's start by asking ourselves three questions: What is the mission of escort operations? When our superiors entrust us with three warships and hundreds of officers and enlisted personnel, what kind of answer sheet do they most hope we will turn in? Can the work we do today withstand the test of actual combat and the test of history?"

The three questions left the Party Committee members present in deep reflection.

"We are the relay runners of the escort mission, not passersby." The Party Committee reached a consensus: they must both accomplish the current escort mission and carry forward the escort spirit, accumulate far-seas experience, and forge a hardened work style—taking responsibility for the mission, for the officers and enlisted personnel, and for follow-on tasks.

A thematic education campaign centered on the theme of "integrated organization, integrated effort, integrated spirit" (合编、合力、合心) was broadly launched. There was no dais, no prepared speeches. Officers and enlisted personnel from more than twenty different units brought their stools and gathered in circles on the flight deck, in the mess, and at their battle stations to speak freely.

Among the materials circulated was a photograph that drew crowds of onlookers: in December 2008, the PLA Navy's first escort task force set out for the Gulf of Aden, opening a new chapter in the PLA Navy's overseas defense of national interests. In the photograph, Chinese sailors aboard an escorted vessel stood in formation on deck, waving warmly toward the motherland's warship, a banner behind them bearing eight characters: 感谢祖国、祖国万岁 ("Thank the Motherland, Long Live the Motherland").

"This yellowed photograph is the most vivid historical footnote to the relay race of the escort mission." When the education session ended, a veteran senior enlisted leader wrote these words in his "escort diary."

"The experience we use today was left to us by those who came before; what we leave behind today will be used by those who come after." The task force commander said: "Escort operations are not a solo sprint—they are a relay race, one baton passed to the next."

From individual thoughts to collective mission, from immediate tasks to long-term legacy, gradually the complaints grew fewer and the shared understanding grew greater.

"Generation after generation of officers and enlisted personnel have relayed their way to the Gulf of Aden—who exactly did we come for? Whose ships are we escorting? For whom are we building this record of achievement? That question is the first lesson in viewing performance and achievement (政绩) along the deep-blue wake." The commander told this reporter that the task force integrated the study and education on cultivating and practicing a correct view of performance and achievement with education on the escort mission, and that this lesson would run from the moment of departure through the end of the mission. The certificate of completion for this lesson is the officers' and enlisted personnel's own conscious sense of being "relay runners" and the responsibility that comes with it.

Not Temporary "Satisfaction," but Lasting "Peace of Mind"

For an escort task force, the work of serving officers and enlisted personnel is easily treated as a "phased task." Can the real difficulties facing officers and enlisted personnel actually be resolved? More than a few had muttered about this privately when they first set out. But the Party Committee set the tone from the very beginning: the human heart is the most solid foundation of combat power; rallying the hearts of the troops has never been a temporary matter—it is foundational work, work that benefits the long term, work whose results are invisible (潜绩).

Before setting sail, the task force had already surveyed the family situations of all officers and enlisted personnel, established a "register of family hardships" (官兵家庭困难台账), and set up a coordinated support mechanism with rear-area units and local governments. During the voyage, a soldier's family member suffered a sudden serious illness. The task force Party Committee immediately contacted the rear, coordinated with the hospital to open a priority channel, and arranged for a dedicated person to handle the full process of support, resolving the soldier's concerns about his family. When that soldier learned through a satellite phone call that his family was being properly cared for, he told the ship's political commissar with deep emotion: "The organization has taken my family matters to heart—I will absolutely complete this mission well." That satellite phone call spanning the ocean was recorded in the "escort diary," silently demonstrating that organizational care is the most powerful magnetic field for cohering combat power.

Stories like this abound in the task force. To address the psychological condition of officers and enlisted personnel during the long voyage, the task force established a "Deep-Blue Heart-to-Heart Room" (深蓝谈心室), where psychological support specialists held one-on-one conversations with officers and enlisted personnel. To address the problem of personnel going long stretches without seeing their families, the task force regularly received video letters from family members transmitted via satellite and organized "family connection" sessions on weekends. To address the monotony of life at sea, officers and enlisted personnel held activities such as the "Deep-Blue Flight Deck Sports Meet," bringing color and vitality to the otherwise tedious long-voyage routine.

"I used to think that running education sessions and organizing activities only counted as achievement if you had records and photographs to show for it. Now I understand more clearly that real achievement is more officers and enlisted personnel smiling, more knots in their hearts untied, more drive at their battle stations." The political commissar of CNS Daqing told this reporter: "There is no warmer answer to the question of 'achievement' (政绩) in the 'escort diary' than this. Even after the follow-on missions end, these good systems and good work styles that warm and rally the hearts of the troops will be passed on to subsequent escort task forces. The warmth of organizational care is the cornerstone of officers' and enlisted personnel's lasting 'peace of mind,' and the fertile ground from which combat power grows."

Not Today's "Visible Achievement" (显绩), but Tomorrow's "Bedrock Confidence"

After the escort mission ends, what will we have left behind? As the study and education on cultivating and practicing a correct view of performance and achievement deepened, this examination question about the essential nature of "achievement" was once again brought out for discussion by the task force Party Committee.

By this point, the mission had been underway for more than six months. The task force had completed the "Peace Will-2026" maritime joint exercise and the China-Pakistan "Sea Guardian-4" maritime joint exercise, executed more than one hundred batches of escort missions, made technical port calls in multiple countries, and achieved solid results across all areas of work.

At this point, the task force Party Committee posed a question for all officers to reflect on: "When we complete our mission and return home, beyond the visible achievements of escort missions, exercise results, and foreign affairs activities, what can we leave behind for subsequent escort task forces? What can we leave behind for the PLA Navy's far-seas combat capability development?"

This question cuts to the essence of the performance-and-achievement view (政绩观): to pursue short-term, visible "manifest achievement" (显绩), or to focus on long-term, foundational "latent achievement" (潜绩)?

Opening the task force commander's "escort diary," the flyleaf bears a conspicuous inscription: 功成不必在我、功成必定有我—"Success need not come in my time; success will surely come through my effort." These twelve characters are the spiritual coordinates for viewing "achievement" (政绩) on the deep-blue voyage.

"Our leg of the relay must not only run a good split—it must also lay the groundwork for the next leg." The task force commander explained that the task force's three warships are themselves a vivid embodiment of "relay escort": CNS Tangshan is executing a Gulf of Aden escort mission for the first time; CNS Daqing is on its second; and CNS Taihu is on its fifth.

"This precious experience cannot remain only within this one mission, written only into our summary reports." The task force Party Committee decided to make "systematizing far-seas escort operational experience, refining emergency response procedures, and developing a cadre of far-seas mission specialists" a sustained effort running throughout the entire mission.

This work starts from the details, yet carries long-term significance. In response to new characteristics and new trends in piracy activity in the Gulf of Aden sea area, the task force organized each vessel to comprehensively revise and refine its emergency response plans based on actual situations encountered during escort operations—from the overall procedures for armed rescue and counter-terrorism/counter-piracy operations at the macro level, down to the operational norms for radar target discrimination and the tactical actions for small-boat interception at the micro level, all were refined and optimized.

"Drawing on the situations we encountered during the mission, we refined every step, every action, every precaution down to the battle station and down to the individual." The commanding officer of CNS Tangshan explained: for example, in driving off suspected pirate small boats, they developed an operational procedure of "graduated warning, tiered response, precision interception" (分级警告、梯次处置、精准拦截), with clear norms established for responses at different distances and in different tactical situations. "This set of norms was not written up sitting in a cabin," he said. "It was ground out in the swells of the Gulf of Aden, through one incident response after another."

At the same time, the task force established a routine mission summary mechanism: every escort run, every training session, every incident was reviewed and analyzed one by one, with detailed records kept of process details, response experience, and improvement measures. They compiled and produced a series of practical operational points covering escort missions, combat-realistic training, and equipment maintenance, allowing escort experience to accumulate continuously and be passed down from generation to generation—achieving the virtuous cycle of "execute one batch of missions, accumulate one batch of experience, develop one batch of specialists."

"We must not only accomplish this batch of escort missions well—we must also pave the road and build the bridge for follow-on missions." The task force commander said: "When the escort wake lines merge into the deep blue, the breadth of spirit embodied in 'success need not come in my time' (功成不必在我), and the precious experience and strong team that this spirit has allowed to accumulate, are the weightiest chapter and the most solid bedrock confidence we leave to the future."

This Edition's Editor in Dialogue with the Commander of the PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force——

Don't Be a Temporary Visitor—Be a Good Relay Runner

As the entire military deepens its study and education on cultivating and practicing a correct view of performance and achievement (政绩观), the PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force, ten thousand li away in the Gulf of Aden, has integrated the performance-and-achievement view into every watch stood, every incident handled, and every stretch of deep-blue wake—embodying the mission commitment of "escort in the far seas, train in the far seas, fight in the far seas." Recently, this edition's editor connected with the commander of the PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force to discuss how to cultivate and practice a correct view of performance and achievement.

Editor: Commander, what is the greatest difficulty for an escort task force in cultivating and practicing a correct view of performance and achievement?

Commander: For us, the greatest difficulty is breaking through the "temporary" mentality. Many officers and enlisted personnel feel that the task force is temporary, the mission is phased, and once it's done they go home—there's no need to expend effort on work that is "invisible, intangible, and of no use to yourself." If this mentality is not eliminated, it easily breeds formalism (形式主义)—producing flashy "showcase projects" (亮点工程) while neglecting the foundational, long-term latent achievement (潜绩). So from the very beginning we emphasized: the identity may be temporary, but the responsibility is not; the tour of duty has a time limit, but the commitment does not.

Editor: How does the task force balance current mission requirements with long-term development?

Commander: The two are not in opposition—they are internally unified. The current mission is the main line; long-term development is the foundation. Only by solidifying the foundation and making up the shortfalls can the current mission be accomplished better. For example, we hold an after-action review after every escort run—it looks like it takes up time, but after the corrections are made, subsequent escorts are safer and more efficient. We develop specialists and pass on experience not only for this escort mission, but also so that when they return to their original units they can play a greater role.

Editor: In your view, where is the task force's achievement (政绩) primarily reflected?

Commander: Successfully completing all escort missions with zero accidents and zero risks is of course very important, but more important is leaving behind a practical body of experience, a strong cadre of specialists, and a refined set of equipment maintenance standards. These things will continue to play a role in subsequent missions. Achievement must be written in the hearts of officers and enlisted personnel, written in the deep-blue wake lines. As long as every escort task force runs its own leg well, the wake lines of the People's Navy will reach ever farther.

There Is No "Temporary" at the Battle Station

■ Zhang Miaosen, Radar Operator, PLA Navy 48th Escort Task Force

[Photo caption: Officers and enlisted personnel of the PLA Navy's 48th Escort Task Force hold a pledge-signing ceremony themed "Advance into the Ocean, March with the Motherland." Photo by Du Jiangfan.]

This is my first time executing a Gulf of Aden escort mission. When I first joined the task force, I genuinely had the "temporary" mindset. I came from a different unit, I'd go back when the mission ended, and I always felt that as long as I didn't make mistakes at my battle station and returned safely, the mission was accomplished. As for things like accumulating experience and developing talent—those had nothing to do with me.

During one watch, a radar signal appeared that I stared at for a long time without being able to identify. It was only thanks to a timely reminder from veteran senior enlisted leader He Yongchao that no harm was done. Afterward, I felt deeply ashamed and told myself to be more careful next time and not make mistakes again.

What I didn't expect was that the after-action review that evening specifically raised my problem. Chief He didn't criticize me—instead, he pressed into my hands his well-worn duty log. In that log, written in different colors of ink, were the data signatures of different target types in different sea areas, along with his own summaries of every mistake he had made. "Our task force may be temporary, but there's nothing temporary about the battle station," Chief He said. "The skills you learn today, you can use when you go back to your original unit. The experience you summarize can also help the next batch of comrades."

The chief's words woke me up. From that point on, I no longer carried the "temporary" mentality. Every watch, I carefully recorded the data on every target; during training, I actively sought guidance from veteran specialists; during rest time, I buried myself in the chief's duty log to study. Gradually, I too became capable of independently completing target identification quickly.

Now, more than six months into the escort mission, I have compiled my own duty notebook. When the mission handover comes, I will pass this notebook on to the next batch of comrades.

亚丁湾上，那次复盘会之后…… ■杜江帆 谭振振 亚丁湾的清晨，海军唐山舰飞行甲板上，伴随着巨大的轰鸣声，旋翼卷起的水雾打湿了地勤人员的作训服，直升机稳稳停在甲板上。 “这次前出查证，比上次提前将近1分钟判明目标。”飞行员小朱摘下头盔，对身边的机长说，“看来复盘定的那几条整改措施，还真管用。” 回到房间，小朱在“护航日记”里记下当天的数据。往前翻日记，看着一组组飞行数据，小朱明白，不断缩短的时间，源于那场“不按常理出牌”的检讨式复盘会。 去年11月，随着巴拿马籍货船“南远宝石”号安全抵达亚丁湾西部某海域，海军第48批护航编队顺利完成独立指挥实施的首批护航任务。 “南远宝石”号的雷达回波逐渐消失，唐山舰则转向执行后续任务。作战室内，灯火通明，一场原定半小时的复盘会，开了将近一个小时。 护航期间，编队根据海区气象变化和船舶实际情况，采取伴随护航和接力护航的方式护送船舶，在重点海区、重点时段还派出直升机进行空中巡逻，全程零特情、零差错。“南远宝石”号的船员们专门致电，对编队表达诚挚谢意。 按惯例，首次独立护航顺利完成，本该总结成绩、鼓舞士气，可复盘会一开始，编队指挥员就开门见山：“成绩不讲跑不了，问题不讲少不了。今天，我们多说问题，少谈成绩。” 话音刚落，作战室里安静下来。会上，大家逐一梳理细节短板：直升机前出查证协同衔接不够紧凑、部分年轻官兵应急处置流程不够熟练、特战分队警戒部署还有优化空间……虽然这些问题并不影响本次护航任务的完成，却直接关系编队后续能力生成。 “护航任务结束后，我们能留下什么？”尚未起航时，海军第48批护航编队党委就反复思考这个问题。 护航编队把商船安全护送到目的地，不出任何问题，就算圆满完成任务；临时“抽的班子”、临时“凑的队伍”，只要不出事、平安回，就是成绩……官兵心里这些想法，编队党委早有察觉。 编队人员来自不同建制单位，护航任务结束便会回归原岗位。如何让多支部队抽组而成、任务周期有限的护航编队，既守牢护航使命、完成阶段性任务，又摒弃临时心态、为长远建设打牢基础？编队指挥员坦言，从2025年10月解缆起航的那一刻，编队就面临着一场关于政绩观的“大考”。 “临时的是建制和任期，长远的是使命和责任。”编队指挥员指着海图上的亚丁湾海域，看向参加复盘会的全体官兵，“编队的任务时间有期限，但战斗力建设没有休止符。今天我们多抠一个细节，多补一块短板，远海护航的根基就更牢固一分。” 针对梳理出的问题，编队现场明确整改举措。接下来的时间里，小朱和战友们一起，白天坚守在战位，晚上加班梳理流程、优化处置方案、开展针对性补训。原本可快速完成的一次护航复盘，变成了“临时编队谋长远”的实战攻坚。 直升机组结合护航实际，完善隐蔽侦察、舰机联合警戒方案；特战分队针对护航警戒细节，优化岗位部署流程，提升应急响应速度；老班长们把自己多次远航积累的经验和技巧，毫无保留地分享给年轻战友，手把手带教实操技能……没有轰轰烈烈的形式，没有华而不实的亮点，官兵们沉下心，把一个个细节抠到位、一项项短板补扎实。 考验不期而至。不久后，编队接到中国籍货船“林海希望”轮的护航申请。了解到其航速慢、干舷低，编队主动提出采取安全监控和伴随护航的方式为其护航。 航渡途中，小朱再次驾驶直升机前出巡逻警戒。优化后的流程投入使用后，同样是顺利完成任务，但他惊喜地发现，舰机通联更顺畅了，取证可疑目标更高效了。 编队护送“林海希望”轮安全抵达亚丁湾某海域，在分航之际，船长和全体船员提笔写下一封名为《纸短情长诉敬意》的感谢信：“这次护航经历，让我们对‘祖国强大’有了最真切、最鲜活的理解：它不是新闻里的数字，不是报告中的论述，而是当我们在异国海域遇到风险时，能第一时间找到的‘中国坐标’；是让我们在与外国船员交流时，能骄傲说出‘我们有中国海军护航’的底气……” 雷达兵张淼森在护航日记里也写下：“以前总觉得，临时编队只需守好当下、完成任务即可，现在才明白，临时编队有临时使命，更有长远担当。” 破立之间，政绩观的标尺愈发清晰。“临时机构也要谋长远，也要做打基础、利长远、惠后续的实在事。”这一观念已然成为第48批护航编队全体官兵的共识。 写在深蓝航迹上的“辨析答卷” ——来自海军第48批护航编队树立和践行正确政绩观的新闻调查 ■孙刚政 杜江帆 海军第48批护航编队护送货轮“南远宝石”号通过亚丁湾。杜江帆摄 不是“我的”政绩，而是“我们的”接力 护航编队如何摒弃短期思维？护航任务，如何留下长远政绩？这是海军第48批护航编队——这支由多艘舰艇、多支部队抽组而成的编队，自起航就必须直面的考题。 立起标尺，先要破除误区。有官兵提出：“护航都这么多年了，我们还能创造多大的成绩？”也有官兵坦言：“再‘出圈’的成绩，回去也就没有了。” 这些想法，被编队党委摆上了桌面：“我们先问自己3个问题：护航的使命是什么？上级把3艘舰艇、数百名官兵交给我们，最希望我们交出什么样的答卷？我们今天做的工作，能不能经得起实战的检验、历史的检验？” 3个问题，让在场的党委委员陷入沉思。 “我们是护航事业的接力者，不是过客。”编队党委一班人形成共识——既要完成好当下护航任务，更要传承好护航精神、积累好远海经验、锤炼好过硬作风，做到对使命负责、对官兵负责、对后续任务负责。 一场以“合编、合力、合心”为主题的专题教育活动广泛展开。没有主席台、没有讲话稿，来自20多个不同单位的官兵搬着小马扎，在飞行甲板、餐厅和战位围坐，畅所欲言。 其中有一张照片被许多人围观——2008年12月，中国海军首批护航编队奔赴亚丁湾，开启了中国海军赴海外维护国家利益的崭新征程。照片里，被护航的中国船员在甲板上列队，向着祖国军舰热情挥手，背后的横幅上，写着8个大字：感谢祖国、祖国万岁。 “这张泛黄的照片，正是护航事业接力赛最生动的历史注脚。”教育结束，一名老班长在“护航日记”中这样写道。 “我们今天用的是前人留下的经验，今天我们留下的，明天也会接着用。”编队指挥员说，“护航不是单打独斗的短跑，是一棒接一棒的接力赛。” 从个人想法聊到集体使命，从眼前任务聊到长远传承，慢慢地，大家的牢骚少了、共识多了。 “一代代官兵接力奔赴亚丁湾，我们到底为谁而来？为谁护航？为谁树绩？这个问题，是我们在深蓝航迹里观政绩的第一课。”指挥员告诉笔者，编队把树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育与护航使命教育结合起来，从起航之初到任务结束，这堂课会贯穿始终。这堂课的结业证书，便是官兵心中那份“接力者”的自觉与担当。 不是临时的“满意”，而是持久的“安心” 对护航编队而言，服务官兵这项工作很容易被当成“阶段性任务”。官兵的实际困难能不能解决？不少官兵刚出发时也私下嘀咕过。但编队党委从一开始就定下了基调：人心是战斗力最坚实的根基，凝聚兵心从来不是临时事，而是打基础、利长远的潜绩。 起航前，编队就提前摸排了所有官兵的家庭情况，建立了“官兵家庭困难台账”，与后方部队、地方政府建立了联动保障机制。航行途中，一名战士的家人突发重病，编队党委第一时间与后方取得联系，协调医院开通绿色通道，安排专人全程对接保障，解决了战士的后顾之忧。当这名战士通过卫星电话得知家人得到妥善照顾时，激动地告诉舰政委：“组织把我的家事放在心上，我一定把任务完成好。”这通跨越重洋的卫星电话，写进了“护航日记”，它无声诠释着，组织的关怀是凝聚战斗力的最强磁场。 这样的故事，在编队里还有很多。针对远航中官兵的心理状态，编队开设了“深蓝谈心室”，由编队心理服务骨干一对一与官兵谈心交流；针对官兵长时间看不到家人的问题，编队定期通过卫星传输接收家属发来的视频信件，在周末组织“亲情连线”；针对海上生活单调的问题，官兵们举办了“深蓝甲板运动会”等活动，让枯燥的远航生活变得有声有色。 “以前总觉得抓教育、搞活动，要留下台账、留下照片，才算有成绩，现在更明白，真正的成绩，是官兵们脸上的笑容多了，心里的疙瘩解了，战位上的劲头足了。”大庆舰政委告诉笔者，“‘护航日记’里关于‘政绩’最温暖的答案，莫过于此。哪怕后续任务结束了，这些暖兵心、聚兵心的好制度、好作风，也会传给后续的护航编队。组织关怀的温度，就是官兵持久‘安心’的基石，更是战斗力生成的沃土。” 不是今天的“显绩”，而是明天的“底气” 护航任务结束后，我们能留下什么？随着树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育深入推进，这道关于“政绩”本质的考题，再次被编队党委拿出来讨论。 此时，任务已经执行半年多的时间，编队完成“和平意志-2026”海上联合演习、中巴“海洋卫士-4”海上联合演习，执行了百余批护航任务，技术停靠了多个国家，各项工作都取得不错的成绩。 此时，编队党委给全体干部出了一道思考题：“等我们完成任务返航，除了护航任务、演习成果、外事活动这些看得见的成绩，我们能给后续的护航编队留下什么？能给海军的远海作战能力建设留下什么？” 这道题，直指政绩观本质：是追求短期的、看得见的“显绩”，还是着眼长远的、打基础的“潜绩”？ 翻开编队指挥员的“护航日记”，扉页上赫然写着——功成不必在我、功成必定有我。这12个字，是深蓝征途上观“政绩”的精神坐标。 “我们这一棒，不仅要跑出好成绩，更要为下一棒打好基础。”编队指挥员介绍，护航编队的3艘舰艇，本身就是“接力护航”的生动缩影——唐山舰是首次执行亚丁湾护航任务，大庆舰是第2次执行护航任务，而太湖舰已经是第5次执行护航任务了。 “这些宝贵的经验，不能只留在这一次任务里，只写进我们的总结报告里。”编队党委决定，把“梳理远海护航实战经验、完善应急处置流程、培养远海任务骨干队伍”，作为贯穿任务全程的一项工作，扎扎实实做下去。 这项工作，从细节处着手，却有着长远的意义。针对亚丁湾海域海盗活动出现的新特点、新动向，编队组织各舰艇结合护航中遇到的实际情况，对应急处置预案进行了全面修订完善，大到武力营救、反恐反海盗的整体流程，小到雷达甄别目标的操作规范、小艇拦截的战术动作，都进行了细化优化。 “我们结合任务中遇到的情况，把每一个步骤、每一个动作、每一个注意事项，都细化到了战位、细化到了个人。”唐山舰舰长介绍，比如驱离疑似海盗小艇，他们总结出了“分级警告、梯次处置、精准拦截”的实操流程，把不同距离、不同态势下的处置，都做了明确的规范。“这套规范，不是坐在房间里编出来的，是我们在亚丁湾的风浪里，在一次次特情处置中磨出来的。”他说。 同时，编队建立常态化任务总结机制，每次护航、每场训练、每起特情，都逐一复盘梳理，详细记录过程细节、处置经验、改进举措。他们整理形成护航任务、实战化训练、装备维护等系列实操要点，让一批批护航经验接续积累、代代传承，实现“执行一批任务、积累一批经验、培养一批骨干”的良性循环。 “我们不仅要完成好这一批护航任务，更要为后续任务铺路搭桥。”编队指挥员说，“当护航的航迹融入深蓝，这份‘功成不必在我’的胸襟和由此沉淀的宝贵经验与坚强队伍，正是我们留给未来最厚重的篇章、最坚实的底气。” 本版编辑对话海军第四十八批护航编队指挥员—— 不做临时客 当好接力人 在全军深入开展树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育之际，海军第48批护航编队在万里之外的亚丁湾，以“护航在远海、训练在远海、战斗在远海”的使命担当，将政绩观融入每一次战位值守、每一场特情处置、每一段深蓝航迹。日前，本版编辑就如何树立和践行正确政绩观，与海军第48批护航编队指挥员进行了连线交流。 编辑：指挥员您好，护航编队树立和践行正确政绩观，最大的难点是什么？ 指挥员：对我们而言，最大的难点是破解“临时”心态。很多官兵觉得，编队是临时的，任务是阶段性的，干完就走，没必要费力气做“看不见、摸不着、自己用不上”的工作。这种心态不除，就容易滋生形式主义，搞一些表面光鲜的“亮点工程”，而忽视打基础、利长远的潜绩。所以我们从一开始就强调，身份有临时，责任无临时；任期有期限，担当无期限。 编辑：编队是怎么平衡当前任务和长远建设的？ 指挥员：两者不是对立的，是内在统一的。当前任务是主线，长远建设是根本。把基础打牢了，把短板补上了，当前任务才能完成得更好。比如，我们每次护航后都开复盘会，看起来耽误了时间，但整改后，后续的护航更安全、更高效。我们培养骨干、传承经验，不仅是为了这次的护航任务，也是为了他们回去之后可以发挥更大的作用。 编辑：在您看来，编队的政绩主要体现在哪些方面？ 指挥员：圆满完成所有护航任务，实现零事故、零风险，当然很重要，但更重要的是，我们要留下一套实用的经验、一支过硬的骨干队伍、一套完善的装备维护规范。这些东西，会在后续的各项任务中继续发挥作用。政绩，要写在官兵的心里，写在深蓝的航迹里。只要每一批护航编队都跑好自己的这一棒，人民海军的航迹就会越走越远。 战位没有“临时”二字 ■海军第48批护航编队雷达兵 张淼森 海军第48批护航编队官兵举行“向大洋挺进、与祖国同行”宣誓签名仪式。杜江帆摄 我是第一次执行亚丁湾护航任务。刚加入编队的时候，我心里确实有“临时”的想法。我来自另一个单位，任务结束就回去，总觉得只要自己在战位上不出差错，安安全全返航，就算完成任务了。至于经验积累、人才培养这些事，和我关系不大。 一次值更的时候，有一个雷达信号，我看了半天没认出来，多亏老班长何永超及时提醒，才没有误事。事后，我特别愧疚，想着下次自己多加小心，别再出错就行。 让我没想到的是，那天晚上的复盘会，专门点了我的问题。何班长没有批评我，而是把他那本翻得起毛的执勤本塞给我。本子上用不同颜色的笔，标着不同海域、不同类型目标的数据特征，还有他每次犯错的总结。“咱们虽然是临时编队，但战位没有临时的。”何班长说，“你今天学会的本领，回到原单位也能用。你总结的经验，还能帮到下一批战友。” 班长的话，点醒了我。从那以后，我不再抱着“临时”心态。每次值班，我都认真记录每一个目标的数据；训练的时候，主动向老骨干请教；休息的时候，就抱着班长的执勤本钻研。渐渐地，我也能快速独立完成目标甄别了。 现在，护航任务过了6个多月，我也整理了一本自己的执勤笔记。任务交接的时候，我会把这本笔记交给下一批战友。 （杜江帆整理）
