Dog Days Sentinel | Above the Clouds: The Parachute Training Director Holds Fast to the "Safety Post"

三伏探哨丨云端之上：伞训主任坚守“安全哨”
PLA Daily (解放军报) 6 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Liu Jie, the parachute training director of an unidentified Air Force brigade, conducts pre-jump equipment inspections and in-flight safety oversight during a summer parachute drop exercise, personally checking every jumper before exit and standing at the cabin door at one thousand meters altitude. The article documents the brigade's institutionalized practice of cadre-led example-setting (以身作则), linking Liu's 'safety post' (安全哨) role, a Party committee standing committee lead-jump during a high-intensity live exercise, and the brigade commander's nightly bunk and sentry checks (查铺查哨) into a single work-style (作风) narrative. The framing—anchored to the brigade's connection with hero Huang Jiguang and a 1958 photograph of Wuhan Military Region commander Chen Zaidao serving as a common soldier—points to a deliberate effort to reinforce cadre-grassroots cohesion as a readiness and morale issue, though the article identifies neither the brigade's unit designator nor its garrison location.

Reporter Visits an Air Force Brigade's Parachute Training Site —— Above the Clouds: The Parachute Training Director Holds Fast to the "Safety Post"

■ Wang Baoqiang, PLA Daily Reporter Wang Yue | Illustrations: Fang Han

Liu Jie (right) inspects parachute equipment one person at a time. Photo by Long Hao

It is the height of the dog days (三伏), and the heat is at its peak. During a visit to an Air Force brigade, this reporter learned that the brigade has a special "safety post" (安全哨)—a post with no dedicated manning slot, no fixed sentry box, yet one whose "sentinel" appears on time for every parachute training mission. The brigade happened to be organizing a parachute drop exercise, so this reporter decided to go and see for himself.

On the airfield runway, the residual heat of burning aviation kerosene still lingered, washing over one's face on gusts of hot wind. After only a short walk, this reporter was drenched in sweat. The roar of fighter engines grew ever closer, and the officers and soldiers about to execute the parachute jump had already assembled under the blazing sun.

"When you're about to land, adjust your direction in time to prevent landing injuries…" Before this reporter had even reached the formation, a loud, resonant voice carried over.

"The one speaking is Liu Jie, the parachute training director—and the 'sentinel' of the 'safety post.'" A staff officer accompanying this reporter explained that Director Liu often says, "One end of the parachute cord is connected to our comrades' safety; the other end is connected to combat effectiveness." Whether in scorching summer or bitter winter, whenever parachute training is held and he is at the unit, he will without fail appear at the training site on time.

As they spoke, this reporter watched Liu Jie walk up to a soldier whose expression was taut with tension: "Feng Zhuo, how do you adjust for a crosswind landing?" The young soldier was momentarily nervous and stumbled through the key operating points.

"The points are right, but your voice is shaking a little—I'm afraid you'll shake the parachute cords loose. Relax!" Liu Jie's words drew a burst of laughter, and Feng Zhuo's expression eased considerably.

"Strap on parachutes!" After reporting the latest meteorological data, Liu Jie issued the order.

The bulky parachute rigs were shouldered by the officers and soldiers; the click of metal buckles and the tug of tightening harness straps sounded one after another. In the sweltering air, Liu Jie meticulously inspected each soldier's equipment.

"As a veteran paratrooper who has safely jumped hundreds of times, Director Liu's eyes are a measuring instrument. When inspecting equipment, he can frequently spot minute deviations that others overlook." A brigade leader present at the training site told this reporter: one year, a new soldier preparing to jump had fastened one of the buckles on his harness system in reverse. Liu Jie spotted it at a glance, had the new soldier redo his self-check, and averted a dangerous situation.

"Tighten that waist belt a bit more," "Re-secure that weapon"… Liu Jie inspected each person's equipment in turn and raised corrective requirements. Only after checking the last member of the formation did he issue the order: "All up to standard—prepare to board!"

Inside the cabin, the deafening roar and stifling heat intertwined. As the aircraft climbed to an altitude of one thousand meters, this reporter moved to Feng Zhuo's side and asked: "You're about to jump—how do you feel?" "A little nervous, but Director Liu helped me check my parachute rig, so I feel confident. It'll definitely be fine!" Feng Zhuo gripped his harness straps tightly, full of confidence.

The cabin door slowly opened, and a rush of cool wind howled in. "Take those three steps steadily, count the seconds as you step to the door, keep your eyes open, and operate calmly!" The exit signal light suddenly lit up. Liu Jie stood beside the cabin door, repeating the key points aloud again and again.

The jumpers proceeded to the cabin door one by one in accordance with the signal. Liu Jie stood like a vigilant sentinel, holding fast to the "safety post" a thousand meters in the sky, helping each officer and soldier eliminate safety hazards. After checking each one, he gave a light pat on the shoulder as a signal and raised a thumbs-up.

Stepping out of the aircraft, tucking legs and bending at the waist… Parachute canopies bloomed one after another in the air, descending toward the designated landing zone.

After all members had exited the aircraft, the plane returned to base as planned. Liu Jie, having completed his "sentinel" mission, did not end his work there—he stepped off the aircraft and walked briskly toward the command post, waiting for news from the landing zone. "All personnel landed safely!" Hearing the report come over the radio, Liu Jie let out a long breath.

After the parachute training concluded, this reporter followed Liu Jie into the brigade's "Model Airborne Company" (模范空降兵连)—the company in which special-class hero Huang Jiguang served before his death.

Inside the company's honor room, a black-and-white group photograph caught this reporter's attention. Liu Jie explained: in September 1958, the former General Political Department issued the "Regulations on Cadres at All Levels of the Military Serving as Soldiers in Companies for One Month Each Year," setting off an army-wide wave of cadres going down to serve in companies. Chen Zaidao, then commander of the former Wuhan Military Region, changed into an ordinary soldier's uniform and came to this company; the photograph records that very scene.

According to the company history, during that month of serving as a soldier in the company, Chen Zaidao worried that the soldiers would not let him stand night sentry duty, so he pretended to be asleep at night and immediately got up to join the soldiers when they changed shifts. Because of his age, Chen Zaidao encountered difficulties when practicing marksmanship. He proactively asked soldiers to teach him techniques and did not rest even on Sundays. In the end, Chen Zaidao mastered the shooting techniques for four types of infantry weapons and achieved an excellent score in the live-fire assessment.

"The fine traditions of a heroic unit have never been slogans hung on the lips—they are demonstrated through every word and deed." Liu Jie told this reporter that over more than sixty years, leading cadres and Party member backbone personnel at all levels of the brigade have woven the fine work style (作风) of personally working at the front line into their very blood, forming the value pursuit of "wherever the combat post is, that is where the cadres' presence will be found" (战位在哪里，干部的身影就出现在哪里).

Not long ago, the brigade organized a high-intensity live parachute jump training exercise. Because of turbulent airflow in an unfamiliar airspace and rapidly changing wind speeds, a number of officers and soldiers participating for the first time developed a sense of apprehension and reluctance. To help the officers and soldiers firm up their confidence, the brigade Party committee standing committee members made a commitment: we will take the lead and jump first!

On the day of the live jump, the Party committee standing committee members were the first to board the aircraft. When the aircraft reached the designated airspace and the cabin door slowly opened, the standing committee members, composed and precise in their movements, resolutely leapt from the cabin. Action is the best demonstration: the brigade leaders' lead jump greatly boosted the morale of the officers and soldiers. Everyone shed their mental burdens, adjusted their state in time, and calmly jumped from the cabin; all personnel landed safely. Reflecting on this jump experience, one soldier remarked with feeling: "With the leading cadres charging out front, we had both the confidence and the drive."

Night had fallen, but the garrison area remained unbearably hot. This reporter strolled through it and was soon drenched in sweat.

Walking to a certain sentry post, this reporter saw a senior colonel (大校) officer conducting a sentry inspection. Drawing closer, this reporter discovered that the senior colonel was none other than the brigade commander himself. "Summer heat is intense, unexpected situations are frequent, and brigade leaders conducting nighttime bunk and sentry checks (查铺查哨) is a tradition we have upheld for decades," he said, signing his name in the "Senior Officers and Staff Duty Roster for Bunk and Sentry Checks" (首长机关查铺查哨值班表) as he spoke. The brigade commander told this reporter that after finishing this post, he still had to proceed to other points within the garrison area to continue his rounds.

Set a Good Example and Take the Lead

■ Wang Yue

Deep in the night, the heat in the heart of the Central Plains remained intense. Having visited this Air Force brigade's "safety post"—a post with no fixed sentry box and no specific markings—this reporter was filled with deep feeling.

Work style (作风) is both image and combat effectiveness. Chairman Xi has pointed out that an excellent work style is a distinctive hallmark and political advantage of our military; work style building must be grasped firmly and implemented solidly as a foundational, long-term task for the military, so as to forever preserve the political character of the people's army, carry good traditions into the new journey, and carry forward good work styles in the new era.

Throughout this reporting visit, whether it was the parachute training director holding fast to the "safety post" or leading cadres persisting in nighttime bunk and sentry checks, this reporter could always see the brigade's leading cadres setting an example and going first.

Set a good example and take the lead. When cadres step forward, the formation has a backbone; when cadres work with their hands, the officers and soldiers have confidence in their hearts. From General Chen Zaidao going down to serve in a company in 1958 and personally rolling up his sleeves at every turn, to today's leading cadres at all levels stepping forward in everything, over more than sixty years the fine tradition of "wherever the combat post is, that is where the cadres' presence will be found" has been passed down like a torch in this brigade. The tradition of leading cadres and grassroots officers and soldiers sharing hardship and joy, and standing together through life and death, has not been lost, and the true character has not changed.

记者探访空军某旅伞降训练现场—— 云端之上：伞训主任坚守“安全哨” ■王宝强 解放军报记者 王 越 插图：方 汉 刘杰（右）逐人检查伞具。龙浩 摄 时值三伏，暑气正盛。记者在空军某旅采访得知，他们旅有一个特殊的“安全哨”——这个哨位既没有专门编制，也没有固定岗亭，但每次伞训任务，“哨兵”都会准时出现。恰逢该旅组织伞降训练，记者决定前往一探究竟。 机场跑道上，航空煤油燃烧的余温尚在，伴着热风直扑面庞。没走一会儿，记者就汗如雨下。战机引擎的轰鸣声越来越近，即将执行跳伞任务的官兵已经在烈日下集结完毕。 “快着陆时要及时调整方向，防止着陆受伤……”还没走到队伍跟前，一个洪亮的声音便传到记者耳里。 “正在说话的是伞训主任刘杰，也是‘安全哨’的‘哨兵’。”同行的机关干事告诉记者，刘主任常说“伞绳一头连着战友安全，一头连着战斗力”。无论酷暑寒冬，每次伞降训练，只要他在单位，一定会准时出现在训练现场。 说话间，记者看到刘杰走到一名神情紧绷的战士身前：“冯卓，侧风着陆如何调整？”年轻的战士一时有些紧张，磕磕巴巴地答出操作要领。 “要领没错，就是声音有点抖，我都怕你把伞绳抖松了，放松点！”刘杰的话引来一阵笑声，冯卓的神情也放松不少。 “背伞！”通报完最新气象数据后，刘杰下达指令。 厚重的伞具被官兵背上肩头，金属锁扣的咔哒声、束带收紧的拉扯声此起彼伏。燥热的空气中，刘杰一丝不苟地为每名战士检查装备。 “作为一名安全跳伞数百次的老伞兵，刘主任的眼睛就是尺子。检查装备时，他经常能发现被大家忽视的细微偏差。”现场跟训的一名旅领导告诉记者，那年，一名新战士跳伞，将背带系统的一处扣节扣反了。刘杰一眼便看了出来，带着这名新兵重新自检，避免了险情发生。 “腰带再系紧些”“武器重新固定好”……刘杰逐人检查装备，并提出改进要求。直到检查完最后一名队员，他才下达指令：“全部达标，准备登机！” 机舱内，震耳的轰鸣和闷热的空气交织。随着战机爬升至千米高空，记者挪到冯卓身侧问道：“马上就要跳伞了，感觉怎么样？”“有点紧张，但刘主任帮我核查过伞具，我心里有底，肯定没问题！”冯卓紧攥伞带，信心满满。 舱门缓缓开启，凉风呼啸着灌入机舱。“三步要走稳，踏机门就数秒，注意观察、沉着操作！”离机信号灯骤然亮起，刘杰站在舱门旁，一遍遍大声重复要点。 跳伞队员按信号指示依次走到舱门前。刘杰如同一名警惕的哨兵，坚守在千米高空的“安全哨”，帮助每名官兵消除安全隐患。检查完一名，他就轻拍官兵肩膀示意，并竖起大拇指。 迈步离机、收腿弯腰……朵朵伞花凌空绽放，向着预定着陆场降落。 所有队员离机后，战机按计划返航。完成“哨兵”使命的刘杰并没有结束工作，下了飞机便快步走向指挥所，等待着陆场的消息。“全员安全着陆！”听到电台传来的报告声，刘杰长长地舒了一口气。 伞降训练结束后，记者在刘杰带领下，走进该旅“模范空降兵连”——这是特级英雄黄继光生前所在连队。 连队荣誉室内，一张黑白合影引起记者注意。刘杰介绍，1958年9月，原总政治部下发《关于军队各级干部每年下连当兵一个月的规定》，在全军掀起干部下连当兵热潮。原武汉军区司令员陈再道换上普通士兵军装，来到了这个连队，照片记录的正是当时的情景。 据连史记载，当兵蹲连的一个月里，陈再道担心战士们不让他值夜哨，于是夜里假装睡着，等战士们交班时立即爬起来跟着去站哨。因为年龄较大，陈再道在练习射击时遇到困难。他主动向战士请教技巧，即便星期天也不休息。最终，陈再道掌握了4种步兵武器的射击技巧，在实弹射击考核中取得优秀成绩。 “英雄部队的优良传统，从来不是挂在嘴边的口号，而是一言一行的示范。”刘杰告诉记者，60多年来，旅队各级领导干部、党员骨干将躬身一线的优良作风融入血脉，形成了“战位在哪里，干部的身影就出现在哪里”的价值追求。 前不久，该旅组织高强度伞降实跳训练。由于陌生空域气流紊乱、风速变化快，个别首次参训的官兵产生畏难情绪。为了帮助官兵坚定信心，旅党委一班人表态：我们带头跳！ 实跳当天，党委常委们率先登机。战机抵达预定空域，舱门缓缓开启，常委们神情沉稳、动作规范，果断跃出机舱。行动是最好的示范，旅领导带头一跃，极大提振了官兵士气。大家卸下思想包袱、及时调整状态，从容跳出机舱，全员安全落地。回顾这次跳伞经历，一名战士感慨：“领导干部冲在前面，我们既有底气又有劲头。” 夜色深沉，营区依旧酷热难耐。记者漫步其中，不一会儿便大汗淋漓。 步行至某哨点，只见一名大校军官正在查哨。记者走近一看才发现，这名大校正是该旅旅长。“夏季高温炎热，突发情况多，旅领导夜间查铺查哨是我们坚持几十年的传统。”他一边说着，一边在《首长机关查铺查哨值班表》上签字。旅长告诉记者，查完这个哨位后，他还要前往营区其他点位继续巡查。 立好样子带好头 ■王 越 深夜时分，中原腹地的暑意依然浓烈。探访完空军某旅这个既没有固定岗亭、也没有具体标识的“安全哨”，记者感慨不已。 作风是形象，也是战斗力。习主席指出，作风优良是我军的鲜明特色和政治优势，必须把作风建设作为军队一项基础性长期性工作抓紧抓实，永葆人民军队政治本色，把好传统带进新征程，将好作风弘扬在新时代。 采访期间，无论是伞训主任坚守“安全哨”，还是领导干部坚持夜间查铺查哨，记者总能看到该旅领导干部率先垂范的身影。 立好样子带好头。干部往前站，队伍就有了主心骨；干部躬身干，官兵心里就有底气。从1958年陈再道将军下连当兵处处躬身实干，到如今各级领导干部事事挺身在前，60多年来，“战位在哪里，干部的身影就出现在哪里”的优良传统在该旅薪火相传，领导干部和基层官兵甘苦与共、生死相依的传统没有丢、本色没有变。
