Tracing the Life Paths of Eleven Disabled Founding Generals, and Reflecting on What a Great Nation's Warrior Should Look Like

追寻十一位伤残开国将军的人生轨迹，感悟大国战将好样子

View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
PLA Daily published a feature on Army Day (August 1) profiling eleven disabled founding generals — including He Bingyan, Yu Qiuli, Zhong Chibing, Yan Fusheng, and Zuo Qi — drawing on reporter visits to hometown memorials, museum exhibitions, and unit heritage sites to reconstruct their conduct during the Long March, the War of Resistance, and the Korean War, and linking them to contemporary figures such as demining soldier Du Fuguo. This is standard Army Day political-work content; its analytical value lies in documenting how PLA media frames institutional loyalty, self-abnegation, and resistance to privilege-seeking as the defining inheritance of the founding generation, with the closing exhortation directed explicitly at 'senior commanders' — the framing points to ongoing concern about elite officer conduct rather than any new policy line.
Translation

A reporter traces the life paths of eleven disabled founding generals, and reflects on what a great nation's warrior should look like—— Spines That Stand Tall ■ PLA Daily Reporter Li Lin

Soldiers of an Army brigade's "Hard Bone Sixth Company" hold a ceremony welcoming new recruits into the company (archival photo). Photo by Ma Jiarui

On the 99th birthday of the People's Army, looking back at history, one photograph has drawn particular attention——

On September 27, 1955, at Huairen Hall in Zhongnanhai, during the ceremony conferring the first military ranks and decorations after the founding of New China, reporter Lin Yang pressed the shutter: among the generals standing in neat formation, one man's right sleeve was empty, another's left sleeve hung flat and hollow, another's trouser legs were empty from the knee down……

That day, among the 1,048 founding generals and marshals who received military ranks, there were 9 one-armed generals, 1 one-legged general, and 1 one-footed general.

On the eve of Army Day, this reporter traced the life paths of 11 disabled founding generals——

One arm holding up the sky, broken bodies standing on the earth. They had lost their arms or legs, yet, just like the revolutionary comrades standing beside them in formation, they straightened the iron spines of Communist Party members and revolutionary soldiers, and set the example of what a great nation's warrior should look like.

One arm, one leg, traversing ten thousand rivers and mountains——

The longest roads, the most dangerous battles — they were never absent

Outside Huairen Hall, a left-handed military salute shook the soul!

Before the rank-conferral ceremony, He Bingyan raised his left hand to salute Chairman Mao. Chairman Mao raised his hand to stop him: "Back in Yan'an I already told you, you don't need to salute……" Premier Zhou took up the words: "He has never feared death — always the first to charge, the last to retreat, wounded in sixteen places in battle."

This scene from the film set the blood of officers and soldiers of an Army brigade surging. They walked into the exhibition hall of He Bingyan's life and achievements at the Yidu City Museum in Hubei Province, and stood for a long time before an old military uniform — the gray cloth faded, torn in multiple places, stitched over densely with thread. Most striking was the right sleeve: it had vanished entirely, leaving only a ragged fringe at the shoulder.

Beside it, a caption read: "Worn by General He Bingyan during his lifetime. When the collar wore through, the right sleeve was cut off to patch it; when the left sleeve wore through, the right sleeve was cut off to replace it. He was still wearing it at the time of his death."

Behind this uniform lies a harrowing story——

In December 1935, on the Long March, 22-year-old Red Army division commander He Bingyan was struck in the right arm by a bullet; the bone was shattered to fragments. The medical director found a carpenter's saw. There was no anesthetic, no disinfectant. He Bingyan had himself tied to a door plank, a towel stuffed in his mouth, and the grinding sound of bone being sawed echoed in the room for more than two hours.

He Long picked up several bone fragments and wrapped them in a handkerchief: "These are He Bingyan's bones — the bones of a Communist. See how hard they are!"

Within days of the surgery, He Bingyan reappeared in the ranks. His wound tore open again and again, scabbed over, and tore open again, but he never once fell behind.

He was not the only one to march the Long March with a severed arm.

On the Long March, Yu Qiuli, political commissar of the 18th Red Regiment, had his left arm shot through — the bone pierced through the flesh, two tendons exposed, the wound rotting and festering. He slung the injured arm across his chest and kept marching, soaking it in cold water each day to dull the pain. His comrades could not bear to watch; he smiled and said: "The enemy shot off one of my arms. I still have my life — I can still make revolution."

Crossing snow mountains, traversing grasslands — for a full 192 days, it was not until the Long March reached Huixian County in Gansu that Yu Qiuli underwent the amputation.

If losing one arm still left a man able to walk, what about losing a leg?

This reporter came to the Red Army battle site at Loushan Pass, where a statue arrests the eye: a Red Army commander with his right leg missing, his right hand gripping a wooden crutch planted in the ground — a recreation of Zhong Chibing completing the Long March on one leg.

In the Battle of Loushan Pass, Zhong Chibing, political commissar of the 12th Red Regiment, had the bone of his lower right leg shattered by a bullet. Without anesthetic, in three separate operations using a wood-chopping knife and a carpenter's saw, his entire right leg was amputated.

Leaning on a wooden crutch, he crossed the snow mountains on one leg — when the crutch gave out, he crawled. His cotton trousers wore through; the wound on his stump festered in the snowmelt; he wrapped it in rags and kept moving forward……

Before the 1955 rank-conferral ceremony began, Chairman Mao specifically asked: "Has Zhong Chibing — the one who walked the Long March on one leg — arrived?" Upon seeing Zhong Chibing, he said with genuine admiration: "That you can stand here today is truly a miracle!"

Among those who created such miracles were also founding Lieutenant General Yan Fusheng and founding Major General Xie Liang.

In the Battle of Luojiabao, Yan Fusheng, political commissar of the 16th Red Division, had his right arm blown off and then fell off a cliff. When he was rescued by local villagers and dragged his wounded body a thousand li to find his unit, the troops had already held a memorial service for him. His comrades had held one such memorial service for him once before as well.

In the Battle of Shandan, Xie Liang, political commissar of the 23rd Red Division, was shot in the left foot; a doctor cut off his forefoot with scissors, raw and unmedicated. After being captured, his wound became infected and required amputation, but he never revealed a single word to the enemy. After being rescued and recovering from his wounds, Xie Liang relearned to ride a horse and fire a weapon, and became known throughout the entire army as the "one-footed general."

On one arm, one leg, one foot, these commanders completed the Long March and marched toward victory.

In the flames of the War of Resistance Against Japan, He Bingyan and Yu Qiuli formed the Eighth Route Army 120th Division Independent Third Detachment — two men, two hands — and led the unit from more than 300 men to over 5,000, earning the name from He Long of the "one-hand" force. In the Yanmen Pass ambush, He Bingyan led the charge at the very front, striking terror into the Japanese army.

The fame of the one-armed generals spread far and wide. Peng Shaohui led the Independent Second Brigade with one arm in the Hundred Regiments Offensive; Yan Fusheng held the line with one arm at the Battle of Heishan for three days and nights; Peng Qingyun led the 141st Division with one arm in 252 engagements over 165 days of fierce fighting against the "United Nations forces" on the Korean War battlefield……

The longest roads, the most dangerous battles — they were never absent.

Merits not traded for titles, battle wounds not leveraged for privilege——

Whatever the organization gives is what it gives — it is not for us to pick and choose

Following the winding course of the Lu River upstream, this reporter and representatives of officers and soldiers from an Army unit arrived at Fusheng Village, Lukou Town, Zhuzhou City, Hunan Province — the hometown of one-armed general Yan Fusheng.

"His old house used to be right over there — the building has long since disappeared!" Following the direction pointed by a local elder, one sees only green fields and vegetable plots at the foot of the hill, cowpeas hanging from their frames, and beside them a stone stele inscribed "Former Residence of General Yan Fusheng."

"All he left to his hometown was this one stele." Stroking the stele, Yan Fusheng's nephew Yan Xiangen said with emotion: "He went through fire and water for the revolution — lost his arm in battle — and after victory never sought special treatment. He always taught us: this life was a gift snatched back from death. How could it be used to claim credit?"

The general's old house collapsed in the 1970s from years of disrepair. At the time, Yan Fusheng was serving as deputy political commissar of the Guangzhou Military Region, yet the thought of rebuilding it never crossed his mind.

Having endured great hardship without staining the original aspiration (初心); having risen to high position without changing one's true character. In the course of this investigation, the story of the "regimental-grade general" was especially legendary and moving.

Su Lu was the first commander of the Changzhi Military Sub-district after the founding of New China. He had lived through the Long March and the Hundred Regiments Offensive, and had his right arm blown off in the Battle of Taiyuan. The organization arranged for him to step down and recuperate; unwilling to sit idle, he repeatedly requested to continue working, and willingly stepped down from his position as a full-division-grade military sub-district commander to serve as a regimental-grade ordnance depot director.

In 1955, Su Lu — still guarding the depot — was awarded the rank of Major General. Premier Zhou clasped his one remaining left hand and praised him for rendering meritorious service to the people. He replied modestly: "I have done far too little. The people have given me far too much."

One-armed general Zuo Qi had said the same. After the founding of New China, he took root with his troops in Xinjiang for more than 20 years. Many comrades returned to the interior and to headquarters organs; he never once applied to the organization for a transfer. Physically disabled, he never once asked the organization for any special treatment.

What, then, did they ask for? A photograph in the Nanniwan Mass Production Memorial Hall seems to offer an answer——

Three commanders of the 359th Brigade stand in a row: Yan Fusheng, Zuo Qi, Peng Qingyun. Three men, three arms, three young faces — all smiling.

The soldiers used mattocks and hoes to break new ground; these men could not grip such tools, so they carried water and food up the hillsides. One noon, soldiers spotted from a distance a man hauling a shoulder pole with only one hand, straining with all his might to keep his balance. When they recognized him as Zuo Qi, political commissar of the 718th Regiment, they said with aching hearts: "How can we bring ourselves to eat this food!" Zuo Qi wiped away his sweat and smiled: "I still have one hand!"

In his later years, Peng Qingyun looked back on those times and said only, lightly: "The soldiers were all watching you!"

To carry the load in hard times, yet not to reach for position when fame and success arrived — this was the common choice of that generation of great-nation warriors.

After the founding of New China, taking into account that Zhong Chibing's right leg had been amputated high up the thigh, the organization arranged for him to leave front-line combat units and take the lead in establishing the Military Commission Civil Aviation Bureau. Zhong Chibing accepted the appointment willingly, built the institution from nothing, and became one of the founders of New China's civil aviation enterprise.

At the time, the organization arranged for one-armed general Chen Bo to transfer into the logistics system and no longer serve as the principal commander of a front-line combat unit. Chen Bo had not a word of complaint: the front lines needed physically whole commanders all the more; logistics support work was equally important. For the ensuing decades, he neither competed for credit nor jockeyed for rank, and devoted himself wholeheartedly to his logistics post.

In midsummer, at He Bingyan's former residence in Yidu, Hubei, dozens of militia members of the "He Bingyan Hard Bone Militia Company" came to pay their respects. He Leisheng, the eldest son of General He Bingyan, told them: "Father often said to the family during his lifetime: whatever the organization gives is what it gives — it is not for us to pick and choose."

In that era, the more a person placed himself low, the higher the organization placed him.

In 1955, He Bingyan had originally been assessed for the rank of Lieutenant General. During the adjustment phase of the promotion list, one more star was added to his shoulders. Among the 57 founding full generals, He Bingyan was the only one at the quasi-corps-army level.

Listening to these stories, one militia Party member wrote in the visitors' book: "Merits not traded for titles, battle wounds not leveraged for privilege — the old general deserved that star!"

Why was the PLA able to win? The answer is not written on the battlefield alone——

Each level leads the next, each level sets the example for the next

The "Hard Bone Sixth Company" exhibition hall is where new recruits receive their first lesson upon joining the company.

Pairs of clear eyes look at an old photograph on the display board: Generals Yu Qiuli and He Bingyan standing together — the one on the right with an empty right sleeve, the one on the left with an empty left sleeve.

The Sixth Company's predecessor was precisely the "one-hand" force led by Yu Qiuli and He Bingyan. To this day, the officers and soldiers of the Sixth Company still remember the words of the old general Yu Qiuli: "To lead soldiers well, you must first be solid yourself."

That solidity was forged in battle, and also in "not reaching out one's hand." During He Bingyan's lifetime, he made a "three-point compact" with his family and children: never forget your roots, never make demands of the organization, never reach out to the government.

When he was serving as commander of the Chengdu Military Region, his four children were boarding at a primary school. A newly assigned guard used an official vehicle on the weekend to bring the children home. He Bingyan stood in the courtyard, his thick eyebrows knitted tight: "The official car is for work. What right do children have to ride in it?"

His wife Jiang Ping served as director of an outpatient department and, by normal procedure, was due for promotion to Lieutenant Colonel. He Bingyan said: "I am the commander — I cannot keep pushing my own wife forward." After four full years of being held back, Jiang Ping asked him in bewilderment: "You won't let me be promoted — what mistake have I actually made?"

He Bingyan was silent for a long time, and in the end only shook his head……

Founding Major General Tong Yansheng had lost both an arm and had a lame leg. In the early 1960s, the organization assigned him a dedicated cook; he sent the cook back: "The country is in economic difficulty — I cannot add to the burden." Without a cook, he led his family in growing vegetables in the courtyard. When the children complained about the plain and simple food, he lost his temper: "If you're already thinking about comfort now, how will you ever be a revolutionary successor?"

One-armed general Chen Bo kept close to his person a Party membership card soaked through with blood, and for decades personally paid his Party dues and made up any arrears. He used this Party card to set rules for his children: no special treatment, no taking advantage.

After the Nationalist Army retreated to Taiwan, many of its generals were still fighting over positions and housing, making a great commotion; meanwhile, their old adversaries — the Communist generals — were doing something else entirely: holding back their wives' promotions, sending back their cooks, using their Party cards to set rules for their children……

Why was the PLA able to win? The answer is not written on the battlefield alone — it is also written in the inheritance and example of each level leading the next and each level setting the example for the next.

In April 2021, the Southern Theater Command Army organized a special interview event titled "A Dialogue Across Time," featuring old and new heroes and models, with representatives from the "Hard Bone Sixth Company" in attendance.

On the stage, "Rolling-Grenade Hero" An Zhongwen recounted how, before a general offensive, he charged into a minefield and rolled over mines, losing his right leg and both eyes: "Even rolling, I had to roll a path to life for my comrades!"

In the audience sat a special soldier — Du Fuguo. While carrying out a manual demining mission on the Yunnan border, Du Fuguo had lost both hands and both eyes. His comrades will never forget his words: "Stand back — let me do it!"

Demining heroes from different eras met at this event.

When it was Du Fuguo's turn to speak, one sentence brought the entire hall to tears: "Although I no longer have eyes, no longer have hands, I still have two complete legs, and I have my comrades' support. I don't look at what I don't have — I only look at what I do have!"

After the event, a soldier from the "Hard Bone Sixth Company" pasted two photographs side by side on the inside cover of his notebook——

One was a photograph of the old general He Bingyan saluting with his left hand; the other was a photograph of Du Fuguo saluting with his maimed arms.

Two special salutes, separated by more than 70 years, seem to speak a thousand words……

Reporter's Notes

Iron Bones, Forever Inscribed with a Patriot's Heart

According to statistics, from the Nanchang Uprising to the founding of New China, more than 3,000 People's Army commanders at the regimental level and above gave their lives; 7 of the Ten Marshals were wounded in battle; 7 of the Ten Generals were wounded; of the 57 founding full generals, only 5 were never wounded……

Chairman Mao once marveled: "From ancient times to the present, how many one-armed generals has China had? There were none in the old era — only our Red Army units could cultivate such unique talents!" The founding generals and marshals' absolute loyalty to the Party, their unswerving commitment to the original aspiration (初心), and their strict demands upon themselves have set for us an eternal example. On the new journey (新征程), commanders at all levels throughout the entire military — and especially senior commanders — should take them as a mirror: firm in faith and conviction, steadfast in sincere original aspiration (赤诚初心), forever preserving an honest and clean character (清廉本色), and setting the example of what a great nation's warrior should look like in the new era for all officers and soldiers of the entire military.

记者追寻十一位伤残开国将军的人生轨迹，感悟大国战将好样子—— 挺立的脊梁 ■解放军报记者 李 琳 陆军某旅“硬骨头六连”举行新兵入连仪式（资料照片）。马佳瑞 摄 人民军队99岁生日当天，回望历史，一张合影备受关注—— 1955年9月27日，中南海怀仁堂，新中国成立后首次授衔授勋典礼上，记者林杨按下快门：整齐列阵的将领中，有人右臂袖管是空的，有人左臂袖管扁塌塌垂着，有人从膝盖以下空荡荡的…… 那天，被授予军衔的1048位开国将帅中，有9位独臂将军、1位独腿将军、1位独脚将军。 八一建军节前夕，记者追寻11位伤残开国将军的人生轨迹—— 独臂擎天，残躯立地。他们失去了臂膀或腿脚，却和队列中的革命战友一样，挺起了共产党人、革命军人的铁脊梁，树起了大国战将的好样子。 独臂、单腿，走过万水千山—— 最远的路、最险的仗，他们从未缺席 怀仁堂外，一个左手军礼震撼人心！ 授衔授勋典礼前，贺炳炎用左手向毛主席敬军礼。毛主席抬手拦住他：“在延安的时候就让你免礼了……”周总理接过话说：“他从不怕死，冲锋在前，撤退在后，身负16处战伤。” 电影中的这一幕，让陆军某旅官兵热血激荡。他们走进湖北省宜都市博物馆贺炳炎生平业绩陈列室，在一件旧军服前久久驻足——灰布褪色，多处破损，针脚密密麻麻。最扎眼的是右袖，不见了踪影，只在肩膀处留下一截参差不齐的毛边。 旁边写着：“贺炳炎将军生前所穿。衣领穿破，把右袖截下来补；左袖磨破，把右袖截下来换。直至逝世，仍在穿用。” 这件军服，背后是一段惊心动魄的往事—— 1935年12月，长征路上，22岁的红军师长贺炳炎右臂被子弹击中，骨头被炸得粉碎。卫生部长找来一把木工锯，没有麻药，没有消毒水，贺炳炎让人把自己绑在门板上，嘴里塞进毛巾，截骨的“嘎吱”声在屋里响了两个多小时。 贺龙捡起几块碎骨用手绢包住：“这是贺炳炎的骨头，共产党人的骨头，你们看有多硬！” 手术后没几天，贺炳炎重新出现在队伍里。伤口一次次撕裂、结痂、再撕裂，但他没掉过一次队。 断臂走长征，非贺炳炎一人。 长征路上，红18团政委余秋里左臂被打断，骨头穿出皮肉，两根筋露在外面，伤口腐烂化脓。他把伤臂吊在胸前继续行军，每天将其浸在冷水里止痛。战友不忍看，他笑着说：“敌人打断我一只胳膊，我还有一条命，照样革命。” 翻雪山、过草地，整整192天，直到长征到达甘肃徽县，余秋里才做了截肢手术。 如果说，缺了一条胳膊还能走路，那么缺了一条腿呢？ 记者来到娄山关红军战斗遗址，一尊雕像动人心魄：一名红军将领右腿缺失，右手紧握的木拐，杵于地面，这是钟赤兵独腿走完长征的形象再现。 娄山关战斗中，红12团政委钟赤兵右小腿骨头被子弹打碎，在无麻药情况下，分3次用砍柴刀和木匠锯进行截肢手术，截去了整条右腿。 拄着木拐，他用一条腿翻越雪山，拐撑不住就爬。棉裤磨穿了，断肢的伤口在雪水中泡烂，他用破布缠一缠，继续往前走…… 1955年授衔授勋典礼开始前，毛主席特意询问：“一条腿走完长征路的钟赤兵来了没有啊？”见到钟赤兵后，他由衷称赞：“你今天能站在这里，真是一个奇迹啊！” 创造这样奇迹的，还有开国中将晏福生、开国少将谢良。 罗家堡战斗中，红16师政委晏福生右臂被炸断后跳崖。当他被群众救起，拖着伤体千里寻到队伍时，部队已为他开完追悼会。这样的“追悼会”，身边战友之前已经为他开过一次。 山丹战斗中，红23师政委谢良左脚中弹，医生用剪刀生生剪断他的前脚掌。被俘后伤口感染截肢，但他始终未向敌人吐露一个字。获救伤愈后，谢良重新学习骑马、打枪，成为全军著名的“独脚将军”。 凭着一只胳膊、一条腿、一只脚，这些将领走完了长征，走向了胜利。 抗战烽火中，贺炳炎和余秋里组建八路军120师独立第三支队，两个人两只手，带着部队从300余人发展到5000多人，被贺龙称为“一把手”队伍。雁门关伏击战，贺炳炎带队冲在最前面，让日军谈之色变。 独臂将军威名远扬。彭绍辉独臂率独立第二旅参加百团大战，晏福生独臂在黑山阻击战坚守三昼夜，彭清云独臂率141师在抗美援朝战场与“联合国军”鏖战165天，打了252次战斗…… 最远的路、最险的仗，他们从未缺席。 功劳不换功名，战伤不索特权—— 组织给什么就是什么，轮不到我们自己挑 沿着渌水蜿蜒而上，记者和陆军某部官兵代表来到湖南省株洲市渌口镇福生村，这里是独臂将军晏福生的家乡。 “他的老宅原来就在那里，现在房子早没了！”顺着当地老人手指的方向望去，只见山脚下一片田地菜畦青青，豇豆垂架，旁边立着一块石碑，写着“晏福生将军故居”。 “他留给家乡的，只有这一块碑。”抚摸着石碑，晏福生的侄子晏贤根动情地说，“他为革命九死一生，打仗时胳膊没了，胜利后从不搞特殊。他总教育我们，这条命是捡来的，哪能用来邀功？” 将军的老宅是上世纪70年代因年久失修倒塌的，当时晏福生担任广州军区副政委，却从未动过重建的念头。 历经苦难而初心不染，身居高位而本色不改。寻访中，“团职将军”的故事尤为传奇和动人。 苏鲁，新中国成立后长治军分区首任司令员。他历经长征、百团大战，在太原战役中右臂被炸断。组织安排他离职休养，他不愿闲居，再三请求继续工作，甘愿从正师职军分区司令员，降任团职军械仓库库长。 1955年，正在守仓库的苏鲁被授予少将军衔。周总理握着他仅存的左手，称赞他为人民立了功。他谦逊地回答：“我做得很不够，人民给我的太多。” 独臂将军左齐，也曾说过这样的话。新中国成立后，他随部队扎根新疆，一干就是20余年。许多战友回到内地和机关，他从未向组织申请调离；身体伤残，他也从未向组织要求任何特殊待遇。 他们要求的到底是啥？南泥湾大生产纪念馆里的一张合影似乎给出了答案—— 359旅三位指挥员站成一排：晏福生、左齐、彭清云。三个人，三只手臂，三张年轻的脸，都在笑着。 战士们用镢头、锄头开荒，他们握不住这些工具，就往山坡上送水送饭。一天中午，战士们远远望见挑担送饭的人只有一只手，用尽力气也难保持平衡。等看清是718团的左齐政委，大家心疼地说：“这饭我们怎么能吃得下去！”左齐擦了一把汗，笑着说：“我还有一只手嘛！” 晚年，彭清云回忆起那段岁月，只淡淡说了一句：“战士们都看着你呢！” 艰苦岁月挑担子，功成名就却不挑位子，是那一代大国战将的共同选择。 新中国成立后，考虑到钟赤兵右腿高位截肢，组织安排他离开一线作战部队，牵头筹建军委民航局。钟赤兵欣然上任，白手起家，成为新中国民航事业的创始人之一。 当年，组织安排独臂将军陈波转入后勤系统工作，不再担任一线作战部队主官。陈波毫无怨言：前线作战更需要身体健全的指挥员，后勤保障工作同样重要。此后数十年，他不争功名、不攀职级，安心深耕后勤岗位。 盛夏，湖北宜都贺炳炎故居，走来“贺炳炎硬骨头民兵连”数十位民兵。贺炳炎将军的长子贺雷生对他们说：“父亲生前常对家人说：组织给什么就是什么，轮不到我们自己挑。” 那个年代，越是把自己放得低的人，越是被组织放到了高处。 1955年，贺炳炎原被定评为中将。在授衔名单调整阶段，他的肩头被加了一颗星。57位开国上将中，准兵团级只有贺炳炎一人。 聆听这些故事，一位民兵党员在留言簿上写道：“功劳不换功名，战伤不索特权，老将军配得上这颗星！” 解放军为什么能赢？答案不只写在战场上—— 一级带着一级干，一级做给一级看 “硬骨头六连”展览馆，新兵下连第一课在这里进行。 一双双清澈的眼睛，看着展板上一张老照片：余秋里、贺炳炎两位将军站在一起，右边的空着右袖管，左边的空着左袖管。 六连的前身，正是余秋里和贺炳炎率领的“一把手”队伍。至今，六连官兵还记得余秋里老将军的话：“要把兵带好，首先要自己过硬。” 这份过硬，硬在战斗中，也硬在“不伸手”。贺炳炎生前，曾经与家属和子女“约法三章”：不忘根本、不向组织提要求、不向政府伸手。 他任成都军区司令员时，4个子女在小学寄读。新来的警卫员周末用专车把孩子们接回来，贺炳炎站在院子里，两道浓眉拧得紧紧的：“专车是工作用的，小孩子怎么有资格坐？” 妻子姜平任门诊部主任，按正常程序该晋升中校。贺炳炎却说：“我是司令员，不能把自己的老婆一个劲地提。”压了整整4年后，姜平不解地问他：“你不让我晋级，我到底犯什么错误了？” 贺炳炎沉默良久，最终还是摇了摇头…… 开国少将童炎生，既独臂又瘸腿。上世纪60年代初，组织给他配备专职炊事员，他退了回去：“国家经济困难，不能添负担。”没了炊事员，他带着家人在院子里种菜。孩子们吃不惯粗茶淡饭，他发了火：“现在就想着享受，怎么当革命接班人？” 独臂将军陈波，贴身带着一张被鲜血染透的党证，数十年亲自交党费、补党费。他用这张党证给儿女立规矩：不搞特殊，不占便宜。 国民党军退守台湾后，许多将领仍在争位子争房子，闹得不可开交；而他们的老对手、共产党的将军们，却在做着另一件事：压住妻子晋升、退回炊事员、用党证给儿女立规矩…… 解放军为什么能赢？答案不只写在战场上，也写在一级带着一级干、一级做给一级看的传承和示范上。 2021年4月，南部战区陆军组织“穿越时空的对话”新老英模专题访谈活动，“硬骨头六连”官兵代表参加。 台上，“滚雷英雄”安忠文讲述自己在总攻前冲入雷场滚雷，失去右腿和双眼的经历：“就是滚，也要为战友们滚出一条生路！” 台下，坐着一名特殊的战士——杜富国。在执行云南边境人工扫雷任务时，杜富国失去了双手和双眼。战友们永远记得他那句话：“你退后，让我来！” 不同时代的扫雷英雄，在这场活动中相遇了。 轮到杜富国发言，他一句话让全场泪目：“虽然我没有了眼睛，没有了双手，但我还有一双完整的腿，还有战友们支持我。我不看我没有的，只看我拥有的！” 活动后，“硬骨头六连”一名战士在笔记本扉页上并排贴了两张照片—— 一张是老将军贺炳炎用左手敬礼的照片，一张是杜富国用残臂敬礼的照片。 两个特殊军礼，相隔70多年，似乎在讲述千言万语…… 记者手记 铁骨长铭赤子心 据统计，从南昌起义到新中国成立，人民军队团职以上指挥员牺牲3000余人，十大元帅有7位负过伤，十位大将有7人负过伤，57位开国上将仅5人未负过伤……
