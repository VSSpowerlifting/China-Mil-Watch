Staggered Leave Cannot Be Applied with a "One-Size-Fits-All" Approach — This Unit Launches a Rectification "Combination Punch"

分段休假不能“一刀切”，这个单位打出整改“组合拳”
PLA Daily (解放军报) 28 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A headquarters human resources section chief at the Xianning Detachment of the Hubei General Corps of the People's Armed Police Force conducted an on-site investigation (蹲点) and found that over 80% of soldiers garrisoned more than 1,500 kilometers from home had their leave consolidated into staggered periods despite applying to take it all at once — a pattern driven by company-level commanders protecting unit readiness scores and annual evaluations rather than by soldier preference. The detachment's Party committee responded with a procedural package: publishing annual task schedules to give companies planning confidence, mandating individual input and public posting of leave plans, and building 'A/B position' (AB岗) backup coverage for key posts to reduce commanders' operational risk calculus. The article documents a recurring PAP institutional tension — grassroots commanders optimizing for collective unit metrics at the expense of individual welfare entitlements — and functions as a political-work case study showing how headquarters-level inspection pressure, rather than company-level initiative, drove the correction.
Translation

"I suggest you split your leave into two periods — that way it won't interfere with later assessments, and you'll get to go home one more time. Two birds, one stone." Not long ago, in the company office of a certain detachment under the Xianning Detachment of the Hubei General Corps of the People's Armed Police Force, Political Instructor Luo held Sergeant Zhao's leave application form and offered this earnest advice.

Sergeant Zhao's home is in Qiqihar, Heilongjiang Province — more than 2,500 kilometers from the detachment's garrison. Every time he goes home, he has to take a plane, then a train, then a bus. Not only does the journey take a long time, but the travel costs are no small sum. He had wanted to take his family visit leave all at once, both to save on travel expenses and to avoid making the trip back and forth multiple times. But after hearing the political instructor's words, and considering that the instructor was genuinely looking out for him, Sergeant Zhao — who had been on the verge of saying something — nodded and agreed.

This scene was witnessed by Section Chief Li of the headquarters' human resources section, who was at the detachment conducting an on-site investigation (蹲点). Political Instructor Luo's suggestion seemed reasonable, but the sight of Sergeant Zhao stopping himself mid-thought left Section Chief Li with a nagging question: was this kind of advice truly based on an understanding of what the soldier wanted, or had it simply become a "habitual reflex" (习惯动作)?

After the on-site investigation concluded, Section Chief Li returned to headquarters and pulled the detachment's leave plans and approval forms from the past three years. What he found weighed on him: the data showed that approximately 85% of soldiers whose hometowns were within 1,500 kilometers of the garrison mostly chose staggered leave, and their actual leave usage was largely consistent with their leave plans. Among the smaller number of soldiers whose hometowns were more than 1,500 kilometers away, over 80% had applied in their leave plans to take all their leave at once — yet nearly all of them ended up being approved only for staggered leave.

To get to the bottom of the matter, Section Chief Li sought out several company-level officers and senior non-commissioned officers to learn more. What he heard was that everyone had their own difficulties. One officer admitted frankly: "We know that some soldiers want to take all their leave at once, but the company's annual mission schedule is tight. When competitions and assessments come up, being short even one key NCO affects the unit's overall performance and annual evaluations." A squad leader did the math: "If leave is staggered, each period is short, and soldiers get back up to speed quickly after returning. If they take all their leave at once, the longer absence not only makes personnel management harder while they're away, but it's also harder for them to quickly readapt to the training tempo when they return."

The officers' and NCOs' words showed Section Chief Li the other side of the problem: soldiers who live close to home spend less on travel, and staggered leave lets them go home more times, so they are more willing to stagger their leave. Soldiers who live far from home, on the other hand, spend upwards of a thousand yuan on travel for a single round trip; staggered leave not only doubles those costs, but also means they barely arrive home before they have to turn around and leave.

Section Chief Li included this situation in his on-site investigation report and presented it in a special briefing, drawing the attention of the detachment's Party committee. They concluded that while leave arrangements may seem like a minor matter, they are in fact directly tied to soldiers' sense of having gained something real (获得感). A soldier's desire to save on travel costs and spend more time with family is a legitimate need; a unit leader's concern about the impact on unit readiness and management effectiveness is also a real consideration. As a result, the fact that most soldiers were willing to stagger their leave had caused "staggered leave is good for everyone" to become an ingrained assumption among unit leaders, causing them to overlook the needs of the minority of soldiers who live farther from home.

"We must both see the soldiers' legitimate needs and relieve pressure on grassroots commanders." The detachment's leadership explained that they addressed the issue at the institutional level, launching a rectification "combination punch" (整改"组合拳"): they compiled and distributed to subordinate units a list of major annual tasks, giving companies a basis for scheduling leave plans and the confidence to approve longer leave periods; they stipulated that leave plans must solicit input from each individual, be deliberated collectively, and be publicly posted, so that every service member's needs can be seen; and they established a leave approval and review system under which, absent special circumstances, leave plans must be strictly implemented — no longer allowing "good for everyone" to become "deciding for you." At the same time, through strengthened discipline education, the introduction of post-return recovery training, and the cultivation of "A/B position" (AB岗) backup mechanisms for key posts, they worked to further alleviate the concerns of grassroots commanders.

Before long, the newly drawn-up leave plans were publicly posted. Sergeant Zhao's application to take all his leave at once was approved without difficulty, and he immediately called his wife: "The leave has been approved — I'll be able to stay home for a few more days this time!"

Watching the smile on Sergeant Zhao's face, Political Instructor Luo also breathed a sigh of relief. He wrote the following in his notebook: "The habits of the majority should not become a burden on the minority. True 'doing what's good for you' should start by asking the soldier, 'What do you want?'"

Original Chinese
“建议你分开休假，既不耽误后期考核，还能多回一次家，一举两得。”前不久，武警湖北总队咸宁支队某中队队部里，罗指导员拿着赵班长的休假申请表，诚恳地给出建议。 赵班长家在黑龙江省齐齐哈尔市，距中队驻地2500余公里。每次回家，他都需要乘飞机、坐火车、再转汽车，不仅耗时长，路费也不是一笔小数目。他本想一次性休完探亲假，既能节省路费，还不用来回奔波。可听完指导员的话，考虑他也确实在为自己着想，想解释一番的赵班长还是点头同意了。 这一幕，被正在中队蹲点的机关人力资源股李股长看在眼里。罗指导员的建议看似合理，但赵班长欲言又止的样子，还是让李股长心生疑问：这样的建议，是真的了解战士想法，还是已经成了一个“习惯动作”？ 蹲点结束后，李股长回到机关，调阅了支队近3年的休假计划和审批表，结果让他心头一沉：数据显示，老家距驻地1500公里内的战士约有85%，他们大多选择分段休假，实际休假情况也与休假计划基本一致；而老家距驻地1500公里以上的战士数量虽少，但在休假计划中申请一次性休完的比例高达8成以上，然而最终获批的几乎都是分段休假。 为了弄清原委，李股长找到几名中队干部骨干了解情况，没想到大家各有苦衷。一名干部坦言：“我们也知道有的战士想一次性休完假，可中队全年任务安排紧凑，遇上比武考核，少一名骨干都会影响整体成绩和年度评比。”一位班长算了笔账：“如果分段休假，每次时间短，归队后状态恢复就快。如果一次性休完假，时间较长，不仅人员在外管理难度增加，归队后也不利于迅速适应训练节奏。” 干部骨干的话，让李股长看到问题的另一面：离家近的战士，路费花销小，分段休假能多回家几趟，所以更愿意分段休假；而离家远的战士，休假来回一趟路费高达上千元，分段休假不仅路费翻倍，而且到家时间不长就要返程。 李股长把这一情况写进蹲点报告，进行了专题汇报，引起支队党委重视。他们认为，休假安排看似是小事，实则关乎官兵的获得感。战士想节省路费、多陪家人是合理诉求，带兵人担心影响单位建设和管理质效也是现实情况，所以多数人愿意分段休假的事实，让“分段休假对谁都好”成了带兵人的一种惯性认知，忽略了少数离家较远战士的诉求。 “既要看见战士的合理需求，也要为基层主官减压托底。”该支队领导介绍，他们从机制入手打出整改“组合拳”：梳理全年大项工作清单下发基层，让中队安排休假计划有依据、批长假有底气；明确休假计划必须逐人征求意见、集体商议并公示，让每名官兵的需求都能被看见；建立休假审批核查制度，无特殊情况必须严格落实休假计划，不再让“对谁都好”变成“替你做主”。同时，通过加强纪律教育、推行归队后恢复性训练和关键岗位“AB岗”培养机制等方式，进一步化解基层主官的顾虑。 很快，重新制订的休假计划公示出来。赵班长的“一次性休完假”申请顺利获批，他第一时间拨通了妻子的电话：“假已经批了，这次可以在家多待几天！” 看着赵班长脸上的笑容，罗指导员也松了口气。他在笔记本里写下这样一句话：“多数人的习惯，不该成为少数人的负担。真正的‘为你好’，应该先问问战士‘想要什么’。”
