Military Camp Observation | The Transformation Path of a New-Type Support Force

军营观察丨一支新型保障力量的转型之路

About this analysis
Summary
A Rocket Force support unit — integrating communications, logistics, engineering, maintenance, and medical elements — has reorganized around a modular grouping model (模块化编组) designed to replace the previous dispersed, single-specialty structure with a unified command and cross-functional response capability, validated in a joint exercise with a missile brigade in the second half of last year. The article documents the unit's post-reorganization effort to close the gap between basic-subject training proficiency and system-of-systems operational effectiveness, a chronic problem the unit's own leadership identified through internal 'brainstorming' (头脑风暴) sessions that surfaced training pathologies — overemphasis on form, individual-soldier metrics, and assessment scores at the expense of coordination and actual-combat performance. The framing around 'new-quality support capability' (新质保障力) and the commander's explicit goal of integrating peacetime and wartime command structures points to ongoing institutional uncertainty about how Rocket Force support units fit into joint operational architecture, though the article does not specify the unit's designation or its precise relationship to named missile brigades.

A "Graduation Answer Sheet" Written on the Training Ground

■ Duan Decheng, PLA Daily Special Correspondent Li Bingfeng, Zhang Xinkai

Late at night, in the communications battalion area of a Rocket Force unit, Battalion Commander Xie and several key personnel sat gathered around the long table in the operations room, spread before them several communications link diagrams and fault lists… They were war-gaming technical problems in the integration of fixed and mobile communications.

Battalion Commander Xie quickly jotted down one suggestion after another. This subject was also the topic of his master's thesis, which he had just successfully defended.

"If the battlefield command chain breaks at the 'last mile,' even the best tactics are castles in the air." He closed his notebook and said to the comrades beside him.

Late last year, Battalion Commander Xie successfully defended his thesis at the Rocket Force Command College and was awarded a master's degree. But what surprised people was that this commander, already holding his degree, is still to this day refining that graduation thesis.

"The thesis defense is only the starting point; the answer sheet for strengthening the military has no finish line." He explained: every additional round of war-gaming brings us one step closer to actual combat; every additional problem discovered adds one more point of probability of victory.

The topic Battalion Commander Xie initially proposed was sent back by his advisor for not being closely enough tied to actual combat. Just as he was puzzling over this, a transformation was quietly unfolding — the battalion he belonged to was accelerating its shift from "ensuring communications in peacetime" to "supporting communications in wartime."

Missile forces operate at a fast combat tempo, with highly centralized command, dispersed deployment positions, and complex electromagnetic environments. How to achieve communications-on-the-move (动中通), communications-under-jamming (扰中通), and communications-after-damage (毁中通)? How to make information flow truly become the "neural network" of battlefield command? His advisor's suggestion made Battalion Commander Xie clearly realize: "Breaking through the problem is not in the study, but on the battlefield."

"Relying on war-gaming alone won't work — answers must be found through confrontation." In the battalion's first organized training after consolidation, problems emerged one after another…

Battalion Commander Xie could no longer sit still. With the urgency of "combat begins tonight," he led key personnel to work out a three-in-one problem-solving model of "situation-driven, joint operations, live-force verification (情况牵引、联合作业、实兵检验)," attempting to eliminate the "temperature differential" between the training ground and the battlefield.

Subsequently, the entire battalion embedded combat-readiness requirements into every link of combat duty, equipment maintenance, and daily routines, gradually tempering and advancing the integration process through repeated emergency drills.

On one occasion, the battalion organized a red-versus-blue confrontation exercise, with officers and soldiers completing their mission under conditions of severed network, severed power, and loss of contact. The process of this confrontation also became solid empirical material in Battalion Commander Xie's thesis.

"The deeper the research goes, the more I feel my capabilities are insufficient." Battalion Commander Xie set a rule for himself: persist in studying cases and continuously update his knowledge base. He also frequently consults the battalion's senior non-commissioned officers on technical details. Master Sergeant First Class Du Jiuzeng remarked with feeling: "It is precisely this spirit of getting to the bottom of things that has influenced everyone — that is how we were able to break through multiple technical barriers."

Networking-on-the-move, damage-and-rebuild… Today, the battalion continues to achieve capability leaps. In a multi-branch joint exercise last year, they flexibly constructed a communications network and ensured stable operation of the command system in a heavy-jamming environment.

After multiple rounds of optimization, Battalion Commander Xie's "graduation answer sheet" has become a "research report" from the exercise ground — an "action guide" for new-type support written at the combat post.

The Transformation Path of a New-Type Support Force

■ Duan Decheng, PLA Daily Reporter Fu Yu, Special Correspondents Li Bingfeng, Zhang Xinkai

In a certain sense, fighting a war means fighting logistics. A Rocket Force unit at the front line of support for victory is working to seamlessly link support elements with combat forces, driving a profound transformation in the domain of operational support.

As the tide of military strengthening surges forward, this entirely new force came into being at the right moment. From the outset, the unit's Party committee focused on its functional positioning, insisting on advancing reorganization and retraining as one, and conducting exercises and capability-building simultaneously.

How to achieve sensitive and efficient battlefield support response and bring into play the new effectiveness of operational support under the new organizational structure — there was no ready experience to draw on, and answers could only be sought through bold exploration and practice. The unit's Party committee led officers and soldiers in laying foundations and building momentum, answering the transformation questions of "Who are we," "What is our mission," and "How should we do our job."

Generating genuine combat power is destined to require blazing one's own trail

"The inability to find a comparable reference system was the first obstacle to the unit's construction and development." After the unit was formed, how should it be built, and how should officers and soldiers train? Because there were no precedents to follow and no templates to rely on, there was as yet no clear "standard answer."

After the unit was formed, its leadership immediately went to relevant units of sister services and branches to observe and learn, drawing on experience in transformation and development. Because the combat styles were markedly different, the "stones from other mountains" had reference value but could not be directly applied.

Consulting training syllabi and regulations, there was only a basic, universally applicable "framework." The unit's leadership recognized that, faced with new-type combat styles, complex battlefield environments, and joint operations requirements, generating genuine combat power was destined to require blazing their own trail.

Each level demonstrates for the level below; each level leads the level below. Confronting new problems in the course of construction, and drawing on the coordinated tasks and support-needs system the unit undertook, they compiled a "Three-Year Construction Plan," conducted coordinated training with missile brigades and forces of different specialties, refined support models through tempering, familiarized themselves with each other's tactics and procedures, and strove to achieve the systemic effectiveness of "united we are strong, combined we win (联则强、合则胜)."

"To take firm steps forward, you must look further ahead." They optimized the internal force composition and resource integration, organically integrating all subordinate elements to ensure precise embedding and effective capability release within system-of-systems operations, bringing into play the systemic advantage of "1+1>2."

Communications Battalion Commander Xie explained that in the past the communications battalion was only responsible for fixed communications station support; now the battalion has a "dual-drive" of mobile and fixed communications, with fuller force configuration and heavier responsibilities. How to integrate into the wartime command chain and clear the critical bottlenecks of communications-on-the-move, communications-under-jamming, and communications-after-damage became the challenge facing all officers and soldiers in the battalion.

In one basic training assessment, the communications battalion, when deploying on a certain subject, reduced the time required by nearly half compared to what the syllabus specified. Yet this seemingly "excellent" scorecard not only failed to earn recognition — it was actually "stopped" — because while the single-subject training had been executed well, the battalion's performance in the subsequent continuous assessment was unsatisfactory.

Similar problems existed to varying degrees across the subordinate battalions: "Assessment items were concentrated heavily on basic subjects, with few actual-combat subjects." On this point, Battalion Commander Yang of one of the unit's battalions had his own understanding: "Basic subject assessment standards are clear and results are straightforward, so they naturally become the focus of training."

"There is nothing wrong with building foundations, but if you only spin your wheels on the basics without leaping to system-of-systems capability, you are marking time in place." The unit's leadership pointed out incisively: the earlier problems are exposed, the more favorable it is for combat power generation.

A top-down "brainstorming (头脑风暴)" session quietly got underway. The unit organized battalion and company commanders and NCO staff officers to conduct a training-by-syllabus intensive course, discussing chronic training ailments such as "emphasizing form over substance (重表轻实)," "emphasizing individual soldiers over coordination (重单兵轻协同)," and "emphasizing assessments over actual combat (重考核轻实战)." Everyone refused to avoid contradictions or conceal shortcomings, sifting through problems one by one.

The target of problems is the growth point of combat power. "Any problem affecting system-of-systems operational effectiveness must be rectified in a closed loop." The unit's Party committee issued a military pledge, optimized the mechanism of "standing committee members helping build organic battalions, departments helping build small, remote, and dispersed units," providing full-coverage assistance in strengthening organizations and developing key personnel, and converting the "problem list" into an "effectiveness list" as quickly as possible.

From "individual soldiers training fast" to "the system linking up," from "getting through the subjects" to "holding up on the battlefield," the unit's training model underwent a profound change.

Battlefield support is not the "primary factor" in winning, but it is the "critical variable" that determines victory or defeat

"In the past, when equipment broke down, all we could do was call for help, and sometimes before the process was even complete, the operational opportunity had long since been lost." Recalling the awkward situation during a cross-region exercise a few years ago, Staff Officer Wan of the unit's operations and training department still feels regret.

It was the final battle of a red-versus-blue confrontation. A transport vehicle in one battalion was suddenly "destroyed by the enemy," its engine stalled, immobilized in mountainous terrain. All the battalion's officers and soldiers were frantic, able only to report up through channels by radio to request support.

"One phone call placed, one round of requests sent up, a long wait — and then support forces finally arrived, tardily." Staff Officer Wan said with a rueful smile.

The result: the mission ended in failure. Staff Officer Wan came to understand: battlefield support is not the "primary factor" in winning, but it is the "critical variable" that determines victory or defeat. If command is not smooth and resource mobilization is inflexible, even forces sharing the same training ground will be fragmented and uncoordinated, inevitably slowing the tempo and losing operational opportunities.

To break the impasse required transformation. By integrating communications, transportation, engineering, chemical defense, repair, and medical support forces, breaking down professional barriers, subordination barriers, and command disconnects, they gradually built a new system of "modular grouping, integrated response, and full-chain support (模块化编组、一体化响应、全链条保障)."

"Now, with one commander on the scene, all subordinate forces can be directly mobilized." Wan Chufang remarked with feeling: no more layer-upon-layer requests for instructions, no more need to "borrow personnel" or "borrow vehicles" — all resources are "coordinated for my use," and the process flows smoothly.

Once the link is open, effectiveness improves. Transportation Support Detachment Commander Zhang also felt this deeply: once a transport route is destroyed, he can directly order the engineering company to deploy, clear the route on the spot, and repair it quickly, ensuring seamless connection between support modules.

To improve the coordinated capability of support modules, the unit established a "two-level, three-category" support system, continuously revised and refined action plans, formulated standards for "modular grouping training confrontation rules" and "modular grouping training assessment scoring," and explored the support pathway of "planning and design — training implementation — after-action review — research and standardization — rectification and improvement," laying the foundation for subsequently expanding training content.

A live-force mission grouping training exercise unfolded in a certain area. A transport vehicle in one battalion suddenly broke down and could not continue forward. Upon receiving the report, the unit activated its emergency mechanism. Shortly thereafter, four technical specialists drawn from the "maintenance module," carrying equipment shelters, arrived at the scene by transport vehicle.

"One commander, multi-directional linkage; one order issued, multi-capability coordination." Staff Officer Wan said: with multiple modules coordinating, the on-scene commander, based on actual conditions, can complete personnel allocation, equipment mobilization, and materiel preparation, and rapidly reach the battlefield.

From "each fighting alone" to "clenching fingers into a fist (攥指成拳)," various support modules are plug-and-play, like field "Transformers."

"For one repair mission, the 'engineering + maintenance + transportation + medical support' modules can be mobilized; for one field bivouac, the 'barracks construction + water and electricity support + materiel delivery' modules can be combined." Section Chief Tang of one of the unit's sections frankly stated: this "modular grouping" model not only achieves "troops allocated by need, groupings organized by battle, dynamically adapted," but also ensures efficient command and rapid response.

How the war is fought is how support is built; what operations need is what support develops

Being able to clearly map out one's own capability indicators and achieve a leap in combat power — this transformation path was not easy for the unit.

As soon as the unit was established, it brought all support elements to a certain area to explore new paths for combined-arms training. The unit's leadership was resolute: "To temper 'new-quality support capability (新质保障力)' in actual combat, we must find the fulcrum that will lever up combat power construction — this exercise is precisely a comprehensive exploration after transformation."

They explored, trained, and organized results simultaneously, delineating "four-level capability indicators benchmarked against actual combat requirements" to refine standards for training and talent development; opened up the "data links" between the command post and each combat unit and support unit; and, relying on an integrated command platform, extended the command network toward the battlefield front.

The changes are visible and tangible. From fuel supply to equipment repair, from hot-meal forward delivery to engineering construction, a new-type chain connected to the information network, gradually achieving "command on a single map, linkage on a single chessboard (一张图指挥、一盘棋联动)."

At one exercise site, faced with a link interruption, a support detachment responded swiftly. The communications detachment dispatched operators carrying individual radios, portable satellite stations, BeiDou terminals, and other multi-type equipment to provide forward support. One person operating multiple devices, one device with multiple capabilities, multiple links fused — communications nodes were rebuilt. "This kind of scenario was something we couldn't have imagined before," remarked one participating soldier with feeling.

The deeper objective of the organizational adjustment is to release the endogenous potential of combat power. They focused on the most urgent generation problems, working to solve such challenges as how each support detachment's forces can efficiently integrate into the combat system and how to achieve precise coupling with missile forces.

In the second half of last year, they proactively aligned with battlefield requirements, organized logistics, equipment, and engineering support forces, and conducted joint training with a missile brigade. From fuel supply and hot-meal forward delivery, to transport support and materiel movement, to equipment repair and road construction, support missions ran through the entire operational process and covered the full mission area.

As soon as the battle began, the exercise control headquarters issued an order: "The engineering support team must open battlefield routes for multiple launch detachments." The mission area shifted; officers and soldiers maneuvered day and night, moving through mountainous forest areas and electromagnetic jamming zones… In the end, multiple launch platforms completed position construction on schedule, and the missiles lifted off as planned.

"How the war is fought is how support is built; what operations need is what support develops." Faced with the new support paradigm of "small groupings, large combined-arms, modular, multi-capable (小编组、大合成、模块化、多能化)" in future information warfare, the unit's traditional "single-skill-type" support model has become difficult to adapt to.

To this end, they used the annual "weak training subject list" as a handle, differentiated by "command, technical, tactical, and common" categories, conducted tiered and graduated training, adjusted and optimized training plans, and ensured improvement in training quality and effectiveness.

In one joint exercise, relying on an auxiliary software system, they achieved precise command and control of terminal elements, improving support response speed by more than 40%.

In recent years, a cohort of composite-type key personnel who "understand operations, know technology, and can command" has accelerated its growth within the unit, and the unit has won 2 team championships in Rocket Force-organized competitions…

The honors are a vivid portrayal of this new-type support force's accelerating transformation, and also a powerful motivation driving them toward victory on the future battlefield.

This Edition's Editor in Dialogue with the Commander of a Rocket Force Unit ——

Forging "New-Quality Support Capability (新质保障力)"

As the need to deepen combat power construction advances, it is necessary to integrate and optimize command support, service support, communications, logistics, equipment, and other diverse forces to build an entirely new support force. Its "newness" lies not only in equipment upgrades or organizational adjustments, but more fundamentally in a root-level reshaping of the logic of support. Faced with the profound transformation of combat methods on the future battlefield, how does this force achieve sensitive support response and efficient operation? Around this topic, this edition's editor conducted a dialogue with the commander of a Rocket Force unit.

Editor: What explorations has the unit undertaken around forging "new-quality support capability (新质保障力)"?

Unit Commander: We have deeply integrated communications, operations, and comprehensive support elements, broken the past dispersed model of "each guarding their own patch," and achieved unified resource allocation and coordinated linkage. The support force integrates data support, information communications, materiel supply, maintenance support, and other multiple functions into one. Once ordered, it can rapidly reach the operational area, striving to provide the command organ and missile forces with precise battlefield data, diverse communications means, and continuous materiel supply.

Editor: To adapt to new combat patterns, how do you drive the transformation and upgrading of support personnel?

Unit Commander: Cultivating new-type support personnel requires the courage to break through traditional thinking and the skill to summarize experience and innovate methods in live-force training. Establish a "support + operations" integrated training mechanism, enabling support personnel to understand operational requirements in a joint operations context and achieve resonance between support plans and operational tempo; implement a "one specialty, multiple capabilities; one post, multiple proficiencies (一专多能、一岗多通)" cultivation model, encourage support personnel to study across specialties and rotate across posts, participate throughout in operational planning, mission war-gaming, and live-force confrontation, and elevate the comprehensive operational level of the support system.

Editor: How do you ensure that all types of support elements are "twisted into a single rope" on the battlefield?

Unit Commander: Future operations compete on system-of-systems capability. "How to set unified objectives, build linkage mechanisms, and forge coordination consciousness (定统一目标、建联动机制、凝协同意识)" must be advanced from multiple dimensions including mechanism and execution: take the operational mission as the sole point of departure, break down information barriers and process blockages, let orders reach the terminal directly and resources be delivered precisely; from commanders to ordinary soldiers, all must engrave coordination consciousness in their minds, forming support effectiveness of "1+1>2."

Editor: How does peacetime support serve wartime victory?

Unit Commander: We insist on running the combat power standard throughout the entire process, achieving precise alignment between the training system and operational requirements. Organized around support actions including command, communications, fuel, transportation, maintenance, and medical support, we organize specialized training in phases and at different levels — basic training to consolidate the foundation, professional training to deepen, and comprehensive exercises to verify. In peacetime, we set live-force conditions such as complex electromagnetic environments, sudden equipment failures, and overlapping multi-directional demands, tempering rapid response, continuous support, and emergency handling capabilities under high-intensity confrontation.

Editor: Please describe the future scenario of "new-quality support capability (新质保障力)."

Unit Commander: In terms of structure, explore and refine the construction of a command structure that integrates peacetime and wartime, coordinates military and civilian, and connects top to bottom, driving the shift from "segmented by line and block (条块分割)" to "system integration (体系集成)," achieving resonance and integrated linkage between operational command and support command. In terms of model, relying on big data and other technologies, drive the construction of an intelligent model to achieve precise resource matching, visible and controllable. In terms of capability, relying on the emergency support system and high-technology, build a support capability foundation that combines peacetime and wartime and enables rapid conversion, and enhance continuous support capability.

一级做给一级看，一级带着一级干。面对建设中的新课题，结合部队承担的协同任务与支援需求体系，他们编撰“三年建设规划”，开展与导弹旅及不同专业力量之间的协同训练，在淬火中完善保障模式，熟悉彼此的战术与流程，力求实现“联则强、合则胜”的体系效能。 一次基础训练考核，通信营在某课目展开时，用时较大纲规定缩短了近一半。然而，这份看似“优异”的成绩单非但未获肯定，反而被“叫停”——原因是单课目训练虽落实得不错，但在后续连贯考核过程中，营队的表现却不尽人意。 类似问题，在所属各营不同程度地存在：“考核项目多集中于基础课目，实战课目却不多。”对此，该部某营杨营长也有自己的理解：“基础课目考核标准清晰、成绩直观，自然就成了训练的重点。” “打基础没错，但若只在基础上打转，不向体系能力跃升，就是原地踏步。”该部领导一针见血地指出，问题暴露得越早，越有利于战斗力生成。 一场自上而下的“头脑风暴”悄然展开。该部组织营连主官、军士参谋开展按纲施训集训，围绕“重表轻实”“重单兵轻协同”“重考核轻实战”等训练顽症痼疾研讨。大家不回避矛盾、不遮掩短板，一个问题一个问题“过筛子”。 问题靶标，就是战斗力生长点。“凡是影响体系作战效能的问题，必须闭环整改。”该部党委立下军令状，优化“常委帮建建制营、部门帮建小远散”机制，全覆盖帮强组织、帮抓骨干，尽快将“问题清单”转化为“成效清单”。 从“单兵练得快”到“体系联得上”，从“课目过得去”到“战场扛得住”，部队的训练模式发生深刻变化。 战场保障虽不是打赢“第一要素”，却是决定胜负的“关键变量” “过去装备一出问题，只能打求援电话，有时流程还没走完，战机早已延误。”回忆起几年前跨区演习时的尴尬情景，该部作训部门万参谋仍心存遗憾。 那是一场红蓝对抗的收官之战。某营一辆运输车突遭“敌”破坏，发动机熄火，瘫痪在山地中。全营官兵急得团团转，只能通过无线电逐级上报请求支援。 “一通电话打过去，一轮轮请示打上去，等待许久，支援力量才姗姗来迟。”万参谋苦笑道。 结果，任务以失败告终。万参谋体会到：战场保障虽不是打赢“第一要素”，却是决定胜负的“关键变量”。若指挥不畅、资源调用不灵，即便同处一个训练场，各力量之间也彼此割裂、互不统属，必然导致拖慢节奏、贻误战机。 要想破局，就要变革。整合通信、运输、工程、防化、修理、卫勤等保障力量，打破专业壁垒、隶属隔阂与指挥断点，逐步构建起“模块化编组、一体化响应、全链条保障”新体系。 “现在一名指挥员在场，就能直接调用所属全部力量。”万初放感慨道，不再需要层层请示，无需再“借人”“借车”——所有资源“为我统筹”，流程畅通。 链路一通，效能提升。运输保障分队张分队长也深有同感：运输线一旦被毁，他可以直接指挥工程连出动，现场抢通、快速修复，确保保障模块无缝衔接。 为提升保障模块协同能力，该部建立“两级三类”保障体系，不断修订完善行动方案，拟制“模块编组训练对抗规则”“模块编组训练考核评分”规范，探索走开“筹划设计—训练实施—复盘总结—研究规范—整改提高”的保障路径，为后续拓展训练内容打下基础。 一场实战化任务编组训练在某地域展开。某营一辆运输车突发故障，无法继续前出。接到报告后，该部启动应急机制。很快，“维修模块”抽组的4名技术骨干便携带器材方舱，乘坐运输车抵达现场。 “一人指挥，多方联动；一令下达，多能协同。”万参谋说，多种模块协同下，现场指挥员根据实际情况，即可完成人员调配、装备调用与物资准备，快速抵达战场。 从“各自为战”到“攥指成拳”，各种保障模块即插即用，如同野战“变形金刚”。 “一次抢修任务，可调用‘工程+维修+运输+卫勤’模块；一次野战宿营，可组合‘营房搭建+水电保障+物资投送’模块。”该部某科唐科长坦言，这种“模块编组”模式，不仅实现“按需配兵、按战编组、动态适配”，还确保指挥高效、响应快速。 仗怎么打，保障就怎么建；作战需要什么，保障就发展什么 能够清晰梳理出自身能力指标，并实现战斗力跃升，这条转型之路，该部走得并不容易。 部队刚成立便将所有保障要素带至某地域，探索合成训练新路。该部领导态度坚定：“在实战中淬火‘新质保障力’，必须找到撬动战斗力建设的支点，这次演练恰好是转型后的综合探索。” 他们边摸索、边训练、边梳理成果，划定了“对标实战需求的四级能力指标”，为训练和人才培养细化标准；打通指挥所与各作战单元、保障单元之间的“数据链路”，依托一体化指挥平台，将指挥网络向战场前沿延伸。 变化，看得见、摸得着。从油料补给到装备抢修，从热食前送到工程抢建，一个新型链条接入信息网，逐步实现“一张图指挥、一盘棋联动”。 一次演练现场，面对链路中断，某保障分队迅速反应。通信分队派出号手携带单兵电台、卫星便携站、北斗终端等多型装备前出支援。一人多机、一机多能、多链融合，重建通信节点。“这种场景，以前想都不敢想。”一名参演战士感慨道。 编制调整的深层目标，在于释放战斗力的内生潜能。他们聚焦最为紧迫的生成问题，破解各保障分队力量如何高效融入作战体系、如何与导弹部队实现精准耦合等课题。 去年下半年，他们主动对接战场需求，组织后勤、装备、工程等保障力量，与某导弹旅开展联动训练。从油料补给、热食前送，到运力支援、物资运输，再到装备抢修、道路抢建，保障任务贯穿作战全流程、覆盖任务全地域。 战斗刚打响，导演部就下达指令：“工程保障队需要为多个发射分队开辟战场通路。”任务区域转移，官兵昼夜机动，穿行于山地林区与电磁干扰区……最终，多个发射架按时完成阵地构筑，导弹如期升空。 “仗怎么打，保障就怎么建；作战需要什么，保障就发展什么。”面对未来信息战争中的“小编组、大合成、模块化、多能化”的保障新样态，该部传统的“单一技能型”保障模式已难适应。 为此，他们以年度“弱训课目清单”为抓手，区分“指挥、技术、战术、共同”类别，分层分级施训，调整优化训练计划，确保训练质效提升。 一次联合演习中，他们依托某辅助软件，实现对末端精准指挥控制，保障响应速度提升40%以上。 近年来，该部一批“懂作战、通技术、会指挥”的复合型骨干加速成长，并在火箭军组织的比武竞赛中获2个团体桂冠…… 荣誉，是这支新型保障力量加速转型的生动写照，也是他们迈向未来战场聚力打赢的强大动力。 本版编辑对话火箭军某部部队长—— 锻造“新质保障力” 随着深入推进战斗力建设需要，必须整合优化指挥保障、勤务、通信、后勤、装备等多元力量，打造全新保障力量。其“新”不仅在于装备升级或编制调整，更在于对保障逻辑的根本性重塑。面对未来战场作战方式的深刻变革，这支力量如何实现保障反应灵敏、运转高效？围绕这一话题，本版编辑与火箭军某部部队长进行了对话交流。 编辑：部队围绕锻造“新质保障力”进行了哪些探索？ 部队长：我们将通信、作战、综合等保障要素深度融合，打破过去“各守一摊”的分散模式，实现资源统一调配与协同联动。保障力量集数据支持、信息通联、物资供应、维修保障等多功能于一体。一旦受命，可快速抵达作战地域，努力为指挥机构和导弹部队提供精确战场数据、多元通联手段和持续物资供应。 编辑：适应新的作战样态，如何推动保障人才转型升级？ 部队长：新型保障人才培养要敢于突破传统思维，善于在实战化训练中总结经验、创新方法。建立“保障+作战”融合训练机制，让保障人才在联合作战背景下理解作战需求，实现保障方案与作战节奏同频共振；推行“一专多能、一岗多通”培养模式，鼓励保障人员跨专业学习、跨岗位轮训，全程参与作战筹划、任务推演和实兵对抗，提升保障体系的综合作战水平。 编辑：如何确保各类保障元素在战场上“拧成一股绳”？ 部队长：未来作战拼的是体系能力，“如何做到定统一目标、建联动机制、凝协同意识”，要从机制、执行等多个维度推进：把作战任务作为唯一出发点，打破信息壁垒和流程梗阻，让指令直达末端、资源精准投送；从指挥员到普通战士，都将协同意识刻入脑中，形成“1+1>2”的保障效能。 编辑：平时保障如何服务战时打赢？ 部队长：我们坚持将战斗力标准贯穿全程，实现训练体系与作战需求精准对接。围绕指挥、通信、油料、运输、维修、医疗等保障行动，分阶段、分层次组织专项训练——基础训练打牢、专业训练深化、综合演练检验。平时设置复杂电磁环境、突发故障、多路需求叠加等实战化条件，锤炼高强度对抗下的快速响应、连续保障和应急处置能力。 编辑：请描述一下“新质保障力”的未来场景。 部队长：体制上，探索完善构建平战一体、军地协同、上下贯通的指挥体制，推动由“条块分割”向“体系集成”转变，实现作战指挥与保障指挥同频共振、一体联动。模式上，依托大数据等，推动构建智能模式，实现资源精准匹配、可视可控。能力上，依托应急保障体系和高新技术，构建平战结合、快速转换的保障能力底座，提升持续保障能力。
