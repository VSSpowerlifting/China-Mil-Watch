One Battle Flag, One Drama — They Made the Silent Battle Flags Speak

一面战旗一部剧，他们让沉默的战旗说话了
PLA Daily (解放军报) 13 May 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A brigade of the 78th Group Army has produced at least five battle-flag reportage dramas (战旗报告剧) since 2023 under a Group Army-wide initiative called 'One Battle Flag, One Drama' (一面战旗一部剧), staging more than 30 touring performances for a cumulative audience exceeding 50,000 personnel, with the goal of eventually producing one drama for each of the Group Army's 266 honor flags. The project documents a deliberate shift in PLA political work methodology away from lecture-based transmission toward what the Group Army's Propaganda Section calls 'soldiers performing soldiers' (兵演兵) — an immersive format designed to address the acknowledged failure of conventional didacticism (说教) to generate personal investment among junior enlisted personnel. The framing that theater attendance drove a 20% increase in self-initiated equipment-tactics study and improved pass rates for mountain driving and nighttime shooting points to how the institution is now measuring political education against training metrics, though the causal link between the two rests entirely on the Group Army's own reporting.

One Battle Flag, One Drama

■ Niu Hui, Li Xingchao

Stepping into the general-purpose hall of a certain brigade under the 78th Group Army, a wave of heat hit us alongside the sounds of battle cries. The reportage drama (报告剧) "Our Troops March Toward the Sun" was in rehearsal here.

This production weaves together the stories of five anti-Japanese war hero collectives, including the "Hundred-Victories Third Battalion" and the "Ma Dingfu Model Company for Loving the People." Private Liu Zhiqiang plays a heroic predecessor from the "Hundred-Victories Third Battalion." When he roared and charged toward the enemy's blockade line, the atmosphere in the entire rehearsal hall froze in an instant. For a moment, I felt as though I had been transported into the smoke and fire of the Pingxingguan battlefield.

"Stop! The emotion is wrong — again!" The sudden command interrupted the performance. Zhang Fengbin, head of the Group Army's Cultural Work Station, walked up to Liu Zhiqiang. Rather than discussing acting technique, he pulled a stack of yellowed battle-history archives from his document folder. "The hand gripping the rifle must not only be powerful — it must carry a resolve to 'throw your life away,'" Zhang said, pointing to a mobilization order inside the archive.

This archival, research-based approach to directing is precisely what makes this reportage drama distinctive. Here, the script is not the final authority — history is. The dense red annotations covering the script reflect not only the creative team's relentless pursuit of historical detail, but also their determination to break the drama free from the mold of conventional didacticism (说教).

Watching the performers gradually find their footing through round after round of adjustments, I felt a spiritual resonance that transcended time and space.

"In the past, passing on tradition usually meant 'I talk, you listen,' and it was very hard for officers and soldiers to feel personally invested," a leader from the Group Army's Propaganda Section told me. To address this, they decided to follow the approach of "sharing resources, forging spirit together" (资源共享、精神共铸), making full use of the Group Army's rich historical resources and rolling out the "One Battle Flag, One Drama" (一面战旗一部剧) cultural project across the board — creating a corresponding battle-flag reportage drama for each and every honor flag.

"The Group Army has 266 honor flags in total — like scattered pearls, waiting for us to string them into a necklace," the leader said.

In the course of my visits, I learned that as the dramas were created and performed, the soldiers of the "Tank-Pulling Hero Company" used old photographs to reconstruct the "earth tanks" of that era, and the first lesson for new recruits joining the "White Tiger Company" was to polish the old battle flag.

Battalion Commander Li of a certain combined-arms battalion said that in the past, when facing difficult training subjects, he always had to rack his brain figuring out how to motivate soldiers to overcome their reluctance. "But after watching the touring performance of 'The First Company to Break Through the Wu River,' everyone's spirit and energy were noticeably different." He opened the training log to show me: over the past month, the number of times soldiers in the battalion proactively studied equipment tactics had increased by nearly 20% compared to before, and the pass rates for difficult and key subjects such as mountain driving and nighttime shooting had steadily improved.

"The silent battle flags have spoken, the sleeping battle histories have awakened, and the battle flags have become a spiritual totem you can touch." The words of the Group Army's leadership moved me deeply. From the sweat-soaked rehearsal hall to the parade ground brimming with fighting spirit, what I witnessed was not merely the birth of a reportage drama — it was a unit's tribute to history and its pledge to the future.

"The Silent Battle Flags Have Spoken"

■ Niu Hui, Hao Shuai, Li Xingchao

[Photo caption: Performance of "The First Company to Break Through the Wu River" by a certain brigade of the 78th Group Army. Photo by Wang Jinpeng.]

"Don't worry about me — pull! As long as the boat gets through, our troops have hope!" A roar pierced through the water curtain on stage like a heavy hammer, striking every member of the audience in the heart.

On stage, dozens of "Red Army soldiers" hauled on ropes as they struggled forward through simulated crashing waves. Soaked through, muscles taut, they were knocked down again and again by the surging current, yet they clung desperately to the rope that symbolized hope, charging toward the "beacon fire" on the opposite bank. This scene unfolded at a performance of the battle-flag reportage drama "The First Company to Break Through the Wu River," staged by a certain brigade of the 78th Group Army. When the hand-sewn battle flag was planted on the high ground across the river amid the "smoke of battle," thunderous applause instantly ignited the entire venue.

As the lights dimmed, Zhang Fengbin, head of the Group Army's Cultural Work Station, stood at the side of the stage, his gaze sweeping across the faces in the audience, each one moved by the drama. A warmth stirred in his chest. Since joining the core creative team of the Group Army's "One Battle Flag, One Drama" project, he has felt increasingly that these are not merely performances — they are dialogues that transcend time and space.

"When the battle flag rises through the smoke of war, the cries of our predecessors and the hot blood of people today converge on the stage. History is no longer cold text — it becomes something you can touch and feel," Zhang said.

It is understood that within this Group Army, "One Battle Flag, One Drama" has become a red cultural brand. As the stories of the battle flags are brought to the stage and the footsteps of predecessors become guiding lights, the spiritual codes (精神密码) of the Group Army's 266 honor flags are being woven into the bloodlines of young officers and soldiers of the new era.

**Abandoning rigid didacticism and giving full play to the vivid form of "soldiers performing soldiers" (兵演兵)**

Zhang Fengbin still remembers that afternoon.

It was the 75th anniversary of Dong Cunrui's death. To mark the occasion, the company to which the Group Army's "Dong Cunrui Squad" belongs had put together a reportage drama and was presenting it for review by brigade headquarters in the rehearsal hall. Although the participating soldiers had been carefully selected as the company's best, they were unusually stiff on stage.

"For a new China — charge!" Corporal Zhang Hongjia, playing Dong Cunrui, flushed red. His voice was loud but his movements were wooden. He held the prop satchel charge aloft, shouting the slogan he knew by heart, yet his eyes conveyed none of the required resolve and burning intensity.

When the performance ended, the brigade leadership furrowed their brows, their tone heavy: "This won't do. A performance like this cannot convey the spirit of a hero, let alone move our comrades."

With the anniversary fast approaching and the creative challenge enormous, this "hot potato" ultimately landed in Zhang Fengbin's hands.

The day he received the assignment, Zhang did not rush to restart rehearsals. Instead, he shut himself in his office. Late that night, staring at the hollow, ornate language of the first draft on his computer screen, he frowned and deleted, word by word, every empty flourish.

"Dong Cunrui is not a cold statue — he was a living, breathing person," Zhang recalled of his thinking at the time. "What we need to show is the real moment when a 19-year-old young man, facing life and death, transcended the limits of his body and made his choice. So we absolutely had to abandon rigid didacticism and give full play to the vivid form of 'soldiers performing soldiers.'"

He replaced every piece of elevated written language with the plain speech of soldiers, cut the lengthy narration, and kept only the details that truly moved people. A new rehearsal plan was quickly finalized, and the entire company immediately sprang into action:

Sergeant Li Zhihui, who often edited videos, tracked down precious archival footage that had been gathering dust for decades — footage recording interviews with Dong Cunrui's comrades-in-arms. "This is the battlefield environment of that time — this is the real Dong Cunrui Squad!"

Senior Specialist Zhang Qiang, who plays instruments, composed music overnight, blending the company's reveille and charge bugle calls into a new arrangement. "I wanted to use bugle calls as the backdrop — they are the horn of the charge, and the soul of our company."

Zhang Hongjia, playing Dong Cunrui, practiced his facial expression in front of a mirror. Wearing the old uniform used in the performance, he rehearsed the motion of blowing up the pillbox over and over, his shoulders rubbed raw by the prop. "I want my comrades to see that heroes are also flesh-and-blood ordinary people — they just, at the critical moment, carried a greater sense of responsibility than ordinary people."

On the day of the anniversary, the auditorium was packed. Every eye in the audience was fixed on the figure on stage.

Zhang Hongjia stood under the spotlight playing Dong Cunrui. The roar of artillery fire around him seemed to dissolve into silence. His gaze shifted gradually from hesitation to resolve. He raised the satchel charge and called out: "For a new China — charge!" This time, the words were no longer hollow lines — they sounded like a cry from the depths of the soul.

At this sight, many officers and soldiers could not hold back their tears. Dong Cunrui's nephew, Dong Jixian, who had been invited to attend, was equally moved to tears: "He's just like him — exactly like him. That's my uncle!"

"This is the kind of battle-flag reportage drama we need." When the performance concluded successfully, the Group Army's leadership expressed full approval of the result. "We must keep making battle-flag reportage dramas and let the red genes shine on the stage."

**Using "successors" (传人) to portray predecessors — letting heroic stories be passed down through soldiers performing and narrating them themselves**

On the whiteboard, a weighty number stood out: 266.

"The entire Group Army has 266 honor flags. Some have distant histories with missing records; others have complex battle histories that are difficult to reconstruct. Turning all these stories into reportage dramas is an enormous task!" At a Group Army headquarters meeting, faced with the sweeping scope of the "One Battle Flag, One Drama" plan, the air in the conference room grew heavy.

"This is a project to carry forward the red bloodline (赓续红色血脉), and we must persist in doing it." The Group Army's leadership settled the matter decisively. "We will use 'successors' to portray predecessors, break the enormous goal down into concrete tasks, and let heroic stories be passed down through soldiers performing and narrating them themselves."

The relevant headquarters departments moved quickly, soon producing a detailed "red genealogy." They designated the Group Army's cultural and propaganda backbone personnel to lead the core creative work, and required that performers for each drama be drawn first from the units to which the battle flag belongs.

The task was passed down like a relay baton. Many grassroots units began tracing the footsteps of their predecessors and excavating historical moments in preparation for creating their battle-flag reportage dramas. The "First Company to Break Through the Wu River" of a certain brigade under the Group Army was among the first pilot units to implement the battle-flag reportage drama initiative.

Before formal rehearsals began, Political Instructor Liu Qiang brought the soldiers participating in the drama into the honors room. Pointing to an old photograph in the display case that captured the battle scene of that time, he said solemnly: "Comrades, why is our company called 'The First Company to Break Through the Wu River'? Because in the bitter cold of winter 1935, our predecessors used their own bodies of flesh and blood to blaze a bloody path through a hail of bullets for the entire army."

"The Wu River was a formidable natural barrier, its current swift and violent. Our predecessors had no advanced equipment, no adequate supplies — only the courage to press forward without hesitation." The instructor's voice was low and forceful. Corporal Huang Yufei looked at the figures in the photograph wading through the current and pressing bravely forward, and felt a deep stirring in his heart.

The instructor's words were like a seed, taking root and sprouting inside him. When the script and rehearsal assignment for "The First Company to Break Through the Wu River" were officially issued, Huang Yufei volunteered, becoming one of the performers playing a "river-crossing soldier."

"One-two, pull! One-two, pull!"

The director called out the rhythm from the bank as Huang Yufei and his comrades simulated the river-crossing movements in a pool. Gripping the rough rope, watching his comrades beside him stroke powerfully through the water and push forward, he suddenly thought of the old photograph in the honors room — of those figures who were just as young as himself — and of the "hail of bullets" and "bodies of flesh and blood" the instructor had described.

In that moment, the pool water beneath his feet seemed to turn into the bone-chilling waters of the Wu River. He felt as though he had returned to that stormy, pitch-dark night — the whistle of bullets in his ears, comrades falling at his side, and before him the desperate reality that "if we can't cross, the whole company will be sacrificed." The rope in his hands seemed truly to be lashed to a rocking wooden boat — and to the lives of countless comrades behind him.

When rehearsal ended that day, Huang Yufei felt as though he had undergone a cleansing of the soul. He had never imagined he could so vividly experience the feelings of his predecessors. The stories of heroes were no longer cold historical records — they had become scorching hot blood, branded into the hearts of every soldier who took part.

On the day of the formal performance, the audience hall was packed. Huang Yufei and his comrades, in soaking-wet "uniforms," charged across the stage with total physical and emotional immersion, their eyes radiating unshakeable resolve.

"I want to engrave into the bloodstream of every viewer the belief that our predecessors held — that no matter what difficulties they faced, they were determined to win," he said.

**Carrying the red genes from the stage to the combat post — transforming the emotion of the theater into battlefield capability**

The touring performances of the battle-flag reportage dramas scattered sparks that ignited a blazing wave across the Group Army's units. From the "Dong Cunrui Squad" sacrificing themselves to blow up the pillbox, to the 37 warriors of the "White Tiger Company" fighting to the last, to the heroic charge of the "Iron Hammer Regiment" — since the "One Battle Flag, One Drama" project launched in 2023, they have completed five drama productions covering eight heroic model units spanning from the Red Army period to the new era.

It is understood that to date, these reportage dramas have toured more than 30 performances within the Group Army, with a cumulative audience exceeding 50,000. Each production innovates in its stage presentation, aiming to deliver an immersive experience for officers and soldiers:

"Our Troops March Toward the Sun" uses a cross-temporal dialogue format, bringing new-era officers and soldiers onto the same stage to interact with historical predecessors. "The First Company to Break Through the Wu River" employs 3D projection technology to recreate a battlefield environment thick with the smoke of war. Some productions incorporate the form of narrative storytelling (情景评书) and other formats that officers and soldiers enjoy, telling heroic stories in ways they find accessible and engaging.

The impact on stage has brought transformation in action.

On the night the "Dong Cunrui Squad" reportage drama tour concluded, the company immediately launched a nighttime tactical training exercise.

On the firing range in the dead of night, the wind was biting cold. Private First Class Qian Guoxiang lay on the gravel, his mind filled with the image of "Dong Cunrui" on stage, inching forward inch by inch through a wall of fire. He pressed his elbows hard into the ground, dragging his rifle, driving himself forward across the rough, stony surface with all his strength, the elbow pads on his arms worn hot from the friction.

"In the past, people trained mostly to meet the standard. Now, it's more like charging forward with everything you've got — to win," Squad Leader Hu Hailong reflected.

Private First Class Chen Mutao of a certain brigade's information support detachment still remembers a scene from "Our Troops March Toward the Sun" — the radio is destroyed, communications are forcibly cut off, and an "old squad leader" uses his own body to reconnect the circuit, roaring as he transmits the final intelligence report. In that moment, Chen Mutao was shaken to his core.

Since taking up the role of radio operator, Chen Mutao had at times doubted whether he could do the job well. Faced with dense circuit diagrams and the tap-tap-tap of Morse code, he was always flustered, and when interference noise struck he would be at a complete loss. But in that moment, watching the figure on stage clutching the radio set in the midst of battle smoke, Chen Mutao suddenly understood: the handset and telegraph key in his hands were connected to the lives of his comrades — and to the outcome of battle.

"A communications soldier is not a simple relay — he is the neuron connecting the battlefield." That reportage drama led Chen Mutao to begin seeing his combat post in a new light. After the performance, he sought out his squad leader on his own initiative: "Squad Leader, I want to practice copying Morse code. I'm not afraid of the tedium anymore."

In the days that followed, to master the skill of "blind copying" (盲抄), Chen Mutao covered his eyes and captured signal pulses in the dark. To build muscle memory, he strapped the telegraph key to his fingers and simulated tapping rhythms even while walking. In the end, he passed all professional subject assessments with top marks and took first place in the "copying under complex electromagnetic environment" subject at the Group Army skills competition.

"'One Battle Flag, One Drama' has not only fully mobilized previously untapped red education resources — it has also achieved unprecedented innovation in the form of education," the head of the Group Army's Propaganda Section told me during the interview. Today, walking into any unit of the Group Army, you can feel the vitality this innovation has brought.

"The reportage dramas are not just performances — they are sparks, helping us carry the red genes from the stage to the combat post and transform the emotion of the theater into battlefield capability," the leader said with conviction. "This is still only the beginning. We will keep creating, one drama at a time, activating the spiritual codes of all 266 flags one by one and passing them down for generations to come."

**This Edition's Editor in Dialogue with the Director of the Propaganda Section of the 78th Group Army:**

**Let Every Battle Flag Become a Torch**

Political work is the lifeline of our army and the fundamental guarantee for rallying the military's will and forging the military's soul. Innovating the forms of red education is an important method for activating this lifeline. The 266 flags in the honors room contain rich red historical resources. How can these battle flags be made to "speak," and how can the weighty historical stories they carry be transformed into spiritual nourishment that officers and soldiers of the new era can absorb and apply? Around this topic, this edition's editor held a discussion with the Director of the Political Work Department's Propaganda Section of the 78th Group Army.

**Editor: What measures has headquarters taken to drive implementation of the "One Battle Flag, One Drama" project?**

**Section Director:** We have adhered to combining top-level design with grassroots initiative, focusing on three key links. First, dedicated task-force advancement (专班推进): headquarters established a dedicated red culture work task force, drawing on backbone personnel from the propaganda section, cultural work station, and military history museum to conduct a comprehensive survey of all honor flags across the Group Army, establishing individual files for each, and ensuring that the resource baseline is clear and the historical facts are accurate. Second, precise diagnosis (精准把脉): we conducted in-depth research at the frontline, and based on the characteristics of the "internet-native generation" (网生代) of officers and soldiers, established an immersive education approach — turning "narrating history" into "performing history" and transforming battle flags from static displays into dynamic teaching materials. Third, using key points to drive the whole (以点带面): we selected eight representative units — including the "Hundred-Victories Third Battalion" and the "First Company to Break Through the Wu River" — as pilot units, organized professional personnel to work alongside the creative teams, and refined model productions such as "Our Troops March Toward the Sun" and "The First Company to Break Through the Wu River," developing a replicable and scalable production process to ensure this project was standardized from the very start.

**Editor: How does the transformation of power from "performing heroes in the drama" to "learning from heroes outside the drama" — from stage to combat post — actually occur?**

**Section Director:** The key to the transformation lies in the sense of identity that immersion (代入感) produces. In the past, when heroes were presented to soldiers, the soldiers were bystanders. Now, when soldiers perform heroes, the soldiers become characters in the story. Having experienced the hot blood and hardship of their predecessors on stage, soldiers more readily develop a kind of psychological suggestion: "As a successor of a heroic company, what does a little hardship like this amount to?" This kind of spiritual potential energy (精神势能) converts directly into combat effectiveness.

Across the Group Army's units, many soldiers have brought the drive of the heroic predecessors they portrayed in the dramas onto the training ground, proactively requesting additional training. These phenomena prove that when political work truly touches the souls of officers and soldiers, it can become a "force multiplier" (倍增器) for combat effectiveness.

**Editor: What plans does the Group Army have for continuing to advance the "One Battle Flag, One Drama" project?**

**Section Director:** Transforming the red resources of all 266 flags into drama productions is a major undertaking that requires sustained, long-term effort. We have incorporated "One Battle Flag, One Drama" into the Group Army's long-range political work plan, advancing steadily according to the approach of "pilot first, full rollout, deepen the transformation" (试点先行、全面推广、深化转化).

At present, we are concentrating our efforts on refining the first batch of benchmark productions, accumulating experience for subsequent drama creation by other honored companies. At the same time, we are exploring the establishment of a "battle flag successor" (战旗传承人) system, whereby the lead performer of each drama becomes the designated advocate for that unit's battle flag — using touring performances, mentoring, and other means to extend the reach of each production to more units. We are working to achieve the virtuous cycle of "one drama activates one flag, one flag drives a group of people," so that the 266 honor flags truly become torches illuminating the march toward a stronger military (强军征程).

时值董存瑞牺牲75周年之际，为迎接纪念日到来，该集团军“董存瑞班”所在连倾力打造了一台报告剧，在排练厅接受旅机关审查。虽然参演官兵都是连队精挑细选的尖子，但在舞台上格外僵硬。 “为了新中国，冲啊！”饰演董存瑞的下士张宏嘉涨红了脸，声音洪亮但动作生硬。他高举着道具炸药包，嘴里喊着那句烂熟于心的口号，眼神里却未传达出该有的决绝与炽热。 一场表演下来，旅领导眉头紧锁，语气沉重：“不行，这样的表演传达不了英雄精神，更打动不了战友。” 眼看纪念日临近，创排难度极大，这个“烫手山芋”，最终落到了张峰宾手里。 接到任务那天，张峰宾没有急于重排，而是把自己关在办公室。深夜，他看着电脑屏幕上辞藻空洞的初版剧本，皱着眉头，一个字一个字删掉了那些虚浮的修饰词。 “董存瑞不是冷冰冰的雕像，他是一个活生生的人。”回忆起当时的心路历程，张峰宾说，“我们要展现的，是一个19岁青年在生死面前，如何超越身体极限、作出抉择的真实瞬间，所以必须摒弃生硬的说教，将‘兵演兵’的鲜活形式发挥好。” 于是，他将所有“高大上”的书面语都换成兵言兵语，删掉冗长的旁白，只留下最打动人心的细节。很快，新的排练方案确定，全连官兵立刻动了起来—— 常常剪辑视频的中士李志辉找到尘封几十年的珍贵影像资料，里面记录了对董存瑞战友的采访：“这就是当年的战场环境，就是真实的董存瑞班！” 擅长乐器的二级上士张强连夜谱曲，把连队的起床号、冲锋号混合到一起重新编曲：“我想用军号声作为背景，那是冲锋的号角，也是我们连队的魂。” 饰演董存瑞的张宏嘉，对着镜子练眼神。他穿着演出用的旧军装，一遍遍模拟着炸碉堡的动作，肩膀被道具磨得通红：“我要让战友们看到，英雄也是有血有肉的普通人，只是在关键时刻，比普通人多了一份担当！” 纪念日当天，礼堂里座无虚席，全场观众的目光都汇聚于舞台上那道身影。 饰演董存瑞的张宏嘉站在聚光灯下，周遭的炮火轰鸣好像已化为寂静，目光逐渐从犹豫变得坚定。他举起炸药包，高声道：“为了新中国，冲啊！”这一声，不再是空洞的台词，而像是发自灵魂的呐喊。 看到这一幕，许多官兵都忍不住红了眼眶。受邀前来观看演出的董存瑞之侄董继先同样热泪盈眶：“像，太像了！这就是我叔叔！” “这样的战旗报告剧，正是我们需要的。”演出圆满结束，该集团军领导对演出效果十分认可，“我们要坚持把战旗报告剧做下去，让红色基因在舞台上焕发光彩！” 用“传人”扮演先辈，让英雄故事在官兵的自演自讲中传承下去 白板上，一个沉甸甸的数字赫然在目：266。 “全集团军一共266面荣誉旗帜，有的历史久远资料缺失，有的战史复杂难以还原，要把这些故事全部做成报告剧，任务太艰巨了！”集团军机关会议上，面对“一面战旗一部剧”这个庞大的规划，会议室里的空气有些凝滞。 “这是一项赓续红色血脉的工程，我们一定要坚持做下去。”集团军领导一锤定音，“我们要用‘传人’扮演先辈，把庞大的目标化成具体的任务，让英雄故事在官兵的自演自讲中传承下去。” 于是，机关相关部门迅速行动，很快梳理出一份详细的“红色家谱”。他们委任集团军文化宣传骨干牵头完成主创工作，并要求每部剧目的演员优先从战旗所属单位遴选。 任务像接力棒一样传了下去。许多基层部队都开始寻访先辈足迹、挖掘历史瞬间，为创作战旗报告剧进行准备。该集团军某旅“突破乌江第一连”，正是第一批推行战旗报告剧的试点单位。 正式创排前，指导员刘强专门将参与剧目的战士带到荣誉室内，指着展柜里那张记录了当年战斗场景的老照片，郑重地说：“同志们，我们连为什么叫‘突破乌江第一连’？是因为1935年那个寒冬，我们的先辈用血肉之躯，在枪林弹雨中为全军蹚开了一条血路。” “乌江天险，水流湍急。先辈们没有先进的装备，没有充足的补给，只有一往无前的勇气。”指导员的声音低沉而有力，下士黄雨飞看着照片上那些蹚着水流勇往直前的身影，内心触动。 指导员的话像一颗种子，在他心里生根发芽。当《突破乌江第一连》的剧本和排练任务正式下达时，黄雨飞主动请缨，成为一名“渡江战士”的扮演者。 “一二，划！一二，划！” 导演在岸上不断喊着口令，黄雨飞和战友在水池中模拟着渡江的动作。握着粗糙的绳索，看着身边战友奋力划开水流向前，他突然想起荣誉室里那张老照片，想起那些和自己同样年轻的身影，想起指导员口中的“枪林弹雨”和“血肉之躯”。 这一刻，脚下的池水仿佛变成刺骨的乌江水，他好像回到了那个风雨如磐的深夜——耳畔是呼啸的子弹，身边是倒下的战友，面前是“渡不过去，全连都得牺牲”的绝境。手中那根绳索，仿佛真正系着一艘摇晃的木船，系着身后万千战友的性命。 那天排练结束，黄雨飞像是经受了一场灵魂的洗练。他从未想过，自己可以如此真切地体会到先辈们的心情。英雄的故事不再是冷冰冰的史料，而化作滚烫的热血，在每一名参演官兵心中烙下印记。 正式演出那天，台下座无虚席。黄雨飞和战友们穿着打湿的“军装”，身心沉浸地在舞台上冲锋，眼神里是无可动摇的坚定。 “我要把先辈面对任何困难都誓死必胜的信念，通过这根绳索，刻进每一个观看者的血脉里。”他说。 把红色基因从舞台带到战位，把剧场里的感动转化为战场上的本领 战旗报告剧的巡演，撒下了一颗颗火种，在集团军各部队掀起火热浪潮。从“董存瑞班”舍身炸碉堡，到“白老虎连”37勇士血战到底，再到“铁锤子团”的英勇冲锋……自2023年“一面战旗一部剧”工程启动以来，他们共完成5部剧目创作，涵盖了从红军时期到新时代的8个英模单位。 据了解，到目前为止，这些报告剧已在集团军内巡演30余场，累计观众超5万人次。每一部剧目都在创新舞台表现形式，旨在为官兵带来沉浸式体验—— 《我们的队伍向太阳》以时空对话形式，让新时代官兵与历史先辈同台交流；《突破乌江第一连》运用3D投影技术，还原了硝烟弥漫的战场环境；还有一些剧目融入情景评书等形式，用官兵喜闻乐见的方式讲述英雄故事…… 舞台上的触动，带来行动上的转变。 “董存瑞班”报告剧巡演结束当晚，该连就展开一场夜间战术训练。 深夜靶场，寒风刺骨。上等兵钱国祥趴在碎石地上，脑海里全是舞台上“董存瑞”在火力网下一寸寸往前挪的模样。他手肘死死抵住地面，拖着枪身，在粗糙的石子地面奋力向前突进，手臂上的护肘被磨得发烫。 “过去大家训练，往往是为达标，现在，更像是为了打赢鼓足劲儿往前冲！”班长胡海龙感慨道。 某旅信息保障队上等兵陈木涛仍记得，在《我们的队伍向太阳》剧中看到的一幕——电台被炸毁，通信被迫中断，一名“老班长”用血肉之躯接通线路，嘶吼着发出最后一条情报。那一刻，陈木涛的心被深深震撼。 自从担任报务员以来，陈木涛一度怀疑自己“能不能干好”。面对密密麻麻的线路图和滴滴答答的摩斯电码，他总是手忙脚乱，遇到杂音干扰甚至会不知所措。可在这一刻，看着舞台上那个在硝烟中死死护住电台的身影，陈木涛突然意识到：手中的话筒和电键，牵系着战友的生命，更关系到战场胜负。 “通信兵不是简单的传声筒，而是连通战场的神经元。”这场报告剧，让陈木涛开始重新认识自己的战位。看完那场演出，他主动找到班长：“班长，我想练抄报，我不怕枯燥了。” 随后的日子里，为练就“盲抄”技能，陈木涛蒙上双眼，在黑暗中捕捉信号跳动；为形成肌肉记忆，他把电键绑在手指上，走路都在模拟敲击节奏。最终，他以全优成绩通过所有专业课目考核，并在集团军比武中夺得“复杂电磁环境抄报”课目的第一名。 “‘一面战旗一部剧’不仅充分调动了未被挖掘的红色教育资源，更对教育形式进行了前所未有的创新。”采访中，该集团军宣传处领导告诉笔者。如今，走进集团军任何一支部队，都能感受到这种创新带来的活力。 “报告剧不仅是演出，更是火种，帮助我们把红色基因从舞台带到战位，把剧场里的感动转化为战场上的本领。”这名领导坚定地说，“现在还只是个开始，我们要坚持一部一部创作，把266面旗帜的精神密码逐个激活，并长久地传承下去。” 本版编辑对话第七十八集团军宣传处处长—— 让每一面战旗都成为火炬 政治工作是我军的生命线，是凝聚军心、铸牢军魂的根本保证。创新红色教育形式，是激活这条生命线的重要方法。荣誉室里的266面旗帜，蕴藏着丰富的红色历史资源，该如何让这些战旗“开口说话”，将厚重的历史故事转化为新时代官兵听得进、用得上的精神养分？围绕这一话题，本版编辑与第78集团军政治工作部宣传处处长展开了交流。 编辑：为了推动“一面战旗一部剧”工程落实，机关采取了哪些举措？ 处长：我们坚持顶层设计与基层首创相结合，重点抓三个环节——一是专班推进。机关专门成立红色文化工作专班，抽调宣传、文化工作站及军史馆的骨干力量，对全集团军的荣誉旗帜进行拉网式普查，逐一建档立案，确保资源底数清、史实准。二是精准把脉。我们深入一线开展调研，针对“网生代”官兵特点，确立了沉浸式教育思路，把“讲历史”变成“演历史”，让战旗从静态陈列变成动态教材。三是以点带面。我们选取了“百战百胜第三营”“突破乌江第一连”等8个典型单位作为试点，组织专业人员跟班创作，打磨出《我们的队伍向太阳》《突破乌江第一连》等模范剧目，总结了一套可复制可推广的创排流程，确保这项工程起步就有规范。 编辑：官兵从“剧中演英雄”到“剧外学英雄”，这种从舞台到战位的力量转化是如何发生的？ 处长：转化的关键，在于代入感带来的身份认同。过去讲英雄，官兵是旁观者；现在演英雄，官兵成了剧中人。战士在舞台上体验过先辈的热血和艰辛，更容易产生一种心理暗示：“作为英雄连队的传人，这点苦算什么？”这种精神势能会直接转化为战斗力。 在集团军各部队，不少人把剧中英雄先辈的拼劲带到训练场，主动要求加练。这些现象证明，当政治工作真正触动官兵灵魂，就能成为战斗力的“倍增器”。 编辑：集团军对于继续推进“一面战旗一部剧”工程还有哪些规划？ 处长：要将266面旗帜的红色资源都转化为剧目，是一项需要久久为功的大工程。我们已将“一面战旗一部剧”纳入集团军政治工作长远规划，按照“试点先行、全面推广、深化转化”的思路稳步推进。 目前，我们正集中力量打磨第一批标杆剧目，为后续其他荣誉连队完成剧目创作积累经验。同时，我们也在探索建立“战旗传承人”制度，让每部剧的主演成为该单位战旗的固定宣讲员，通过巡演、带教等方式，将一部剧目辐射到更多单位，努力实现“一部剧激活一面旗，一面旗带动一群人”的良性循环，让266面荣誉旗帜真正成为照亮强军征程的火炬。
