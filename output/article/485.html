Summary
AVIC's Chengdu Aircraft Industry Group and Chengdu Aircraft Design and Research Institute hosted a state-owned enterprise open day at their western Chengdu campus, showcasing the J-5A, J-10, J-20, and the 'Yunying' (Cloud Shadow) UAV alongside exhibits on the facilities' history from State-Owned Factory No. 132's 1958 founding through current CNC and composite-material manufacturing programs. The article documents AVIC Chengdu's institutional self-presentation on technology indigenization—specifically the 1999 'S-shaped test piece' that became China's first international machine-tool precision standard in 2020, and engineer Nie Haiping's independently developed fiber-placement software architecture—framing both as evidence that core aviation manufacturing competencies are no longer import-dependent. The talent-pipeline narrative centered on chief designer Song Wencong and his successor Academician Wang Haifeng extends this framing to workforce depth, with the UAV team's average age of 28 at the Yunying program's launch offered as the concrete data point.

A Visit to the Aviation Industry Corporation of China State-Owned Enterprise Open Day——

Iron Wings Soar to the Heavens, the Tempered Sword Points to the Sky (铁翼凌霄汉 淬剑向苍穹)

■ PLA Daily Reporter Qi Chenfei

Banners unfurl along the Qingshui River, iron wings roar across the vast sky. Not long ago, on the fertile ground of western Chengdu—where banners reading "Serve the Nation Through Aviation" hang high—the Aviation Industry Corporation of China (AVIC) State-Owned Enterprise Open Day was grandly inaugurated.

The J-5A, J-10, J-20… one combat aircraft after another has emerged from this place, becoming the steel backbone guarding the nation's air and space. Strolling through this modernized campus, every detail bears the firm imprint of aviation workers who relied on self-reliance and dared to scale new heights, inscribing a magnificent chapter in which generation after generation of devoted aviation sons and daughters forged their loyalty and wisdom into the nation's most powerful weapons and lifted the war eagles to soar through the heavens.

Chengdu Aircraft Industry Theme Park. Photo by Yu Xiang.

Laying the Foundation Along the Qingshui River: Self-Reliance Forges the "Cradle" of the War Eagle

Entering the Historical and Cultural Boulevard of Chengdu Aircraft Industry (Group) Co., Ltd. (hereinafter "AVIC Chengdu Aircraft"), a scroll of entrepreneurial history gradually unfurled before this reporter.

"One brick, one shovelful of earth, pulled by hand and carried on shoulders…" The narrator's voice transported us back to Huangtianba more than 60 years ago.

On October 18, 1958, along the Qingshui River on the western outskirts of Chengdu, aviation construction workers from all corners of the country gathered here. In a simple yet spirited atmosphere, the predecessor of AVIC Chengdu Aircraft—State-Owned Factory No. 132—officially broke ground. At that moment, perhaps no one could have foreseen that this factory, starting from rice paddies and thatched haystacks, would rise to become the backbone of China's aviation industry and give birth to a series of aerial swords that would awe the world.

Standing on the Cultural Boulevard, the hardships of those years leap out from the exhibits. "Hard struggle, thrift in building the nation, determined striving, self-reliance (艰苦奋斗、勤俭建国、发奋图强、自力更生)"—these sixteen characters painted on the factory gate were the most authentic expression of their entrepreneurial patriotism at the time.

Sleeping in crude sheds open to the wind on all sides; at the most difficult times, having nothing but a rationed portion of steamed rice and a small pinch of salt stir-fried in oil as a side dish… Under such conditions, the speed of Factory No. 132's construction was nothing short of miraculous: six years later, in the winter, the factory's basic construction passed full national acceptance inspection.

Alongside the national acceptance certificate for Factory No. 132 in the scroll hangs a military product type-certification certificate: in 1964, China's first all-weather interceptor designed, surveyed, and manufactured entirely through domestic efforts—the J-5A—soared into the sky for the first time, filling the gap in our military's all-weather combat capability.

The birth of the J-5A was a heroic song of self-reliance. The young chief designer Tu Jida led his team, relying solely on compasses, set squares, and steel rulers to manually survey and repeatedly calculate, performing reverse engineering design and verification on every component. What sustained them was the conviction to "win honor for the motherland." On the day of the maiden flight, when that silver-white combat aircraft rolled down the runway and climbed proudly into the sky, every worker and technician present was moved to tears. The character of "self-reliance and selfless dedication (自力更生、无私奉献)" was planted like a seed deep in the fertile soil of Huangtianba.

As the factory buildings of Huangtianba roared with machinery, the Republic's aviation dream continued to extend westward. In May 1970, a special train carrying more than 300 scientific researchers pulled into Chengdu and took root in the simple dormitories of the former Air Force No. 028 Aviation School beside Wuhou Shrine. This was the predecessor of the Chengdu Aircraft Design and Research Institute (hereinafter "AVIC Chengdu Institute"), hailed as the "cradle of fighter aircraft design"—the "J-9 Brigade." They likewise labored on this same fertile ground, embarking on a new journey.

Although the J-9 fighter ultimately never flew due to overly ambitious specifications, its pioneering exploration of the "canard aerodynamic configuration (鸭式气动布局)" theory and the "intercept weapon system (拦射武器系统)" were like golden seeds sown in the earth, laying a critically important technical foundation for the future J-10 fighter.

One shovelful of earth at Huangtianba, one room beside Wuhou Shrine—AVIC Chengdu Aircraft and AVIC Chengdu Institute, these "brother factory and institute" partners rooted in western Sichuan, used sweat and wisdom on what was once barren land to together plant the firm roots that would support the Republic's airpower in taking flight.

Breaking Through the Ice on Core Technologies: Pioneer Standards Lead the Way to Equipment Self-Reliance

In the CNC intelligent workshop exhibition hall of AVIC Chengdu Aircraft, an "S-shaped test piece (S形试件)" gleaming with a cold light draws the eye. This seemingly simple metal block is the "measuring stick" for evaluating the manufacturing precision of critical structural components of combat aircraft.

In the 1990s, when AVIC Chengdu Aircraft introduced advanced CNC machine tools to manufacture complex aviation structural components, it discovered that the international inspection standards that had been in use for many years had limitations.

Faced with this global challenge, the AVIC Chengdu Aircraft team did not retreat. They continuously extracted and analyzed problems arising in machine tool processing, and after repeated demonstration and testing, finally designed in 1999 a new test piece capable of meeting machine tool precision testing and evaluation standards—the "S-shaped test piece." Professional verification confirmed that it effectively guaranteed the precision manufacturing of complex structural components.

In 2020, the "S-shaped test piece" officially became an international standard—China's first international standard in the field of machine tool precision inspection, hailed as the "Chinese Dragon."

If the "S-shaped test piece" set the "benchmark" for machine tools, then developing the S-shaped skin panel for the J-10 fighter was the "decisive battle" that gave the aircraft its wings. This is the core of aerodynamic performance, and its manufacturing difficulty is extreme.

At the time, AVIC Chengdu Aircraft lacked relevant experience and equipment. When seeking foreign cooperation, the other party flatly declared: "With your equipment and technology, it is absolutely impossible!" These words cut deeply into the Chengdu Aircraft team. "We absolutely must win this one!" They joined forces with Beihang University (Beijing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics), boldly retrofitting old equipment and independently tackling the challenge.

At the trial production site, the atmosphere was tense. Too little force and the forming would be insufficient; too much force and the skin panel would be ruined, or even the equipment damaged. The skin panel blank was positioned, the equipment slowly started… When that perfect S-shaped curve was finally precisely rendered, thunderous applause erupted in an instant!

This kind of "starting from zero" is something the people of AVIC Chengdu Aircraft have experienced far too many times.

"Core technologies cannot be begged for, bought, or borrowed—only through independent research and development can we truly stand tall!" Senior Engineer Nie Haiping, a recipient of the "National May Day Labor Medal," holds this conviction firmly. His work concerns the "bones and sinews" of composite material wings for combat aircraft—automated fiber placement technology.

Initially, when Nie Haiping operated imported equipment, he could not even adjust the parameters; the admonition that he "could not bear the responsibility" weighed on him like a thorn in his back. Faced with the predicament of being unable to obtain licenses for imports and having core technologies blocked at every turn, Nie Haiping made up his mind: independent research and development, starting from zero!

With no ready-made algorithms, he derived them himself; with no process experience, he plunged into the workshop to learn from veteran craftsmen. During that period, the whiteboard in Nie Haiping's office was covered densely with formulas and architectural diagrams. Countless days and nights of formula derivation, architectural design, and process exploration… In September 2020, they finally built a completely independent software architecture for fiber placement that better suited domestic production realities. The moment the on-site test succeeded, the laboratory erupted in cheers. Even the veteran process engineer who had initially raised doubts offered sincere praise: "This software now is practical, efficient, reliable, and easy to use!"

Looking back on the journey—from the piercing pain of dependence on others, to advanced combat aircraft soaring through the sky, to "ready-to-hand" domestically produced software—the people of AVIC Chengdu Aircraft have proven with iron facts, through one charge after another of "starting from zero": every breakthrough in independent innovation of core technologies is a critical leap toward standing tall and winning the future!

Passing the Torch to Foster Pillars of Talent: Fertile Ground for Talent Nurtures a New Elite Force

In the central lawn of the AVIC Chengdu Aircraft factory grounds, a majestic J-10 fighter stands quietly.

It is not only a pride of China's aviation industry, but also a silent monument, inscribed with the core concept passed down through generations of China's aviation workers—"succeed with the aircraft type, succeed in your own development (型号成功我成才)." These heartfelt words, born from the J-10 development process, have long since become the shared creed of growth for aviation workers at both AVIC Chengdu Aircraft and AVIC Chengdu Institute.

This foresight in cultivating talent can be traced back to the starting point of the J-10 project. In 1986, the J-10 aircraft was formally approved as a program, and Song Wencong, then 56 years old and deputy director of AVIC Chengdu Institute, was appointed chief designer.

At the time, when faced with the concern that "age is unforgiving—will this aircraft be finalized under your watch?" Song Wencong smiled and replied: "Whether it gets finalized under my watch is not for me to say, but one thing is certain: through the development of this aircraft, a large cohort of talent in modern aircraft design and development will definitely emerge in China!"

History, through the glorious course of the J-10, confirmed the profound vision of this chief designer. Over 18 years, the J-10 project served as a great crucible. AVIC Chengdu Institute and AVIC Chengdu Aircraft, as the primary design and primary manufacturing units, not only forged a national strategic weapon but also tempered and trained a large cohort of core technical talent.

Academician Wang Haifeng is one of them. As the first graduate student recruited by Song Wencong, he still remembers to this day his mentor's open-minded approach of "cultivating talent without regard to convention (不拘一格育人才)." When discussing coordination plans for a new engine, faced with the major choice between "retrofitting the old aircraft" or "starting fresh," Song Wencong actively invited young people to speak their minds directly before senior veterans, and ultimately decided to adopt the innovative plan proposed by the young people. This trust in and willingness to let go of young talent was the key to igniting the spark of innovation.

Song Wencong's earnest expectations for his successors flowed not only through the details of daily work but also into the depths of heartfelt sentiment. "The old horse strolls leisurely over small hills; the young steed gallops vigorously to the white clouds above. The deep, rugged, and steep path leads to the heavens; united in heart, we fear nothing and climb as we will (老马信步小众山，青骥奋蹄白云端。深幽崎峻通天路，同心无畏任我攀)." In the display case of the Academician Song Wencong Memorial Exhibition Hall at AVIC Chengdu Institute, this poem, written with vigorous brushstrokes, caught this reporter's attention.

According to the narrator, on Youth Day (May 4th) in 2004, the then 74-year-old Song Wencong, at the invitation of AVIC's youth, joined them in climbing Longquan Mountain on the eastern outskirts of Chengdu. Gazing into the distance from the summit, filled with surging emotion, Elder Song took up his brush and wrote this poem—both a condensed summary of the arduous struggles of the past and a heartfelt expression of his earnest expectations for the new generation of aviation workers to spread their wings and soar.

This baton of lifting up the young and passing the torch has been firmly taken up by a new generation of leaders such as Wang Haifeng. In recent years, a large cohort of post-1985 and post-1990 young aviation science and technology backbone personnel at AVIC Chengdu Institute have rapidly grown and are boldly shouldering heavy responsibilities at key technical posts.

A few years ago, a newly hired doctoral graduate in the electromechanical department of AVIC Chengdu Institute successfully calculated a key performance parameter for a project during a team research effort. The leadership of the electromechanical department directly recommended that he report to Wang Haifeng, then chief designer of AVIC Chengdu Institute.

A newcomer less than three months into the job, able to report directly to the chief designer and an academician—at AVIC Chengdu Institute, regardless of seniority, as long as one's technical skills are solid and thinking is clear, one can take on major responsibilities in one's professional field and receive full support. This atmosphere of selecting people purely on merit has allowed generation after generation of young talent to grow rapidly and distinguish themselves.

Today, the vitality nurtured by this fertile ground for talent shines especially brilliantly in the emerging field of unmanned aerial vehicles. At the event, the flight test team presented to reporters multiple UAV types including the "Yunying (Cloud Shadow)."

Speaking of the "Yunying" UAV that soared through the sky during the CCTV Spring Festival Gala, Zhao Chuangxin, head of the UAV research and development department at AVIC Chengdu Aircraft, recalled: "When 'Yunying' was approved as a program, the team's average age was only 28. A group of like-minded young people spent four years honing their craft, sending an innovative concept into the blue sky."

Nie Yonggang, who has been deeply engaged at AVIC Chengdu Institute for more than 20 years, remarked with feeling: "In fact, as early as 1996, AVIC Chengdu Institute began laying out UAV research. At that time, the chief designers of several aircraft types led us young people in fighting shoulder to shoulder on the front lines of research, laying a solid foundation for today's accumulated strength."

Entering the exhibition hall of the AVIC Chengdu Institute research building, a set of conceptual aircraft designs conceived by young aviation workers in an "Innovative Design Competition" draws attention. These conceptual designs, though still at a forward-looking stage, carry the ambition of a new generation of aviation workers to "define the future."

The young steed has already begun to gallop—the future is surely full of promise!

An immersive experience of the combat aircraft's formidable presence. (Li Sixuan)

走进中国航空工业集团国企开放日活动—— 铁翼凌霄汉 淬剑向苍穹 ■解放军报记者 戚辰飞 清水河畔旌旗展，铁翼凌云啸长空。前不久，在成都西郊这片“航空报国”标语高悬的热土之上，中国航空工业集团国企开放日活动盛大启幕。 歼-5甲、歼-10、歼-20……一架架战机从这里横空出世，成为守护祖国空天的钢铁脊梁。漫步这片充满现代化气息的园区，每一处细节都烙印着航空人自力更生、勇攀高峰的坚实足迹，书写着一代代航空赤子将忠诚与智慧熔铸成国之重器、托举战鹰翱翔九天的恢宏篇章。 航空工业成都飞机工业主题公园。余 翔摄 清水河畔奠基业 自力更生铸就战鹰“摇篮” 走进航空工业成都飞机工业（集团）有限责任公司（以下简称“航空工业成飞”）的历史文化大道，一幅创业画卷在记者面前徐徐展开。 “一块砖、一锹土，手拉肩扛……”解说员的声音，将我们带回到60多年前的黄田坝。 1958年10月18日，成都西郊清水河畔，来自五湖四海的航空建设者齐聚于此。在简朴而热烈的氛围中，航空工业成飞前身，国营132厂正式破土动工。此刻，也许无人能预见，这座从稻田和茅草堆里起步的工厂，将崛起为中国航空工业的脊梁，孕育出一系列震慑寰宇的空中利剑。 驻足文化大道，当年的艰辛透过展品扑面而来。“艰苦奋斗、勤俭建国、发奋图强、自力更生”——刷在厂门口的16个大字，成为他们当时最真实的创业报国心声。 夜宿四面透风的简易工棚，最困难时，除了定量的一块蒸饭，就只有一小撮用油炒了的盐巴当菜吃……在这样的条件下，132厂的建设速度堪称奇迹：6年之后的冬天，工厂基本建设全面通过国家验收。 画卷中，与132厂国家验收鉴定书并列的，还有一份军工产品定型证书——1964年，我国首款自行测绘设计试制的全天候截击机歼-5甲一飞冲天，填补了我军全天候作战能力的空白。 歼-5甲的诞生，是自力更生的壮歌。年轻的主任设计师屠基达带领团队，仅凭圆规、三角尺、钢皮尺手工测绘、反复推算，对每个零部件进行反求设计与验证。支撑他们的，是“为祖国争口气”的信念。首飞当日，当那架银白色的战机滑出跑道、昂首冲天，在场的工人、技术员无不热泪盈眶。“自力更生、无私奉献”的品格，如同种子，深植于黄田坝的沃土。 当黄田坝的厂房机器轰鸣，共和国的航空梦想继续向西延伸。1970年5月，一列载着300多名科研人员的专列驶入成都，在武侯祠旁的原空军028航校简陋校舍扎下根来，这便是被誉为“战斗机设计摇篮”的航空工业成都飞机设计研究所（以下简称“航空工业成都所”）的前身——“歼-9大队”。他们同样躬耕于这片热土，开启了新的征程。 虽然歼-9战机因指标过高最终未能飞上蓝天，但其开创性探索的“鸭式气动布局”理论和“拦射武器系统”，却如同埋下的金种子，为日后的歼-10战机奠定了至关重要的技术基石。 黄田坝的一锹土，武侯祠旁的一间屋，航空工业成飞与航空工业成都所，这对扎根西蜀的“兄弟厂所”，在昔日的荒芜之地，用汗水和智慧，共同扎下了支撑共和国空中力量腾飞的坚实根脉。 核心技术破坚冰 先锋标准引领装备自强 航空工业成飞数控智能车间展厅里，一件泛着冷光的“S形试件”引人注目。这看似简约的金属块，是衡量战机关键结构件制造精度的“标尺”。 上世纪90年代，航空工业成飞引入先进数控机床制造复杂航空结构件时，发现沿用多年的国际检测标准存在局限。 面对这一世界性难题，航空工业成飞团队没有退缩。他们不断提取、分析机床加工出现的问题点，经过反复论证、测试，终于在1999年设计出一种能满足机床精度测试和评价标准的新试件——“S形试件”。专业验证表明，它有效保障了复杂结构件的精密制造。 2020年，“S形试件”正式成为国际标准，这是我国在机床精度检测领域的首项国际标准，被誉为“中国龙”。 如果“S形试件”是为机床立下“准绳”，那研制歼-10战机上的S型蒙皮，则是为战机插上翅膀的“关键一役”。这是气动性能的核心，制造难度极大。 彼时，航空工业成飞缺乏相关经验与设备。寻求外方合作时，对方断言：“凭你们的设备和技术，绝无可能！”这句话深深刺痛了成飞团队。“一定要争这口气！”他们联合北京航空航天大学，在旧设备上大胆改造，自主攻关。 试制现场，气氛凝重。力小则成形不足，力大则蒙皮报废，甚至损毁设备。蒙皮毛料就位，设备缓缓启动……当那道完美的S形曲线最终精准呈现，雷鸣般的掌声瞬间爆发！ 这样的“从零开始”，航空工业成飞人经历了太多次。 “核心技术是要不来、买不来、讨不来的，唯有自主研发，才能真正挺直腰杆！”“全国五一劳动奖章”获得者、高级工程师聂海平对此信念坚定。他的工作，关乎战机复合材料机翼的“筋骨”——自动铺丝技术。 最初，聂海平操作进口设备时连参数都不能调整，“责任担不起”的告诫如芒在背。面对进口拿不到许可证、核心技术被层层封锁的困境，聂海平下定决心：自主研发，从零起步！ 没有现成算法，就自己推导；没有工艺经验，就扎进车间向老师傅讨教。那段日子，聂海平办公室的白板上，密密麻麻全是公式和架构图。无数个日夜的公式推演、架构设计和工艺摸索……2020年9月，他们终于搭建出完全自主、更贴合国内生产实际的铺丝软件架构。现场测试成功的那一刻，实验室被欢呼点燃。连最初提出质疑的老工艺师也由衷赞叹：“现在这套软件，实用高效，可靠顺手！” 回望来路，从受制于人的锥心之痛，到振翅长空的先进战机，再到“称手”的国产软件，航空工业成飞人在一次次“从零开始”的冲锋中，用铁一般的事实证明：核心技术自主创新的每一次突破，都是挺直脊梁、制胜未来的关键一跃！ 薪火相传育栋梁 人才沃土孕育新锐之师 在航空工业成飞厂区的中央草坪上，一架威武的歼-10战机静静伫立。 它不仅是我国航空工业的骄傲，更是一座无声的丰碑，铭刻着中国航空人薪火相传的核心理念——“型号成功我成才”。这句源自歼-10研制历程的肺腑之言，早已成为航空工业成飞与航空工业成都所航空人共同的成长信条。 这份人才传承的远见，可以追溯到歼-10项目的起点。1986年，歼-10飞机正式立项，时年56岁的航空工业成都所副所长宋文骢被任命为总师。 当时，面对“年龄不饶人，这架飞机能否在你手里最终定型”的关切，宋文骢笑着答道：“能不能在我手里定型，我说了不算，但有一点可以肯定的是，通过这架飞机的研制，中国一大批现代飞机设计研制的人才肯定会成长起来！” 历史，以歼-10的辉煌历程，印证了这位总师的深邃眼光。18载春秋，歼-10项目如同一个巨大的熔炉，作为主设计与主制造单位的航空工业成都所和航空工业成飞，不仅铸就了国之重器，更淬炼锻造出一大批技术中坚力量。 王海峰院士便是其中的一员。作为宋文骢招收的首位研究生，他至今铭记导师“不拘一格育人才”的胸怀和作风。在讨论新发动机协调方案时，面对“改造旧机”还是“另起炉灶”的重大抉择，宋文骢力邀年轻人在资深前辈面前直抒己见，并最终拍板采纳了年轻人提出的创新方案。这种对青年才俊的信任与放手，是点燃创新火种的关键。 宋文骢对后辈的殷切期许，不仅流淌在工作点滴，更融入血脉情怀。“老马信步小众山，青骥奋蹄白云端。深幽崎峻通天路，同心无畏任我攀。”在航空工业成都所宋文骢院士纪念展厅橱窗内，这幅笔力遒劲的诗作引起了记者的注意。 据讲解员介绍，2004年“五四”青年节，已74岁高龄的宋文骢应航空工业青年之邀，共同攀登成都东郊的龙泉山。登顶远眺，壮怀激烈，宋老挥毫写就这首诗，既是对过往披荆斩棘奋斗历程的凝练总结，更饱含他对航空新生代展翅高飞的殷切期许。 这份托举青年、薪火相传的接力棒，被王海峰等新一代领军者稳稳接住。近年来，航空工业成都所一大批85后、90后航空青年科技骨干迅速成长，在关键技术岗位上勇挑大梁。 几年前，一位刚入职航空工业成都所机电部的博士，在一次团队攻关中成功计算出了一个项目的关键性能。机电部领导直接推荐其向时任航空工业成都所总师王海峰汇报。 入职未满3个月的新人，即可直面总师和院士——在航空工业成都所，不论资历深浅，只要技术过硬、思路清晰，就能在专业领域担当重任，并获得充分支持。这种唯才是举的氛围，让一代代青年才俊得以快速成长、崭露头角。 如今，这方人才沃土孕育的生机，在新兴的无人机领域尤为璀璨。活动现场，试飞团队向记者展示了“云影”等多型无人机。 提及在央视春晚上，凌空展翅的“云影”无人机，航空工业成飞无人机研发部门负责人赵创新回忆道：“‘云影’立项之时，团队平均年龄只有28岁。一群志同道合的年轻人四年磨一剑，将一个创新构想送上了蓝天。” 已在航空工业成都所深耕20余年的聂勇刚感慨道：“其实早在1996年，航空工业成都所便开始布局无人机研究。当年，几个型号的总师带领我们这批年轻人并肩奋战在攻关一线，为今日的厚积薄发打下了坚实根基。” 走进航空工业成都所科研大楼展厅，一组由青年航空人在“创新设计大赛”中构思的概念机型引人注目。这些概念机型，虽尚属前瞻阶段，却承载着新一代航空人“定义未来”的雄心壮志。 青骥已奋蹄，未来定可期！
