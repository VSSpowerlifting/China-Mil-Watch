Political Work

A love that traveled a thousand li, carried on the border winds and held within the shared vigil of two people

千里奔赴的深情，落进边关的风里也落进两个人共同的守候里
PLA Daily (解放军报) 25 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A PLA Air Force radar station operator named Xia Yuhang, stationed at over 4,000 meters elevation in southern Tibet (Shannan area), receives a visit from his wife Ling Ziyan, a civilian bank employee from Ziyang, Sichuan, who traveled more than 2,000 kilometers by plane, car, and military vehicle to reach him around the Dragon Boat Festival. This is standard political work content — a human-interest narrative published in official military media to valorize the sacrifices of border garrison families and reinforce the concept of 戍边 (guarding the border) as a shared civilian-military commitment. Its value is as a record of how the PLA frames spousal loyalty and frontier hardship for a general audience, not as evidence of unit activity or changed posture at the garrison.
Translation

Where Wind and Snow Watch Over Each Other

■ Liao Jilong, Zhu Haitao; illustrated by Chen Lei

The morning light of southern Tibet spills over the rolling snow peaks, and the wind, carrying a thin chill, sweeps across the garrison of an Air Force unit. Ling Ziyan steps down from the vehicle and, in an instant, spots Xia Yuhang in the crowd — he has been waiting for a long time. In that moment, more than 2,000 kilometers of longing finally settles, solid and still.

Ling Ziyan is a bank employee in Ziyang, Sichuan, living a steady, ordinary life. Her husband, Xia Yuhang, is stationed at a radar station at an altitude of more than 4,000 meters, keeping company with wind and snow year-round. Most of the time, they can only see each other through a phone screen.

A video call just before the Dragon Boat Festival planted in Ling Ziyan's mind the idea of going up to the plateau. On the screen, her husband said that duty assignments had been heavy lately and that, once again, he would not be able to come home. His tone was calm; his face carried the weariness of someone who had just come off watch. Ling Ziyan ended the call and sat on the sofa for a long time. A thought gradually took shape: "If he can't come back, then I'll go to him."

The journey west was harder than she had imagined. She flew from Chengdu to Lhasa, then transferred to a car, and from Shannan City boarded a military vehicle — more than 300 kilometers that took six hours to cover. At first the scenery outside the window was still green; gradually the trees gave way to low-lying alpine meadows, and finally even the meadows disappeared, leaving only bare rock and yellow earth. The mountain road wound upward in loop after loop. Above 4,000 meters, Ling Ziyan began to feel her temples throbbing, her chest as though weighted by a stone, every breath requiring deliberate effort. As they crossed a mountain pass, the driver caught her pale face in the rearview mirror and stopped the vehicle to let her rest.

Ling Ziyan got out of the car. The mountain wind hit her full in the face, cold enough to make her shudder. It was the height of summer, yet the wind carried pellets of snow that stung her cheeks. She pulled her jacket tight and leaned against the car door, closing her eyes to picture his face — that face roughened by the plateau sun, lips perpetually cracked. He had traveled this road back and forth for years, every leave, every return to the unit, enduring this same jolting journey. Yet he had never said a word about how hard the road was. Every time, on the phone, he only said, "Everything's fine" and "I'm used to it."

The vehicle finally drove into the garrison. The officers and soldiers stood in neat formation, and their loud, repeated cries of "Welcome, sister-in-law!" blew away all the fatigue she had carried on the road. Xia Yuhang came striding toward her, holding a bouquet of wildflowers. He stood before Ling Ziyan, looked at the limp oxygen bag, and his eyes immediately reddened: "More than 2,000 kilometers, all that way — you've worked hard."

Ling Ziyan took the flowers and smiled: "I walked the road you walk every day, I saw the snow mountains you see every day. Consider it standing watch alongside you once — it doesn't feel so hard anymore."

A comrade nearby jumped in: "Sister-in-law, the platoon leader keeps your photo under his pillow every night!" Xia Yuhang immediately cut him off, saying quietly, "Don't talk nonsense." But the corners of his eyes could not hide his smile. In that moment, Ling Ziyan understood all the more that warm, unspoken longing of Xia Yuhang's — the kind he was never good at putting into words.

Only when she actually settled in did Ling Ziyan truly feel the weight of the two characters "guarding the border" (戍边). The temperature here is low year-round; the wind cuts at the face like a knife; the air is so thin that even walking quickly leaves one gasping. Patrolling the mountains, standing watch, and making emergency equipment repairs are the daily routine for Xia Yuhang and his comrades. Once, she rode along with a supply vehicle to the radar station. A comrade traveling with her pointed to a steep slope outside the window and said: "Sister-in-law, last winter the road was blocked by heavy snow and supplies couldn't get through. Your Lao Xia led a team down the mountain and, pack after pack on his back, trip after trip, he got every bit of those supplies up here. By the time he reached the camp, his hands were so frozen he couldn't hold a pair of chopsticks." Ling Ziyan listened, her gaze falling on that snow-covered slope, and she stared at it for a long time.

Inside the cramped radar van, equipment hummed steadily. Xia Yuhang stared at the screen without moving. Ling Ziyan waited for him from a distance and suddenly realized: every time they video-called, Xia Yuhang was always smiling cheerfully. All those days, he had been here like this — keeping watch over the equipment, keeping watch over the border line — quietly tucking his longing for home into the wind and snow.

During the days his wife was visiting the unit, Xia Yuhang trained as usual and maintained combat readiness as usual. Ling Ziyan was not idle either. She washed her husband's clothes, prepared hot meals, and tidied the small room in the family quarters until everything was in order. In her spare time, she joined the soldiers in breaking ground on an open plot beside the garrison. The earth was frozen solid; a single swing of the pickaxe left only a white mark, and it took several people taking turns for half a day to turn over even a small patch. She scattered grass seeds she had brought from home, then crouched beside the plot and stared at it for a long while, hoping: if only something green could truly grow on this plateau, how wonderful that would be.

After one duty shift ended, Xia Yuhang shed his gear and sat down with Ling Ziyan in the courtyard, arm in arm. The plateau night was so low it seemed you could reach up and touch the stars. The Milky Way poured down from overhead, spreading across the entire snowfield. Ling Ziyan tilted her head back and gazed in silence, her heart soft. Xia Yuhang turned to look at her and said quietly: "Before, I could only show you this sky through my phone, and I always worried the photos couldn't do it justice. Every time I looked at this Milky Way, I couldn't help thinking of you. Now, finally, I can watch the stars with you. I feel very happy — very complete."

The mountains and rivers lay still; the river of stars kept them company. The bitter cold of the border seemed to recede into the distance, leaving only the calm and quiet of two people together.

Time spent together always passes too quickly. On the evening before her departure, Xia Yuhang took Ling Ziyan up to a high point to look out over the mountains and rivers he guards day and night. Cradled among the snow peaks, a lake lay quietly, its surface dyed pale gold by the setting sun. Xia Yuhang said that everyone calls it "Acacia Lake" (相思湖) — the officers and soldiers, stationed on the plateau year-round and unable to return home, come and sit by the lake when they miss home, telling their troubles to the water. Over the years, the lake has come to seem as though it holds all the longing of the entire garrison, bearing witness, year after year, to the steadfast vigil of generation after generation of border guards.

Early the next morning, the two walked side by side along the road. The distant mountains stood majestic; a sea of clouds surged and rolled; the morning light edged the snow peaks in a soft glow.

"Ziyan," Xia Yuhang called to her softly, "you came all this way, thousands of li — do you feel it was unfair to you?"

Ling Ziyan leaned against his shoulder, gazing at the unbroken line of snow mountains, her voice gentle but certain: "Unfair? You said you would guard our country's border; I said I would always be your support. Neither of us broke our word. A reunion like this — it's worth it."

Xia Yuhang tightened his grip on her hand, his palm rough but warm: "Next time I'm on leave, I'll go back to your hometown with you and make up for all the time together I owe you."

"I'll wait for you."

The morning light was clear; the snow mountains were silent; the mountains and rivers bore witness. This love that traveled a thousand li was carried on the border winds — and held within the shared vigil of two people.

风雪相望处 ■廖继龙 朱海涛 陈 磊绘 藏南的晨光漫过连绵雪峰，风裹着稀薄的凉意掠过空军某部营区。凌子晏走下车，一眼就在人群里望见了等候多时的夏雨航——那一刻，攒了2000多公里的思念，终于稳稳落了地。 凌子晏是四川资阳一家银行的职员，守着一方安稳的烟火日常。她的丈夫夏雨航驻守在海拔4000多米的雷达站，常年与风雪为伴。大多数时候，他们只能隔着手机屏幕相见。 端午节前的一次视频通话，让凌子晏心里产生了上高原的念头。屏幕里，丈夫说最近值守任务重，这次又回不了家了。他的语气很平静，脸上带着刚下哨的倦意。凌子晏挂了电话，在沙发上坐了许久，一个念头渐渐清晰：“他回不来，那我就过去。” 西行之路，比想象中更难。从成都飞到拉萨，再换乘汽车，到山南市坐上军车，300多公里的路程走了6个小时。起初窗外还有绿意，渐渐地，树木变成贴地的高山草甸，最后连草甸也消失了，只剩下裸露的岩石和黄土。盘山公路一圈一圈往上绕，海拔过了4000米，凌子晏开始觉得太阳穴“突突”地跳，胸口像压了块石头，每一口气都要用力才能吸进去。翻过一个垭口时，司机从后视镜里见她脸色发白，停下车让她缓缓。 凌子晏下了车，山风迎面扑来，冷得人一激灵。明明已是盛夏，风里竟裹着雪粒，打在脸上生疼。她裹紧外套靠在车门上，闭眼想他的模样——那张被高原日光晒得粗糙的脸，嘴唇常年裂着口子。他在这条路上来来回回走了好些年，每次休假、归队，都要经历这番颠簸。可他从没说过这条路有多难走，每次只在电话里说“都挺好”“习惯了”。 车子终于驶进营区。官兵整齐列队，一声声响亮的“欢迎嫂子”，把她一路的疲惫都吹散了。夏雨航手捧一束野花快步迎上来。他站在凌子晏面前，看着干瘪的氧气袋，眼眶一下就红了：“2000多公里，一路奔波，辛苦你了。” 凌子晏接过花，笑了：“走过你天天走的路，看过你天天看的雪山，就当陪你站了一次岗，感觉也没那么辛苦了。” 一旁的战友抢着说：“嫂子，排长天天把你的相片放在枕头底下！”夏雨航立即打断了他，低声道：“别乱说。”他的眼角却藏不住笑意。那一刻凌子晏更加懂了夏雨航那份不善表达却暖融融的惦念。 真正住下来，凌子晏才切身体会到“戍边”二字的重量。这里常年低温，风刮在脸上像刀子，氧气稀薄得连走快了都喘。巡山、值守、抢修设备，是夏雨航和战友们的日常。有一回，她跟着给养车去雷达站。同行的战友指着窗外一道陡坡说：“嫂子，去年冬天大雪封路，物资上不来。你家老夏带人下了山，背着背囊一趟又一趟，硬是把物资给弄上来了。到了营地，他那双手冻得筷子都拿不住。”凌子晏听着，目光落在那道被雪覆盖的山坡上，盯着看了很久。 雷达方舱狭小的空间里设备嗡嗡作响，夏雨航盯着屏幕一动不动。凌子晏远远地等他，忽然想到：每次视频通话，夏雨航总是笑呵呵的。那么多日子，他就这样守着设备，守着边境线，把对家的思念悄悄藏进风雪里。 妻子来队探亲的那些天，夏雨航照常训练，照常战备。凌子晏也没有闲着，她给丈夫洗衣、备好热饭，把家属院的小屋收拾得妥妥帖帖。闲暇时，她跟着战士们去营区边的空地上开荒。土冻得硬邦邦的，一镐下去只留个白印子，几个人轮番刨了半天才翻出一小片。她撒下从家乡带来的草籽，蹲在旁边瞅了半天，心里盼着：这高原上要是真能长出点绿意来，该有多好。 一次执勤结束，夏雨航卸下满身装具，挽着凌子晏的手在院中坐下。高原的夜，低得好像一伸手就能够着星星。银河从头顶倾泻而下，铺满了整片雪原。凌子晏仰头静静看着，心里一片柔软。夏雨航侧过头望向她，轻声说：“以前只能在手机里给你看这片星空，总怕拍不出它的好。每次看到这条银河，我都会忍不住想起你。现在终于能和你一起看星星了，我觉得很幸福、很圆满。” 山河寂静，星河作伴。边关的苦寒仿佛都退远了，只剩两个人的安稳与静好。 相聚的时光总是过得飞快。临别前一天傍晚，夏雨航带凌子晏登上一处高地，眺望他日夜守护的这片山河。雪山环抱之间，一汪湖水静静卧着，水面被夕阳染成淡金色。夏雨航说，大家都叫它“相思湖”——官兵常年驻守高原回不了家，想家的时候就到湖边坐坐，把心事说给湖水听。日子久了，这湖像是装满了整座营区的牵挂，岁岁年年，见证着一代代戍边人的坚守。 第二天一早，两人并肩走在路上。远山巍峨，云海翻涌，晨光给雪峰镶上一层柔光。 “子晏，”夏雨航轻声唤她，“千里迢迢跑这一趟，委屈吗？” 凌子晏靠在他肩头，望着连绵的雪山，声音轻柔却笃定：“委屈什么？你说过，要守好祖国的边关；我也说过，做你永远的后盾。咱俩都没食言，这样的相聚，值得。” 夏雨航握紧她的手，掌心粗糙却温热：“等下次休假，我陪你回老家，把欠你的陪伴都补上。” “我等你。” 晨光澄澈，雪山静默，山河为证。这段千里奔赴的深情，落进了边关的风里，也落进两个人共同的守候里。
