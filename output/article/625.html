The Star Chart Is Spread, the Brush Poised to Write; the Bauhinia Will Bloom in the "China Palace" — A Record of the Shenzhou-23 Astronaut Crew

About this analysis
Summary
Shenzhou-23 launched from Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center at 23:08 Beijing Time on May 24, crewed by mission commander Zhu Yangzhu (third cohort, second spaceflight), spacecraft pilot Zhang Zhiyuan (third cohort, first spaceflight), and payload specialist Lai Jiaying — a Hong Kong Police Force cybersecurity veteran and China's first fourth-cohort astronaut to fly, on a mission that includes at least one crew member completing a one-year-duration stay. The article is a pre-launch crew profile in official military media; its analytical value is as a record of how the institution frames Hong Kong integration and generational astronaut succession, with Lai Jiaying's background and explicit gratitude to 'the motherland' carrying clear political-messaging weight distinct from the operational content.
Translation

The Star Chart Is Spread, the Brush Poised to Write; the Bauhinia Will Bloom in the "China Palace" — A Record of the Shenzhou-23 Astronaut Crew

In early summer along the Ruoshui River, the poplar forests have just turned fresh green. Deep in the heart of the Badain Jaran Desert, the launch tower stands in silence, awaiting a new group of heroes about to set out on their mission.

At 11:00 on May 23, the Shenzhou-23 astronaut crew made their first public appearance at the Wentian Pavilion of the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center —

The crew is commanded by spaceflight engineer Zhu Yangzhu, who is making his second spaceflight and is the first mission commander from China's third cohort of astronauts. Spacecraft pilot Zhang Zhiyuan is also from the third cohort. Payload specialist Lai Jiaying, who comes from Hong Kong, is China's first fourth-cohort astronaut to fly in space; both will be embarking on their first journey to space.

By decision of the mission's general command, China is targeting a launch of the Shenzhou-23 crewed spacecraft at 23:08 Beijing Time on May 24.

The first crew composed of third- and fourth-cohort astronauts; the first fourth-cohort astronaut to fly in space; the first time an astronaut from Hong Kong has flown; the first time a crew member is executing a one-year-duration mission… Each of these "firsts" is like a weighty key, placed into Zhu Yangzhu's hands.

This is the second time Zhu Yangzhu has stood at the Wentian Pavilion to take questions from Chinese and foreign reporters, and the first time he has led a crew into the mission as commander.

In 2020, Zhu Yangzhu — then an associate professor at a university — passed a rigorous selection process to become a member of China's third cohort of astronauts. In 2023, he traveled to space aboard the Shenzhou-16 crewed spacecraft, fulfilling his dream of a first spaceflight.

On July 20 of that year, the Shenzhou-16 crew conducted their first extravehicular activity. Zhu Yangzhu, dressed in an extravehicular spacesuit, stepped out into the depths of space and performed approximately eight hours of high-intensity extravehicular operations, becoming China's first spaceflight engineer to carry out an extravehicular mission.

Two months later, Zhu Yangzhu transformed into a "space teacher," demonstrating alongside his crewmates the spherical flame experiment, the remarkable "ping-pong ball" experiment, the conservation of momentum experiment, and the gyroscope experiment, delivering a spectacular popular science lesson from space for young people.

After returning to Earth, Zhu Yangzhu was awarded the honorary title of "Heroic Astronaut" and the "Third-Class Space Meritorious Service Medal."

"Returning from space is not the end — it is a new beginning." He set aside his honors, reset everything to zero, and threw himself into new training and preparation.

In 2025, China organized its first astronaut cave training exercise, and Zhu Yangzhu was in the first group of participants.

Deep inside a natural cave — dark, damp, and confined — he suggested to his teammates that they briefly switch off their headlamps together.

"The instant the light disappeared, boundless darkness pressed down on us. It felt exactly like an extravehicular excursion in space, with the profound universe all around and only one's heartbeat and breathing to prove one's existence," Zhu Yangzhu said.

After being appointed commander of the Shenzhou-23 crew, he thought not only about how to make himself stronger, but also about how to make three people function as one. Zhu Yangzhu summed it up this way: train coordination, train habits, train tacit understanding — think with one mind and exert effort in one direction; this is "1+1+1=1." Push each person's strengths to their fullest expression so that the collective force far exceeds the simple sum of the individuals; this is "1+1+1>3."

At the beginning of this year, the crew participated in a two-week full-mission flight procedure simulation exercise. Inside a 1:1 scale space station combination test module, the three of them conducted a fully enclosed drill in complete accordance with the rhythm of work in space — eating, sleeping, and working all strictly following on-orbit procedures.

"Whenever someone had work assigned to them, someone else always stepped in to lend a hand; whenever there was an operational detail, someone always offered a timely reminder; during breaks in training, we always encouraged each other," Zhu Yangzhu said. "There is no distinction between 'your work' and 'my work' — only 'our mission.'"

At the press conference, Zhu Yangzhu spoke on behalf of the crew with confidence before Chinese and foreign reporters: "We will absolutely act as one crew, unite heaven and ground as one, exert our efforts in the same direction and sustain them without letup, focus on every task with full readiness and to the highest standard, and accomplish with full success the glorious mission entrusted to us by the Party and the people."

Zhang Zhiyuan was born in 1986 on the banks of the Yellow River. His parents were local teachers — educating students on weekdays and working the fields on weekends.

That year, the Air Force came to his school to recruit pilots. Recalling the roar of fighter jets streaking across the sky during the 1999 National Day parade, Zhang Zhiyuan signed up without hesitation.

Perhaps owing to the foundation built by swimming in the Yellow River and running through the fields since childhood, he sailed through the initial and secondary selection rounds and fulfilled his wish of becoming a flight cadet.

The day of his first flight finally arrived. His instructor took him soaring into the clouds — climbing, diving, rolling. He had no time to savor the romance of flight; his mind was racing to record the key points of every procedure, every set of data.

"The moment I truly experienced the joy of flying was my first solo flight," Zhang Zhiyuan said, tracing the route with his hands, recalling the clouds churning beneath his feet and the mountains and rivers spreading out under his wings. "That vastness, that freedom — it is a romance that belongs exclusively to fighter pilots."

After graduation, he left Changchun in Jilin and headed to the northwest of the motherland. There, he transitioned to new aircraft types, participated in combat-realistic exercises, and carried out diverse missions, accumulating more than 1,200 flight hours.

When news came that China was selecting its third cohort of astronauts, Zhang Zhiyuan signed up without hesitation. Medical examinations, psychological tests, the centrifuge… He passed through one gate after another and entered the astronaut corps.

Aerodynamics, thermodynamics, the design principles of rockets and spacecraft, flight vehicle attitude control… Thick textbook after thick textbook was placed before him. He gritted his teeth and started from scratch — asking instructors and teammates when he didn't understand, consulting references — and forced his way through one "checkpoint" after another.

"In truth, the hardest part was not the study and training," Zhang Zhiyuan paused. "It was how to face not being selected."

From the day one becomes an astronaut, who does not yearn to fly to space as soon as possible? Starting with the Shenzhou-16 mission, astronauts from the same cohort successively realized their dreams of reaching the heavens, yet Zhang Zhiyuan missed the chance to fly time and again.

"Looking back today, at the time I did not have a deep enough understanding of the patterns governing crewed spaceflight missions," he said.

After years of aerial flight, Zhang Zhiyuan was accustomed to completing all maneuvers in the shortest time possible. But crewed spaceflight is different — it places greater emphasis on steadiness, on precision, on absolute certainty.

"For example, in underwater training, the heavy underwater suit constrains you, and if you simply pursue speed, your physical strength is depleted very quickly," he said.

When he learned he had been selected for the Shenzhou-23 crew, Zhang Zhiyuan felt both excited and calm in that moment: "I will absolutely cherish this rare opportunity to fly, temper my will, and deepen myself."

The Yellow River — the place of his birth — holds the hometown he has long been separated from, and the parents he holds dear.

Born, schooled, employed, married, and raising a child — Lai Jiaying's life had always revolved around Hong Kong. It was not until 2024 that she embarked on one of the rare long journeys of her life.

"I miss the road at the entrance to my home, lined with blooming bauhinia flowers." During breaks in training, she often thought of home — of that small path she had walked countless times.

Lai Jiaying was born in November, precisely the season when bauhinia flowers bloom. The bauhinia just beginning to open along the roadside became woven from that point on into the fabric of her life.

In 2001, she was admitted to the computer science program at the University of Hong Kong. After graduation, she joined the Technology Services Division of the Hong Kong Police Force, specializing in electronic forensics and cybersecurity.

In one case, critical surveillance footage had been almost entirely destroyed. She and her colleagues worked day and night, restoring the data frame by frame and segment by segment, ultimately recovering the erased truth and bringing the suspect to justice.

"The police force tempered my will and cultivated in me the qualities of perseverance, fearlessness in the face of difficulty, and careful attentiveness," Lai Jiaying said candidly. That experience, she noted, planted the earliest seeds that would one day make her a qualified astronaut.

When she first arrived on the mainland, language was the first barrier. Her Mandarin, accented at first, was halting and stumbling; with the company of instructors and teammates, she practiced word by word and sentence by sentence, improving day by day, and gradually felt the warmth of a family within the collective.

Pressurization training in the intravehicular spacesuit once put her through considerable hardship. "During training, the intravehicular spacesuit has to be pressurized, and before you have mastered the technique, your ears are extremely uncomfortable," Lai Jiaying said.

Surviving six days and five nights in a dark, narrow cave; trekking through jungle teeming with snakes and insects… Every item was a dual extreme test of body and mind.

"No matter how difficult something is, it will 'reach its end.' My heart is full of gratitude and a sense of honor, and I feel deeply the glory of the mission and the weight of the responsibility," she said. She expressed sincere thanks to the motherland for providing astronauts with a career platform and a stage on which to realize their dreams, and also heartfelt thanks to the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region government and her fellow Hong Kong citizens for their wholehearted support.

She wants to look through the porthole at the prosperous and thriving motherland, to see the outline of her hometown, to see whether the stars truly "arrange themselves into a river"… Lai Jiaying is filled with anticipation for the space journey that lies ahead. During her on-orbit stay, she will be primarily responsible for space science experiments and daily life support tasks.

"I will give everything I have, shoulder the mission of a spacefarer, live up to the expectations of Hong Kong citizens, and even more so live up to the trust placed in me by the motherland," Lai Jiaying said. Flying to space is not only a personal dream — it is also a vivid testament to Hong Kong and the motherland moving forward with one heart and in one direction. (Li Guoli, Zhan Kang, Liu Yi, Huang Yichen)

Original Chinese
星图已展待起笔 紫荆将绽“中国宫”——记神舟二十三号航天员乘组 初夏的弱水河畔，胡杨林刚换新绿。巴丹吉林沙漠腹地，发射塔架静静矗立，等待着新一批即将出征的英雄。 5月23日11时，神舟二十三号航天员乘组在酒泉卫星发射中心问天阁首次公开亮相—— 指令长由航天飞行工程师朱杨柱担任，二次飞天的他是我国第三批航天员中首个指令长。航天驾驶员张志远是第三批航天员，来自香港的载荷专家黎家盈是我国首位飞天的第四批航天员，二人都将迎来首次太空之旅。 经任务总指挥部研究决定，我国瞄准北京时间5月24日23时08分发射神舟二十三号载人飞船。 首个由第三、四批航天员组成的乘组，首位飞天的第四批航天员，首次迎来香港的航天员，首次有人执行一年期飞行任务……一个个“首次”，像一串沉甸甸的钥匙，交到了朱杨柱手中。 这是朱杨柱第二次站在问天阁接受中外记者提问，也是他第一次以指令长的身份率队出征。 2020年，还是大学副教授的朱杨柱，经过严格选拔，成为我国第三批航天员。2023年，他搭乘神舟十六号载人飞船抵达太空，首次飞天梦圆。 那年7月20日，神十六乘组实施首次出舱活动。朱杨柱穿着舱外航天服步入深邃太空，进行约8小时高强度出舱作业，成为我国首位执行出舱任务的航天飞行工程师。 两个月后，朱杨柱又变身“太空教师”，和队友们演示了球形火焰实验、奇妙“乒乓球”实验、动量守恒实验以及又见陀螺实验，为青少年们带来一场精彩的太空科普课。 返回地球后，朱杨柱被授予“英雄航天员”荣誉称号和“三级航天功勋奖章”。 “从太空归来不是终点，而是新的起点。”他收起荣誉，将一切归零，投入新的训练备战。 2025年，我国组织首次航天员洞穴训练，朱杨柱是第一组参训队员。 在黑暗、潮湿、幽闭的天然洞穴深处，他向队友提议，一起短暂关闭头灯。 “光亮消失的那一瞬间，无边无际的黑暗压下来。感觉像极了在太空出舱，周围是深邃的宇宙，只有心跳与呼吸证明存在。”朱杨柱说。 被任命为神舟二十三号乘组指令长后，他不仅想着自己怎么更强，还琢磨三个人如何更像一个人。朱杨柱总结道，练配合、练习惯、练默契，心往一处想、劲往一处使，这叫“1+1+1=1”；把每个人的长处发挥到极致，让整体的力量远超个体的简单相加，这叫“1+1+1＞3”。 今年初，乘组参加为期两周的全任务飞行程序模拟训练。在1:1的空间站组合体试验舱内，三个人完全按照太空工作节奏进行全封闭演练，吃饭、睡觉、工作都严格遵循在轨流程。 “分配的工作，总有人主动搭把手；操作的细节，总有人及时提个醒；训练的间隙，总会彼此鼓把劲。”朱杨柱说，“没有‘你的活’‘我的活’之分，只有‘我们的任务’。” 见面会上，朱杨柱代表乘组向中外记者自信表态：“我们一定乘组一心、天地一心，同向发力、持久用力，以满格的状态、顶格的标准，专注于每一项工作，圆满完成党和人民赋予的光荣使命。” 1986年，张志远出生在黄河边。父母是当地教师，平时教书育人，周末下地劳作。 那年，空军来校招飞行员。想起1999年国庆阅兵时战机划破长空的轰鸣，张志远立即报了名。 或许是从小在黄河里凫水、田地间奔跑打下的底子，他初选、复选一路绿灯，如愿成为一名飞行学员。 飞行的那一天终于来了。教员带着他冲上云端，跃升、俯冲、横滚。他没有时间感受飞行的浪漫，大脑飞速记录着每一个程序的要领、每一组数据。 “真正体验到飞行的快乐，是第一次放单飞。”张志远用手比划着当年的航路，回忆着云层在脚下翻涌，山川在机翼下铺展开去，“那种辽阔，那种自由，是独属于战斗机飞行员的浪漫”。 毕业后，他离开吉林长春去往祖国西北。在那里，他改装新机型、参加实战化演练、遂行多样化任务，累计飞行时长超过1200小时。 我国选拔第三批航天员的消息传来，张志远毫不犹豫报名。医学检查、心理测验、离心机……他一关一关闯过来，走进了航天员大队。 空气动力学、热力学、火箭和飞船的设计原理、飞行器姿态控制……一本本厚厚的教材摆在面前，他咬着牙从头开始，不懂就问教员、问队友、查资料，硬是攻克了一个个“关卡”。 “其实，最艰难的不是学习训练。”张志远顿了顿，“而是如何面对落选。” 成为航天员的那一天起，谁不盼着早日飞天？自神舟十六号任务起，同批航天员陆续圆梦苍穹，张志远却一次次与飞天梦想擦肩而过。 “今天回过头看，当时的我对载人航天任务的规律理解不够深刻。”他说。 常年空中飞行，张志远习惯在尽可能短的时间里完成所有动作。但载人航天不同，更强调稳，强调准，强调万无一失。 “比如在水下训练中，厚重的水下服限制着你，如果一味求快，体力会消耗得很快。”他说。 得知入选神舟二十三号航天员乘组的消息，那一刻，张志远内心激动而平静：“我一定珍惜这次难得的飞行机会，磨练意志、沉淀自己。” 黄河，他出生的地方，那里有他分别许久的故乡，有他牵挂着的爹娘。 出生、念书、工作、结婚、生子，黎家盈的生活半径一直在香港。直到2024年，她才开启人生中少有的一次远行。 “想念家门口的那条路，开满了紫荆花。”训练间隙，她常常想家，想起那条走过无数遍的小路。 黎家盈出生在11月，恰是紫荆花开的时节。路旁初绽的紫荆花，从此织入她生命的脉络。 2001年，她考入香港大学计算机科学专业，毕业后加入香港警务处技术服务部，专攻电子取证与网络安全。 有一宗案件，关键监控录像损毁殆尽。她与同事不舍昼夜，逐帧逐段修复数据，最终还原了被抹去的真相，将嫌疑人绳之以法。 “警队磨砺了我的意志，培养了我坚持不懈、不惧困难、细心谨慎的品质。”黎家盈坦言，这段经历，为日后成为一名合格的航天员埋下了最早的伏笔。 初到内地，语言是第一道关。带着口音的普通话，起初磕磕绊绊，在教员和队友的陪伴下，她一字一句练，一天一天进步，也在集体中渐渐感受到家的温暖。 舱内服加压训练，曾让她吃尽苦头。“训练时，要对舱内航天服进行加压，在还没掌握要领的时候，耳朵里会非常难受。”黎家盈说。 幽暗狭窄的洞穴中生存六天五夜，蛇虫出没的丛林里穿越跋涉……每一项都是对生理与心理的双重极限碾压。 “再艰难的事情，也是会‘到点’的。我的内心充满感恩与荣幸，也深感使命光荣、责任重大。”她说，衷心感谢祖国为航天员提供的事业平台与圆梦舞台，也由衷感谢香港特区政府和同胞们给予的全力支持。 想透过舷窗看看繁荣昌盛的祖国，看看家乡的轮廓，看看星星是不是真的会“排成河”……对于即将到来的太空之旅，黎家盈充满期待。在轨驻留期间，她主要负责空间科学实验和生活照料等任务。 “我会全力以赴，肩负起航天人的使命，不辜负香港市民的期望，更不辜负祖国的重托。”黎家盈说，飞天不仅是个人梦想，更是香港与祖国同心同向的生动见证。（李国利、占康、刘艺、黄一宸）
