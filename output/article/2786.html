A Confession Beneath the Galaxy — Getting Close to Wang Ji, Researcher at the College of Computer Science, National University of Defense Technology

银河下的告白——走近国防科技大学计算机学院研究员王戟
PLA Daily (解放军报) 3 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Wang Ji, a researcher at NUDT's College of Computer Science and a member of the institution's 'Galaxy' (银河) supercomputer lineage, has worked through two serious illnesses — a 2013 surgical crisis requiring more than ten operations and a 2021 uremia diagnosis requiring dialysis — while continuing to contribute to Tianhe supercomputer development and supervise graduate students from a wheelchair. His wife Huang Chun, chief designer of the Tianhe compiler system, worked from his hospital bedside during his first illness and took their daughter to Tianjin for Tianhe integrated testing during the second. The article is a PLA Daily profile in the institutional hagiography genre; its value is as a record of how NUDT frames the 'Galaxy spirit' (银河精神) — sacrifice, collective loyalty, and continuity across three generations of researchers — for a junior military-academic audience, not as evidence of a specific technical or programmatic development.

A Confession Beneath the Galaxy — Getting Close to Wang Ji, Researcher at the College of Computer Science, National University of Defense Technology (Part II)

■ PLA Daily Correspondents Wang Tonghua, Zhang Lin, Wang Tianyi; Special Correspondent Xu Xin

Wang Ji attending a working discussion meeting. Photo by PLA Daily correspondent Luan Cheng.

Above, there is a Milky Way — countless stars forming a river, stretching across the vault of heaven, brilliant for ten thousand years.

Below, there is a "Galaxy" (银河) — on the banks of the Xiang River, at the National University of Defense Technology (NUDT), the "Galaxy" team has lit a beam of Eastern starlight on the global map of computing power.

In one lifetime, how many roads must a person walk before encountering a river that belongs to them?

Wang Ji, researcher at NUDT's College of Computer Science, was not yet 14 years old when he first encountered this "Galaxy." The young boy plunged headlong into it and became part of "Galaxy." From that moment on, his eyes were filled with nothing but the scattered starlight of the Galaxy.

Who would he meet? What would he most want to say? Beneath the Galaxy, every flicker of starlight seems to be his heartfelt confession.

From a Single Beam of Light to a River —

"I Am a Star in the Galaxy; the Galaxy Spirit Illuminates Me"

In the early hours of that morning in 2013, Wang Ji collapsed from illness. Those who came to visit him had prepared a stomach full of comforting words, but standing at his bedside, no one could get them out — because Wang Ji smiled first. Moving his fingers, he said: "My brain and my hands aren't broken. As long as I can type on a keyboard, I'm fine."

The seed of resilience had long been planted in his heart. In that moment, the past came flooding back.

The year Wang Ji walked through the gates of NUDT, he was not yet 14. That same year, the "Galaxy-I" (银河-Ⅰ) supercomputer burst onto the scene, making China the third country capable of independently designing and manufacturing a supercomputer.

During his graduate studies, his advisor Chen Huowang would always tell him stories about the "Galaxy" team. "Sitting on an old sofa at home, my advisor would talk while tracing the shape of punch cards in the air with his hands." At the time, Wang Ji was just over 20. His advisor's words fell into his heart one by one, like nails driven into wood.

Later, Wang Ji witnessed too many scenes like this: researchers staying up until dawn over a single line of parameters, arguing until their faces turned red over a single bug, then patting each other on the shoulder the next morning and saying "let's go again"; people collapsing from exhaustion in the computer room. He listened to the sound of keyboards merging into a continuous roar, like rain — "rain" that fell night after night — as the speed of China's supercomputers leaped from "hundreds of millions of operations" to "hundreds of trillions of operations."

Inside "Tianhe Tower" (天河楼) hangs a bronze plaque engraved with a sentence: "The Galaxy is connected to Zhongnanhai." This is also the voice of Wang Ji and his colleagues — every breakthrough made here carries the earnest expectations of the Party and the nation.

In 2008, Wang Ji's advisor Chen Huowang fell critically ill and kept calling Wang Ji's name in his final days. At the time, southern China was in the grip of an ice and snow disaster; flights were grounded and trains suspended. Wang Ji, away on a business trip, went to extraordinary lengths to make it back to Changsha and saw his advisor one last time.

The moment they met, his advisor asked: "Is the research project progressing smoothly?" In that instant, Wang Ji understood — for a "Galaxy person," even when breathing stops, the cause does not.

Here, certain sounds and certain images have diffused into the air and long since become part of the very act of breathing. Ci Yungui, the chief designer of "Galaxy-I," already past sixty, made a vow: "Even if it costs me this old life, I will get China's supercomputer built!" In the terminal stage of a fatal illness, Jian Xianfu continued working from his hospital bed; at 43, he passed away in the ward after writing the supercomputer's standard subroutines. Stricken by disease, Wang Yumin said not a word; at 41, he collapsed with a test pen still in his hand. Running a high fever that would not break, Yu Wulong held his post; at 36, he kept working for five days and five nights through illness before closing his eyes forever.

"The reason the Galaxy is brilliant is that every star is shining." Wang Ji said that after spending so long immersed in the "Galaxy" team, without anyone having to teach you, you know what must be relinquished and what must be held fast.

Two years after his illness, Wang Ji returned to his post. As the car drove into the campus and the three characters "Galaxy Building" (银河楼) struck his eyes, he sat in the car for a long time, composing himself. The scenes that had appeared countless times in his dreams became, in that moment, impossibly vivid.

During the time he was away, the "Galaxy people" had not stopped. In June 2013, Tianhe-2 was successfully developed; thereafter, the college won multiple National Science and Technology Progress Awards, and the team he belonged to also received a Second Prize National Teaching Achievement Award. "Even though I was temporarily off the track, I could always hear my comrades' running footsteps," he said.

During that period, colleague Chen Zhenbang led the charge on a national key laboratory project, taking the team through round after round of testing and parameter tuning, hitting every project milestone without fail. Colleague Chen Liqian led a team with an average age under 30, throwing themselves into a cutting-edge technology direction. They wrote code and built prototypes day and night. At the busiest times, the team was simultaneously pushing forward several major projects, and some people set up cots in the computer room.

A documentary left Wang Ji with an unforgettable image — a horse trapped in a swamp, impossible to pull out no matter what. A farmer brought a herd of horses to run circles around it; the trapped horse struggled and clawed its way out through the vibrations of its companions' galloping, and finally broke free.

"I was that horse," Wang Ji said. "After falling ill, the reason I was able to pull through was that the team was there beside me. It's like fighting in the same trench — everyone is moving forward, and everyone is also holding you up as you move forward."

After Wang Ji returned, his colleagues discussed whether to hold a welcome ceremony, but ultimately dropped the idea. "Because the best welcome ceremony is to produce worthy results and share them with him!"

Chen Zhenbang recalled that on that day in the conference room, colleagues competed to report on their project progress, and Wang Ji smiled with particular joy, as if he had never left.

Rejoining the ranks, setting out again. From then on, one more sleepless light was added to the night.

In countless late nights, gazing at the lights of "Galaxy Building" and "Tianhe Tower," Wang Ji thought of his advisor, his predecessors, and the many comrades who had marched forward at his side, and felt that he was not alone: "I am a star in the Galaxy; the Galaxy spirit (银河精神) illuminates me, and I too must strive to emit my own light."

From One Person to One Family —

"Eyes Fixed Forward, Walking Forward Together"

Wang Ji's wife Huang Chun had never imagined that signing her name could feel so heavy.

In the deep of that night in early 2013, the lights in the hospital corridor were a ghastly white, and the surgical consent form was dense with text. The doctor stood across from her, explaining the risks — "a mortality rate as high as 20%."

She gripped the pen, her fingertips ice-cold. "The tip of the pen pressed against the paper, but I couldn't summon any strength; my hand was shaking badly."

Back home, her daughter, just over two years old, was sleeping soundly. She sat on the edge of the bed, staring into the boundless darkness outside the window, thinking of her paralyzed father-in-law and her critically ill husband, and tears came flooding out.

That night, this "Tianhe" woman warrior (天河女将) forcefully wiped away her tears and told herself: "Let the grief come freely, but let it pass quickly. This family cannot fall."

From that point on, she never cried again. Over six months, more than ten surgeries, dozens of critical-condition notifications. Every risk disclosure form was signed by Huang Chun; her hand went from trembling to steady.

When Wang Ji came to, the first thing he saw was Huang Chun's gentle smile as she leaned over him. "Wang Ji, you're awake…" she called softly, as if he had merely slept a long sleep.

Wang Ji did not know how many cold notification forms his wife had signed during that time, how many sleepless nights she had endured on the long benches in the hospital corridor. All he saw was her warm smile.

Later, someone asked Huang Chun how she had gotten through those days. She smiled and said: "The difficulties were only temporary. My responsibility was to take good care of the family and do my work well."

Huang Chun — a core member of the "Tianhe" team and chief designer of the compiler system. Through those grueling days, she set up her laptop at her husband's bedside, keeping watch over Wang Ji while writing code.

After Wang Ji woke from the intensive care unit, bandages still wrapped around him, a laptop was already propped up at his bedside. The two of them each occupied a corner of the hospital room, heads down over their respective tasks, occasionally looking up to exchange a glance. There was no breakdown of "what do we do now," only the companionship of "thank goodness you're here." Through the posture of standing side by side, she told him: we are still on the same front line.

Wang Ji would sometimes stop and look at her from across the room. Huang Chun, head bent over her keyboard, her profile bleached white by the screen's glow, brow slightly furrowed. She was there, and he felt completely at ease.

Three months after his illness, Liu Guizhi — who had been at home caring for her paralyzed husband — called Wang Ji for the first time. She gripped the receiver tightly, took a deep breath, and said: "Son! There are plenty of people who are physically disabled but not in spirit! If you can't face this, people can only sigh and say, 'What a pity about that child.' If you can face it, everyone will reach out a hand and give you a push!"

"Mom, I'll do as you say." On the other end of the line, Wang Ji's voice was the same as always.

"If you listen to your mother, your mother can rest easy." She put down the phone and sat for a long time, tears streaming down her face. She knew that the breath of resolve in her son's heart had been drawn up.

Later, someone asked her where she found such confidence. Liu Guizhi said: "As long as his mind is intact, he will definitely stand back up." But she knew in her heart that her son's survival had not depended on him alone. That night, school leaders rushed to the hospital and earnestly told the doctors: "Save him at any cost!"

"Wang Ji's life was saved by the organization." She said this to everyone. "If the school and the hospital had not done everything in their power to treat him, my son would have been gone long ago."

"The only thing you can do is to do your work well. However you repay the organization, it is only right." His mother's words were carved into Wang Ji's heart from that moment on.

In 2021, Wang Ji was diagnosed with uremia and needed to go to Nanjing for treatment. At the time, the new-generation Tianhe supercomputer was entering its final critical push, and Huang Chun needed to go to Tianjin for integrated testing. She hesitated; before she could say a word, Wang Ji said: "Go without worry. I'll be fine."

Huang Chun arranged a leave of absence for their daughter, who was in primary school, and brought her along. Every day she worked from early morning until deep into the night; their daughter would sit alone in the Tianhe computer room doing homework and reading, and when she saw that everyone was too busy, she would proactively hand over screwdrivers and stick on labels. Everyone called her "Tianhe's little tail."

Over those two months, every time Wang Ji called, he said "I'm doing fine." Later, Huang Chun learned that on several occasions his blood pressure had dropped too low after dialysis, and he had to lie in bed for a long time before he could sit up.

This is the deepest unspoken understanding of this family: never let your own hardship become the other person's burden.

Colleagues said this couple truly had big hearts — "their hearts are filled entirely with big things." Wang Ji smiled and said: "People with big hearts are blessed."

In 1997, Wang Ji and Huang Chun took half a day off to go to the civil affairs bureau to register their marriage, then went back to work in the afternoon and continued working overtime in the evening. No wedding, no honeymoon. The couple felt that "the way to love your family is to do the nation's work well together." One month later, "Galaxy-III" (银河-Ⅲ) officially passed its acceptance review. Huang Chun later said that was their most precious wedding gift.

What is family? Wang Ji said: "Family means eyes fixed forward, walking forward together."

Today, Wang Ji sits and Huang Chun pushes; the silhouettes of the two of them are woven into the scenery of the campus and into the hearts of many more people. The morning light sometimes stretches the outlines of the wheelchair and its occupant very long — one tall, one low — yet always pointing in the same direction.

From a Pair of Hands to Countless Hearts —

"This World Is Beautiful; I Am Not Afraid to Go Out and Travel Far"

Professor Wang Ting's car trunk has a space permanently reserved in it, enough to hold a folding wheelchair.

Every morning, Wang Ting drives around to the foot of Wang Ji's building. Wang Ting gets out first, opens the passenger door, then helps Wang Ji into the car. At the end of the workday he drives him back, watches Wang Ji wheel himself into the elevator, and only then turns to leave.

Eight years. Nearly 3,000 mornings and evenings.

Someone asked Wang Ting how he had kept it up. His eyes grew moist: "Wang Ji has a kind of force about him — once you get close, you don't want to leave."

Around Wang Ji, there is more than one "Wang Ting."

After Wang Ji was hospitalized, colleagues spontaneously drew up a duty roster, taking turns delivering meals, providing care, and looking after his elderly parents. Some came straight to the hospital after a night shift; some brought homemade soup in insulated containers; some sat with Wang Ji's mother and talked, staying for an entire afternoon.

That duty roster is still posted on the cabinet by his front door. The paper has long since yellowed and curled at the edges, but every name and every time slot remains legible. Every time he passes it, Wang Ji's gaze lingers briefly. That single glance says more than a thousand words.

The night neighbor Liang Yan took Wang Ji's daughter away was the life-or-death moment of Wang Ji's surgery. The child, two years and seven months old, cried and fussed without stopping; Liang Yan held her tightly in her arms and sat through the entire night.

She cared for the child for a full year. When Liu Guizhi tried to give her a caretaking fee, Liang Yan held back tears and said: "Grandmother, if it were for money, I would not have taken this child, because the responsibility is too great. I cannot bear to watch such an outstanding family collapse because of this misfortune. Please never bring up money again." After that, Liang Yan's family also took over cooking meals for the elderly woman, and came monthly to cut hair, change light bulbs, and handle plumbing and electrical repairs.

"In five years, two major upheavals in the family were nothing short of a catastrophic blow to me… and the selfless dedication of Liang Yan's family let me feel the true warmth of humanity." Liu Guizhi wrote sincerely in a nomination letter; Liang Yan was consequently recognized as a "Changsha Good Person · Neighborhood Lei Feng."

On a snowy night in 2017, physician Yu Fang received a call from Wang Ji. The amputation wound had become red, swollen, and bleeding from prolonged pressure while sitting. After examining the wound, Yu Fang scolded Wang Ji for not taking care of himself. Wang Ji smiled faintly: "I have to work."

A person without both legs — not only working, but also traveling on business trips. In all her years working in orthopedics, Yu Fang had never seen anything like it. She silently and carefully debrided the wound, then proactively came to his home multiple times afterward to change the dressings: "I can't explain why — I just felt I had to come."

The first time community Party secretary Zhang Jinhua stepped into Liu Guizhi's home, the pile of instant noodles in the kitchen stung her. "From now on, one phone call and the food arrives!" For ten years without exception, hot meals have been delivered on time. The staff's words were plain and warm: "Your son and daughter-in-law are soldiers defending the homeland. They charge to the front; we take care of you — that is how we guard the rear."

Fate had taken some things away, yet at the deepest valley of his life, it let Wang Ji see the most brilliant light of the human constellation — the goodwill of those who, asking nothing in return, still bent down to draw close to him at his lowest point.

From a pair of hands to countless hearts, this goodwill continued to extend. During his treatment at the Eastern Theater Command General Hospital (东部战区总医院), Wang Ji worked whenever he could find a gap between dialysis sessions. The medical staff urged him to rest, but no one could persuade him. "Don't worry — it will get better." Faced with anxious medical personnel, he instead smiled and reassured them.

After Wang Ji was discharged, staff from the Eastern Theater Command General Hospital specifically sought out the school and insisted on writing a report about him: "We have treated many patients, but a person like Wang Ji is someone you encounter perhaps once in a lifetime. He taught us that a person's body can be confined by illness, but the spirit cannot."

This is a scene often visible at Changsha Huanghua Airport — Wang Ji, seated in his wheelchair, traveling on business trips just as before. That figure left a deep impression on airport worker Wang Dan: "Especially his smile — it is particularly healing."

"This world is beautiful; I am not afraid to go out and travel far." Wang Ji said that over the years he has encountered too many warm-hearted people who proactively lent a hand. "Without everyone's help, I could not manage on my own. My ability to continue taking on assignments after falling ill is in fact the trust of the organization and of everyone around me. My ability to travel to various places on business trips may look like one person going alone, but in reality it is the support of a collective, of a society."

From a Single Pen to a Blazing Fire —

"Every Step You Take Is Paving the Road for Those Who Come After"

What kind of teacher is a good teacher? Wang Ji answered without hesitation: a teacher like his advisor Chen Huowang.

From his advisor Chen Huowang's hands, Wang Ji received the mantle. He carried on his advisor's "whiteboard teaching method" (白板教学法): one pen, one whiteboard, guiding students to derive and prove step by step.

One weekend, a master's student complained in a group chat about the difficulty of the Mathematical Logic course. Wang Ji saw it and immediately replied: "Anyone who wants to learn — come to the small conference room."

That day, Wang Ji sat before the whiteboard, one hand braced on the wheelchair armrest to steady himself, the other hand sweeping the pen across the board to derive formulas. For a full two hours, dense formulas climbed across the whiteboard like vines; he did not take a single sip of water, and the students watched with their mouths agape.

"Writing by hand exposes gaps in thinking."

At the weekly academic discussions, students write on the board and explain; he sits in his wheelchair, pressing and guiding with questions, often leaving students feeling they have "shed a layer of skin" — yet also having "grown a backbone."

On one occasion, doctoral student Liu Xingpeng argued with Wang Ji for over an hour about a point in his thesis, and neither could convince the other. Wang Ji sighed: "How can you not see this — you're even more Yu Shiwen than Yu Shiwen." Yu Shiwen was Liu Xingpeng's senior fellow student, famously stubborn. Having said it, Wang Ji laughed first.

After the argument, it was time for a meal. Wang Ji sat in his wheelchair; Liu Xingpeng pushed him to the cafeteria. The two of them chatted idly, as if nothing had happened. "When we argue, neither gives an inch; when it's over, he's still my teacher and I'm still his student, and we eat our meal." Liu Xingpeng said with a smile.

Inheritance is not mere replication — it also requires collision. "Through collision, they grow faster." The gaze Wang Ji turns on his students is full of hope: "I want them to grow up as quickly as possible, to take over as quickly as possible."

Professor Liu Wanwei was one of Wang Ji's most outstanding students in those years. During his master's studies, Wang Ji noticed Liu Wanwei's talent in mathematics and directly pushed him onto the podium for the graduate problem-solving class. The large classroom was packed; Liu Wanwei walked to the podium with trepidation, but the more he taught, the steadier he became, and the better he got.

In his day, Wang Ji too had been pushed onto the podium by his advisor.

At a 1992 international academic symposium, advisor Chen Huowang pushed the fledgling Wang Ji onto an international stage to deliver a report in English. His palms were sweating with nerves. "You can do it — speak boldly!" His advisor's trust was an anchor. When the applause broke out, a Danish scholar remarked: "Huowang always wants to push his students to the very forefront."

Today, Wang Ji has become that kind of advisor himself, pushing his students one by one toward a broader world. Wang Ji encourages students to go out and attend conferences, broaden their horizons, and provides as many academic opportunities as possible. Student Wang Peijie said with longing that if she could one day stay in the research group and work with Professor Wang, it would be a very happy thing.

Today, Liu Wanwei's students have also stepped onto the podium. His doctoral student Yi Xin graduated and stayed at the school, and has grown into a backbone of the team. The way Liu Wanwei mentors Yi Xin is unmistakably a "replica" of Wang Ji — deriving at the whiteboard, pressing with questions, starting over.

One pen, one whiteboard, three generations of teachers and students, passing on the same rigor and passion, flame to flame.

As graduation approached, student Zhou Jingwen was anxious to publish papers and entertained the idea of "padding the numbers first and lowering the standard." When Wang Ji found out, he said only one thing: "Scholarship is a lifelong matter. When you one day have results that can stand on their own, and you look back at this paper written just to pad the count, you will feel it is not fit to be shown."

From that point on, this sentence was like a needle, puncturing every impulse Zhou Jingwen had to take the easy way out.

In July of this year, the teaching team Zhou Jingwen belongs to successively won the Grand Prize at the National Digital-Intelligent Education Innovation Competition (全国数智教育创新大赛) and the Grand Prize for Outstanding Teaching Courseware at the National Higher Education Computer Curriculum Conference (全国高等学校计算机课程教育大会). The course their team presented in competition, Introduction to University Computing (大学计算机基础), is the MOOC course Wang Ji originally created. That course was the one Wang Ji, after lying unconscious for days and finally waking, still kept thinking about — the course that "cannot stop."

After winning the award, Wang Ji sent his congratulations at the first opportunity. In that moment, Zhou Jingwen suddenly understood: "No matter how far I go, the teacher is always watching."

Wang Ji still has more than ten master's and doctoral students under his supervision. Whenever he is not receiving treatment, Wang Ji is always in the office. "Online is not the same as face to face. I want to be closer to the students, closer to 'Galaxy.'" That figure in the wheelchair has become a reassuring lighthouse for the teaching and research office.

In the memory of student Zhang Zhihang, Professor Wang Ji keeps his phone in the most visible spot on his desk at all times — not to check the time, but to wait for messages from students. "Whether it's late at night or on a weekend, as long as a student sends a question, he replies almost instantly." When students asked him why he responds so quickly, he said: "The fact that you are willing to ask me shows you trust me. If I don't answer right away, I might hold up your thinking."

One weekend, student Cai Yali walked out of the laboratory late at night and saw that the light in the adjacent office was still on: "Professor Wang was sitting in his wheelchair, body leaning slightly forward, fingers flying across the keyboard, the screen filled with dense lines of code."

"Professor Wang doesn't stand on the bank teaching swimming — he jumps into the water and paddles alongside the students." During one project push, Wang Ji worked overtime until the early hours for several consecutive days. Yet at the next day's group meeting, he was still full of energy, listening to every student's report. After the meeting, one student quietly said: "Professor, please get some rest." He waved it off and said: "You're all charging forward — I can't stop. The Galaxy spirit (银河精神) is not something you shout; it's something you do."

One person, a group of people, several generations of people. Light is passed on this way — never the burning of a single person, but one igniting another, one illuminating another.

When the reporter was conducting interviews, a new cohort of doctoral dissertation defenses happened to be underway, and Wang Ji, serving as a reviewer, was in back-to-back sessions nearly every day. During those days, no fatigue showed on his face — instead, there was excitement and gratification.

The way those young people defended their dissertations looked very much like the person who had once handed them the pen. In this moment, Wang Ji especially wanted to send them one sentence: "You have grown faster than I imagined. Every step you take is paving the road for those who come after."

Wheeling through the campus, looking at one young face after another, Wang Ji's heart is often stirred by a particular sense of mission and happiness — "the belonging of a drop of water merging into a great river, and the call of the wave ahead to the wave behind."

Above, the Milky Way flows on through the ages; below, the "Galaxy" surges ever forward.

"Every star is striving to shine; every beam of light is quietly passing on the torch." This silent brilliance is the deep and enduring confession of the "Galaxy people" and "Tianhe people" to their country, to their era, and to one another.

银河下的告白 ——走近国防科技大学计算机学院研究员王戟（下） ■解放军报记者 王通化 张 琳 王天益 特约记者 许 鑫 王戟参加工作讨论会议。解放军报记者 栾铖 摄 天上有条银河——众星成河、横亘苍穹，璀璨千万年。 地上有条“银河”——湘江之畔、国防科大，这里的“银河”团队，在全球算力版图上亮起一束东方的星光。 人这一生，要走过多少路，才能遇见一条属于自己的河？ 国防科技大学计算机学院研究员王戟，最初遇见这条“银河”时，不满14岁。彼时的少年纵身跃入其间，成为“银河”的一部分，从此他双眼盛满的，都是银河的点点星光。 他会遇见谁？最想说什么？银河之下，每一次星光闪烁，都仿佛是他在深情告白。 从一束光到一条河—— “我是银河一颗星，银河精神照耀着我” 2013年那个凌晨，王戟病倒了。去探望他的人备好一肚子安慰话，到了病床前谁也说不出口——因为王戟先笑了，他活动着手指说：“我的脑袋和手没有坏，只要能敲键盘就行。” 坚强的种子，早已种在心间。那一刻，往事历历在目。 走进国防科技大学校门那年，王戟不满14岁。那一年，“银河-Ⅰ”巨型计算机横空出世，中国成为第三个能独立设计制造巨型计算机的国家。 读研究生时，导师陈火旺总给他讲“银河”团队的故事。“坐在家里的旧沙发上，导师一边说话，一边用手在空中比画着打孔卡片的形状。”那时，王戟20岁出头。导师的话一字一句落进他心里，像钉子钻进木头。 后来，王戟见过太多这样的画面：科研人员为一行参数熬到天亮，为一个漏洞争得面红耳赤，第二天清早又拍着肩膀说“再来”；有人累得晕倒在机房里……他听键盘声连成一片，像下雨，“雨”下了一夜又一夜，中国巨型机的速度从“亿次”跳到“亿亿次”。 “天河楼”里有一块铜牌，上面刻着一句话：“银河连着中南海。”这也是王戟和同事们的心声——这里的每一次突破，都承载着党和国家的殷切期望。 2008年，导师陈火旺病危，临终之际还念叨着王戟。当时正赶上南方冰雪灾害，航班停飞、火车停运。出差在外的王戟费尽周折赶回长沙，见了导师最后一面。 一见面，导师问：“课题进展得顺利吗？”那一刻，王戟明白了——对“银河人”来说，即使呼吸停了，事业也不会停。 在这里，有些声音、有些画面，弥散在空气中，早已成为呼吸的一部分。年过花甲的“银河-Ⅰ”总设计师慈云桂立下誓言：“就是豁出这条老命，也要把我国的巨型机搞出来！”绝症晚期，蹇贤福仍在病床上坚持工作，43岁的他在病房写下巨型机标准子程序后离世；病魔侵袭，王育民一声不吭，41岁的他倒下时手中还握着测试笔；高烧不退，俞午龙仍坚守岗位，36岁的他带病坚持工作5天5夜后永远闭上了双眼…… “银河之所以璀璨，是因为每一颗星都在发光。”王戟说，在“银河”团队里泡久了，不必任何人教，就知道什么该舍弃、什么该攥紧。 病后两年，王戟重返岗位。车子驶入校园，“银河楼”3个字撞进眼底，他在车里缓了很久。那些梦里出现过无数次的景物，这一刻变得无比清晰。 他不在的那段日子，“银河人”没有停下脚步。2013年6月，天河二号研制成功，此后，学院接连获得多项国家科技进步奖，他所在团队也获得国家级教学成果二等奖。“虽然暂时不在跑道上，但我始终能听见战友奔跑的脚步声。”他说。 那段时间，同事陈振邦牵头攻关一个国家级重点实验室项目，带着团队一遍遍跑测试、调参数，项目节点一个没落。同事陈立前带着一支平均年龄不到30岁的团队，扑在一个前沿技术方向上。他们没日没夜地写代码、搭原型。最忙的时候，团队同时推进好几个重大项目，有人在机房里铺了行军床…… 一部纪录片，让王戟难忘——一匹马陷入沼泽，怎么也拽不上来。农夫牵来一群马围着跑，陷进去的那匹在同伴奔跑的震动中挣扎、攀爬，最终脱困。 “我就是那匹马。”王戟说，“生病后，我之所以能够走出来，是因为有团队在身边。就像一个战壕里打仗一样，大家都在往前走，也都扶着你往前走。” 王戟回来后，同事们商量要不要搞一个欢迎仪式，最终还是打消了这个念头。“因为最好的欢迎仪式，就是拿出像样的成果和他一起分享！” 陈振邦说，那天在会议室，同事们争相说着课题进展，王戟笑得特别开心，好像从没离开过。 归队之后再出发。从此，不眠的灯光又多了一盏。 无数个深夜，凝望“银河楼”、“天河楼”的灯光，王戟想起自己的导师、前辈以及许多与自己并肩前行的战友，感到自己并不孤单：“我是银河一颗星，银河精神照耀着我，我也要努力发出自己的光。” 从一个人到一家人—— “一心往前看，一起往前走” 妻子黄春从没有想过，签字会那么沉重。 2013年初那个深夜，医院走廊的灯光惨白，手术同意书上的字密密麻麻。医生站在对面，交代着风险——“死亡率高达20%”。 她捏着那支签字笔，指尖冰凉，“笔尖抵在纸面上，却怎么也使不上劲，手抖得厉害”。 回到家，两岁多的女儿睡得正甜。她坐在床边，望着窗外无边的黑暗，想到瘫痪的公公、病危的丈夫，泪水汹涌而出。 那个夜晚，这位“天河”女将用力擦去泪水，对自己说：“让悲伤尽情来吧，但要尽快过去。这个家，不能倒。” 从那以后，她再也没哭过。半年间，十多次手术，几十次病危通知。每一张风险单，都是黄春签的，她的手从颤抖到沉稳。 王戟醒来，第一眼看见的，是黄春俯身时温婉的笑容。“王戟，你醒了……”她轻声唤着，仿佛他只是睡了个长觉。 王戟不知道，那段时间，妻子签了多少张冰冷的告知书，在医院的走廊长椅上熬过多少个不眠之夜。他看到的，只有她温暖的笑容。 后来有人问黄春，那段日子是怎么熬过来的？她笑着说：“困难只是一时的。我的责任就是把家庭照顾好，把工作做好。” 黄春，“天河”团队核心骨干，编译系统主任设计师。那些艰难的日子，她在丈夫病床前支起电脑，一边守着王戟，一边敲代码。 从重症监护室醒来后，王戟身上还缠着纱布，电脑已经支在了床前。两个人各占病房一角，埋头各自的任务，偶尔抬头交换一个眼神。没有“怎么办”的崩溃，只有“幸亏有你”的陪伴。她用并肩的姿态告诉他：我们还在同一条战线上。 王戟有时候会停下来，隔着半个病房的距离看她。黄春埋头敲着键盘，侧脸被屏幕的光映得发白，眉头微微皱着。她在那里，他就觉得无比安心。 病后3个月，一直在家照顾瘫痪老伴的刘桂枝，第一次跟王戟通电话。她紧紧握住话筒，深吸一口气说：“儿子！身残志不残的人，多了去啦！你要是不能面对，别人只能叹息一声：‘这孩子可惜了’；你要是能面对，大家都会伸出手，推你一把！” “妈，我听你的。”电话那头，王戟的声音一如往常。 “听妈的，妈就放心了。”放下电话，刘桂枝久久坐着，泪流满面。她知道，儿子心里那口气，提起来了。 后来有人问她，哪来那么大信心？刘桂枝说：“只要他脑子没坏，就一定能站起来。”可她心里清楚，儿子能活下来，靠的不只是他自己。那个深夜，学校领导赶到医院恳切地对医生说：“不惜一切代价抢救他！” “王戟的命是组织救回来的。”她对每一个人说，“如果没有学校和医院全力救治，我儿子早就没了。” “你唯一能做的，就是把工作做好。怎么报答组织，都是应该的。”母亲这句话，从此刻进王戟的心里。 2021年，王戟查出尿毒症要去南京治疗。彼时“天河”新一代超级计算机进入最后攻关，黄春要去天津联调。她犹豫，话还没出口，王戟就说：“放心去，我可以。” 黄春给正在上小学的女儿办了休学带在身边，每天从早忙到深夜，女儿便一个人在天河机房里写作业、看书，看大家忙不过来，主动递螺丝刀、贴标签，大家都叫她“天河小尾巴”。 两个月里，王戟每次打电话都说“挺好的”。后来，黄春才听说，有好几次他透析完血压太低，躺在病床上缓了好久才能坐起来。 这，就是这个家最深的默契：不把自己的难处变成对方的负担。 同事们说，这两口子真是心大，“心里装的全是大事”。王戟笑着说：“心大的人，有福。” 1997年，王戟和黄春请了半天假去民政局领结婚证，下午继续上班，晚上继续加班。没有婚礼，没有蜜月……两口子觉得，“爱家的方式，就是一起把国家的事干好”。一个月后，“银河-Ⅲ”正式通过验收。黄春后来说，那是他们最珍贵的结婚礼物。 什么是家人？王戟说：“家人就是一心往前看，一起往前走。” 如今，王戟坐着、黄春推着，两个人的身影嵌进校园的风景、嵌进更多人的心底。晨光有时候把轮椅和人的轮廓拉得很长，一高一低，却始终朝着同一个方向。 从一双手到无数颗心—— “这个世界很美好，我不怕出门远行” 教授王挺的汽车后备箱，常年留出一个位置，确保能放下一辆折叠轮椅。 每天早晨，王挺都会开车绕到王戟家楼下。王挺先下车，拉开副驾驶的门，然后协助王戟上车。下班再送回来，看着王戟摇着轮椅进了电梯，王挺才转身离开。 8年，将近3000个清晨与黄昏。 有人问王挺怎么坚持下来的，他湿了眼眶：“王戟身上有一种力量，让人靠近了就不想走。” 王戟身边，不止一个“王挺”。 王戟住院后，同事们自发排了值班表，轮流送饭、陪护、照顾他年迈的父母……有人下了夜班直接往医院赶，有人把自家炖好的汤装在保温桶里拎过去，有人陪着王戟母亲说话，一坐就是一下午。 那张值班表至今还贴在他家门口的柜子上，纸页早已泛黄卷边，但每一个名字、每一个时间段，依然清晰。每次经过，王戟的目光总会短暂停留。那一眼，胜过万语千言。 邻居梁妍接走王戟女儿的那晚，正是王戟做手术的生死关头。两岁七个月的孩子哭闹不止，梁妍紧紧把她搂在怀中，硬是坐着熬过了一整夜。 一照顾，就是一年。刘桂枝要给她照顾费，梁妍噙着眼泪说：“奶奶，如果为了钱，我是不会带这个孩子的，因为责任太大了！我不忍心看到你们这么一个优秀的家庭，因为这次出事垮下去。以后千万不要再提钱的事……”此后，梁妍一家更包揽了老人的饭菜，每月上门理发、换灯泡、修水电。 “5年间，家庭的两次重大变故，对我来说无疑是灭顶之灾……而梁妍一家的无私付出，让我感受到人间真情。”刘桂枝在推荐信中真诚地写道，梁妍因此获评“长沙好人·身边雷锋”。 2017年的一个雪夜，医生俞芳接到王戟的电话。截肢的伤口因久坐挤压，红肿出血。检查完伤口，俞芳责怪王戟不爱惜自己。王戟淡淡一笑：“我得工作呀。” 一个没有双腿的人，不仅工作，还要出差，俞芳在骨科工作这么多年，从没见过。她沉默着仔细清创，此后多次主动登门换药：“说不清为什么，就觉得必须来。” 社区书记张金华第一次踏入刘桂枝家，厨房堆积的方便面刺痛了她。“以后，一个电话，饭就到！”十年如一日，热饭热菜准时送达。工作人员的话朴实又暖心：“您儿子儿媳是保家卫国的军人，他们冲在前面，我们照顾好您，就是守好大后方！” 命运夺走了一些东西，却让王戟在最深的谷底，看见了人性星河最璀璨的光芒——那些不计得失、不求回报、在他最低处仍弯腰靠近的善意。 从一双手到无数颗心，这份善意不断延续。在东部战区总医院治疗期间，王戟一边透析，一边见缝插针地工作。医护人员劝他休息，可谁也劝不住。“不要担心，会好的。”面对忧心忡忡的医护人员，他反倒笑着安慰。 王戟出院后，东部战区总医院的工作人员特意找到学校，坚持要为他写一篇报道：“我们治疗过的病人很多，但像王戟这样的人，一辈子很难遇到。他教会我们，一个人可以被疾病困住身体，却困不住精神。” 这是长沙黄花机场经常可见的一幕——坐着轮椅的王戟，与以前一样到外地出差。那个身影，给机场工作人员王丹留下深刻印象：“尤其是他的笑容，特别治愈。” “这个世界很美好，我不怕出门远行。”王戟说，这些年，他遇到太多主动搭把手的热心人，“没有大家的帮助，我一个人是不行的。我能够在生病后继续承担任务，实际上是组织和大家的信任；我能够到各个地方出差，看似一个人，实际上是一个集体、一个社会的助力。” 从一支笔到一团火—— “你们的每一步，都是在为后来的人铺路” 什么样的老师是好老师？王戟脱口而出：像导师陈火旺那样的老师。 从导师陈火旺手中，王戟接过了衣钵。他沿袭了导师的“白板教学法”：一支笔、一块白板，让学生一步步推导证明。 一个周末，一名硕士研究生在群里吐槽《数理逻辑》课程难度大。王戟看到，立刻回复：“想学的，来小会议室。” 那天，王戟坐在白板前，一手撑住轮椅扶手稳住身体，一手挥笔推导公式。整整两小时，密密麻麻的公式如藤蔓爬满白板，他一口水未喝，学生们看得目瞪口呆。 “手写，能暴露思维漏洞。” 每周的学术讨论，学生板书讲解，他坐在轮椅上追问、引导，常常让学生“脱一层皮”，却也“长了一身骨”。 有一次，博士刘兴鹏为论文中的一个观点和王戟争了一个多小时，两人谁都说服不了谁。王戟叹了口气：“你怎么就想不明白，你比余诗文还余诗文。”余诗文是刘兴鹏的师兄，出了名的倔。说完，王戟自己先笑了。 争论完，到了饭点。王戟坐在轮椅上，刘兴鹏推着他去食堂，两个人有一搭没一搭地聊着，好像刚才什么都没发生。“争的时候谁都不让，争完了，他还是我的老师，我还是他的学生，该吃饭吃饭。” 刘兴鹏笑着说。 传承，不只是复制，更需要碰撞。“有碰撞，他们才能更快成长。”王戟投向学生的目光，满眼都是希望，“我希望他们尽快地成长、尽快地接班。” 教授刘万伟是当年王戟最得意的学生之一。读研时，王戟发现刘万伟在数学上有天赋，直接把他推上研究生习题课的讲台。大教室坐得满满当当，刘万伟战战兢兢走上讲台，越讲越稳，越讲越好。 当年，王戟也是被导师推上讲台的。 1992年国际学术研讨会，导师陈火旺将初出茅庐的他推上国际讲台作英文报告。他紧张得手心冒汗。“你行的，大胆讲！”导师的信任如定海神针。掌声响起时，一位丹麦学者感叹：“火旺，总想把自己的学生推到最前沿。” 如今，王戟也成了这样的导师，将他的学生，一个个推向更广阔的天地。王戟鼓励学生出去参会、开阔视野，提供尽可能多的学术机会。学生王佩婕憧憬地说，将来如果能留在课题组跟王老师一起工作，是件很幸福的事。 如今，刘万伟的学生也站到了讲台上。他带的博士易昕毕业留校，已成长为团队骨干。刘万伟带易昕的方式，俨然是王戟的“复刻版”——白板前推导、追问、重来。 一支笔，一块白板，三代师徒，将同一种严谨与热忱，薪火相传。 临近毕业那年，学生周竞文着急发论文，动了“先凑数量、降低标准”的念头。王戟知道后，只说了一句：“做学问是一辈子的事。等你将来有了站得住脚的成果，再回看这篇为了凑数而写的论文，会觉得它拿不出手。” 从此，这句话就像一根针，扎破周竞文所有想省力的念头。 今年7月，周竞文所在教学团队连获全国数智教育创新大赛特等奖、全国高等学校计算机课程教育大会优秀教学课件特等奖。他们团队参赛所讲的《大学计算机基础》，正是王戟当年创建的慕课课程。那门课，是王戟昏迷多天醒来后仍惦记着的“不能停”的课。 获奖后，王戟第一时间发来祝贺信息。那一刻，周竞文忽然明白：“无论自己走多远，老师都在看着。” 王戟名下的硕士、博士生至今仍有十多名。只要不接受治疗，王戟总会出现在办公室。“线上不如面对面，我想离学生近点，离‘银河’近点。”那个轮椅上的身影，成了教研室令人心安的灯塔。 在学员张志航的印象里，王戟老师常年把手机放在桌上最显眼的位置，不是看时间，而是等学生的消息。“不管是深夜还是周末，只要学生发来问题，他几乎秒回。”学生问他为什么这么快，他说：“你们愿意问我，说明信任我；我不立刻回答，可能会耽误你们的思考。” 一个周末，学生蔡亚莉走出实验室时已是深夜，她看到隔壁办公室还亮着灯：“王老师正坐在轮椅上，身体微微前倾，手指在键盘上飞快地敲击，屏幕上是密密麻麻的代码……” “王老师不是站在岸边教游泳，而是跳进水里，和学生一起划。”一次项目攻关，王戟连续几天加班到凌晨。可第二天组会，他依然精神饱满地听每个学生汇报。会后，一名学生悄悄说：“老师，您休息一下吧。”他摆摆手，说：“你们都在往前冲，我不能停。银河精神不是喊出来的，而是做出来的。” 一个人，一群人，几代人。光就是这样传下去的——从来不是一个人的燃烧，而是一个点燃一个、一个照亮一个。 记者采访的时候，恰逢新一批博士毕业论文答辩，担任评审的王戟几乎每天都连轴转。那几天，他的脸上看不出疲倦，反倒多了激动和欣慰。 那些年轻人答辩的样子，像极了当年给他们递笔的人。此时此刻，王戟特别想送给他们一句话：“你们成长得比我想象的还要快，你们的每一步，都是在为后来的人铺路。” 摇着轮椅，行进在校园里，望着一张张年轻的面孔，王戟的心间常常涌动着一种特别的使命感和幸福感——“那是滴水汇入大河的归属，也是前浪对后浪的召唤。” 天上的银河，亘古流淌；地上的“银河”，奔涌向前。 “每一颗星都在奋力发光，每一束光都在默默接力。”这无声的璀璨，便是“银河人”“天河人”对祖国、对时代、对彼此，深切恒久的告白。
