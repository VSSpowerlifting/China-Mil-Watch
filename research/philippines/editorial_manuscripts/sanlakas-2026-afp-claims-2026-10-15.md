# Three AFP Reports, One Exercise: What Sanlakas 2026 Establishes

*Indo-Pacific Record — Philippines Desk. Unpublished original IPR editorial manuscript candidate, prepared October 15, 2026. NOT source-approved, rights-cleared, owner-approved, automatically published or admitted to the production archive.*

An interagency exercise can demonstrate that agencies practiced working together. It cannot, by itself, prove how they would perform in a real emergency. That distinction is essential to reading the Armed Forces of the Philippines' accounts of **Pagsasanay Sanlakas 2026**, an exercise it described as involving the military, national police and coast guard from September 30 to October 2.

The AFP's three reports present different stages of what its closing release calls the **second Interagency Exercise (IAX 02-2026)**, led by the Philippine Coast Guard. An account published on October 1 says the exercise formally opened on September 30. An account published on October 2 places a field-training stage at the Humanitarian and Natural Disaster Training Center–National Capital Region in Intramuros, Manila, on October 1. The October 3 closing article places the concluding ceremony at the Eva Macapagal Super Terminal, Pier 15, on October 2. These are **reported event dates**, not the dates the articles appeared online.

The closing account says more than **530 personnel** participated in tabletop and field exercises modeled on a large earthquake, including search and rescue, humanitarian assistance, triage and the evacuation of maritime casualties. That is a substantive description of the scenario the participating institutions practiced. It is not a published performance assessment, a measured improvement in disaster response, or evidence that a future joint operation would succeed. The three AFP articles give useful stage-by-stage detail, but they remain **one originating institution's account**, not three independent AFP, police and coast guard confirmations.

The distinction is also important for a neighboring but separate development. The AFP's September 30 article reports that the **sixth National Joint Peace and Security Coordinating Center meeting** took place the previous day in Malate, Manila. It describes the signing of a National JPSCC Cyber Plan and discussions connected to West Philippine Sea security concerns. The participants overlap with those of Sanlakas, but the coordinating-center meeting was not the exercise's opening or another exercise stage. Its cyber-plan announcement should be assessed as a separate institutional claim, not folded into a single generalized story about interagency readiness.

Two details remain unsettled in the source record. The AFP's opening article has a Camp Aguinaldo dateline and wording that may locate the September 30 ceremony there, while a separately discovered Philippine Coast Guard announcement points to PCG National Headquarters. The precise opening venue therefore should **not** be treated as verified. Separately, two AFP releases spell the coast guard commandant's surname differently, “Galvan” and “Gavan”; this manuscript does not resolve or silently standardize the conflicting source strings. Neither uncertainty needs to be guessed away to explain the reported exercise chronology.

For the Philippines Desk, the value of this material is a bounded account of interagency planning and reported training. The next evidentiary step is to compare the complete archived AFP originals with independently issued coast guard and police accounts, establish any venue correction, and seek after-action assessments that say what the exercise actually revealed. Until then, Sanlakas is evidence of a **reported exercise and its stated activities**, not independently established operational effectiveness.
