The PLA Watch: Carriers, Drones, and the Rocket Force's Manning Problem

Most of this week’s 41 PLA Daily articles belonged to the institution’s regular churn. Three threads cut through it.

First, Liaoning returned to Qingdao after more than 40 days of training in the South China Sea and Western Pacific. The important detail was not simply the length of the deployment. PLA Daily reported that the carrier strike group trained alongside an amphibious assault ship formation. That pairing brings carrier aviation and amphibious power projection into the same far-sea exercise cycle. One report does not prove a mature combined formation, but it puts the concept into the public record.

Second, a Rocket Force brigade tested what it called “extreme reduced-manning operations.” During a live-fire exercise, one operator reportedly performed the work of four crew positions. The claim is narrow and comes from an institutional source, so it should not be treated as proof of force-wide readiness. What it does reveal is the problem the brigade is trying to solve: how a specialized missile unit keeps operating after losing personnel.

Third, the CMC Equipment Development Department and Training Management Department jointly hosted “Bee Shadow 2026,” the PLA’s first military-wide competition for small and micro drones. Sixty-nine teams competed in flying, modification, networking, and management. The sponsorship matters. The PLA is treating drone proficiency as both an equipment problem and a training problem, suggesting that hardware has spread faster than common standards for using it.

Read together, the three reports show a force working on resilience beneath the headline story of modernization. The common problem is less about acquiring new platforms than keeping people, equipment, and networks functioning when operations become difficult.

The clearest signal this week was Liaoning training in the Western Pacific alongside an amphibious assault ship formation. I will be watching for whether that pairing appears again, whether reduced-manning language spreads beyond one Rocket Force brigade, and whether “Bee Shadow 2026” produces wider training guidance.
