One PLA Daily article changed the texture of an otherwise predictable anniversary week.

On June 30, Southern Theater Command said PLA naval and air forces had conducted 战备警巡, or combat readiness patrols, around Huangyan Island, better known as Scarborough Shoal. The command also said it had strengthened those operations throughout June.

The statement was brief. Its wording was not.

战备警巡 places the activity in a combat-readiness category rather than treating it as a routine patrol or scheduled exercise. The announcement also looked backward across an entire month, presenting joint naval and air activity around the shoal as sustained military responsibility rather than a single reaction.

The statement blamed “infringement and provocation” without naming the Philippines. That ambiguity gives Beijing room to connect the operation to Philippine activity while leaving the exact trigger undefined.

PLA Daily is an official outlet, not an independent operational record. The statement does not tell us exactly what happened each day in June. It tells us how Southern Theater Command wanted the month understood. Public language is part of the operation.

Why it matters

Scarborough Shoal sits where China's territorial claims, Philippine resistance, and the U.S.-Philippines alliance meet. China has effectively controlled access to the feature since 2012, while the Philippines regards it as lying within its exclusive economic zone.

Much of Beijing's pressure in the South China Sea is described through coast guard and maritime law-enforcement language. This statement used PLA naval and air forces and called their activity combat readiness patrols in Chinese “territorial waters” and airspace. That does not, by itself, prove a sharp escalation. It does show how Beijing is normalizing a military role around the shoal and how it wants that role described.

What routine looked like

The rest of the week followed the July 1 Party anniversary calendar: model soldiers, Red Army discipline, honor flags, political loyalty, and unit history. Routine does not mean useless. Repetition shows what the institution wants to preserve.

Several articles also exposed practical frictions. A 71st Group Army piece described units training for rankings and year-end commendations rather than combat needs. A 73rd Group Army aviation brigade used internal lectures to teach pilots about neighboring services because formal joint training had not closed basic knowledge gaps. Other reporting returned to promotion corruption, formalism, and Rocket Force cross-training.

These were not dramatic revelations. They were institutional admissions, which can be more useful.

What I am watching next

The main question is whether July brings another formal Scarborough Shoal statement, sharper language, or silence. I am also tracking the Qi Jiguang and Kunlunshan formation after back-to-back diplomatic visits involving Russia and Vietnam, as well as whether the PLA's political rectification coverage shifts from diagnosing problems to declaring them solved.
