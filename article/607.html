An Army Unit Strictly Regulates the "Social Circles" of Leading Cadres

陆军某部从严规范领导干部“交往圈”
PLA Daily (解放军报) 23 May 2026

About this analysis
Summary
At a clean governance (廉政) education session, an unnamed leading cadre at an unidentified Army unit recounted three favor-seeking calls he received during leave—from a former comrade angling to discuss procurement, a cousin seeking preferential treatment in military civilian personnel (军队文职) recruitment, and a construction company manager offering leisure fishing after losing a bid—and described refusing each by citing specific regulations. The article documents how the unit uses 'one-on-one' reminder talks, case-study sessions on 'encirclement and hunting' (围猎), and discipline inspection oversight embedded in procurement and recruitment to operationalize the 'three circles' (三圈) framework—life circle, social circle, entertainment circle—as a behavioral compliance mechanism for cadres. The framing points to continued institutional anxiety that leave periods and informal social contact remain the primary vectors through which outside actors penetrate military procurement and personnel decisions.
Translation

An Army Unit Strictly Regulates the "Social Circles" of Leading Cadres——

During Leave, He Turned Away 3 Favor-Seeking Phone Calls

■ Liang Xiuqiong, He Tao, PLA Daily Reporter Wang Yue

Military Party member cadres do not live in a vacuum. Necessary interpersonal contact is unavoidable, but such contact must be governed by principles and rules, with a "firewall" and "filter" always in place. In this installment, we follow a leading cadre who recently took leave and, through several phone calls he received during that period, examine how Party member leading cadres use regulations and discipline to restrain and pressure themselves at all times—managing their own life circles (生活圈), social circles (交往圈), and entertainment circles (娱乐圈)—and consistently conduct themselves with integrity and clean hands.

—— The Editors

"Be cautious when alone, be cautious in small matters, be cautious at the start, be cautious at the end (慎独慎微，慎始慎终)—these eight characters capture what I genuinely felt during my most recent leave." Recently, at a clean governance (廉政) education session at an Army unit, a leading cadre spoke from personal experience, recounting three favor-seeking phone calls he received during leave and the three "special tests" he faced.

Caller: Old Comrade Liu

"Old comrade, a few of us who have already transferred out of the military want to get together—come join us!" At the very start of his leave, this leading cadre received a call from Old Liu, a comrade he had worked alongside for many years. "It's been a long time since we've all seen each other. You're the only one still in the military now—come out, let's catch up."

He was on leave, and getting together with old comrades of many years seemed entirely reasonable on the surface. But this leading cadre quickly recalled a cautionary education session the unit had organized not long before: a cadre from a sister unit had been drawn into a circle at a similar comrades' reunion, under the guise of "catching up," gradually accepted requests for favors, and ultimately committed irregularities in materiel procurement, for which he was seriously disciplined.

The line from the discipline session—"Comradeship cannot become a gap in discipline"—remained fresh in his memory.

In the course of deepening political rectification (政治整训), the unit to which this leading cadre belongs routinely conducts clean governance training. The unit Party committee secretary and deputy secretary hold "one-on-one" reminder talks with committee members and personnel in key sensitive areas, conducting in-depth analysis of "encirclement and hunting" (围猎) cases that have occurred close to home, so that the red lines of discipline are truly tightened in people's minds.

"Friendship is friendship; discipline is discipline. Our comradeship doesn't need to be sustained by eating and drinking." His tone was calm but firm. "If it's convenient for you, let's go play a round of badminton tomorrow morning—work up a sweat, have a chat. How does that sound?"

The next day, the two men traded shots on the court, their sportswear soaked through with sweat. During a break, Old Liu probed: "Does your unit need to procure any training equipment recently? I have resources and channels on my end…"

Hearing this, the leading cadre waved him off: "Old Liu, if that's what you want to talk about, we stop here today. You know the military's regulations. We're comrades, but personal relationships count for nothing in the face of rules. Don't make me commit a mistake."

Old Liu was silent for a few seconds, then nodded repeatedly: "Understood—we talk only about comradeship, not about business!"

Caller: Older Cousin from Hometown

Early one morning, his older cousin called from their hometown. She got straight to the point: "Since you're on leave and not busy, I have something to ask of you…"

There was a note of urgency in her voice: "Your nephew graduated from university this year. A few days ago he applied for a military civilian personnel (军队文职) position at another unit in your garrison area. Can you put in a word with whoever is in charge of the recruitment, and give him a little 'extra consideration'?"

"That word I cannot put in, and I have no way to put it in. Military civilian personnel recruitment now—from question-setting to written exams, interviews, physical examinations, and political review—is embedded with discipline inspection oversight at every stage, and every step is open and transparent." On the other end of the line, his cousin was full of incomprehension: "You've been in the military all these years—you don't even have that much face to show?"

"This is not a question of face. It is a question of the disciplinary bottom line." The leading cadre's tone was grave. "This year, the discipline inspection committee secretary gave a discipline session to all personnel, specifically emphasizing that leading cadres must not use their authority to seek benefits for relatives. If your nephew truly wants to pass the exam, tell him to study diligently and let his ability speak for itself. That is what is truly responsible—to him, to the military, and to the family."

The line went silent. After a moment, his cousin sighed: "You're right. I was thinking too simply about this. I'll tell him to buckle down and review."

After hanging up, the leading cadre thought back to the clean governance talks the unit had organized at the start of the year, in which Party committee members had each pledged in turn to exercise decision-making authority strictly, genuinely manage their relatives, and resolutely prevent leading cadres from interfering in the normal operation of work.

This ever-present oversight may seem cumbersome, but it is in fact the most solid "firewall" protecting cadres.

Caller: Manager of a Certain Construction Company

Near the end of his leave, the phone rang again. The screen displayed an unfamiliar number.

"Leader, I heard you're on leave. Our company has just opened a new leisure fishing park in the suburbs—you're welcome to come by and relax whenever you have time…" "We know the rules—absolutely no talk of work. We just want to make a friend…" The voice on the phone was unfamiliar but full of eager attentiveness.

After thinking carefully, the leading cadre recalled that the caller was a project manager from a local construction company in the garrison area.

Not long before, that company had participated in the bidding for a small maintenance project at the unit. But the company had ultimately failed to make the shortlist because its quoted price was too high and its proposal too rough.

"During leave—no talk of work!" Seeing that the call was about to be ended, the other party immediately explained: "Leader, you're overthinking it—it's purely for leisure. We won't bring up the contract…"

The manager's attitude remained warm, but the leading cadre's words were equally firm: "To be frank: your company's failure to win the bid last time was not because you lacked acquaintances in the military. It was because your quoted price and proposal genuinely fell short compared to the winning unit."

A typical case reported by a higher authority flashed through the leading cadre's mind: a cadre at a certain unit had been lured in by a contractor under the guise of "leisure fishing," starting with accepting a few cartons of cigarettes and a few bottles of liquor, gradually escalating to accepting cash and interfering in construction projects, and ultimately bringing ruin upon himself.

"All of the military's engineering projects—from budgeting to bidding to acceptance—are fully open and transparent, with discipline inspection oversight throughout. As long as you meet the conditions, there is no need to wine and dine anyone; if you don't meet the conditions, it doesn't matter who you wine and dine!" Without waiting for a reply, he hung up.

"When the calls come in, how do you turn away the favors?" This leading cadre's conduct says it all: when every leading cadre puts the rules first and lays his cards on the table openly, he is instead able to win genuine understanding and recognition.

Before the education session ended, the leading cadre shared these heartfelt words with everyone: "Hold the disciplinary bottom line; consciously purify the 'three circles' (三圈). As has been repeatedly emphasized at all levels: the exercise of authority must have a basis; social contact should leave a trace (用权需有据、交往应有痕). Only by drawing a clear line between public and private can one move forward steadily and far!"

Reflection

Do Not Abandon the Rules During "Leisure" Time

■ Fu Qiang

To know what is straight and level, one must have a plumb line and a level; to know what is square and round, one must have a compass and a square. This ancient maxim profoundly reveals the importance of discipline and rules in conducting oneself, performing one's duties, and fulfilling one's responsibilities.

Leave, for officers and soldiers—and especially for leading cadres—is both an opportunity to shed fatigue and restore body and mind, and a touchstone for testing Party spirit and adhering to discipline.

When the busyness of work temporarily pauses, whether one's words and conduct "outside the eight hours" (八小时以外) can keep the string of discipline taut and hold the bottom line of regulations at all times is a test of whether Party member cadres hold discipline and rules in awe, and is an important measure of their cultivation of Party spirit (党性修养).

Keeping the rules means not crossing the line by even half a step. Comradeship runs deep, but genuine bonds between comrades-in-arms are absolutely not a "fig leaf" for private exchanges of favors. If, during leave, one cannot refuse invitations to banquets out of regard for personal feelings, or half-heartedly accepts local specialty gifts, it may seem to warm the relationship but in fact corrupts the rules. True care is the clean simplicity of handling public matters by the book; pure friendship is the openness of jointly holding the bottom line. Putting discipline first and speaking plainly upfront means refusing improper benefits and gaining inner peace and professional integrity in return.

Keeping the rules means not loosening the string of principle. Leading cadres hold public authority and will often become the targets of requests for "help" from relatives and friends. When returning home on leave and facing relatives' entreaties regarding transfers, examination admissions, special consideration, and the like, one must hold to principle as firmly as a mountain and strictly observe disciplinary regulations, and must absolutely never play at the margins or open loopholes because one "cannot bear to say no." Authority belongs to the public, not to the private individual. Leading cadres must especially cultivate a sound family ethos, treat managing their relatives as a basic discipline skill, hold to principle as firmly as defending a position, and win the understanding and support of family members through personal integrity.

Keeping the rules means harboring no fluke mentality. A small number of individuals with ulterior motives often use pretexts such as "having a meal and some tea" or "making a friend," while in fact coveting the authority in a leading cadre's hands. Against this, one must maintain a high degree of vigilance at all times, bearing firmly in mind the behavioral norms for social contact of military leading cadres and the requirements of being both "close" and "clean" (亲清). In contacts with local personnel, one must make the regulations plain and keep the bottom line fixed in one's heart—not only refraining from crossing the line oneself, but also guiding the other party to walk the right path and abide by the rules, ensuring that authority always operates in a regulated manner within the cage of the system.

Do not abandon the rules during "leisure" time. Only when leading cadres at all levels maintain at all times a sense of awe toward discipline and rules—holding firm in private and in small matters to the tests of social contact, authority, and family affection (交往关、权力关、亲情关)—and internalize compliance with regulations and discipline as daily habit and conscious action, can they stand up to scrutiny at any time. Only thus can they, in the openness of abiding by rules and regulations with clean hands, work with peace of mind, act with a free hand, and live up to their mission and the trust placed in them.

军队党员干部不是生活在真空中，必要的人际交往是不可避免的，但交往必须有原则、有规矩，时刻给自己装上“防火墙”“过滤网”。本期，我们跟随一名前不久休假的领导干部，从他休假期间接到的几个电话中，了解党员领导干部如何时刻用法规纪律自我约束、自我加压，管好自己的生活圈、交往圈、娱乐圈，始终做到清清白白、干干净净。 ——编 者 “慎独慎微，慎始慎终——这8个字，是我在最近一次休假经历中得到的真真切切的体会。”近日，陆军某部一堂廉政教育课上，一名领导干部现身说法，讲述自己在休假期间接到的3个人情电话，经历的3次“特殊考验”。 来电人：战友老刘 “老战友，我们几个已经转业的兄弟想聚一下，你参加一下吧！”假期伊始，这名领导就接到曾经共事多年的战友老刘打来的电话，“大家好久没见了，现在就你还在部队，咱们出来聚一聚、叙叙旧。” 正值休假，多年老战友相聚看似无可厚非，但这名领导很快想起了单位前不久组织的警示教育—— 一名兄弟单位的干部，正是在类似的战友聚会上，被老战友以“叙旧”为名拉进圈子，逐渐接受请托，最终在物资采购中违规操作，受到严肃处理。 纪律课上那句“战友情谊不能成为纪律缺口”，令他记忆犹新。 持续深化政治整训过程中，这名领导所在单位常态化开展廉政培训，单位党委书记、副书记对班子成员和重点敏感领域人员进行“一对一”提醒式谈话，深刻剖析发生在身边的“围猎”案例，让纪律红线真正在思想上紧起来。 “感情归感情，纪律归纪律。咱们的战友情分不用靠吃吃喝喝来维系。”他语气平和而坚定，“如果你方便，明天上午咱们去打场羽毛球，出出汗、聊聊天，多好。” 第二天，两人在训练馆里挥拍对打，汗水湿透了运动服。休息时，老刘试探着提起：“你们单位最近需要采购训练器材吗？我这边有资源和渠道……” 听到这话，这名领导挥手制止：“老刘，你要是聊这个，今天就到此为止。部队的规定你也知道，咱们是战友，但规矩面前没有私交，你可别让我犯错误。” 老刘沉默了几秒，随即连连点头：“懂了，那就只谈战友情，不谈生意经！” 来电人：老家表姐 这天清晨，远在老家的表姐打来了电话。表姐开门见山：“趁你休假不忙，正好有个事麻烦你……” 表姐的声音带着几分焦急：“你外甥今年大学毕业，前些天报考了你们驻地另一单位的军队文职岗位。你能不能跟负责招考的人打声招呼，多‘关照关照’？” “这个招呼我不能打，也打不了。现在文职人员招考，从命题到笔试、面试，再到体检、政审，全程由纪检部门嵌入监督，所有环节公开透明。”电话那头，表姐满是不解：“你在部队这么多年，连这点面子都没有？” “这不是面子问题，是纪律底线。”这名领导语气郑重，“今年，纪委书记给全体人员上了纪律课，专门强调领导干部不得利用职权为亲属谋取利益。外甥要是真想考进来，就让他踏实备考，凭本事说话。这样对他、对部队、对家庭，才是真正的负责。” 电话那头沉默了。过了一会儿，表姐叹了口气：“你说得对，是我想简单了，我这就让他好好复习。” 挂断电话，这名领导想起年初单位组织的廉政谈话，党委班子成员逐一表态，要严格决策用权、切实管好亲属，坚决避免领导干部干预正常工作运行。 这种无处不在的监督，看似繁琐，实则是保护干部最坚实的“防火墙”。 来电人：某建筑公司经理 假期尾声，当电话铃声再次响起，屏幕上显示的是一个陌生号码。 “领导，听说您休假了，我们公司在郊区新开了一个休闲垂钓园，您可以抽空过来放松娱乐一下……”“规矩我们懂，绝对不谈工作，就是交个朋友……”电话里的声音有些陌生，却满是殷勤。 这名领导仔细回忆后想起来，打来电话的，是驻地一家建筑公司的项目经理。 不久前，对方曾参与过单位一次小型维修项目的竞标。但该公司当时因为报价过高、方案粗糙，最终未能入围。 “休假期间，不谈工作！”眼看电话要被挂断，对方立即解释：“领导您想多了，就是纯休闲，合同的事咱不提……” 这名经理态度依旧热情，但领导言辞也十分坚决：“实话实说，你们上次没能中标，不是因为在部队没有熟人，而是你们的报价和方案与中标单位相比确实有差距。” 这名领导脑海闪过一个上级通报的典型案例——某单位一名干部被承包商以“钓鱼休闲”为名拉下水，从收受几条烟、几瓶酒开始，逐步演变成收受现金、干预工程，最终身败名裂。 “部队所有的工程项目，从预算到招标到验收全程公开透明，纪委全程监督。只要符合条件，不需要请任何人吃饭玩乐；不符合条件，请谁也没有用！”未等对方回复，他随即挂断了电话。 “电话来了，人情怎么挡？”这名领导的态度说明了一切——当每名领导干部把规矩立在前面、把底牌亮在明处，反而能够赢得真正的理解和认同。 教育结束前，这名领导语重心长地与大家分享：“守住纪律底线，自觉净化‘三圈’。正如各级反复强调的那样：用权需有据、交往应有痕。公私分明，方能行稳致远！” 感悟 “清闲”之时莫乱规矩 ■付 强 欲知平直，则必准绳；欲知方圆，则必规矩。这句古训，深刻揭示了纪律规矩对于立身行事、履职尽责的重要意义。 休假，对官兵特别是领导干部来说，既是卸下疲惫、调养身心的契机，更是检验党性、恪守纪律的试金石。 当繁忙的工作暂时停歇，“八小时以外”的言行举止，能否始终绷紧纪律之弦、恪守法规底线，能检验党员干部是否对纪律规矩存有敬畏之心，更是衡量其党性修养的重要标尺。 守规矩不可越雷池半步。战友情深，但真挚的袍泽之情，绝非私相授受的“遮羞布”。倘若休假期间碍于情面，对宴请来者不拒，对土特产半推半就，看似暖了人情，实则坏了规矩。真正的关心，是公事公办的清爽；纯粹的友谊，是共守底线的坦荡。把纪律挺在前面，把丑话讲在明处，拒绝的是不当利益，收获的是内心踏实与事业纯粹。 守规矩不能松原则之弦。领导干部手握公权，常会成为亲友“求助”的对象。休假回乡，面对亲戚关于调动、考学、关照等种种请托，必须坚持原则如山，严守纪律规定，决不能因“抹不开面子”而打擦边球、开口子。权力姓公不姓私。领导干部尤其要涵养良好家风，把管好亲属作为守纪的基本功，像坚守阵地一样守住原则，以一身正气赢得家人的理解与支持。 守规矩不得存侥幸之心。少数别有用心者常以“吃饭喝茶”“交个朋友”为幌子，实则觊觎领导干部手中的权力。对此，必须时刻保持高度警觉，牢记军队领导干部社会交往行为规范和“亲”“清”要求。与地方人员交往，要把法规亮在明处，把底线定在心间，不仅自己不越雷池半步，更要引导对方走正道、守规矩，确保权力始终在制度的笼子里规范运行。 “清闲”之时莫乱规矩。各级领导干部唯有时刻保持对纪律规矩的敬畏之心，在私底下、细微处守住交往关、权力关、亲情关，把遵规守纪内化为日常习惯和行动自觉，方能任何时候都经得起检验。唯此，才能在遵规守纪、清清爽爽的坦荡中，安心工作、放手干事，不负使命、不负重托。
