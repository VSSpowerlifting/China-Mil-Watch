From "Remote Mountain Cold Outpost" to "Iron Outpost": The "Six Networks" Give Soldiers Greater Confidence in Guarding the Border and Greater Drive in Patrol Duties

“深山寒哨”变“钢铁前哨”，“六张网”让战士们守边更有底气、巡逻执勤更有干劲
MOD China (国防部) 21 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Staff Sergeant First Class Ma Tengfei of the 'Motuo Border Defense Model Battalion,' Tibet Military District, describes in a first-person account the logistical transformation of a Himalayan outpost at 3,500 meters elevation over his 19-year posting — from pre-highway isolation requiring multi-day foot marches and backpack resupply to cold-chain fresh-food delivery every three days via the 'Snowy Plateau Delivery' (雪域配送) system, plus cellular coverage and remote medical consultation added within the past three years. The article documents the PLA's ongoing 'Six Networks' (六张网) border infrastructure program as the institutional frame for these improvements, though the piece does not specify what the six networks are or their current completion status. Its value is as a ground-level record of how Tibet Military District logistics and communications have changed at the outpost level, and of how official military media uses soldier testimony to build a welfare-and-readiness narrative around frontier garrison duty.
Translation

■ Staff Sergeant First Class Ma Tengfei, "Motuo Border Defense Model Battalion," Tibet Military District

Early in the morning, the clouds and mist had not yet lifted from the mountains of Motuo, Tibet, but the parade ground was already bustling. As usual, a white cold-chain transport vehicle pulled into the garrison on schedule and came to a steady stop. Everyone crowded around and unloaded basket after basket of fresh fruits, vegetables, meat, and eggs from the truck.

"Look at these greens — so fresh and vibrant, like they were just picked from the field." "These fish are still jumping!" Before us lay an abundance of all kinds of ingredients, like a mobile fresh-food market. Comrades were noisily discussing what to have for dinner, every face lit up with smiles.

The outpost where I am stationed sits in dense forest on the southern slopes of the Himalayas, at an elevation of 3,500 meters, far from the county seat. Fresh perishable supplies delivered every three days have already become standard here.

Seeing this scene, I feel genuinely happy from the bottom of my heart. In the past, such a sight was beyond imagining.

I have guarded the border for 19 years, and my first experience entering Motuo remains vivid in my memory. At the time, the Motuo Highway had not yet opened. I followed the column into the mountains on foot — crossing snow-covered peaks, scaling cliffs — and it took a full three days to make it into Motuo, then another day to reach the company. My feet were covered in blood blisters, and I had no idea how many times leeches had bitten me.

Those days without a road were truly brutal.

The outpost and the company headquarters were connected only by a narrow mountain path. All supply transport depended entirely on us carrying loads on our shoulders and backs, with a round trip taking several days. Once, a sick comrade wanted to eat eggs. We wrapped them carefully and guarded them with great care, but no matter how cautiously we walked, we stumbled the whole way, and by the time we reached the outpost, the egg yolks had already soaked through an entire backpack. Even now, thinking back on that feeling is painful. Every winter, when heavy snow sealed off the mountains, the outpost became an "isolated island in a sea of snow." The dining table held nothing but turnips and canned goods; leafy green vegetables became a rare luxury. In those days, as the cook, I worried every single day about three meals.

In October 2013, the Motuo Highway was completed and opened to traffic. Later, soldiers and civilians together blasted through mountains and built roads connecting the outposts and opening up patrol routes. A dense and efficient border road network gradually took shape.

Once the roads were open, all manner of construction followed, pushing into the front-line border areas, and the bottlenecks constraining border defense development were broken one after another.

The national logistics network "wove" remote outposts into its fabric — from supply materials to online shopping deliveries, efficient transport transformed the logistical support here. Now we use the "Snowy Plateau Delivery" (雪域配送) system: we place orders and select dishes independently online, fresh goods arrive via cold-chain transport, and fruits, meat, and eggs are replenished as we consume them. On the dining table, braised prawns, dry-pot spare ribs, seasonal vegetables, and fresh fruit have long since become everyday fare.

After meals, everyone likes to take out their phones and chat with family for a while.

On the subject of network signal, I have the most to say. When I first arrived at the outpost, making a phone call meant climbing to a hilltop five kilometers away and holding the phone up while searching in every direction for a signal. That "signal tree" at the summit had been climbed so many times by everyone that the bark had been worn smooth. Intermittent signal was the norm; both ends of the call would be shouting, and neither side could hear the other clearly.

Three years ago, communications network coverage finally reached the outpost. Now, in any corner you like, you can pull out your phone and video chat with family. The outpost has also activated a remote medical consultation system: if anyone is bitten by a venomous insect or develops an acute illness, they can connect with specialist physicians for a consultation at any time.

Tides surge at the border; the changes have been immense. Over 19 years through cold and heat, I have witnessed with my own eyes the transformation of a "remote mountain cold outpost" into an "iron outpost." This year, the state is pressing ahead with full intensity on the "Six Networks" (六张网) construction. We look forward to more of the fruits of national development reaching the border, giving soldiers greater confidence in guarding the border and greater drive in their patrol duties.

■西藏军区“墨脱戍边模范营”一级上士 马腾飞 一早，西藏墨脱群山间的云雾还未散尽，操场上已经热闹起来。和往常一样，白色的冷链运输车准时驶入营区，稳稳停下。大家围上去，从车上卸下一筐筐新鲜的瓜果蔬菜、肉蛋。 “看这青菜翠绿欲滴，跟刚从地里摘回来似的。”“这鱼还活蹦乱跳哩！”眼前，各种食材琳琅满目，俨然一个流动的小型生鲜市场。战友们正热闹地商量晚上吃啥，个个喜笑颜开。 我驻守的哨点位于喜马拉雅山脉南麓的密林中，海拔3500米，远离县城。3天一送的生鲜副食在这里已是标配。 看着这场面，我打心眼里高兴。搁以前，这样的光景想都不敢想。 我在边防守了19年，第一次进墨脱的经历，至今记忆犹新。那时，墨脱公路还没通车。我跟着进山队伍徒步翻雪山、爬峭壁，走了整整3天才闯进墨脱，又走了一天才到达连队。我的双脚磨出血泡，身上不知被蚂蟥咬了多少口。 不通路的日子，实在太难了。 哨点和连部之间只有羊肠小道连接，物资运补全靠我们肩扛背驮，往返一趟要好几天。有一次，生病的战友想吃鸡蛋。我们把鸡蛋包裹严实，小心翼翼地护着，无奈走一路摔一路，到了点位，蛋液早已流了一背囊。那种滋味，现在想起来都难受。一到冬季大雪封山，哨点成了“雪海孤岛”。餐桌上只有萝卜、罐头，绿叶菜成为稀罕物。那时，身为炊事员的我，天天为三顿饭发愁。 2013年10月，墨脱公路建成通车。后来，军民一起开山筑路，连接哨点，畅通巡逻点……一张紧密高效的边防路网逐渐铺开。 路通了，各种建设也跟着涌进边关一线，制约边防发展的瓶颈相继被破除。 国家物流网把偏远哨点“织”入其中，从补给物资到网购快递，高效运输改善了这里的后勤保障。现在，我们使用“雪域配送”系统，网上自主点单选菜，冷链运输新鲜直达，瓜果肉蛋随吃随补。餐桌上，油焖大虾、干锅排骨、时令蔬菜、新鲜水果，早已是家常菜。 吃完饭后，大家都喜欢拿出手机和家人聊会儿天。 说到网络信号，我最有发言权。刚来哨点那会儿，打个电话得爬到5公里外的山头，举着手机四处找信号。山顶上那棵“信号树”，都被大家爬秃噜皮了。信号断断续续是常事，电话两头都在吼，谁也听不清谁。 3年前，通信网络终于覆盖哨点，随便哪个角落，掏出手机就能和家人视频聊天。哨点还开通远程医疗系统，一旦有人被毒虫咬伤或者出现紧急病症，随时可以连线专家会诊。 潮涌边关，沧桑巨变。19年寒来暑往，我亲眼见证“深山寒哨”变成“钢铁前哨”。今年，国家紧锣密鼓推进“六张网”建设，我们期待更多国家发展成果惠及边关，让战士们守边更有底气，巡逻执勤更有干劲。
