About this analysis
Summary
A brigade of the 71st Group Army, based in northern Jiangsu and eastern Anhui, has restructured its communications training around an "information field day" (信息场日) regime that replaces the traditional vehicle-and-artillery maintenance cycle, requiring operators to master multi-band networking, cross-equipment interoperability, and data fusion under randomized, combat-realistic conditions. The article documents a specific institutional problem the brigade's Party committee names explicitly: commanders prioritized visible short-term achievements (显绩) from fielding new equipment over the unglamorous foundational work (潜功) of actually integrating those systems, producing a communications failure during a higher-echelon competition. The framing — built around the concept of a corrected "outlook on political achievement" (政绩观) — fits a recurring pattern in PLA Daily of using unit-level case studies to push back against incentive structures that reward appearance over combat readiness, though what remains unknown is how widely the brigade's specific interoperability solutions have been adopted across the 71st Group Army.

Devoted Cultivation, Weaving Dense "Battlefield Nerves"

— An Investigative Report from a Brigade of the 71st Group Army

■ PLA Daily Correspondent Hou Yongbo, Special Correspondents Song Shijie and Liu Yihan

The brigade organizes exercises in unfamiliar field terrain. Photo by Wang Ruimin

In the depths of northern Jiangsu, at the first light of dawn. Inside a communications shelter on the exercise and training ground of a brigade of the 71st Group Army, screens flicker as situational information surges and flows — aerial reconnaissance data, ground fire coordinates, and forward element positions converge in real time into a single stream; an encrypted order completes verification and reaches every combat unit in an instant.

This is a heroic unit forged in the fires of the War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea. In those years, the forebears of this brigade's predecessor units built "steel links" with their flesh and blood, upholding victory with the sincerity of those who believe "success need not be credited to oneself." Time and space have flowed on, but the bloodline continues. Facing the challenges of the future informatized and intelligentized battlefield, the brigade's Party committee has continuously recalibrated its outlook on political achievement (政绩观), pouring its energy into foundational construction whose results are difficult to see in the short term, and resolutely eliminating the shortsighted behavior of burying systemic operational risks for the sake of "looking impressive."

Willing to do preparatory work, willing to tackle unfinished business. The brigade's Party committee has established and practiced a correct outlook on political achievement (正确政绩观), leading officers and soldiers to anchor themselves to the demands of system-of-systems training (体系练兵) and joint operations, continuously pressing forward toward building a new-type network information system (新型网络信息体系) that is fully interconnected across all domains and highly resilient, allowing the invisible "battlefield nerves" to extend ever further toward the front lines.

**Drilling the "Invisible" Hard Skills, Forging a "New Engine" for Victory**

Visiting the brigade, this reporter happened to arrive on the traditional "vehicle and artillery field day," and witnessed an unusual scene.

"Successfully networked, document transmission error-free, data flow smooth…" Inside a communications shelter, a multi-element, multi-node communications connectivity test was underway. Multiple communications means and various data parameters washed over the operators like a tide; Master Sergeant Wan Renfeng calmly guided his team members through the operations, handling the data with ease.

As one of the brigade's first cohort of communications technicians, Wan Renfeng told this reporter that this was an "information field day" (信息场日) custom-designed for informatized equipment — officers and soldiers use equipment adjustment and calibration opportunities to conduct "one specialty, multiple capabilities" (一专多能) training, running both their own professional abilities and the performance of their equipment through a thorough sieve in the face of the data flood.

What is the deeper significance of the shift from "vehicle and artillery field day" to "information field day"? The brigade's leadership said: there are no shortcuts to drilling the "invisible" hard skills — only day-after-day grinding through training.

In the past, the core mission of communications soldiers was to maintain communications; circuit laying, radio operation, and cipher conversion were their signature skills. In recent years, as multiple advanced communications equipment types have been fielded by the force, the data flood has become the new language of the battlefield, and the capability demands on communications soldiers have grown with each passing day.

During the interviews, one defeat was repeatedly mentioned by officers and soldiers. After a new type of communications shelter was fielded, the brigade participated in a higher-echelon competition for the first time. Because they had not thoroughly grasped the "temperament" of the new equipment, video transmission was delayed and they could only rely on older equipment to maintain operations, resulting in an unsatisfactory final score.

"We wanted to 'take first place,' but instead we stumbled." In the after-action review, officers and soldiers found that the new equipment placed higher and stricter professional demands on everyone — they needed to simultaneously master multi-band networking, and at times a single operator had to operate multiple radio types simultaneously to flexibly respond to "enemy" situations. Communications transmission had entered the "expressway," requiring every communications soldier to be a "generalist."

"Equipment upgrades do not automatically mean combat power rises with the tide." The brigade's communications battalion commander, Battalion Commander Li, reflected: "Because we were eager to display the 'visible achievements' (显绩) brought by fielding new equipment, we instead neglected the 'latent work' (潜功) of raising soldiers' professional levels and opening up communications links between different equipment types. Little did we realize that these are precisely the 'latent achievements' (潜绩) that require sustained, long-term effort."

To address this, the brigade has adhered to the standard of "treat peacetime as wartime, everyone capable of serving as a backup" in building the "information field day." This not only specifies maintenance priorities such as comprehensive debugging of various information systems and testing of the technical and tactical performance of information equipment, but also integrates multiple subjects such as "situational sharing" (态势共享) and "data fusion" (数据融合), conducting joint debugging and testing of various information equipment in a combat-realistic environment to ensure equipment is connected and operational at all times.

Spending the "information field day" alongside officers and soldiers, this reporter observed that communication windows were randomized, "enemy" situation backgrounds were complex, and completion time limits were compressed… The high-intensity tempo had officers and soldiers exclaiming it was "satisfying."

"To forge a 'new engine' for victory, one must not only specialize and refine on the daily exercise and training ground, but also devote oneself to building strength, putting in hard work and drilling hard skills in obscurity." The brigade's leadership explained that as more and more equipment incorporates communications modules and the data flood surges ceaselessly, communications soldiers are no longer simple "message relays" but more like "data helmsmen." To avoid falling behind in the transformation and upgrade, one must have the sense of urgency that "sailing against the current, if you don't advance, you fall back."

Today, whenever major tasks such as combat-realistic exercises or research breakthroughs arise, the brigade's officers and soldiers go through an "information field day" — this has become an important measure for consolidating the foundation of information support. Not long ago, during a communications support exercise, Corporal Long Xingyi single-handedly served as a "generalist," completing networked connectivity for multiple equipment types in a short time and keeping network data in a "hot backup" state at all times.

**Willing to Pave the "Information Expressway," Passing the Baton to Open Up Joint Links**

Late at night, the professional classrooms at the brigade's field training site were still brightly lit. Gathered here were staff officers, battalion commanders, engineers, and technical backbone personnel of various types — a "brainstorming" session on opening up joint communications links was underway.

Assistant Engineer Chen Peng is a familiar face at such research breakthrough sessions. In his view, the process of integrating the various services and arms into the network has no "completed tense" — it is always in the "present continuous."

This feeling began at the time of the brigade's formation. That year, Chen Peng received the mission of establishing the first network and building the first link, and immediately ran into a hard obstacle: platforms from the various services and arms were speaking different "dialects," and achieving unification was not the work of a single day.

Just as everyone was pressing through the difficult points, higher-echelon organs came to inspect the phased results. Someone proposed: we have already put in a great deal of effort — why not "package" the existing results and set up a "demonstration observation point" to make a good impression? The brigade's Party committee rejected this on the spot: "Any 'making do' in the communications network is a 'fatal wound' on the future battlefield. To build a good 'information expressway' for system-of-systems operations, we must keep at it generation after generation. Pressing hard to overcome difficulties today is paving the roadbed for tomorrow."

Forgoing short-term impressiveness, standing firm on long-term development. During the inspection process, the brigade's Party committee spoke not of achievements but only of problems, and requested that higher echelons coordinate solutions to certain thorny issues. Thereafter, they joined hands with academies and research institutions to establish a research "think tank," upgrading software platforms and standardizing data standards. Targeting problems such as unreasonable network-link construction, inflexible system employment, inadequate security protection, and slow dynamic adjustment, they optimized links level by level — from individual elements to the system of systems, from single links to multi-domain joint operations — continuously testing and verifying in practice.

Engineer Zhang Tao has a deep appreciation for this: "Just as the junctions between different units on the battlefield tend to become weak points in the defense, the 'junctions' of the information system are also the points most prone to breaking. Our mission is to be the 'adhesive' — to repair each segment of the information link and connect them into a network."

Opening up links is not only a technical breakthrough — it is also a protracted battle of directly confronting problems.

"Chairman Xi has pointed out that solving problems requires genuine effort and real exertion — one cannot respond perfunctorily and go through the motions." In this brigade, the operator at every communications station has one habit: promptly recording each problem discovered, categorized by type, in a "problem list."

During one mountain offensive exercise, the communications node where Corporal He Shiyu was stationed had its command link severed mid-maneuver due to strong electromagnetic jamming by the "enemy." After the exercise, He Shiyu entered the exposed problems into the "problem list," and through repeated study of jamming patterns and equipment anti-jamming performance, worked out multiple countermeasures.

"Don't fear a long 'problem list' — fear that the problems on the list go unsolved." The brigade's leadership pointed out: only by emerging from defeat can one advance toward victory. This results-oriented approach of "seeking truth over appearance" (唯实不唯虚) has enabled officers and soldiers to dare to confront problems and shortcomings head-on.

Chen Peng is not alone in his devoted pursuit of breakthroughs. Corporal Ni Peng has studied information interoperability technology for multiple communications terminal types, becoming an expert in cross-module connectivity; Company Commander Jiang Tiichi led a technical team to develop a certain operating method that increased the deployment timeliness of the command information system several-fold; one battalion, targeting complex battlefield environments, innovated network tactics for different scenarios, effectively improving the quality and efficiency of communications connectivity while on the move… From individual soldiers to the system of systems, the brigade's officers and soldiers have passed the baton in paving the road, allowing the "information expressway" to extend ever further.

Late into the night, the "brainstorming" continued. Chen Peng's notebook was filled densely with the link bottlenecks to be overcome in the next phase. He knows well that in the process of bringing the "information expressway" to full connectivity, every node they reach will become the starting point from which those who come after will charge forward.

**Persisting in Unfinished Business, Pushing Information Nodes to the Battlefield Front Lines**

The morning mist had not yet lifted, but a certain training ground was already a scene of busy activity. A new type of communications node vehicle rolled through muddy tidal flats and completed camouflaged deployment in a depression only a few hundred meters from the "engagement line."

Several kilometers away, another communications element was providing accompanying support closely behind an armored assault group. As the combat vehicle jolted, operator Zhao Sida tapped at his keyboard, pushing a fire strike request to the command post and fire units. In just a few seconds, the first-round strike coordinates had completed verification and been transmitted back.

"In the past, our positions were often set up in the rear." Staff Officer Hu of the brigade's operations and training section pointed to the points of light on the screen and said: "Today, communications nodes move with the mission and deploy close to the front lines — wherever the combat forces advance, our 'battlefield nerves' extend."

From "static support from the rear" to "communications on the move at the front," there is no ready experience to follow, no standard answer to copy — one can only feel one's way across the river. On this road of exploration, the brigade's officers and soldiers have pressed forward one after another.

Not long ago, in order to further break down the information barriers between the various services and arms, they embedded a "joint module" (联合模块) in the communications platform, working together with neighboring units to broaden the airspace perspective, open up maritime links, and weave the "information network" denser and cast it wider.

During one combat-realistic exercise, the brigade's communications network was blocked and severed. They rapidly activated backup links and dynamically reconstituted the network, not only maintaining basic command continuity but also supporting real-time sharing of reconnaissance intelligence and fire unit information. After the exercise, the command post convened an after-action review at the first opportunity, identifying the "symptoms" behind the various "blockages" in communications support during the exercise.

"The closer you are to the battlefield front lines, the more difficult points and bottlenecks you encounter." The brigade's leadership told this reporter: these problems cannot be encountered sitting in a rear shelter, and cannot be thought of through simulated deduction alone. These real-combat subjects — always pressing closer, always requiring a breakthrough — are precisely the unfinished business they must press on with day after day: "If we don't tackle it today, tomorrow we will pay in blood."

In order to reach new benchmarks of combat power, the brigade's officers and soldiers have proactively stepped out of their "comfort zone," accelerating the updating of knowledge, expanding capabilities, and upgrading skills.

On coastal tidal flats, they probe the limit performance of equipment, eliminate information blind spots, and improve long-distance data transmission rates.

In mountain highlands, commanders at all levels combine shared battlefield situational awareness to "plan and command simultaneously" (边筹划、边指挥), innovating communications methods in complex battlefield environments.

In hilly wilderness, they conduct combat-realistic exercises together with neighboring units, organizing reconnaissance, fire, and support elements in system-of-systems operational deductions, evaluating and optimizing plans in real time…

"Today's battlefield space is shifting from the 'tangible' toward the 'intangible,' and communications elements are also continuously shedding their former 'supporting role' identity, drawing closer to the battlefield front lines and gradually becoming the 'decisive factor' (胜负手) that determines the outcome of the battle." The brigade's Party committee holds that pressing on with these unfinished matters that are "invisible" and "intangible" today is a necessary act in support of winning.

In the depths of eastern Anhui, a "manned + unmanned" (有人+无人) mode "battlefield review" (沙场点兵) allowed the brigade's officers and soldiers to envision the "future mode" of communications networking — unmanned equipment networked in "three dimensions," information tentacles breaking through the barriers of multi-dimensional battlefield space; from detection to destruction, reconnaissance front ends and fire units build an "order-based" (订单化) chain through an integrated platform; enemy situation, friendly situation, and other information are shared in real time in "cloud-based situational awareness" (云端态势), forming a dynamically updated data "resource pool."

"The communications soldier's battlefield is no longer 'a telephone line and a radio set,' but the entire system of systems. Looking to the future, we are striding forward toward an ever broader system-of-systems battlefield and joint battlefield." Said Section Chief Meng of the brigade's information support section.

As the sun set in the west, the smoke of the exercise and training ground gradually dissipated. Inside a communications shelter of the brigade, officers and soldiers were still conducting an after-action review of the day's communications connectivity, the "information network" on the screen flickering with a faint light. With the posture of being "always online," they are devoting themselves to cultivating and weaving dense the "battlefield nerves" for winning future wars.

潜心耕耘密织“战场神经” ——来自第七十一集团军某旅的调查报告 ■解放军报记者 侯永波 特约记者 宋世杰 刘益涵 该旅在野外陌生地域组织演练。王瑞民摄 苏北腹地，晨光初露。第71集团军某旅演训场通信方舱内，荧屏闪烁，态势信息奔涌流转——空中侦察数据、地面火力坐标、前沿分队位置实时汇聚成流，一条加密指令完成校验，瞬间直达各作战单元。 这是一支从抗美援朝战火中淬炼出来的英雄部队。当年，该旅前身部队的先辈们用血肉之躯架起“钢铁链路”，以“功成不必在我”的赤诚托举胜利。时空流转，血脉赓续。面对未来信息化智能化战场的挑战，该旅党委不断校正政绩观，将心力倾注于一时难见明显效果的基础建设，坚决杜绝为“出彩”而埋下体系运转隐患的短视行为。 甘于做铺垫性的工作，甘于抓未成之事。该旅党委树立和践行正确政绩观，引领官兵锚定体系练兵与联合作战需求，持续向构建全域贯通、韧性十足的新型网络信息体系发力冲锋，让无形的“战场神经”不断向战场前沿延伸。 苦练“看不见”的硬功，锻造打赢“新引擎” 走访该旅，恰逢传统的“车炮场日”，记者看见了别样的景象。 “已成功入网，文电传输无误、数据流转顺畅……”通信方舱内，一场多要素多节点通联测试正在进行。多种通信手段与各项数据参数如潮水般扑面而来，三级军士长万人峰指导班组成员从容操作，处理数据游刃有余。 作为全旅首批通信技师，万人峰告诉记者，这是一场专为信息化装备定制的“信息场日”，官兵利用装备调整检校时机开展“一专多能”训练，在数据洪流面前，将自己业务能力和手中装备性能好好过一遍筛子。 从“车炮场日”到“信息场日”，背后有何深意？该旅领导说，想要练好“看不见”的硬功，没有捷径可走，唯有日复一日地打磨训练。 过去，通信兵的核心任务是保通，线路架设、电台操作、密语转换是看家本领。近年来随着多型先进通信装备列装部队，数据洪流成为新的战场语言，对通信兵的能力要求也与日俱增。 采访中，一次失利被官兵屡屡提及。某新型通信方舱列装后，该旅首次参加上级比武，因对新装备“脾性”摸得不透，视频传输延时，只能靠老装备维持运转，最终成绩不理想。 “本想‘拔头筹’，反栽了跟头。”复盘总结中官兵发现，新装备对大家专业能力要求更高更严——需同步掌握多频段组网，有时一个人要同时操作多型电台以灵活应对“敌”情，通信传输已驶入“高速路”，要求每名通信兵都得是“多面手”。 “装备升级不等于战斗力‘水涨船高’。”该旅某通信营李营长反思，“由于急于展现新装备列装带来的‘显绩’，反而忽略了提升官兵业务水平、打通各型装备之间通信链路等‘潜功’。殊不知这些才是更需久久为功的‘潜绩’。” 为此，该旅坚持“平时当战时、个个能备份”标准打造“信息场日”，不仅明确各类信息系统综合调试、信息装备技战术性能检测等维护保养重点，还把“态势共享”“数据融合”等多个课目整合其中，在实战化环境中开展各类信息装备的联调联试，确保装备全时通联、常态运行。 和官兵一起过“信息场日”，记者看到，通联时段随机、“敌”情背景复杂、完成时限压缩……高强度节奏让官兵直呼“过瘾”。 “要想锻造打赢‘新引擎’，不仅要在日常演训场上专攻精练，更要潜心蓄力，在默默无闻处下苦功、练硬功。”该旅领导介绍，随着越来越多的装备具备通信模块，数据洪流奔涌不息，通信兵早已不是简单的“传话筒”，更像是“数据掌舵者”。想要不让自己在转型升级中落伍掉队，必须有“逆水行舟、不进则退”的紧迫感。 如今，每逢实战化演练、课题攻关等重大任务，该旅官兵都要过一次“信息场日”，这已经成为他们夯实信息保障根基的重要一招。前不久，一场通信保障演练中，中士龙星轶一人担当“多面手”，在短时间内完成多型装备入网通联，实现网络数据全时处于“热备份”状态。 甘为“信息高速”铺路，接力打通联合链路 夜深，该旅野外驻训场的专业教室仍灯火通明。这里聚集了机关干部、营长、工程师、技术骨干等各类人员，一场关于打通联合通信链路的“头脑风暴”正在进行。 助理工程师陈鹏是这种攻关场景的老面孔。在他看来，各军兵种融合入网的过程没有“完成时”，而是始终处于“进行时”。 这种感受，始于旅队组建之初。那年，陈鹏受领首次组网建链任务，迎面就是一块“硬骨头”：各军兵种平台操着不同“方言”，想要统一绝非一朝一夕之功。 正当大家攻坚之际，上级机关前来验收阶段性成果。有人提议：已经付出了很多努力，不如先把现有成果“包装”一下，搞个“观摩示范点”出出彩。该旅党委当场否决：“通信网的任何一处‘将就’，都是未来战场的‘致命伤’。想要建好体系作战的‘信息高速’，必须要一茬茬接着干。今天奋力攻克难关，就是为日后铺好路基。” 放弃短期出彩，立足长远发展。迎检过程中，该旅党委不讲成绩，只讲问题，并请求上级协调解决部分棘手问题。此后，他们与院校、科研机构等联手成立攻关“智囊团”，升级软件平台、规范数据标准。针对网链构建不合理、系统运用不灵活、安全防护不托底、动态调整不迅速等问题，他们从要素到体系，从单个链路到多域联合，逐级优化链路，不断在实践中检验。 工程师张涛对此深有体会：“就像战场上不同部队的接合部容易成为防御薄弱区域一样，信息体系的‘接合部’也是易断点。我们的任务，就是做‘黏合剂’，把一段段信息链路修好、连成网。” 打通链路，不只是技术攻关，更是一场直面问题的持久战。 “习主席指出，解决问题必须下真功、使实劲，不能虚以应对走过场。”在该旅，每个通信台站的操作员都有一个习惯，就是将每次发现的问题，及时分门别类地记录在“问题清单”里。 一次山地进攻演练，中士何士玉所在的通信节点在机动中由于遭“敌”强电磁干扰，导致指挥链路中断。演练结束，何士玉将暴露的问题记入“问题清单”，并通过反复研究干扰样式与装备抗干扰性能，摸索出多种应对招法。 “不怕‘问题清单’长，就怕清单里的问题没解决。”该旅领导指出，从失利中走出来，才能走向胜利。这种“唯实不唯虚”的政绩导向，让官兵敢于直面问题和短板。 像何士玉这样潜心攻关的并非个例。中士倪鹏钻研多型通信终端信息互通技术，成为跨模块连接的行家里手；连长江铁池带领技术团队探索出某操作方法，将指挥信息系统部署时效提升数倍；某营针对复杂战场环境，创新不同场景网络战术，有效提升了动中通联质效……从单兵到体系，该旅官兵接力铺路，让“信息高速”不断延伸。 夜深了，“头脑风暴”还在继续。陈鹏的笔记本上，密密麻麻记满了下一阶段要攻克的链路堵点。他深知，“信息高速”全线贯通的过程中，每一段他们抵达的节点，都会成为后来者冲锋的起点。 久抓未成之事，将信息节点推向战场前沿 晨雾未散，某训练场已是一派忙碌景象。一辆新型通信节点车碾过泥泞滩涂，在距“交火线”仅数百米的洼地中完成伪装部署。 数公里外，另一支通信分队紧随装甲突击群实施伴随保障。战车颠簸中，操作手赵斯达敲击键盘，将火力打击请求推送至指挥所与火力单元。短短数秒，第一轮打击坐标已完成校验回传。 “过去，我们的阵地往往设在后方。”该旅作训科胡参谋指着屏幕上的光点说，“如今，通信节点跟着任务走、贴着前沿布——作战力量推进到哪里，我们的‘战场神经’就延伸到哪里。” 从后方“静态保”到前方“动中通”，没有现成经验可循，没有标准答案可抄，只能摸着石头过河。在这条探索之路上，该旅官兵前赴后继。 前不久，为了进一步打通各军兵种之间的信息壁垒，他们在通信平台中嵌入“联合模块”，与友邻单位一同拓宽空域视野、打通海上链路，将“信息网”织得更密、撒得更开。 在一次实战化演练中，该旅通信网络受阻中断。他们迅速启用备用链路，动态重组网络，不仅保持了基本指挥不断，还支撑了侦察情报与火力单元信息的实时共享。演练结束，指挥所第一时间召开复盘会，针对演练中通信保障的各种“梗阻”查找“病症”。 “离战场前沿越近，遇见的难点堵点就越多。”该旅领导告诉记者，这些问题，坐在后方方舱里碰不到，靠模拟推演也想不到。这些永远在逼近、永远需突破的实战课题，正是他们必须日复一日抓下去的未成之事，“今天不抓，明天就要付出血的代价”。 为了达到战斗力新标高，该旅官兵主动跳出“舒适区”，加速推进知识更新、本领扩容、能力升级。 在海岸滩涂，他们探究装备极限性能，消除信息盲区，提升远距离数据输送速率。 在山地高原，各级指挥员结合共享战场态势“边筹划、边指挥”，创新复杂战场环境下通信方法。 在丘陵荒野，他们与友邻部队一起进行实战化演练，组织侦察、火力、保障等要素进行体系作战推演，实时评估优化方案…… “今天的战场空间正从‘有形’趋向‘无形’，通信分队也在不断摆脱以往‘配角’身份，朝着战场前沿靠近，逐渐成为左右战局的‘胜负手’。”该旅党委认为，抓这些今天“看不见”“摸不着”的未成之事，是支撑打赢的必要之举。 皖东腹地，一次“有人+无人”模式的“沙场点兵”，让该旅官兵畅想起通信组网的“未来模式”——无人装备“立体”入网，信息触角打破多维战场空间壁垒；从发现到摧毁，侦察前端与火力单元通过一体化平台搭建“订单化”链条；敌情、我情等在“云端态势”中实时共享，形成动态更新的数据“资源池”。 “通信兵的战场早已不是‘一根电话线、一部电台’，而是整个体系。放眼未来，我们正朝着更为广阔的体系战场、联合战场大步前行。”该旅信息保障科孟科长说。 夕阳西下，演训场的硝烟渐渐散去。该旅某通信方舱内，官兵仍在复盘当天的通联情况，屏幕上的“信息网”闪烁着微光。他们正以“永远在线”的姿态，潜心耕耘密织打赢未来战争的“战场神经”。
