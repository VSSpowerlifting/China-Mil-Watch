From "Post-Hoc Announcement" to "Full-Process Transparency": How This Brigade Made "Sensitive Matters" No Longer Sensitive

从“事后公示”到“全程公开”，这个旅让“敏感事项”不再敏感
PLA Daily (解放军报) 27 May 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A brigade of the 82nd Group Army has institutionalized full-process transparency (党务公开) for sensitive personnel decisions—NCO promotions, Party membership development, and merit commendations—by converting closed Party committee deliberations into enlarged meetings where battalion commanders, Party delegates, and atmosphere supervisors (风气监督员) observe standing committee members debate, vote by show of hands, and announce tallies on the spot. The article documents a specific internal credibility problem the PLA acknowledges openly: opaque promotion and Party-membership processes had generated persistent suspicion of favoritism, depressed performance motivation, and eroded trust in branch leadership. The framing situates the fix within ongoing political rectification training (政治整训), presenting procedural transparency as the mechanism for restoring that trust rather than as a structural reform—leaving open whether the practice extends beyond this brigade or carries any enforcement backstop if a Party committee reverts to closed deliberations.

From "Post-Hoc Announcement" to "Full-Process Transparency"

■ PLA Daily Special Correspondent Liu Fan

In the study room of a certain company under a certain brigade of the 82nd Group Army, political instructor Qu Feipeng was reporting to all officers and soldiers of the company: "On behalf of the Party branch, I am reporting to everyone on Party member development during the first quarter and annual work considerations…" From the cultivation and assessment of Party membership applicants, to the criteria for determining development candidates, to the annual development priorities—he explained every item clearly and thoroughly.

Below the podium, officers and soldiers listened with focused attention. Corporal Quan Tishu noticed that the content was entirely consistent with what he had heard when he attended the Party branch committee meeting as an atmosphere supervisor (风气监督员).

After the Party member development work was launched, the company's Party branch strictly followed procedures—conducting extensive heart-to-heart conversations, reviewing training files and mission records—and after five days of in-depth study, ultimately identified three development candidates.

"In the past, when developing Party members, we often only announced the list of names, and people would wonder, 'Why him and not me?'" Staff Sergeant Second Class Meng Baojiang knows this feeling well. He had been dejected after several unsuccessful applications, had even developed misunderstandings about the Party branch, and his work enthusiasm was affected. Later, the political instructor carefully laid out the reasons and deeds that had earned the selected comrade recognition, and only then did Meng Baojiang face his own shortcomings squarely, strive to catch up, and ultimately join the Party with honor. He remarked with feeling: "If the branch had explained all this earlier, would I have had any grievances at all?"

"In the past, the decision-making stage was often seen as the business of branch committee members. After results were announced, everyone only knew 'who joined the Party' but not 'why him'—speculation and estrangement were inevitable," Qu Feipeng said candidly. To change this situation, the company began last year to draw on the brigade Party committee's successful practices, explaining the entire process of Party member development to officers and soldiers in full and without omission.

It turned out that, in order to advance Party affairs transparency (党务公开) and strengthen democratic oversight, the brigade had expanded the scope of Party committee meetings that deliberate on non-commissioned officer promotion matters to include battalion commanders, select department section chiefs, and had invited delegates to the brigade Party committee's Second Congress of Party Member Representatives and atmosphere supervisors to attend and observe the entire proceedings, ensuring that deliberations were transparent and decisions were open.

After completing the report, Qu Feipeng swept his gaze across the room: "If anyone has questions or opinions, raise them now—I will answer them on the spot. You may also report directly to the battalion Party committee or the brigade's discipline inspection and supervision section." The words had barely left his mouth when a soldier raised his hand and asked: "Instructor, for comrades who were not selected this time, will the organization point out their shortcomings? Will there be another chance?"

"Of course there will be!" Qu Feipeng answered affirmatively. "We will have individual heart-to-heart talks with each activist who was not selected, explaining both their strengths and their shortcomings. Joining the Party is a lifelong matter—as long as you keep working hard and meet the standards, the door of the Party organization will always be open." These words brought a renewed brightness to the eyes of several "unselected" soldiers, who expressed one after another their determination to redouble their efforts and strive to meet the standards as soon as possible.

From "post-hoc announcement" to "full-process transparency," openness and transparency have become the working norm and behavioral instinct of this brigade in handling sensitive matters—like a solid "bridge connecting hearts" (连心桥), tightly uniting the spirit and strength of all officers and soldiers of the brigade on the journey of strengthening and revitalizing the military.

Making "Sensitive Matters" No Longer Sensitive

—A News Investigation into a Certain Brigade of the 82nd Group Army's Party Committee Opening the Deliberation Process on Sensitive Matters to Public View

■ PLA Daily Special Correspondent Liu Fan

"As long as we are rooted in our combat positions and work diligently, we can leave our growth and advancement to the organization!" After a squad affairs meeting, Sergeant Ling Naibo of a certain brigade of the 82nd Group Army encouraged several squad members involved in the second-half-of-the-year promotion cycle to plan for the long term and keep working hard. In March of this year, in accordance with relevant regulations and following rigorous assessment, college-educated soldier Ling Naibo—whose overall quality was outstanding—"skipped" the corporal stage and was successfully promoted to sergeant. He was also recommended by his squad and platoon to serve as assistant squad leader, becoming a role model for younger comrades in the company.

There was a time when some officers and soldiers harbored doubts about the handling of sensitive matters affecting their personal interests, and private grumbling such as "sensitive matters have hidden dealings" and "it's better to be good at working relationships than good at the work itself" was common. Wang Ze, the political instructor of the company where Ling Naibo serves, told the reporter that over the past two years there have been quite a few soldiers in the brigade who have grown in the same way as Ling Naibo, and he has clearly sensed that everyone's enthusiasm for striving has grown markedly stronger. This is largely attributable to the brigade's atmosphere and environment of fairness and impartiality.

Behind this transformation lies the brigade Party committee's bold exploration: breaking with the longstanding tradition of "closed-door meetings and post-hoc announcements," and proactively opening every stage of the deliberation process on sensitive matters that directly affect officers' and soldiers' vital interests. This self-revolution—placing decision-making power in the sunlight—is riding the momentum of deepening political rectification training (政治整训) and injecting a steady stream of positive energy into the brigade's atmosphere-building efforts.

One "Imperfect" Attempt—

Aligning "What Officers and Soldiers Want to Know" with "What We Think Officers and Soldiers Want to Know"

"Can Party committee decisions really be shown to us?" When word spread in September 2024 that the Party committee meeting deliberating on non-commissioned officer promotion matters would be made public, the first reaction of some officers and soldiers in the brigade was skepticism.

"The reaction of the officers and soldiers was precisely a reflection of how deep-rooted the chronic ailments and accumulated malpractices were." This was the brigade's first attempt to open NCO promotion-related work to public view. The brigade Party committee leadership unanimously agreed that they needed to summon the resolve and make the effort to cut into the stubborn, chronic problems that had previously been difficult to discuss and unwilling to be touched, and to respond to officers' and soldiers' concerns about the fairness of the promotion process.

On that occasion, the brigade Party committee and its organs put in enormous effort—examination personnel were kept under closed management throughout, examination sites were recorded on video throughout, different units conducted cross-supervision of examinations, atmosphere supervisors oversaw the entire process, and any doubts were verified and answered on the spot. After the examinations concluded, sure enough, no one raised objections to the scores. Many officers and soldiers praised the brigade for its serious and rigorous attitude.

They say "the beginning is always the hardest," but what the brigade leadership had not anticipated was that the real difficulty in this matter lay not in "the beginning." As planned, the brigade Party committee converted the Party committee meeting deliberating on NCO promotion matters into an enlarged Party committee meeting, expanding attendance to include battalion commanders, relevant organ section chiefs, delegates to the brigade Party committee's Second Congress of Party Member Representatives (hereafter "Party delegates"), and atmosphere supervisors.

During the meeting, organ section chiefs first reported on the organization of each examination subject and the conduct of integrity oversight. When it came time for battalion commanders to take turns reporting their considerations for personnel selection and their proposed recommendations, only the standing committee members of the Party committee, the reporting battalion commander, and the Party delegates remained in the room. As a result, "noise" about this open Party committee meeting spread through the ranks: "Why did the atmosphere supervisors have to leave? Even though the Party delegates were there, it still felt like something was being concealed…"

The director of the brigade's Political Work Department said: "We were also puzzled—the procedures were this strict, so why were people still not satisfied?" Later, taking advantage of a paired-assistance visit, they went down to the grassroots to investigate. The words of one veteran soldier were thought-provoking: "The examinations were strict—the whole brigade saw that. But NCO promotion isn't just about one exam. How the Party organization weighs who works hard day-to-day and who steps up when missions come—that's what everyone is uncertain about."

The brigade Party committee leadership came to realize that their previous approach had only addressed "whether the examinations were fair," without touching "whether the decision-making was transparent." Formal rigor is not the same as officers and soldiers feeling substantive fairness. What officers and soldiers truly cared about was not just "who scored how much," but practical questions like "how does the Party organization evaluate me" and "between someone who performs well once and someone who consistently performs well, which one will the organization choose?"

The strict examination procedures also placed considerable pressure on the organs. Taking the operations and training section and the human resources section as examples, in order to ensure the examination process was flawless, several organ cadres devoted the vast majority of their energy to examination work, and the progress of other work was affected.

"Excessively inflated standards are themselves a form of formalism (形式主义); they only consume the energy of the organs and the trust of the grassroots, and cannot be sustained." As political rectification training deepened and became more substantive, the brigade leadership reflected deeply: they must proceed from actual effectiveness, truly align "what officers and soldiers want to know" with "what we think officers and soldiers want to know," and place the decision-making process in the sunlight in a lawful and compliant manner.

Following this reflection, the brigade Party committee focused on better guaranteeing officers' and soldiers' right to be informed, and studied ways to implement the open Party committee meeting format. In the end, they decided that going forward, atmosphere supervisors would no longer be asked to step aside during the stage when standing committee members deliberate and vote.

One "Fully Transparent" Deliberation—

Leadership Takes the Lead with Open Integrity; Officers and Soldiers Feel Clarity in Their Hearts

"If we open things up, we open them up completely—otherwise, how can we be worthy of the trust of our officers and soldiers…"

The then-chief of the brigade's human resources section, Qiao Haiyang, still cannot forget those words, spoken by a brigade leader standing by a window watching officers and soldiers train in the garrison area.

Moved by that leader's resolve, Qiao Haiyang also felt some apprehension: how should he report this to the other brigade leaders?

To his surprise, the brigade leaders' responses were unanimous—

"Full-process transparency? This is a good thing—it should have been done long ago!"

"When your hands are clean, you act with an open conscience."

"I fully support it. The more sensitive the matter, the more it tests Party character and conduct. To put political rectification training into practice means having the courage to make your position clear through action on sensitive matters!"

……

And so, the brigade's second enlarged Party committee meeting deliberating on NCO promotion matters was convened as scheduled. Unlike the agenda of the first meeting, when a certain battalion reported, only that battalion's commander, the Party delegates, and the atmosphere supervisors remained—but during the brigade Party committee standing committee's deliberation and voting process, that battalion's Party delegates and atmosphere supervisors did not step aside.

Recalling that meeting, grassroots Party delegate and Staff Sergeant First Class Wang Tuo of a certain battalion said repeatedly that he "hadn't expected" it—

A brigade leader randomly asked the political instructor of a certain battalion to explain the battalion Party committee's reasons for recommending a particular soldier to remain in service. The political instructor explained one by one, drawing on specific data and materials covering examination score rankings, professional talent reserves, day-to-day performance, and peer evaluations. The leaders present nodded repeatedly as they listened.

The meeting proceeded battalion by battalion—reporting battalion by battalion, voting battalion by battalion. The brigade Party committee standing committee members deliberated in front of the atmosphere supervisors and Party delegates, conducted a show-of-hands vote, and announced the tally on the spot, counted the votes on the spot, and published the results on the spot.

Leadership took the lead with open integrity; officers and soldiers felt clarity in their hearts. What the attending officers and soldiers saw and heard spread through the brigade without prompting—

"So this is how the organization thinks through these things. Who is capable and who is not—there is solid evidence for all of it."

"Before, I always thought who got promoted was just a matter of what a leader said. Now I know that every vote they cast is made with great care."

Hearing the officers' and soldiers' assessments, one standing committee member who had attended the meeting was deeply moved: "With pairs of eyes from the grassroots watching me make decisions, how could I not be careful? When deliberating on each comrade, we were especially cautious—the reasons had to be absolutely sufficient and able to withstand any questioning."

The seeds of trust quietly took root through each successive act of openness. NCO promotion—once a "sensitive matter" occasionally entangled with complex factors—has now become far less sensitive.

The chief of the brigade's human resources section, surnamed Zhang, told the reporter that as of March of this year, the brigade has convened four enlarged Party committee meetings focused on NCO promotion work. Each time, the procedures have been standardized and the transparency has been thorough. Now, atmosphere supervisors and Party delegates from each battalion can not only hear the report for their own battalion—they can also learn the situation of every officer and soldier in the entire brigade who is being considered for retention.

The company commander of a certain company in the brigade, Gao Lijian, still remembers that in early March of this year, when seeing off retiring veterans, a soldier who had not been retained said to him solemnly: "Company commander, I didn't get to stay—I truly regret it. But I leave with no grievances. Don't worry—I'll keep working hard after I go home!"

From "One Matter" to "One Category of Matters"—

Letting a New, Upright Atmosphere Generate the Drive to Forge Ahead

The "butterfly effect" brought by the open and transparent Party committee meetings, like spring rains, has silently transformed the political ecology at the grassroots level and changed the spirit of every officer and soldier.

A certain company in the brigade drew on this approach during the 2025 merit and honor commendation nomination process, breaking the previous model of "deciding candidates behind closed doors and posting announcements afterward." After the nominations were completed, the branch specially convened an all-personnel military assembly, at which company political instructor Wang Ze reported in detail to officers and soldiers on the selection criteria, nomination process, democratic evaluation results, and branch ballot results for third-class merit citations, commendations, and "Four Haves" (四有) Outstanding Soldier designations, explaining the branch's decision-making rationale for each one.

"Now I know where my gaps are—I'll keep working hard next year." When the reporter met Staff Sergeant First Class Diao Xianqun, a keen and resolute determination could be seen in his eyes. Like Diao Xianqun, most of the company's officers and soldiers who had not gotten what they hoped for had their enthusiasm largely unaffected.

"Proactively opening the deliberation process on sensitive matters is meant to drive officers and soldiers of the brigade to transform their thinking and conduct." The brigade leadership unanimously agreed: placing leaders' individual deliberation opinions, policy judgments, and ballot results entirely before officers and soldiers means that every decision must be able to withstand the scrutiny of officers and soldiers and the scrutiny of history. This demands strict adherence to exercising power according to regulations, exercising power with prudence, and exercising power with integrity—and resolutely discarding selfish and impure motives while upholding Party character principles.

In the past, some officers and soldiers had a "just get by" mentality day-to-day and only scrambled at the last minute when promotion time approached. Now, everyone knows that the Party organization looks at consistent performance, not a single moment or event. "Doing well every ordinary day matters more than anything else" has become a shared understanding among officers and soldiers. Decisions made at all levels of organization have won the recognition and support of officers and soldiers, and have firmly established the clear orientation of "standing on the strength of one's quality and advancing on the basis of one's actual performance" (靠素质立身、凭实绩进步).

In September of last year, Sergeant Xie Zhonghao of a certain company was successfully promoted on the strength of his solid capabilities. Shortly after his rank was elevated, he was invited by several younger comrades about to go through the NCO promotion process to serve as a small-unit instructor for intensive training. "Everyone trusts the organization and relies on the organization—everyone wants to advance on the basis of their actual performance," Xie Zhonghao said.

Reporter's Dialogue with the Director of the Political Work Department of a Certain Brigade of the 82nd Group Army—

Public Credibility Comes from a Public-Minded Heart

■ PLA Daily Special Correspondent Liu Fan

At present, units at all levels are continuously deepening political rectification training, strictly rectifying discipline and conduct, and driving the atmosphere at the grassroots level to become ever purer and the political ecology to continuously improve. How can one directly confront sensitive issues during political rectification training and build a solid foundation of trust among officers and soldiers through openness and transparency? Around this topic, the reporter held a dialogue with the director of the Political Work Department of a certain brigade of the 82nd Group Army.

Reporter: Why did the brigade Party committee resolve to break with the convention of "closed-door meetings and post-hoc announcements"?

Director: In the past, we were concerned about officers and soldiers talking, so for sensitive matters that officers and soldiers cared about, we only announced results without opening up the process. This appeared compliant, but in reality it cut off effective oversight and bred suspicion. Those who performed outstandingly but did not get what they hoped for easily suspected that "connections" were at play; those with average performance might shift their attention toward "working the relationship network"; individual cadres who dared not uphold principles led some officers and soldiers to entertain "crooked ideas." If these problems are not resolved, they will chill the hearts of soldiers, scatter the military's spirit, and damage combat effectiveness. We must turn the knife inward in self-revolution, truly implement officers' and soldiers' right to be informed and their right to oversight—only then can we unite hearts around the organization.

Reporter: From "closed-door deliberations" to "open-door meetings," what was the greatest difficulty? How was it overcome?

Director: The greatest difficulty was the transformation of thinking within the Party committee leadership itself. A public-minded heart (公心) is the prerequisite for daring to be open. Proactively placing power under oversight is not easy to put into practice. At the time we also had concerns: Is it compliant to openly discuss deliberation opinions? Does it affect the authority of the Party committee? In response, we studied deeply the important statements of Chairman Xi and relevant regulations and institutional provisions, and came to a profound understanding: the authority of the Party committee derives from public credibility, not from "mystification"—the more open and transparent, the more trust is won; the more proactively oversight is accepted, the more support is gained. The affirmation and support of higher-level Party committees also strengthened our resolve. Drawing on the specific provisions of regulations across various functional domains, we studied and clarified what content the regulations permit to be made public during the deliberation process for various categories of sensitive matters, as well as the appropriate forms of disclosure commensurate with responsibilities and authority. Practice has proven that after the brigade Party committee opened the deliberation process on sensitive matters to public view, the cohesion of the leadership body became more consolidated, the grasp of the situation became more comprehensive and accurate, and the scientific quality of decision-making improved significantly.

Reporter: In connection with deepening political rectification training, what lessons does this initiative offer for strengthening grassroots-level building in the new era?

Director: Sunlight is the best disinfectant; openness is the strongest source of public credibility. Strengthening grassroots-level building in the new-era military requires that openness and transparency permeate the entire work process. Once the content that officers and soldiers care about, the basis for decisions, and the decision-making process are made open, formalism (形式主义) and bureaucratism (官僚主义) have nowhere to hide. Opening the decision-making process on sensitive matters can eliminate backroom operations and power-seeking from the source, ensuring that those who work hard are respected and rewarded, while those who seek shortcuts find no market and face sanction—establishing the orientation of "advancing on the basis of actual performance and standing on the strength of one's ability" (凭实绩进步、靠能力立身), cultivating a political ecology that is clean and upright, and guiding officers and soldiers to devote themselves wholeheartedly to mastering military skills and strengthening capabilities. Grassroots officers and soldiers are the main body of the force; letting them understand decisions and oversee work can strengthen their sense of ownership and激发 their sense of responsibility and mission. When every officer and soldier feels fairness and justice, the wholesome atmosphere of "everyone trusts the organization and everyone relies on the organization" will naturally take shape.

会议过程中，先由各机关科长汇报各课目考核组织与廉政监察情况。到了各营主官轮流汇报选人用人考量与拟推荐结果时，现场只留下了党委常委和正在汇报的营主官以及党代表。于是，关于这场公开党委会的“杂音”在基层散开：“为什么风气监督员离场了？虽然党代表在，但感觉还是遮遮掩掩的……” 该旅政治工作部主任说：“我们也很困惑，流程都走这么严了，怎么大家还不满意？”后来，他们利用挂钩帮建的时机，沉到基层调研，一名老兵的话发人深思：“考核严，全旅都看到了。但军士选晋又不是只看一次考试。谁平时干得多、谁任务冲在前，这些党组织是怎么考量的，大家心里都打鼓。” 该旅党委一班人意识到，之前的做法只是解决了“考核公不公正”，却没有触及“决策透不透明”。形式上的严格，不等于官兵感到了实质上的公正。官兵真正关心的不只是“谁考了多少分”，更是“党组织怎么评价我”“一次考得好和一直干得好，组织会选谁”这类实际问题。 严格的考核程序也令机关感到压力颇大。以作训科和人力资源科为例，为了保证考核过程没有瑕疵，几名机关干部绝大部分精力用在考核工作上，其他工作的进度受到了影响。 “过度拔高的标准，本身就是一种形式主义，只会消耗机关的精力和基层的信任，长久不了。”随着政治整训走深走实，该旅领导深刻反思：必须从实效出发，真正把“官兵想知道的”和“我们认为官兵想知道的”统一起来，合法合规地把决策过程“晒”在阳光下。 这次反思后，该旅党委聚焦更好地保证官兵的知情权，研究推行党委会公开的形式。最终，他们决定，以后党委常委研究表决环节，风气监督员不再回避。 一场“全透明”的议事—— 领导带头坦荡荡，官兵心里亮堂堂 “公开就要彻彻底底地公开，否则怎么对得起官兵的信任……” 该旅人力资源科时任科长乔海洋至今难以忘记，一名旅领导站在窗边看着营区内官兵训练时说的这句话。 被这名领导的决心感动之余，乔海洋心里也有些犯难：要怎么和其他几位旅领导汇报？ 没想到，旅领导们的反馈很一致—— “全程公开？这是好事，早该这么做！” “清清白白的事，就要坦坦荡荡地做。” “我完全支持，越是敏感的事越考验党性和作风。把政治整训落到实处，就是要敢在敏感事上用行动亮明态度！” …… 就这样，该旅第二次讨论涉及军士选晋事项的党委扩大会如期召开。与第一次会议议程不同的是，某个营汇报时，只留下了本营主官、党代表和风气监督员，但旅党委常委的讨论过程与表决过程，该营党代表和风气监督员均未回避。 回忆起那次会议，基层党代表、某营一级上士王拓连说“没想到”—— 一名旅领导随机询问某营教导员，该营党委推荐某位战士留队的原因。这名教导员从考核成绩排名、专业人才储备、日常表现、群众评议等方面，立足具体数据和材料一一做出解释，在座领导听得频频点头。 会议采取逐营汇报、逐营表决的方式，旅党委常委当着风气监督员和党代表的面进行讨论，并进行举手表决，当场唱票、当场计票、当场公布结果。 领导带头坦荡荡，官兵心里亮堂堂。列席官兵的所见所闻在旅队不胫而走—— “原来组织是这么考虑问题的，谁行谁不行，都有实打实的依据。” “以前总觉得谁选晋是领导一句话的事，现在才知道，他们每一票都投得很慎重。” 听到官兵的评价，一位与会常委深有感触：“一双双来自基层官兵的眼睛看着我做决策，能不慎重吗？研究每一名同志时，我们都格外审慎，理由必须绝对充分，经得起任何追问。” 信任的种子，在一次次公开中悄然发芽。军士选晋这个过去偶尔掺杂复杂因素的“敏感事”，如今变得不再那么敏感了。 该旅人力资源科张科长告诉记者，截至今年3月，该旅已经围绕军士选晋工作召开了4次党委扩大会，每一次，程序都很规范，公开都很彻底。如今，来自各营的风气监督员和党代表不仅能听取所在营的汇报，全旅每位留队官兵的情况，大家也能知道。 该旅某连连长高礼健至今记得，今年3月初送别退役老兵时，一名没能留队的战士郑重地对他说：“连长，我没留下，真的遗憾，但我走得服气。你放心，回家后我会继续努力！” 第82集团军某旅一名风气监督员（中）正在监督军事理论考核。陈力博摄 从“一件事”到“一类事”—— 让新风正气催生奋进动力 公开透明的党委会带来的“蝴蝶效应”，就像一场场春雨，润物无声地改变着基层的政治生态，也改变着每一名官兵的精神面貌。 该旅某连借鉴这个做法，在2025年度功勋荣誉表彰推荐工作中，打破了以往“关门定人选、事后贴公示”的模式。推荐结束后，支部专门召开全体军人大会，该连指导员王泽向官兵详细报告了三等功、嘉奖、“四有”优秀士兵的评选标准、推荐过程、民主测评结果和支部票决情况，并逐一说明党支部的决策原因。 “这下我知道了自己的差距在哪里，明年我会继续努力。”记者见到一级上士刁先群时，能从他眼中看到一种热切的坚定。和刁先群一样，该连那些未能如愿官兵的积极性大多没有受到影响。 “主动公开敏感事项议事过程，是为了以此带动旅队官兵转变思想、转变作风。”该旅领导一致认为，把领导个人的讨论意见、政策把握、票决结果全部摆在官兵面前，就意味着每一个决策都要经得起官兵的检验，经得起历史的检验，这就必须严格做到依规用权、审慎用权、廉洁用权，要坚决摒弃私心杂念，坚守党性原则。 过去，一部分官兵平时有“得过且过”的想法，快到选晋了才临时抱佛脚。现在，大家都知道，党组织看的是一贯表现，不是一时一事。“干好平时每一天，比什么都重要”成为官兵的共识。各级组织的决策得到了官兵的认同和拥护，更立起了“靠素质立身、凭实绩进步”的鲜明导向。 去年9月，某连中士谢中浩凭借过硬实力选晋成功。军衔晋升后不久，他被即将参加军士选晋的几位年轻战友请去当了强化训练的小教员。“大家相信组织、依靠组织，都想凭实绩进步。”谢中浩说。 记者对话第八十二集团军某旅政治工作部主任—— 公信，从公心中来 ■解放军报特约记者 刘 凡 当前，部队各级持续深化政治整训，严抓风纪整改，推动基层风气不断纯正、政治生态不断向好。如何在政治整训中直面敏感问题、以公开透明筑牢官兵信任根基？围绕这一话题，记者与第82集团军某旅政治工作部主任进行了对话交流。 记者：当初旅党委为何决心打破“闭门开会、事后公示”惯例？ 主任：过去，我们顾虑官兵议论，对官兵关切的敏感事项信息，仅公示结果、不公开过程。这看似合规，实则隔绝了有效监督，滋生了猜忌。表现突出者未如愿，易疑“关系”起作用；表现一般者可能把心思转向“关系场”；个别干部不敢坚持原则，使部分官兵动起“歪心思”。这些问题不解决会寒兵心、散军心、损战力。必须刀口向内自我革命，真正落实官兵的知情权、监督权，方能凝聚人心于组织周围。 记者：从“闭门议事”到“开门开会”，最大的困难是什么？如何克服？ 主任：最大的困难是党委班子自身的思想转变。公心是敢于公开的前提。主动将权力置于监督下，实践起来并不容易。当时我们也有顾虑：公开讨论意见是否合规？是否影响党委权威？对此，我们深入学习习主席重要论述和相关法规制度，深刻认识到：党委权威源于公信力，而非“神秘化”，越公开透明越能赢得信任，越主动接受监督越能获得支持。上级党委的肯定与支持也坚定了我们的决心。结合各业务领域法规具体条款，我们研究明确了各类敏感事项议事过程中法规允许的公开内容，以及权责相宜的公开方式。实践证明，旅党委公开敏感事项议事过程后，班子团结更巩固，掌握情况更全面真实，决策科学性显著提升。 记者：结合深化政治整训，此举对加强新时代基层建设有何启示？ 主任：阳光是最好的防腐剂，公开是最强的公信力。加强新时代军队基层建设，必须将公开透明贯穿工作全程。公开官兵关切的内容、决策依据和过程后，形式主义、官僚主义便无处遁形。公开敏感事项决策过程，能从源头杜绝暗箱操作和权力寻租，让实干者受尊重、得实惠，投机者没市场、受惩戒，树立“凭实绩进步、靠能力立身”导向，涵养风清气正的政治生态，引导官兵全身心精武强能。基层官兵是部队主体，让他们了解决策、监督工作，能增强其主人翁意识，激发责任感使命感。当每名官兵都感受到公平正义，“人人相信组织、人人依靠组织”的良好氛围自然形成。
