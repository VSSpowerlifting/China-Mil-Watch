Barracks Observation | Dream-Chasers on the "Dream Screen"

军营观察丨“梦想屏”上的追梦人
PLA Daily (解放军报) 29 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Political Instructor Zhang Wei of the Mechanized Infantry Third Company, a certain brigade of the 82nd Group Army, reversed a years-long motivational stagnation in his unit by introducing a public multimedia display he called the 梦想屏 (Dream Screen), on which soldiers posted personal goals — merit citations, skills competition placements, military academy admission — that were removed only when achieved; the company's first collective honor in twenty years, a second-class merit citation, followed within a year. The article documents a grassroots political work method for restoring 'striving culture' (争先创优) in a company that had gone dormant after the 2016–2020 PLA restructuring, a period when many units lost institutional momentum as formations were reorganized and honor lineages were disrupted. The framing points to ongoing PLA concern that post-restructuring units still carry motivational deficits that standard political education has not resolved, and positions peer accountability and public goal-setting as the preferred remedial tool at company level.

The Company's Honor Room Welcomes Three Second-Class Merits

■ PLA Daily Special Correspondent Qian Hao

"They're here! They're here!"

No one knew who shouted first, but the steps at the entrance of the Third Company of a certain brigade of the 82nd Group Army were instantly packed with people. Every gaze locked onto the military vehicle slowly pulling through the garrison gate.

The doors opened. The company commander, the political instructor, and one comrade stepped out with large red flowers pinned to their chests, holding gleaming second-class merit medals and plaques, their strides firm and resonant. Applause erupted and quickly swelled into a roaring sea. Two individual second-class merit medals and one collective second-class merit plaque were passed carefully from one pair of rough, strong hands to the next, then solemnly placed beneath the bright spotlight in the honor room.

For a moment, everyone fell silent in the honor room. Not because there was nothing to say, but because this honor had been waited for far, far too long.

After the restructuring and reform, this company seemed to have been forgotten by time in some corner. Training had not fallen behind; tasks were carried out without hesitation — yet the honor board remained as blank as a sheet of rice paper on which no brush had ever touched. One year, two years, three years… Ordinariness settled like a thick layer of dust, slowly covering everyone's heart. It was not that the officers and soldiers were not working hard — it was that after all the effort, there was no echo in return, and gradually, everyone seemed to lose the drive to strive for excellence and be first.

The word "dream," in the monotony of day after day, had become something extravagant and distant — until the arrival of these three second-class merits.

The night the medals and plaque were placed in the honor room, Political Instructor Zhang Wei noticed an interesting change: the multimedia display screen in the barracks foyer had come alive. Someone posted a photo of the medal with a four-character caption: "Next time, it's me." Someone replied below: "Get in line — I'm first." Someone else quietly changed their profile picture to an image of martial prowess…

Zhang Wei suddenly recalled how, when he was a new platoon leader, he had once stood transfixed before the yellowed old photographs in the honor room, imagining that someday his own name might be preserved there. But now, standing at the honor room doorway, watching the light reignite in those soldiers' eyes, he felt something different stir within him.

The greatest value of an outstanding leader of troops is not defined simply by medals and awards, but in ensuring that every soldier has a dream — and doing everything possible to help them realize it.

He glanced at the multimedia screen. Beneath that post, the profile pictures of the company's officers and soldiers were lighting up one by one. No comments — only silent likes, like lamps being lit one after another in the night.

Zhang Wei thought for a moment, then added a line of text: "Chase the wind and the moon without pause — beyond the flat wilderness lies a mountain of spring (追风赶月莫停留，平芜尽处是春山)."

Dream-Chasers on the "Dream Screen"

■ PLA Daily Special Correspondents Qian Hao and Li Zhuoyu

"I Want to Become Someone the Company Can Be Proud Of"

Inside the barracks foyer of the Third Company of a certain brigade of the 82nd Group Army, Political Instructor Zhang Wei was studying the large multimedia screen. Scrolling across it were rows of names and words — those who wanted to pursue further education, those who wanted to compete in skills competitions, those who wanted to earn merit citations, those who wanted to be named model soldiers… a dense, crowded display. This screen had become the liveliest spot in the company — everyone, coming or going, would involuntarily look up at it.

Looking at those words, Zhang Wei thought back to a year ago, when this screen had shown nothing but training schedules and duty rosters.

A year ago, Zhang Wei transferred from a staff officer position at a higher headquarters to take up the post of political instructor at the Third Company. Before he reported, a leader told him: "Third Company is a good company — it's just been dormant too long." Only when he actually arrived did he come to understand the taste of "dormant."

When the whole company assembled, the battle cry was not quiet, but it always felt like something was missing. During the five-kilometer armed cross-country run, no one surged to the front, and no one fell behind — everyone maintained a tacit "good enough" pace. He chatted with the soldiers and asked what goals they had. A veteran scratched his head: "Nothing much — just do the job well." He asked whether anyone had thought about earning merit citations. The veteran smiled: "How would that ever come to us? Things are fine as they are."

The tone was flat, but that "things are fine as they are" made Zhang Wei's heart tighten. He gradually worked it out: these soldiers did not lack the desire to improve — they had simply gone too long without tasting what improvement felt like. That night, Zhang Wei lay in bed tossing and turning, and suddenly recalled a line from a film: "Life is like archery, and dreams are like the target. If you can't even find the target, what's the point of drawing the bow every day?" He understood: he first had to help everyone find their "target" again.

At one political education class, he wrote only two characters on the blackboard: 梦想 — "dream." "No lecture today. Everyone take a turn coming up and talking about your dream."

The study room fell quiet for a moment. A senior private stood up: "This year I want to score excellent across the board in physical fitness." Right after, a sergeant stood up: "I want to be a good squad leader."

Dreams need not be grand or distant — as long as one dares to set up a "target" in one's heart, the most important step has already been taken. "Think some more, everyone. Whatever your dream is, say it boldly."

After a pause, a voice came from the very back row: "I want to become someone the company can be proud of."

The study room suddenly went quiet. Zhang Wei looked toward the voice. It was a young corporal named Zhang Zezhi.

Zhang Wei asked: "So what do you think it would take for the company to be proud of you?" Zhang Zezhi paused: "Placing in a skills competition? Or earning a merit citation? It's just — I feel like, if one day my comrades in the company mention my name, they'd all give a thumbs-up."

Zhang Wei nodded and led the applause.

That applause was like a key. More people stood up: someone said they wanted to pursue further education and become an officer; someone said they wanted to be a top-rated trainer; and one new soldier said timidly: "I want to earn a merit citation."

When that class ended, Zhang Wei said: "I've written down everyone's dreams. Starting tomorrow, they'll scroll on the multimedia screen in the company foyer every day. When a dream is realized, it comes down."

Someone laughed: "Instructor, isn't this just 'public exposure'?" Zhang Wei laughed too: "Exactly — I want you all to feel too embarrassed not to try."

The next morning, as soldiers walked into the foyer, they immediately saw their own names on the big screen. At first, everyone felt awkward. But gradually, everyone wanted to put their dream up there and "air it out."

At the start, Sergeant Ge Shuxiang wrote down his wish: to be rated a special-class marksman. When that goal was achieved, his name was taken down from the screen. But unwilling to let up, within a few days he voluntarily put himself back on the list, setting a new ambition: to compete for the title of outstanding "four-competency" instructor (优秀"四会"教练员).

Senior Private Zhang Shaowu's initial goal was simple — to bring his pull-up score up to the "good" standard. He achieved it in just one month. Zhang Wei saw that he had strong learning ability and a heart set on improvement, and continued to encourage him to set higher and more distant aspirations, pressing forward toward the goal of gaining admission to a military academy.

The officers' and soldiers' dreams filled the screen and were no longer soliloquies hidden in the heart — they were seen by the entire company. Some gave likes, some offered encouragement, some were moved to follow — these responses were the truest echo of a dream.

"How Good It Is to Have Comrades Striving Together"

Zhang Wei pressed play, and the television screen lit up at once. On screen, a young man was riding a motorcycle along a muddy mountain road, jolting with every bump.

"It's Zhang Xue!" a sharp-eyed soldier recognized him immediately. In the video, 19-year-old Zhang Xue had ridden over a hundred kilometers through the rain with a television production crew, all for one chance to show what he was made of. He fell again and again, and got up again and again. Finally, covered in mud, he stood before the camera, gasping, and shouted: "If you have a dream, go chase it! Because I was brave, my life is more brilliant!"

Sergeant Wang Yuwei, sitting in the third row, lowered his head and glanced at the ribbon rack on his chest. The second-class merit ribbon was fastened at the very front. A year ago, he would never have believed that ribbon would end up on his chest.

At the time, he had only recently become a squad leader. When the notice came down for the Group Army's "Hundred Squad Leaders" skills competition, the whole company was buzzing. Wang Yuwei shrank into a corner and said nothing — he felt his seniority was too shallow, and he had no confidence to go. Political Instructor Zhang Wei found him: "You're the only squad leader in the company who qualifies and hasn't signed up." Wang Yuwei looked down: "Instructor, I'm afraid I won't make it." Zhang Wei didn't respond to that — he just asked one question: "Do you want to go?"

In the end, Wang Yuwei signed up. But in the brigade's first preliminary selection, he barely scraped a passing score. That "pass" next to his name was harder to bear than elimination. That night, Wang Yuwei sat alone on the drill ground, thinking of going to find the instructor to say "forget it" — but Zhang Wei found him first.

"I'm also entering the political instructor competition," Zhang Wei said. "Let's keep each other company — I'll move my overtime work into the study room."

From that day on, in the study room, Zhang Wei sat on the left working through theory books, and Wang Yuwei sat on the right studying lesson plans. The two didn't talk much, but their shadows under the lamp leaned on each other. Wang Yuwei knew his foundation was weak, so he practiced his pronunciation in front of a mirror, correcting his mouth shape over and over. Zhang Wei never interrupted — he would only say something when Wang Yuwei paused: "That last run-through was better than yesterday."

Wang Yuwei's scores climbed bit by bit: from passing to good, from good to excellent. In the end, he placed near the top in the Group Army competition and was awarded a second-class merit citation. Zhang Wei also stood out in the political instructor competition and likewise earned a second-class merit citation.

Now, sitting in the study room, Wang Yuwei looked down at that second-class merit ribbon, and his nose stung. A year ago, he didn't believe dreams could come true. A year later, the dream was pinned to his chest.

"How good it is to have comrades striving together!" He looked up at Zhang Wei standing by the lectern. Without that lamp, without that "better than yesterday," he probably would never have believed that dreams could actually be realized.

Staff Sergeant Second Class Yang Wu felt as though something had suddenly struck him in the chest. He instinctively turned his head, and his gaze passed over several rows of comrades and met the eyes of Corporal Xue Haodong.

During last year's field training exercise, Yang Wu had been a shooting technician for less than a month — transitioning from gunner to instructor, he had a head full of experience but couldn't get it out. How to bring the whole company's shooting scores up was a difficult problem set before him. Similarly, Xue Haodong had just converted to infantry fighting vehicle gunner. Learning that combat shooting was about to begin, with his foundation not yet solid and his operations not yet fluent, he faced a difficult question of his own: go forward or not.

"I want to bring the whole company's shooting scores up." "I want to improve my own shooting scores." … On the big screen, two different dreams seemed to converge at the same point. Xue Haodong proactively sought out Yang Wu to ask for guidance on shooting techniques, and Yang Wu held nothing back, passing on everything he knew. At the same time, Yang Wu organized his teaching experience and developed a shooting lesson plan suited to the company's officers and soldiers.

Starlight does not fail those who press on. The painstaking study shared day and night, the diligent practice repeated day after day, all became the "admission ticket" to the dream. Later, the battalion commander announced the combat shooting results: the company's overall rating was excellent, and the vehicle crew to which Xue Haodong belonged was rated excellent.

"As long as you dare to chase a dream, there is nothing that cannot be achieved." The company's officers and soldiers were all running hard toward their own dreams, supporting one another, growing together, witnessing each other's continuous breakthroughs. Zhang Wei knew that more and more people had found the meaning of "dream."

Photo caption: The Third Company of a certain brigade of the 82nd Group Army regularly organizes award ceremonies for officers and soldiers who have realized their dreams. Photo by Chen Yi.

"I Also Want to Try — What If I Can Do It Too?"

When one person starts running, the people around them begin to move their feet. When several people start running, it becomes a wind that pushes against everyone's backs — carrying the "hesitant" forward, lifting the "weary" upward — like a calm lake surface stirred by wind into ripples, spreading outward in ring after ring.

"I also want to try — what if I can do it too?" Platoon Leader Hui Chengdong was the first who could no longer sit still. As a platoon leader who had graduated just over a year ago, many felt he was still too junior and needed more tempering. But he boldly wrote his dream on the screen: I want to earn a third-class merit citation.

He did not begin blindly. He set his sights on the ten-event all-around course at the brigade's military sports meet. From then on, his figure could often be seen putting in extra training on the track at the first light of dawn and at the equipment grounds as night fell…

Sergeant Li Jinshui, at last year's brigade military sports meet, won first place in the pull-up event outright. And his original dream, written at the start, was this: to prove to others that our company can do it too…

On that small screen, every dream was shining, and that light was illuminating the path for more and more people. A group of people gradually began to run, and one name after another moved from the screen into the honor room.

To sustain everyone's drive to keep chasing their dreams, the company launched a series of coordinated measures. Backbone personnel helped everyone break their dreams down into small, achievable sub-goals: if the goal was to get into a military academy, it was broken down into how many vocabulary words to memorize each week and how many problems to work; for those aiming to place in a skills competition, a weekly specialized training plan was drawn up. The road to a dream must be walked steadily, one step at a time, climbing rung by rung, before the destination can be reached.

A system of tiered accountability was also adopted: the company commander was responsible for the platoon leaders, the platoon leaders for the squad leaders, and the squad leaders for the soldiers in their squads. This was not supervision — it was a safety net. One person chasing a dream is easy to give up on, but when a whole row of people is standing behind you, you can't stop even if you want to.

Finally, whenever a dream was realized — no matter how large or small — it could be recorded in the honor room's ledger. It didn't have to be a merit citation or award: breaking a company record, successfully gaining admission to a military academy, developing an advanced squad — as long as it was accomplished, the name and story could be written in. Zhang Wei said: "A company's honor should be the trail of footprints left by everyone chasing their dreams."

The company's evenings gradually changed in character. In the study room, someone was bent over books; in the gym, someone was drenched in sweat…

Zhang Wei stood in the foyer, looking at the multimedia screen. It now had a new name: the Dream Screen (梦想屏) — the officers and soldiers had named it themselves. Looking at it, they were reminded of their own dreams and could see the direction they were heading. On the screen, some of the original dreams had already been taken down… The spaces left behind had been filled with new dreams and new names.

The screen kept scrolling. At the end of last year, the Third Company, in recognition of its outstanding performance throughout the year, was awarded a collective second-class merit citation. When the news came, the whole company erupted — this was the company's first major collective honor in twenty years.

Zhang Wei stood in the honor room, looking at the newly hung plaque on the wall, and opened the dog-eared ledger, thinking back to the moment a year ago when he had written the two characters 梦想 — "dream" — on the blackboard. Now, more and more dreams were appearing on the big screen, and more and more young lives were finding their "echo" (回响)…

“是张雪！”一位眼尖的战士一下认了出来。视频中19岁的张雪跟着电视台摄制组冒雨骑行上百公里，只为一次展现自己的机会。一次次摔倒，一次次爬起，最后，他浑身泥水地站在镜头前，喘着粗气喊出：“有梦就去追！因为勇敢，我的人生更精彩！” 坐在第三排的中士王渝伟，低头看了一眼胸前的勋表。那枚二等功略章，缀在最前方。一年前，他绝不相信这枚略章会挂到自己胸前。 那时他刚当班长不久，集团军“百名班长”比武通知下来，全连都在议论。王渝伟缩在角落没吭声——他觉得自己资历太浅，去了也没底气。指导员张伟找到他：“全连符合条件的班长，就差你没报了。”王渝伟低头：“指导员，我怕不行。”张伟没接话，只问了一句：“你想不想去？” 最后王渝伟还是报了名。可旅里第一次预选，他只是卡线及格，名字后面那个“及格”，比淘汰更让人难受。那晚王渝伟一个人坐在操场，想去找指导员说“算了吧”，可张伟却先一步找来了。 “我也要参加指导员比武。”张伟说，“咱俩搭个伴，我把加班的阵地搬到学习室去。” 从那天起，学习室里，张伟坐左边翻理论书，王渝伟坐右边研究教案。两人没有太多话，但灯下的影子彼此支撑着。王渝伟知道自己底子薄，对着镜子练发音，一遍遍纠口型。张伟从不打断，只偶尔在他停下来时说一句：“刚才那遍，比昨天好。” 王渝伟的成绩一点点往上挪：从及格到良好，从良好到优秀。最终，他在集团军比武中名列前茅，荣立二等功。张伟也在指导员比武中脱颖而出，同样将二等功收入囊中。 此刻，王渝伟坐在学习室里，低头看着那枚二等功略章，鼻子一酸。一年前，他不信梦想能实现。一年后，梦想就挂在胸前。 “有一起努力的战友，真好！”他抬头看了一眼讲台边的张伟。如果没有那盏灯，没有那句“比昨天好”，他大概永远都不会相信，梦想真的会实现。 二级上士杨武感觉心里好像猛地被什么东西撞了一下，他下意识地转过头，目光越过几排战友，正撞上下士薛浩东的眼睛。 去年野外驻训时，他刚上任射击技师不到一个月，从炮手到教头，满肚子经验却倒不出来。怎么把全连的射击成绩提上来，成了摆在他面前的一道难题。同样，薛浩东也是刚刚转为步兵战车射手，得知马上就要开展战斗射击，基础还没打牢，操作还不熟练的他面前也有一道难题：上还是不上。 “我想把全连的射击成绩提上去”“我想提升自己的射击成绩”……大屏上，两个不同的梦想好像有了同一个交点。薛浩东主动找到杨武，想向他请教射击知识，而杨武也毫无保留，将自己所学倾囊相授。同时，杨武也将教学经验进行梳理，打造适合连队官兵的射击教案。 星光不负赶路人。朝夕相伴的刻苦钻研，日复一日地勤学苦练，都化作通往梦想的“入场券”。后来，营长公布战斗射击成绩：连队总评优秀，薛浩东所在车组优秀。 “只要敢于追梦，没有什么是不能实现的。”连队官兵都在朝着自己的梦想努力奔跑，他们相互支持，共同成长，见证着彼此不断突破自我的过程。张伟知道，越来越多的人找到了“梦想”的意义。 第82集团军某旅三连定期为实现梦想的官兵组织颁奖仪式。陈 熠摄 “我也想试试，万一我也能行呢” 一个人跑起来，身边的人就会跟着迈开步子。几个人跑起来，就形成了风，推着所有人的后背，把“犹豫”的往前送，把“疲倦”的往上托，就像平静的湖面被风吹起涟漪，一圈一圈地荡开了。 “我也想试试，万一我也能行呢？”排长惠成东第一个坐不住了。作为一名毕业刚满一年的排长，很多人都觉得他资历尚浅，还需要多磨炼。但是他在屏幕上大胆写下自己的梦想：我要立三等功。 没有盲目开始，他瞄准了旅军事体育运动会上的十项全能课目。从此，晨曦微露的跑道上，夜幕低垂的器械场，经常能看到他加练的身影…… 中士李金水在去年的旅军事体育运动会上，一举夺得单杠引体向上第一名。而最初，他给自己定的梦想是——向别人证明，我们连也可以…… 方寸屏幕之上，每一份梦想都在闪光，而这些光，正在照亮更多人的路。一群人慢慢开始跑了起来，一个个名字从屏幕走进荣誉室。 为了激发大家持续追梦的动力，连队打出了一系列“组合拳”。骨干帮大家把梦想拆成一个个可以实现的小目标：想考上军校，就拆解成每周背多少单词，做多少题；想比武获得名次的，制定每周的专项训练计划。通往梦想的路途需要踏实走好每一步，爬上一级级台阶，才能到达终点。 同时采取逐级负责的办法，连长对排长负责，排长对班长负责，班长对班内战士负责。这不是监督，是托底。一个人追梦容易放弃，但当你身后站着一排人的时候，想停都停不下来。 最后，但凡实现梦想的，无论大小，都能被记录在荣誉室的那本册子里。不一定是立功受奖，破了连队纪录、考学成功、带出了先进班——只要做到了，名字和故事就能写进去。张伟说：“一个连队的荣誉，应该是所有人的追梦足迹。” 连队的夜晚渐渐变了模样。学习室里有人埋头看书，健身房里有人挥汗如雨…… 张伟站在门厅里，看着那块多媒体大屏，现在它有了新的名字：梦想屏，这是官兵们给它起的，看到它，就想起了自己的梦想，看到了自己前进的方向。屏幕上，有些最初的梦想已经被撤下来了……空出来的地方，又补上了新的梦想、新的名字。 屏幕还在继续滚动。去年年底，三连因全年工作表现突出，荣立集体二等功。消息传来，全连沸腾，这是连队时隔20年再一次获得重要集体荣誉。 张伟站在荣誉室里，看着墙上新挂上去的牌匾，翻开那本已经卷了边的册子，想起一年前他在黑板上写下“梦想”两个字的时候。现在，越来越多的梦想在大屏上出现，越来越多的青春在“回响”……
