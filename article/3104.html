Examining the Real-Combat Grounding of Political Rectification Training Through the Correction of a Fuel Support Shortfall

从纠治一次燃油保障短板看开展政治整训的实战落点
PLA Daily (解放军报) 10 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A staff officer from the PAP Guigang Detachment's Operations and Training Section describes how a fuel shortfall during a real flood-relief operation—assault boats running critically low on premixed fuel while maneuvering across multiple disaster sites—exposed systemic empiricism and inertial thinking (经验主义与惯性思维) in war-readiness planning, prompting the detachment Party committee to launch a special after-action review and overhaul contingency plans, stockpile standards, and training content. The article inaugurates PLA Daily's new 'Analysis of Typical Cases' column under the political rectification training (政治整训) framework, and its value is as a baseline record of how the PAP institutionally frames the gap between peacetime routine and real-combat demands for junior officers. The framing points to persistent concern at the unit level that extended stability has eroded adversarial awareness (敌情意识) and bottom-line thinking (底线思维)—a problem the column is explicitly designed to surface and correct through documented case studies.
Translation

The floodwaters recede, exposing the "thorn" lodged in the heart — Examining the real-combat grounding of political rectification training (政治整训) through the correction of a fuel support shortfall

■ Liang Yu, Staff Officer, Operations and Training Section, PAP Guigang Detachment

Editor's Note: Problems are the voice of the times, and also the targets of rectification training. In carrying out all work related to training for war preparedness, ideological corrosion (思想上的锈蚀) is often more dangerous than the rusting of guns and artillery. Correcting empiricism and inertial thinking (经验主义与惯性思维) in training for war preparedness, and breaking through war-readiness shortfalls, is precisely the essential meaning of driving political rectification training deeper and grounding it more firmly in reality.

Beginning today, the PLA Daily launches the "Analysis of Typical Cases" column, which, through "dissecting the sparrow" (解剖麻雀) and perceiving the greater through the smaller, presents the vigorous practice of grassroots units in correcting entrenched problems and advancing development through political rectification training. Through one typical case after another carrying the "smell of soil" and "smell of gunpowder," the column records the vivid stories of Party organizations at all levels and the broad ranks of Party member officers and soldiers tempering Party character (党性) through the correction of problems, forging work style through after-action review and self-examination, and elevating capabilities through identifying and filling gaps. We invite your attention.

"After continuously maneuvering across multiple disaster-affected sites, the premixed fuel for the outboard motors of the assault boats was insufficient, requiring on-site improvised mixing, which posed an extremely serious hazard."

At the after-action review of the flood-relief operation conducted by the Mobile Company of the PAP Guigang Detachment, the remarks of Corporal Xiao Hou struck me — sitting nearby as a participant in the review — like a sudden weight in my chest, plunging me into deep self-criticism and reflection.

In fact, more than a month earlier, when we were deploying preparatory work for flood prevention and emergency response, our working group had conducted a special assessment of the fuel support requirements for the assault boats. Based on a comprehensive analysis of the flood conditions at the garrison over the years, everyone concluded that no large-scale severe inundation had occurred in the area for more than a decade; even the most serious flood-relief operation two years prior had consisted mainly of reinforcing embankments, with the assault boats deployed infrequently and covering short distances. On the basis of the assessment that the probability of an extreme situation arising was not great, we made the judgment: premixed fuel is not suitable for long-term storage after mixing; given the limited intensity of flood conditions in previous years, preparing too much would result in expiration and scrapping, easily causing waste; calculated roughly according to the task situation in previous years, conventional combat-readiness requirements would be fully met. In the end, the allocation quantity was fixed according to the established conventional standard.

Reality, however, developed precisely in the direction of "not very probable." When the floodwaters swept in, officers and soldiers drove the assault boats to maneuver continuously for hours across a vast expanse of water, moving between multiple disaster-affected sites, and fuel consumption far exceeded that of any previous mission. By the time the unit transferred to the third disaster-affected area, the reserve premixed fuel began to run critically low. Officers and soldiers could only use bulk fuel carried on the vehicles to mix on the spot, with the ratio controlled entirely by experience. The rescue soldiers feared both that an incorrect mixing ratio would damage the engines and that delays would cost precious rescue time, and for a time were in a state of frantic confusion. The panic of that period has since lodged itself in the hearts of the rescue commanders like a thorn that cannot be pulled out.

In this round of reflection on the problem, I did not evade responsibility, but instead stepped forward to conduct a thorough self-criticism: the shortage of a few cans of fuel seems like a small matter, but what it reflects is an inertial pattern of thinking (思维惯性) that has long been latent within me in the work of training for war preparedness.

Political rectification training was born of war and is rectified for war; its ultimate objective is to forge a steel force capable of winning battles. Over the years, the detachment's real-combat-oriented (实战化) development has been continuously advanced, and the overall war-readiness foundation is solid. The detachment Party committee has organized study and discussion on multiple occasions, repeatedly emphasizing that preparing for war and fighting must be treated as the first priority, driving the deep integration of political rectification training with real-combat-oriented training and major exercise and training missions, so that Party member officers and soldiers are tested under complex environments and extreme conditions, and tempered in the hard skills needed to win through resolving the problems of "two insufficiencies" (两个能力不够) and "five incapabilities" (五个不会).

During study and discussion, I understood all of these principles, but when it came to concrete practice, I still could not break free from the constraints of old experience. "On the battlefield, every kind of extreme situation can occur. In peacetime, lacking adversarial awareness (敌情意识) and planning inadequately — only doing things by the book and by convention — means that once an unexpected situation arises, all one can do is watch helplessly as things fall apart." This experience gave me a lesson burned into my bones.

After the after-action review concluded, I systematically organized these problems into a report and submitted it to the detachment. The detachment Party committee acutely recognized that this was not a minor matter of a single category of material stockpile, but rather a common problem caused by empiricism and inertial thinking in war-readiness work, and decided to use this as an entry point to conduct a special after-action review and examination of war-readiness work.

In the course of the examination, we discovered that the "unexpected" situations exposed by this flood-relief operation extended far beyond fuel alone: it was unexpected that the rate at which the flood situation escalated far exceeded that of the same period in previous years, and the conventional response tempo could not keep pace with the changing disaster conditions; it was unexpected that the scale of civilian evacuation exceeded the estimates in the contingency plans, leaving life-saving and resettlement supplies insufficient; it was unexpected that the complex aquatic environment far exceeded the scenarios of routine training, and many special-situation responses had no ready-made contingency plans to follow…

Every single one of these, at root, demonstrated that the war-readiness string was not drawn taut enough, that there remained a habit of using old experience to address new situations, and of using conventional standards to anticipate extreme risks.

Having reflected deeply on the pain, the detachment Party committee took the lead in conducting comprehensive rectification. Departments across the headquarters proactively claimed responsibility for problems and converted the fruits of reflection into institutional mechanisms. Each department, benchmarked against real-combat standards, launched a "look-back" (回头看) review of the contingency plan system, revising and improving all categories of contingency plans for sudden-onset missions, refining response procedures for extreme scenarios, and ensuring that contingency plans are transformed from "documents in a drawer" into "action guides ready for use at any moment." A comprehensive review of material stockpile standards across all domains was conducted, and a tiered-response, dynamically escalating stockpile mechanism was established. Real-combat-oriented training content was adjusted to add subjects including extended continuous operations, maneuver without rear-area resupply, and special-situation response in complex environments, breaking fixed exercise patterns.

In the course of rectification, the operations and training department took the lead in adjusting the stockpile model, separately sealing gasoline and engine oil in matched containers according to the mixing ratio, and establishing a flood-warning-linked replenishment mechanism — when the warning level is elevated, supplies are simultaneously adjusted and allocated as needed, both avoiding idle waste and ensuring sufficient stockpiles under extreme conditions.

The floodwaters have receded, but the scene of mixing fuel on the spot keeps surfacing in my mind. This thorn lodged in my heart keeps me ever vigilant: in preparing for war and fighting, there has never been a fixed answer that settles the matter once and for all. Thinking one layer further toward risk, and pressing one degree closer to real combat, is the only way to seize those most critical few minutes when each test arrives.

(Compiled by Pan Xin, Ni Xiuhao, and reporter Chen Xiaojie)

Reporter's Notes

The "Unexpected" Is the True Test

A minor material shortfall in a flood-relief operation reflects deep-seated problems that exist broadly in the domain of emergency war preparedness. Compared with the visible shortfalls in equipment and materiel, the inertia and experience-dependence embedded deep in thinking are the most concealed and most lethal risks constraining training for war preparedness.

"Of all the troubles under heaven, the most impossible to address is that which goes by the name of peace and order with nothing happening, while in reality harboring unforeseen calamity." A prolonged stable environment, year after year of routine missions, and ossified work standards can very easily cause officers and soldiers to develop a "habitual sense of security" (惯性安全感). Defining future risks by past experience, responding to extreme contingencies with conventional plans, and substituting established standards for extreme-condition calculations — this may appear to be acting by the rules and performing one's duties prudently, but in reality it reflects insufficient baseline thinking (底线思维) and inadequate warfighting consciousness.

The essence of training for war preparedness is to contend with uncertainty. The reason battlefield special situations and sudden contingencies are so difficult to handle is precisely that they are always "unexpected." Clinging to old experience and old methods, entrusting safety to luck and victory to chance, is a form of habitual cognitive laziness — a formalism (形式主义) that must be resolutely guarded against in war-readiness work.

Confronting the changes in technology, the changes in warfare, and the changes in adversaries, all levels must use political rectification training as the leading force, correct the formalism of "training to be watched, exercises to be watched" (练为看、演为看), break through the peacetime accumulated malady of "not conducting training when danger looms, not drilling troops when risk is present" (危不施训、险不练兵), dig out root causes from the depths of thinking, and apply real effort to correcting entrenched problems, making the most thorough preparations based on the worst-case assumptions. Always thinking one layer further toward risk and preparing one additional contingency is the only way to stabilize the overall situation amid changing circumstances, and to seize total victory amid the unexpected.

Original Chinese
洪水退去，露出扎在心头的那根“刺” ——从纠治一次燃油保障短板看开展政治整训的实战落点 ■武警贵港支队作训股参谋 梁 宇 编者按问题是时代的声音，也是整训的靶标。开展练兵备战各项工作，思想上的锈蚀往往比枪炮的锈蚀更为可怕，纠治练兵备战中的经验主义与惯性思维，破解备战短板，正是政治整训往深里走、往实里落的题中之义。 即日起，解放军报开设“典型实例解析”专栏，通过“解剖麻雀”、见微知著，呈现基层部队开展政治整训中纠治积弊、推动建设的火热实践。透过一个个带着“泥土味”“硝烟味”的典型实例，记录各级党组织和广大党员干部在纠治问题中历练党性、在复盘检视中锤炼作风、在查漏补缺中提升本领的鲜活故事。敬请关注。 “连续转战多个受灾点位，冲锋舟舷外机预混燃油准备不足，现场临时调配，存在极大隐患。” 在武警贵港支队机动中队抗洪抢险复盘会上，中士小侯的发言，让坐在一旁参与复盘的我心头猛地一沉，陷入深深的检讨与反思。 其实，一个多月前部署防汛抢险筹备工作时，我们工作组就曾专题研判过冲锋舟油料保障需求。结合驻地历年汛情综合分析，大家认为，驻地十几年未曾发生过大范围严重积涝，最严重的前年抗洪，也不过以加固堤坝为主，冲锋舟动用频次低、行驶里程短。基于出现特情几率不大的考虑，我们作出判断：预混燃油调配后不宜久存，往年汛情强度有限，配多了到期报废，容易造成浪费，按往年任务情况大致测算，常规战备需求完全够用。最终，调配量按既定常规标准敲定。 然而，现实偏偏朝着“几率不大”的方向发展了。当洪水席卷而来，官兵驾冲锋舟在汪洋中连续机动数小时、辗转多个受灾点位，燃油消耗量远超以往历次任务。转战至第三片受灾区域时，备用预混燃油开始告急。官兵只能用随车携带的散装油料现场调配，比例全凭经验把控。搜救战士既怕配比偏差损伤机器，又怕耽搁搜救时机，一时手忙脚乱。那段时间的慌乱，从此扎在救援指挥员心头，成为一根拔不掉的“刺”。 这次反思问题，我没有回避，而是主动站出来作了深刻检讨：几桶燃油的短缺，看似是小事，映照出的，却是自己长期在练兵备战中潜藏已久的思维惯性。 政治整训因战而生、为战而整，最终目标是锻造能打胜仗的钢铁劲旅。这些年，支队实战化建设持续推进，整体战备基础牢靠。支队党委多次组织学习讨论，反复强调要把备战打仗作为第一要务，推动政治整训与实战化训练、重大演训任务深度融合，让党员干部在复杂环境、极限条件下经受考验，在解决“两个能力不够”“五个不会”等问题中锤炼打赢硬功。 学习讨论时，这些道理我都明白，但到了具体实践中，还是没能跳出老经验的束缚。“在战场上，什么极端情况都有可能发生。平时缺乏敌情意识、筹划不周，只会按规定办、按惯例干，一旦遇到突发情况，就只能眼睁睁看着掉链子。”这次经历，给了我一次刻骨铭心的教训。 复盘结束，我将这些问题系统梳理，形成报告上报支队。支队党委敏锐察觉到，这不是某一项物资储备的小事，而是战备工作中经验主义、惯性思维导致的共性问题，决定以此为切入点，开展一次战备工作专项复盘检视。 检视中，我们发现，这次抗洪暴露出的“没想到”，远不止燃油一项：没想到汛情升级速度远超历年同期，常规响应节奏跟不上灾情变化；没想到群众转移规模超出预案测算，救生、安置类物资储备不足；没想到复杂水域环境远超日常训练场景，不少特情处置没有现成预案可循…… 桩桩件件，归根到底，都说明战备弦绷得不够紧，仍习惯用老经验应对新情况，用常规标准预判极端风险。 痛定思痛，支队党委牵头开展全面整改，机关各部门主动认领问题，将反思成果转化为制度机制。各部门对照实战标准，启动预案体系“回头看”，重新修订完善各类突发任务预案，细化极端场景处置流程，确保预案从“抽屉里的文本”变成“随时能用的行动指南”；全面梳理各领域物资储备标准，建立分级响应、动态提级的储备机制；调整实战化训练内容，增加超时长连续作业、无后方补给机动、复杂环境特情处置等课目，打破固定演练模式。 整改中，作训部门牵头调整储备模式，将汽油与机油按调配比例分桶配套封存，并建立汛情预警联动补充机制——预警升级时同步按需调配，既避免闲置浪费，也保证极端情况下存量充足。 洪水已退去，可那个现场兑油的情景，总在我脑海里浮现。这根扎在心头的“刺”，让我时刻警醒：备战打仗，从来没有一劳永逸的固定答案。多往风险处想一层，多往实战上靠一分，才能在每一次考验来临时，抢出那最关键的几分钟。 （潘昕、倪秀豪、记者陈晓杰整理） 记者手记 “意料之外”才是真考验 一场抗洪抢险中的物资小纰漏，折射出应急备战领域普遍存在的深层问题。相比于装备、物资的显性短板，思想深处的惰性与经验依赖，才是制约练兵备战最隐蔽、最致命的风险。 “天下之患，最不可为者，名为治平无事，而其实有不测之忧。”长期平稳的环境、连年常规的任务、固化的工作标准，极易让官兵滋生“惯性安全感”。用过往经验定义未来风险，用常规预案应对极端变局，用既定标准替代极限考量，看似依规办事、稳妥履职，实则是底线思维欠缺、打仗意识不足。 练兵备战的本质是与不确定性博弈。战场特情和突发情况之所以难以应付，就因为永远在“意料之外”。固守老经验、老套路，把安全寄托于运气、把胜利寄托于侥幸，是一种习惯性思维怠惰，是战备工作中必须力戒的形式主义。 面对科技之变、战争之变、对手之变，各级必须以政治整训为牵引，纠治“练为看、演为看”的形式主义，破除“危不施训、险不练兵”的和平积弊，从思想深处挖根源、在纠治积弊上用实劲，以最坏的打算做最充分的准备。时刻多想一层风险、多备一手后手，才能于变局中稳大局，于意外中夺全胜。
