Correct Outlook on Achievements, Practiced in the Barracks | "Plant the Cedar" at the Grassroots Level

正确政绩观 践行在军营｜“雪松”种到基层去
PLA Daily (解放军报) 16 June 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
The People's Armed Police Yunnan General Corps Dali Detachment has institutionalized a weekly 'Grassroots Work Day' (基层工作日) in which a standing committee member leads unannounced working groups from headquarters departments directly into squads, platoons, storerooms, and training grounds — producing, over several months, 87 documented problems, 42 resolved on the spot and 35 within a set deadline. The program is framed explicitly around correcting a distorted 政绩观 (outlook on achievements), using local Party model Yang Shanzhou as the ideological anchor. The article documents a PAP detachment-level response to a recognized institutional pathology — headquarters cadres managing grassroots units through documents and meetings rather than direct presence — and fits a broader PLA and PAP campaign to enforce mass-line work style, though the detachment's own summary meeting concedes the roots are 'just beginning to take hold.'
Translation

"Plant the Cedar" at the Grassroots Level

■ Meng Wei, Li Qican, PLA Daily Reporter Li Youzhi Illustration: Fang Han

Among the mountain ranges of western Yunnan, Shanzhou Tree Farm is set into the vast land like a piece of jade. What were once barren hills strewn with gravel are now a sea of swaying pines and lush greenery.

At the foot of those mountains, before the morning mist had lifted, the camp broadcast of the People's Armed Police Yunnan General Corps Dali Detachment sounded reveille. In front of the headquarters building, another group of leaders and headquarters cadres participating in "Grassroots Work Day" were gathering their gear and preparing to set out……

The Cang Mountains layered in green, the Erhai Lake rippling with waves. Inside the detachment's training regiment, a scene of vigorous work and training was underway. Unlike ordinary days, those busy in every corner of the camp included not only the training regiment's officers and soldiers, but also leaders and headquarters cadres moving briskly about.

"Instructor Cao, we heard the communications signal at the training regiment has been somewhat unstable—today we came specifically to fix that." Walking into the online learning room, the leader heading the group sat down at a computer to check various network parameters. Nearby, several headquarters cadres were bent over adjusting the network signal in the server room.

The headquarters cadres moved with practiced ease; the grassroots officers and soldiers took it as a matter of course. Such a harmonious and familiar scene has now become the norm at the detachment.

Previously, things were not like this. When difficulties arose at the grassroots level, officers and soldiers often had to run back and forth to the headquarters to report them—that word "run" carried within it the exhaustion and helplessness of going back and forth repeatedly. When leaders and headquarters cadres went down to the grassroots, that word "down" was not without the air of superiority of the "headquarters attitude" and the emotional distance it implied.

Today, "going down" (下) to the grassroots has become "going to" (到) the grassroots. A change of one character reflects a transformation in the outlook on achievements (政绩观) of the detachment's Party committee and headquarters. That transformation begins with a cedar tree.

Shanzhou Tree Farm is not far from the detachment's garrison. It is both an "ecological oasis" and a "spiritual treasure trove." Officers and soldiers are well acquainted with the story of Yang Shanzhou, the former secretary of the Baoshan Prefectural Party Committee of Yunnan Province, and the Party committee and headquarters have organized Party members and cadres on multiple occasions to conduct special study sessions there and receive ideological edification.

"Communist Party members should not hide in headquarters offices growing as potted ornamentals (盆景); they should go among the people and grow as cedars (雪松)." This was a favorite saying of Yang Shanzhou during his lifetime.

These words were copied neatly by the detachment's Political Commissar Li onto the flyleaf of his notebook, beside which he had pasted an old photograph clipped from a newspaper: Yang Shanzhou standing on Mount Daliang, a towering cedar rising behind him.

Political Commissar Li often recounted this story during Party lessons: when Secretary Yang Shanzhou retired, he carried the potted ornamentals he had tended for many years up to Mount Daliang. That cedar, which had originally grown in an office, took root in the barren mountain soil, weathered wind and rain, and ultimately grew into a towering tree.

The old secretary transplanted the cedar from its pot onto Mount Daliang, and in doing so planted his own roots in the barren mountain earth and planted his heart among the people. For 22 years he never ceased his voluntary tree-planting and afforestation work, ultimately handing over to the Party and the people a "green treasury" worth 300 million yuan free of charge—erecting a monument of achievement (政绩丰碑) to the wholehearted dedication of Communist Party members to the people.

"Do some of our leaders and headquarters cadres resemble potted ornamentals?" At a special self-examination session held as part of the study and education campaign on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on achievements (正确政绩观), Political Commissar Li pointed to an elegantly shaped potted ornamental on the windowsill and said: "Staying inside the office building, writing reports that sound perfectly organized, revolving around the office, the conference table, and briefing materials—some people think this constitutes 'achievements.' But sitting in the headquarters building, where the door is easy to enter and faces are easy to look at, things are not necessarily easy to get done. In the eyes of grassroots officers and soldiers, isn't that just a collection of exquisite 'potted ornamentals'?"

"Do we truly understand what the grassroots urgently needs and worries about? Have we planted our roots in the hearts of officers and soldiers?"

The questions struck at people's hearts, and the room fell silent. Behind that silence lay a distortion in the outlook on achievements. In recent years, although the detachment had consistently required adherence to the principle of "grassroots first, soldiers first," investigations revealed that many leaders and headquarters cadres had grown accustomed to sitting in their offices, following routine procedures, handling official business through the "back-and-forth of documents and messages," while lacking the "presence of heart when people come" that comes from putting oneself in others' shoes and serving wholeheartedly. Grassroots needs were mostly left unaddressed until formally reported upward, and some matters were even left unresolved for extended periods due to poor information flow and layer-upon-layer review.

During the study and education campaign on establishing and practicing a correct outlook on achievements, in order to help leaders and headquarters cadres clear away ideological confusion and cultivate a sense of dedication to soldiers (为兵情怀), the detachment arranged an on-site visit and study session, organizing everyone to go to the local "Yang Shanzhou Memorial Forest" and revisit the moving deeds of the old secretary Yang Shanzhou.

"The old secretary spent his life working and building—not by sitting in an office issuing orders, but by staying in the fields during droughts, planting trees and collecting fruit seeds, leading by example in everything, putting hard work first in every place, and at all times working for the benefit of the people……" Strolling through the forest and listening to one moving story after another about Yang Shanzhou, many leaders and headquarters cadres fell into deep reflection.

"Yang Shanzhou moved the potted cedar from his office up to the mountains—why can't we move our desks to the training ground?" The detachment decided to establish "Grassroots Work Day": each week, one member of the Party committee standing committee leads a team, with personnel dispatched from each headquarters department to form a working group. Without prior notice, without listening to briefings, without requiring an escort, they go directly into squads and platoons, into storerooms, and onto training grounds, listening to officers' and soldiers' voices on the front line and resolving urgent needs and concerns.

Plant the "cedar" in the soil of the grassroots; write "achievements" on the hearts of officers and soldiers.

On the first "Grassroots Work Day," medical officer Li Yunxi of the medical company accompanied the working group into the Second Guard Company—the most remote unit in the entire detachment. This company's garrison is located between two mountain ranges and three counties, in a remote location with rugged mountain roads; the one-way drive from headquarters exceeds five hours, making it extremely inconvenient for officers and soldiers to seek medical care.

After a bumpy journey to the company, Li Yunxi immediately took out diagnostic equipment and conducted health examinations for the officers and soldiers. In a single day, he identified more than ten cases of training injuries and chronic conditions—all mild, in the early stages of onset. Shortly afterward, a brief meeting was convened in the company's study room, where the detachment working group deliberated on the spot and worked out a support mechanism: the medical company would rotate selected backbone personnel on a quarterly basis to provide on-site medical services, resolving the difficulty officers and soldiers faced in accessing medical care.

Not long after, Li Yunxi returned to the company, this time bringing various medical supplies and equipment. The first thing he did was conduct a person-by-person survey of health conditions and establish electronic health records for the company's officers and soldiers. During his stay, he conducted daily scheduled "ward rounds and consultations": walking through every dormitory and every guard post, learning in detail about the health conditions, diet, and daily routines of officers and soldiers, conducting thorough physical examinations, proactively relieving ideological stress, and delivering heartfelt care to the front-line grassroots.

The "cedar" is planted at the grassroots, its branches and leaves growing ever greener. Leafing through the work log of "Grassroots Work Day," the entries are detailed and specific, line by line: updating training equipment, optimizing combat-readiness facilities, coordinating school enrollment for soldiers' children…… Every grassroots request is clearly registered, and every problem has a clearly defined division of responsibility, a deadline for rectification, and measures for follow-through.

After Corporal Xiao Liu's family moved to join him at the garrison, the question of enrolling his child in a nearby school became a source of worry for the couple. During a "Grassroots Work Day" visit and heart-to-heart talk, detachment leaders learned of the situation and immediately took the lead in following up and coordinating, proactively contacting the local education authorities multiple times, repeatedly going through procedures and handling paperwork, and ultimately resolving the child's enrollment—untying the knot in Xiao Liu's heart and enabling him to devote himself fully to work and training.

Several months later, the detachment convened a phased summary meeting for "Grassroots Work Day." A set of vivid figures was placed on the table: 87 problems collected in total, 42 resolved on the spot, 35 resolved within a set deadline, and 10 addressed with explanations—with officer and soldier satisfaction noticeably improved.

"The old secretary Yang Shanzhou moved the cedar from its pot up to Mount Daliang and spent 22 years planting a whole forest. We have only been at this for a few months, and we've identified 87 problems—that sounds like a lot, but think about it: how many of these problems should we have discovered and resolved long ago? Now that people have gone down there, the roots are just beginning to take hold. But we are still far from being a 'cedar,' and even further from being 'an entire forest.'" At the meeting, detachment leaders examined their shortcomings with clear heads: "Serving soldiers should mean every day is a 'Grassroots Work Day.'"

The "cedar question" (雪松之问) strikes at the hearts of the detachment's leaders and headquarters cadres, inspiring everyone to establish and practice a correct outlook on achievements—to "plant" themselves as cedars in the broad soil of the grassroots, to sink their roots into the earth, and to remain always heart-to-heart with officers and soldiers.

Viewpoint

Do Not Hide in Headquarters Growing as a "Potted Ornamental"

■ Zhang Yuqing

"Communist Party members should not hide in headquarters offices growing as potted ornamentals; they should go among the people and grow as cedars." Yang Shanzhou's words have crossed time and space to take root in the People's Armed Police Dali Detachment, transforming into concrete action by leaders and headquarters cadres to establish and practice a correct outlook on achievements.

A potted ornamental sits comfortably in a greenhouse, possessing only a polished appearance, unable to withstand wind and rain; a cedar takes root in the mountains and fields, tempered through cold and heat, and only then can it stand firm and unyielding. In reality, a small number of leaders and headquarters cadres rely on documents to serve the grassroots, rely on meetings to drive work forward, and rely on notifications to resolve problems. This may appear to be fulfilling their duties, but in fact they are detached from the grassroots. Such leaders and headquarters cadres are precisely like "exquisite potted ornamentals"—attractive in appearance but shallow-rooted, unable to withstand wind and rain, unable to develop real capabilities, let alone achieve genuine results.

Not being a "potted ornamental" but being a "cedar" is, in essence, a transformation in work style (作风). Sitting in headquarters, one reads documents and listens to briefings—the further one is from the grassroots, the more easily the outlook on achievements goes astray. Going among officers and soldiers, one breathes the soldiers' air and hears the soldiers' situation—naturally one knows "what to do and how to do it." Leafing through the People's Armed Police Dali Detachment's "Grassroots Work Day" work log, it is not difficult to see that those practical matters that have benefited the grassroots and satisfied officers and soldiers were not conjured up while sitting in an office—they were accomplished by leaders and headquarters cadres who, like "cedars," sank down to the front line, braved sun and wind, and worked with their feet planted firmly on the ground. This news item reminds leaders and cadres at all levels that only by planting roots in the grassroots and keeping hearts close to officers and soldiers can one achieve real results and achieve results that are truly real.

“雪松”种到基层去 ■孟 伟 李奇灿 解放军报记者 李由之 插图：方 汉 滇西的群峰之间，善洲林场如一块翡翠镶嵌于苍茫大地。曾经石砾遍地的荒山，如今已松涛如海、绿意葱茏。 群山脚下，晨雾还未散去，武警云南总队大理支队的营区广播便响起号声。机关楼前，又一批参加“基层工作日”的领导和机关干部整理行装、准备出发…… 苍山叠翠，洱海扬波。支队教导队里，一派热火朝天的工作、训练景象。与往日不同的是，忙碌在营区各个角落的，除了教导队官兵外，还有步履匆匆的领导和机关干部。 “曹指导员，听说教导队通信信号不太稳定，今天我们专门来解决这个问题。”走进网络学习室，带队领导坐到电脑前，查看各项网络参数。不远处，几名机关干部正俯身调试机房网络信号。 机关干部轻车熟路，基层官兵习以为常。这样融洽熟络的场景，如今在支队已成常态。 此前，情形并非如此。基层有困难，官兵常常要跑机关反映，一个“跑”字里，包含基层官兵辗转往返的疲惫与无奈。领导和机关干部下基层，一个“下”字里，不乏高高在上的“机关架子”和情感隔阂。 如今，“下”基层变成了“到”基层。一字之变，折射的是该支队党委机关政绩观的转变。转变，要从一棵雪松说起。 善洲林场离支队驻地不远，这里既是“生态绿洲”，更是“精神富矿”，官兵对云南省原保山地委书记杨善洲的故事耳熟能详，党委机关更是多次组织党员干部开展专题学习，接受思想洗礼。 “共产党员不要躲在机关里做盆景，要到人民群众中去当雪松。”这是杨善洲生前的一句口头禅。 这句话被支队李政委整齐地抄在笔记本扉页上，旁边还贴了一张从报纸上剪下的老照片：杨善洲站在大亮山上，身后是一棵高耸入云的雪松。 李政委经常在党课中给大家讲起这个故事：杨善洲老书记退休时，把自己养了多年的盆景搬上了大亮山。那棵原本生长在办公室里的雪松，在贫瘠的山地扎下根去，任凭风吹雨打，最终长成了参天大树。 老书记把盆景中的雪松种在了大亮山，也把自己的根扎在了荒山大地，把心“扎”在了群众中间。22载义务植树造林不辍，将一座价值3亿元的“绿色宝库”无偿交给党和人民，立起了共产党人赤心为民的政绩丰碑。 “我们有些领导和机关干部，像不像盆景？”在树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育的一次专题剖析会上，李政委指着窗台上一盆造型精致的盆景说：“待在办公楼里，汇报写得头头是道，围着办公室、会议桌、汇报材料转，有人觉得这些就是‘政绩’。但坐在机关楼里，门好进、脸好看，事不一定好办，在基层官兵眼中这不就是一盆盆精致的‘盆景’吗？” “我们是否真正了解基层急难愁盼？是否把根系扎进官兵心中？” 问号叩击心灵，众人鸦雀无声。这份沉默背后，是政绩观的偏差。近年来，虽然支队一直要求坚持“基层至上、士兵第一”，但调研发现，不少领导和机关干部习惯了坐在办公室里按部就班、公事公办的“文来电往”，却少了设身处地、倾心服务的“人来心到”。基层有诉求大多等到上报了才去解决，有些事项甚至因信息不畅、层层审核久拖不决。 在树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育中，为帮助领导和机关干部廓清思想迷雾、培塑为兵情怀，该支队特意安排实地参观见学环节，组织大家来到驻地“杨善洲纪念林”，重温老书记杨善洲的感人事迹。 “老书记一生干事创业，不靠坐办公室发号施令，靠的是抗旱守在田间、植树捡拾果核，事事以身作则、处处实干为先、时时为民造福……”漫步林间，听着杨善洲的一个个感人故事，不少领导和机关干部陷入沉思。 “杨善洲把办公室里的盆景移到大山上，我们为什么不能把办公桌搬到训练场？”该支队决定设立“基层工作日”，每周由一名党委常委带队，机关各部门派人组成工作组，不打招呼、不听汇报、不需陪同，直接进班排、入库房、上训练场，在一线倾听官兵心声、解决急难愁盼。 把“雪松”种在基层大地上，把“政绩”写在官兵心坎上。 第一个“基层工作日”，卫生队军医李允熙随工作组走进全支队最偏远的执勤二中队。这个中队驻地位于两山三县之间，位置偏僻、山路崎岖，从机关到这里，单向车程超过5个小时，官兵平时看病就医十分不便。 一路颠簸到达中队，李允熙立即拿出诊疗设备为官兵开展健康检查。一天时间，他查出十余项训练伤、慢性病，都是病情轻微、发病初期的伤病。随后，一场简短的会议在中队学习室召开，支队工作组当场研究帮扶机制：卫生队每季度轮流选派骨干力量，提供送医上门诊疗服务，为中队官兵解决就医难的问题。 不久后，李允熙带着各类医疗物资器材，再次来到中队。这一次他做的第一件事，就是逐人摸排健康状况，为中队官兵建立电子健康档案。驻队期间，他每天定点“巡班问诊”：走遍每间宿舍、每个执勤点位，详细了解官兵的健康状态、饮食作息，为大家细致检查身体、主动化解思想压力，把暖心关怀送到基层一线。 “雪松”种基层，枝叶绿意浓。翻看“基层工作日”工作台账，一行行记录翔实具体：训练器材更新、战备设施优化、军娃入学协调……每项基层诉求都清晰登记在案，每个问题都明确了责任分工、整改时限、推进举措。 警士小刘家属随军落户后，孩子就近入学的问题成了夫妻俩的烦心事。“基层工作日”走访谈心时，支队领导了解到这一情况，当即牵头跟进对接，多次主动联系驻地教育部门，反复跑流程、办手续，最终顺利解决了孩子入学事宜，解开了小刘的心结，也让他能够全身心投入工作训练。 数月后，支队召开“基层工作日”阶段性总结会。一组鲜活数据被摆上桌面：累计收集问题87个，当场解决42个，限期解决35个，解释说明10个，官兵满意度明显提升。 “老书记杨善洲把雪松盆景移上大亮山，用了22年植出一片森林。我们现在只走了几个月，发现87个问题，听起来不少，可大家想想，这些问题里，有多少是我们本应该早就发现、早就解决的？现在人下去了，根才开始往下扎。但离‘雪松’还差得远，离‘整片森林’更差得远。”会上，支队领导查摆差距、头脑清醒，“为兵服务，应该天天都是‘基层工作日’。” “雪松之问”，叩击着支队领导和机关干部的心灵，激励大家树立和践行正确政绩观，把自己这棵雪松“种”在基层广袤的大地，把根扎进泥土，始终与官兵心连心。 视点 不要躲在机关做“盆景” ■张雨晴 “共产党员不要躲在机关里做盆景，要到人民群众中去当雪松。”杨善洲的这句话穿越时空，在武警大理支队落地生根，化为领导和机关干部树立和践行正确政绩观的具体行动。 盆景安居温室，徒有光鲜造型，经不起风吹雨打；雪松扎根山野，历经寒暑磨砺，方能屹立不倒。现实中，少数领导和机关干部服务基层靠文件、推动工作靠会议、解决问题靠通报，看似履职尽责，实则脱离基层。这样的领导和机关干部恰如“精致盆景”，好看却根浅，经不起风雨、练不出本领，更遑论做出政绩。 不做“盆景”当“雪松”，本质是作风的转变。坐在机关，看的是文件、听的是汇报，离基层远了，政绩观自然容易跑偏；走到官兵中去，闻的是兵味、听的是兵情，自然知道“干什么、怎么干”。翻看武警大理支队那本“基层工作日”工作台账不难发现，那些让基层受益、官兵满意的实事，不是坐在办公室凭空想出来的，而是领导和机关干部像“雪松”一样，沉到一线顶着风吹日晒，脚踏实地干出来的。这则新闻提醒各级领导干部，只有把根扎进基层、把心贴近官兵，才能真出政绩、出真政绩。
