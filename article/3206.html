About this analysis
Summary
PLA Daily's page 11 launched a new column, 'A Loyal Heart Forever Toward the Party · Shining Red Stars,' timed to the 90th anniversary of the Long March's conclusion, anchored here by the son of Ma Zhixuan — a 108-year-old veteran of the 769th Regiment, 129th Division, Eighth Route Army who died May 24, 2026 — recounting his father's service from the 1932 Red Army enlistment through the Yangmingbao airfield raid and three decades guarding underground defense storage facilities. The column is standard commemorative political-work content; its institutional value is as a record of how PLA Daily frames Long March Spirit (长征精神) for a current-force audience, specifically by linking living successor units — here an Army brigade whose 'Red Third Company' traces its lineage to the 769th Regiment — to named veterans, a format that documents the PLA's ongoing effort to operationalize historical memory as unit cohesion and loyalty messaging.
Translation

**Editor's Note:** On July 22, 2026, Wang Quanying, a 105-year-old veteran Red Army soldier, passed away peacefully in Sichuan. In the brilliant galaxy of stars, another shining red star has been added. Hu Zhengxian, Ma Zhixuan, Wang Quanying… Since the beginning of this year, several veteran Red Army soldiers who once climbed snow mountains, crossed grasslands, and walked the arduous Long March road have left us one after another.

The grueling Long March tempered the People's Army, forged the People's Army, shaped the People's Army, and opened a new starting point for its development. Time does not stand still, but the inheritance never ceases. The figures of the Red Army soldiers grow ever more distant, yet the great Long March Spirit (长征精神) grows richer with the passage of time, inspiring us to continually set out on new Long Marches.

This year marks the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Chinese Workers' and Peasants' Red Army's Long March. Beginning today, page 11 of the PLA Daily launches the column "A Loyal Heart Forever Toward the Party · Shining Red Stars," inviting surviving veteran Red Army soldiers to look back on their magnificent years of military service, while also asking the families of deceased veteran Red Army soldiers, people from all walks of life who have retraced the Long March route, and staff of Long March–themed memorial venues to share their memories of the veteran Red Army soldiers.

We believe: the experience of one veteran Red Army soldier is a story; the collective portrait of the veteran Red Army soldiers is history. When we engrave the figure of each veteran Red Army soldier upon our hearts, those shining red stars will illuminate our new journey forward.

**Gazing at the Receding Figure**

**— The Son of Veteran Red Army Soldier Ma Zhixuan Remembers His Father**

■ Ma Chaoping

**Brief Biography:** Ma Zhixuan was born in February 1918 in Pingchang, Sichuan. He joined the Red Army in December 1932, personally experienced the Long March, and joined the Chinese Communist Party in May 1938. He served as reconnaissance platoon leader of the 769th Regiment, 129th Division, Eighth Route Army, and as company commander of the special operations battalion, among other posts. He participated in battles including the surprise raid on Yangmingbao and the Xiangtangpu ambush. After the founding of the People's Republic of China, he stood guard in deep-mountain underground storage facilities for nearly 30 years. He was successively awarded the Third-Class August First Medal, the Third-Class Independence and Freedom Medal, the Third-Class Liberation Medal, and the Second-Class Red Star Medal of Merit and Honor.

On May 24, 2026, my 108-year-old father left us forever. Looking at the empty room, sorting through his belongings, my siblings and I could not calm our hearts. Memories surged like a tide. I gathered my thoughts and quietly recalled the course of my father's life. I know that within his receding figure, there is still an endless story to be told.

**For Whom Did Father Shed Tears**

From childhood to adulthood, I almost never saw my father cry.

Until October 2016, when a leader from the Shanxi Provincial Military District (山西省军区) placed in his hands the commemorative medal for the 80th anniversary of the victory of the Chinese Workers' and Peasants' Red Army's Long March, my father's tears fell before he could speak.

Father said that receiving this commemorative medal filled his heart with guilt. I knew why he said this. He must have been thinking again of my grandfather, of my third uncle, of the comrades-in-arms who gave their lives during the years of war.

In 1932, my father was 14 years old. To have something to eat, he worked as a young monk at the temple where his grandfather Ma Tianfu served as an odd-job laborer, responsible for herding cattle. His three older brothers had long since gone out to work as farmhands for landlords. The days of hardship seemed to have no end in sight — until a column carrying red flags arrived in Pingchang, striking down local tyrants and distributing land, giving them hope for the future. Before long, my father and grandfather joined the Red Army together, and from that point on followed the red flag with single-minded devotion.

When the Long March began, my father and grandfather were assigned to different units. Before setting out, grandfather encountered my father, urged him to "be careful in all things," and divided his own rations to give to him. After a brief meeting, father and son each set off on the Long March.

Throughout the march, my father kept his grandfather's admonition in mind. But this was the grueling Long March! Crossing the grasslands, my father and his comrades would often march an entire day without finding drinkable water or edible wild vegetables. They had no choice but to risk eating mushrooms. The mushrooms were poisonous; eating them caused the whole body to swell.

When comrades ahead fell, my father pressed his grief deep into his heart and kept moving forward. He longed for the day of victory, when he would be reunited with his grandfather.

Three crossings of the grasslands, two ascents of snow mountains. My father lived to see victory, but never lived to see the reunion. He later learned that during the crossing of the snow mountains, amid raging wind and snow, his grandfather — who served as a cook at the general hospital of the Fourth Front Army (红四方面军) — refused to abandon the marching cooking pot, and fell with the pot into a deep ravine.

Keep moving forward, follow the red flag. In August 1937, my father's unit was incorporated into the 769th Regiment, 129th Division, Eighth Route Army. At 19, he immediately crossed the Yellow River eastward with his unit and rushed to the front lines of the anti-Japanese war in Shanxi.

Years later, my father still remembered the night of the surprise raid on Yangmingbao Airfield: "The moon hung in the sky, and the weather was very fine." When he spoke of the flames that rose after the Japanese aircraft were successfully destroyed, his eyes lit up; when the subject turned to fallen comrades, he would suddenly fall into a long silence.

In recent years, when speaking to visiting reporters, my father recalled the scene of October 1, 1949, when he stood beneath Tiananmen Gate Tower to attend the founding ceremony of the People's Republic. Before him, red flags billowed and tens of thousands rejoiced — yet his grandfather, who had joined the Red Army alongside him, and many comrades who had shared life and death with him, could not witness this moment. In the year of the Long March's victory, my father had unexpectedly learned that around the time he and his grandfather enlisted, his third elder brother Ma Lixuan had also joined the Red Army. Now that the People's Republic had been founded, where was his third brother?

In 2017, with the help of the Red Army Western Route Army Descendants' Association (红军西路军后代联谊会), my father finally learned that his third uncle had been killed as early as 1937 in the Battle of Machang Beach (马场滩战斗). That day, my father was silent for a long time, and then said to me: "Go and get your third uncle's martyrs' certificate processed."

In 2018, the Chuanshan Soviet Area Generals and Commanders Monument Forest Memorial Hall (川陕苏区将帅碑林纪念馆) had the deeds of my grandfather, third uncle, and father inscribed on a single commemorative stele. The inscription opens with these words: "Three members of one family served in the Red Army; father and son both gave their lives…"

On May 30 of this year, we brought my father's ashes to this monument forest. Grandfather, third uncle, and father were at last reunited in their homeland.

**What a Party Member Should Look Like**

In his later years, nothing made my father happier than hearing that a younger member of the family had joined the Party. Every time, he would offer a few words of instruction: "Work hard; if you can contribute to the Party and the nation, give it your whole heart and effort."

As a veteran Party member with more than 80 years of Party standing, my father placed great hopes in the younger generation. In recent years, as his health declined, he still did his best to communicate with officers and soldiers who came to visit.

"What a Communist Party member is — you can see it very clearly on the battlefield." My father told young officers and soldiers on multiple occasions that those on the battlefield who held firm in their convictions, charged to the front, and were unafraid of sacrifice were certainly Communist Party members.

He had reason to say so.

During the surprise raid on Yangmingbao Airfield, the 3rd Battalion commander Zhao Chongde, who led the unit in the main assault, charged at the front and engaged the Japanese in close combat. When blood poured from his chest, Battalion Commander Zhao summoned the strength to urge the comrades beside him: "Don't worry about me, hurry — hurry and destroy the aircraft…" These were the last words he left in this world.

During the Xiangtangpu ambush, ammunition was scarce; often, after firing only the three available bullets and throwing one round of grenades, they had to engage the enemy in hand-to-hand combat. My father's comrade Zhao Denglü grappled tightly with a Japanese platoon leader, and in the struggle bit through his own teeth.

At over a hundred years of age, my father could still call out these comrades by name: "They were all Communist Party members!"

The story of my father's joining the Party has been reported by the media many times. In 1936, as the Long March neared its end, my father was listed by the Party organization as a candidate for development. However, one evening at a bivouac, when my father went to a local villager's home to borrow an earthen jar to draw water from a well, he accidentally broke the jar. Red Army soldiers received no allowance, and my father had no means to compensate for it; in the end, the company commander and political instructor had to go in person to apologize before the matter was settled. My father received a disciplinary sanction for this, and his candidacy for Party membership was revoked. It was not until 1938 that he formally joined the Party organization.

Whenever he told this story, my father would always sigh to us: "One important reason our Party was able to grow and strengthen continuously in such harsh conditions was strict mass discipline (群众纪律)… Whether one refrains from taking or encroaching upon the interests of the masses — not even a hair's breadth — this bears directly on the Party's image and reputation among the masses, and on where the people's hearts lie!"

In the memories of us children growing up, my father was like an "iron-faced Judge Bao." After the founding of the People's Republic, my father was appointed director of a national defense storage facility, and the family lived with him in the mountains.

I remember that in those days supplies in the mountains were scarce, and the troops supplemented their rations by hunting. Once, we children gathered a few sheep's trotters and boiled them to stave off hunger; when my father discovered this, he "confiscated" them without a word. One Spring Festival, I was playing in the mountains with friends and accidentally started a fire that caught the trees. The flames were extinguished in time and no greater damage was done, but when my father heard about it, he had us all line up in a row — regardless of whether you were the political commissar's child or the director's child — and criticized each one in turn…

"Collective interests come first." "Public property is not ours to use." … Years later I finally understood that my father's refusal to "tolerate even a grain of sand in his eye" (不揉沙子) was precisely what a Communist Party member should look like.

**Walk the New Long March Road Well**

In the honor room of the Taiyuan Fifth Retired Cadres' Rest Home (太原第五离职干部休养所) of the Shanxi Provincial Military District, a pair of footprints that my father made a few years ago are preserved. A group of young officers and soldiers stood before those footprints for a long time.

This was a scene from September 2025, when officers and soldiers of the "Red Third Company" (红三连) of an Army brigade traveled over a thousand li to "seek their roots." The predecessor unit of that brigade was none other than the old unit my father held dear in his heart — the 769th Regiment, 129th Division, Eighth Route Army.

At the time, my father's health was already not good, but on a sunny morning he insisted on meeting with the officers and soldiers. That day, my father rose early and sat in his wheelchair, an old blanket across his legs. When the officers and soldiers entered the room, his gaze immediately fell upon them, and he broke into a smile.

"We are soldiers of the 769th Regiment, and we've come to see you!" The officer leading the group took my father's hand and spoke close to his ear.

"769, the old unit…" My father's voice was somewhat indistinct, but everyone heard him clearly.

The officer leading the group produced a metal badge engraved with a red star design and a brief history of the unit's development, and gently placed it in my father's hand: "This is the brigade's commemorative badge. Please rest assured — the road you walked back then, we will continue to walk."

My father's hand trembled slightly. He lowered his head to look at the badge, then raised his head to look at the young faces of the officers and soldiers, and slowly formed the words in reply: "Good… good…"

The visit was not long. Before the officers and soldiers left, my father raised his arm with effort and waved to them.

Before New Year's Day this year, the rest home received another phone call from the officers and soldiers. These young men were very concerned about my father's condition, and also asked us family members to convey a message to him: in the annual military training assessment that had just concluded, they had performed excellently — the special operations squad had taken first place overall in the theater command competition, and two soldiers had been awarded second-class merit citations.

At that time, my father, lying in his sickbed, could no longer speak. My elder sister leaned close to his ear and relayed the soldiers' words to him one sentence at a time. My father gently opened his eyes. He had heard what the officers and soldiers said.

Now, my father has left us. On the bookshelf at home, there still stands a story collection titled *The Journey* (《征途》), compiled by the rest home some years ago for him and several other veteran cadres at the facility. More than 90 years ago, countless Red Army soldiers like my father, following the fluttering red flag, set out on the Long March without hesitation or regret. Their figures are gradually receding. Their greatest hope is that we today stride boldly onto a new journey, and walk the new Long March road well.

*(Compiled by Wen Jianru and Ma Huixin)*

前些年，面对前来采访的记者，父亲曾回忆起1949年10月1日，站在天安门城楼下参加开国大典的情景。眼前红旗翻滚、万众欢腾，而一起参加红军的爷爷、许多曾经肝胆相照的战友，却看不到这一幕。长征胜利那一年，父亲意外得知在他和爷爷参军前后，他的三哥马礼选也参加了红军。如今新中国成立了，三哥又在哪里呢？ 党员应该是什么样 父亲晚年最开心的事，莫过于听说家里有小辈入了党。每一次，他都要嘱咐几句：“要好好干，能为党和国家贡献力量，就尽心尽力去做。” 作为一名党龄超过80年的老党员，父亲对年轻一代寄予厚望。近些年身体不如从前，有官兵前来探望时，他仍尽可能跟大家交流。 “什么是共产党员，战场上看得很清楚。”父亲曾多次对年轻官兵谈到，战场上信念坚定、冲锋在前、不怕牺牲的，肯定就是共产党员。 他有理由这么讲。 百岁高龄，父亲仍能叫出这些战友的名字：“他们都是共产党员啊！” 关于父亲的入党故事，媒体报道过多次。1936年长征接近尾声时，父亲被党组织列为发展对象。然而，一日宿营，父亲到老乡家借陶罐到井中取水时，不慎将陶罐摔碎。红军战士没有津贴，父亲无力赔偿，最终还是连长和指导员登门道歉才了结此事。父亲为此背了个处分，党员发展对象资格也被取消。直到1938年，他才正式加入党组织。 每每讲到这个故事，父亲总会冲我们感叹一番：“我们党能在那种艰苦环境下不断成长壮大起来，一个重要原因就是有严格的群众纪律……是否对群众利益做到不贪不占、秋毫无犯，这可关系到党在群众中的形象、声誉，关系到民心所向啊！” 在我们儿女的成长记忆里，父亲则像个“铁面包公”。新中国成立后，父亲被任命为某国防仓库库长，此后家里人随他在山中生活。 记得那时候山里物资紧缺，部队靠打猎补充口粮。一次，我们几个孩子捡来几个羊蹄子煮水充饥，父亲发现后，二话不说予以“没收”。有一年春节，我和伙伴在山中玩耍，不小心点火烧着了树。火苗被及时扑灭，没有造成更大的损失，但父亲听说这件事后，让我们几个站成一排，不管是政委还是主任的孩子，挨个批评了一通…… “集体利益在第一位”“公家的东西，我们不能占用”……多年后我终于明白，父亲眼里的“不揉沙子”，恰是一名共产党员应有的样子。 走好新的长征路 山西省军区太原第五离职干部休养所荣誉室里，保存着父亲前些年拓下的一对足印。一群年轻官兵，在足印前久久驻足。 这是2025年9月，陆军某旅“红三连”官兵跨越千里前来“寻根”的一幕。该旅前身部队，正是父亲心心念念的老部队——八路军第129师769团。 当时，父亲的身体已不太好，可他仍在一个阳光明媚的上午，坚持要与官兵见见面。那天，父亲早早起床，坐在轮椅上，腿上盖着旧毛毯。当官兵走进房间，他的目光立刻落在来人身上，露出笑容。 “我们是769团的兵，来看您了！”带队干部拉着父亲的手，附在他耳边说道。 “769，老部队……”父亲的声音有些含糊，但大家都听清了。 带队干部拿出一枚刻有红星图案和部队简明发展历史的金属徽章，轻轻放在父亲手中：“这是旅队的纪念徽章，请您放心，您当年走过的路我们接着走。” 父亲的手微微颤动着，他低头看看徽章，又抬头看看官兵年轻的脸庞，缓缓吐字回应：“好……好……” 见面的时间不算长。官兵离开前，父亲用力抬起胳膊，朝他们挥了挥手。 今年元旦前，干休所再次接到官兵打来的电话。这群年轻小伙子非常关心父亲的情况，还请我们家属转告父亲：在刚刚结束的年度军事训练考核中，他们成绩优异，特战小队在战区比武中夺得总分第一，两名战士荣立二等功。 那时，病床上的父亲已无法开口说话。姐姐附在他耳边，把官兵的话一句句转达。父亲轻轻睁开眼睛，他听到了官兵们的话。 如今，父亲离开了我们。家中书架上，仍摆放着干休所早些年为他和所里多位老干部制作的故事集《征途》。90多年前，许许多多如父亲一样的红军将士，追随飘扬的红旗，义无反顾踏上长征路。他们的身影正渐渐远去。他们最大的期望，就是我们今天阔步迈向新征途，走好新的长征路。 （温建如、马慧鑫整理）
