Dialogue with Navy Senior Master Sergeant Wang Pengfei: Exploring the Spiritual Legacy of Submarine Officers and Sailors 'Fulfilling the Mission with Their Lives'

对话海军某部三级军士长王鹏飞，探寻潜艇官兵“用生命履行使命”的精神传承
PLA Daily (解放军报) 27 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Wang Pengfei, a Grade 3 Senior Master Sergeant in a submarine crew detachment under the Northern Theater Command Navy, recounts the unit's founding-era deep-dive test from the late 1980s, in which crew member Dong Fusheng wrote a last letter before the dive and the detachment subsequently earned the CMC-awarded honorary designation 'Underwater Vanguard Submarine' (水下先锋艇). This is standard political work content — a 'Traditions in Today's Discussion' (传统在今天) format interview — and its value is as a record of how the PLA Navy frames sacrifice and mission commitment to enlisted submarine crews through unit lineage and predecessor letters, not as evidence of changed operational posture or readiness. The anecdote about Senior Corporal Chen Chao sealing a pipeline leak bare-handed during a recent long-duration voyage documents how the institution links contemporary crew behavior back to that inherited framing.
Translation

In the military history museum of a unit under the Northern Theater Command Navy, a letter with yellowed edges lies quietly behind a glass display case. The characters written in blue-black ink, after more than thirty years, still seem to press through the paper with force—this is a last letter written to his family by Dong Fusheng, a crew member of a submarine crew detachment in that unit, on the eve of carrying out a mission more than thirty years ago.

The paper is short, but the sentiment runs deep; every word carries the weight of a thousand pounds. The spirit of dedication embodied in this letter has always been forged into the bloodline of generation after generation of submarine officers and sailors. In this installment of "Traditions in Today's Discussion," we speak with Wang Pengfei, a crew member and Senior Master Sergeant (Grade 3) of the unit, to explore the spiritual legacy of submarine officers and sailors "fulfilling the mission with their lives."

Answer: Throughout the hundred-year history of the Party, many Communist Party members have left behind final letters, final poems, and final writings. Deng Zhenqian wrote in "Final Words from Prison": "No matter how many heads are cut off, the blood will never stop flowing"; Liu Shaonan wrote in "Song of Heroic Sacrifice": "One day, blood will be repaid with blood; a blade at the throat holds no fear"; Zhao Yiman, in her last letter to her son before her execution, said, "My child must continue the struggle in your mother's place"…

Such final words do not resemble an ending—they resemble a rebirth. They carry within them entrusted wishes, confidence, and strength. Countless successors, having felt the force of their convictions, followed the path they blazed, overcame one difficulty after another, won one victory after another, and ultimately welcomed the dawn.

This last letter carries the same meaning. It was written not only for family, not only for history, but also for us.

In the late 1980s, the predecessor unit of our formation organized an underwater deep-dive test of a certain type of submarine. Before carrying out this mission, the crew members who participated all prepared themselves to "fulfill the mission with their lives," and crew member Dong Fusheng wrote this last letter to his family.

There is one sentence in it that moved me deeply: "For society to progress and the nation to develop, it has always taken generation after generation pressing forward, shedding blood and sweat to make it happen. If we are to strive and advance, there must be sacrifice."

On the day of the test, the submarine slowly submerged beneath the water, and all crew members entered a state of intense combat readiness. As the depth gauge needle descended steadily, each compartment maintained close monitoring, reporting conditions once for every ten meters of descent. The depth continued to increase; the hull, under pressure, emitted low groans; the angle iron supporting the depth gauge silently deformed… In the face of a life-and-death test, every officer and sailor held their post like a rivet, operating with calm composure, until the test depth was reached and the mission was successfully completed.

Thereafter, the crew detachment, relying on the spirit of daring to sacrifice, successfully completed various missions on multiple occasions and was awarded the honorary designation "Underwater Vanguard Submarine" (水下先锋艇) by the Central Military Commission.

Question: What force enabled Dong Fusheng and his comrades to press forward without hesitation when facing the possibility of sacrifice?

Answer: A letter received by our unit last year can answer this question. The sender was the grandson of veteran predecessor Hao Jisheng, one of the 36 crew members of the first-vessel reception crew (接艇队) for a certain type of submarine in our unit.

We got in touch with Elder Hao, and from him we learned of that extraordinary period. More than fifty years ago, 36 officers and sailors were called to duty from across the country in a time of urgent need, forming the first-vessel reception crew for a certain type of submarine.

"Back then there were no computers—we could only grind away every day with abacuses and slide rules. At night we curled up on wooden plank beds, and even in our dreams we could hear the clatter of abacus beads." Elder Hao recalled that the day before the trial voyage, the political commissar gathered them together to write their last letters.

Hao Jisheng gripped his pen, his hand trembling slightly. A comrade patted him on the shoulder: "What's there to fear? If we really go under, the thirty-six of us will serve as ballast for the submarine." In the end, they successfully navigated that "steel leviathan" out toward the deep blue.

I believe that the willingness to sacrifice for the mission was not the choice of Dong Fusheng alone, nor only the choice of those 36 men. Generation after generation of revolutionary predecessors fused the spirit of loyalty, courage, and fearlessness in the face of sacrifice into the bloodline of the crew detachment through their actions—and that is why those who came after them press forward without hesitation and advance with courage when confronting severe trials.

Answer: Today, equipment is being updated and replaced at an accelerating pace, and the types of missions have also changed greatly—but the weight carried by the five characters "Underwater Vanguard Submarine" has not changed, and the drive of "advancing toward death to find life" (向死而生) must not change.

Before every important mission departure, we walk into the military history museum to draw strength from the spirit of our predecessors. We revisit the predecessors' last letters, reflect on the vow that "if we are to strive and advance, there must be sacrifice," and contemplate the inner journey of men who built achievements starting from nothing. If we were to shrink back when difficulties arise, how could we face these old predecessors?

Not long ago, we completed a long-duration voyage mission and were returning to port when a certain pipeline suddenly leaked, and high-pressure gas mixed with water mist instantly sprayed out. Senior Corporal (Grade 1) Chen Chao reached bare-handed for the valve and pressed down hard to seal the leak, holding it until the emergency was resolved. Afterward he said that at the time his mind held no other thought—he only knew that as long as the man was there, the submarine was there, and the mission was there.

Later, at a story-sharing session, he recounted this experience, and the audience below erupted in applause. I looked out at the young faces in the audience and felt that the course charted by our predecessors through their striving and dedication is being extended by us in relay, reaching ever further.

Original Chinese
在北部战区海军某部军史馆的玻璃展柜里，静静躺着一封边角泛黄的遗书。蓝黑墨水写下的字迹历经30余载，依旧力透纸背——这是30多年前，该部某艇员队艇员董福生在执行任务前夕，写给家人的遗书。 纸短情长，字重千钧。这封信承载的奉献底色，始终熔铸在一代代潜艇官兵的血脉之中。本期“传统今日谈”，我们对话该部艇员、三级军士长王鹏飞，探寻潜艇官兵“用生命履行使命”的精神传承。 答：百年党史中，留下了不少共产党人的绝笔信、绝笔诗、绝笔文章。邓贞谦在《狱中绝笔》中写道，“杀不尽头颅流不尽鲜血”；刘绍南在《壮烈歌》中写道，“有朝一日，血要用血还，刀放头上不胆寒”；赵一曼在就义前给儿子的遗书中说“我的孩子要替母亲继续斗争”…… 这样的绝笔，不像一种终结，更像一场新生——它蕴含着嘱托、信心、力量。无数的后来者，就是感受到了他们的信念，沿着他们开辟的道路，战胜一个又一个困难，夺取一个又一个胜利，最终迎来了黎明。 这封遗书，也有同样的意义。它不仅是写给家人的、写给历史的，也是写给我们的。 上世纪80年代末，我部前身部队组织某型潜艇水下深潜试验。执行这项任务前，参加任务的艇员们都做好了“用生命履行使命”的准备，艇员董福生给家人写了这封遗书。 里面有一句话，深深打动了我——“社会要进步，国家要发展，都是靠一代代人前赴后继流血流汗换来的，既然要奋斗、进步，就要有牺牲。” 试验当日，潜艇缓缓没入水中，全体艇员进入紧张战备状态。伴随着深度计指针沉稳下行，各舱室严密监控，每下潜10米就通报一次情况。深度持续增加，艇体受压发出阵阵低鸣，支撑深度计的角钢悄然变形……在生死考验面前，每名官兵都如铆钉般坚守战位、沉着操作，直至达到试验深度，任务圆满完成。 此后，艇员队凭借敢于牺牲的精神，多次圆满完成各项任务，被中央军委授予“水下先锋艇”荣誉称号。 问：是什么力量，让董福生和战友们在面对可能的牺牲时，如此义无反顾？ 答：去年单位收到的一封来信，就能够解答这个问题。寄信的人，是我部某型潜艇首艇接艇队的36名艇员之一、老前辈郝继生的孙子。 我们联系上郝老，从他口中得知了那段峥嵘岁月。50多年前，36名官兵从全国各地临危受命，组建某型潜艇首艇接艇队。 “那时没有计算机，只能每天和算盘、计算尺硬磕，晚上蜷在木板床上，梦里都是算盘珠子噼啪作响的声音。”郝老回忆，试航前一天，政委召集他们写遗书。 郝继生握着钢笔，手有些抖，战友拍了拍他的肩膀，“怕啥，要是真沉了，咱36个给潜艇当压舱石”。最终，他们成功驾驭这艘“钢铁巨鲸”驶向深蓝。 我想，为了使命敢于牺牲，不只是董福生一人的选择，也不只是这36人的选择。一代代革命先辈用实际行动将忠诚、勇敢、不怕牺牲的精神融入艇员队血脉，后来者才会在面对严峻考验时义无反顾、勇往直前。 答：如今，装备加速更新换代，任务类型也发生了很大变化，但“水下先锋艇”5个字的分量没有变，“向死而生”那股劲头不能变。 每次重要任务出航前，我们都会走进军史馆感悟先辈力量。重温先辈的遗书，感悟“既然要奋斗、进步，就要有牺牲”的誓言，体味他们一穷二白都能开创业绩的心路历程，我们要是碰上困难就退缩，何以面对这些老前辈？ 前段时间，我们完成长航任务返航，途中某管路突然泄漏，高压气体带着水雾瞬间喷射而出。一级上士陈超徒手摸到阀门并死死堵住泄漏，直至险情排除。事后他说，当时脑子里什么都没多想，就知道人在、艇在、任务在。 后来在故事分享会上，他讲起这段经历，台下掌声雷动。我望着台下一张张年轻的面庞，感到前辈们用拼搏和奉献开辟的航迹，正由我们接力延伸、越走越远。
