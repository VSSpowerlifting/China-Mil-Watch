The Quiet Sky — A Reporter's Conversation with J-16 Female Pilot Tian Jing

静静的天空——记者对话歼-16女飞行员田静
PLA Daily (解放军报) 13 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Tian Jing, a 1994-born Sichuan native and member of China's first cohort of female J-16 pilots, flew the rear seat of the lead fighter jet over Tiananmen at the September 2025 parade marking the 80th anniversary of victory in the War of Resistance Against Japan — the first aircraft over the square that day. This is a PLAAF human-interest profile produced by official military media; its value is as a record of how the institution frames female combat aviators within the 备战打仗主责主业 (primary mission of preparing for and winning wars) framework, presenting the J-16 transition and parade role as proof that women now occupy frontline fast-jet billets rather than ceremonial or support roles.
Confirms that the rear-seat pilot of the lead J-16 at the 80th anniversary of the War of Resistance parade was female (Tian Jing, b. 1994), identifying her as among the first cohort of female J-16 pilots and providing the first named, detailed public profile of a female fourth-generation fighter pilot in this role.
Translation

Our interview subject ran off — it was a first for us too.

The suddenly vanishing interview subject made us realize

that the sky above our heads

is not, in fact, quiet at all.

Her name is Tian Jing.

She is among the first cohort of female J-16 pilots in our country.

You may not know her,

but you have certainly seen her fly.

At the parade marking the 80th anniversary of victory in the War of Resistance Against Japan,

the first fighter jet to fly over Tiananmen —

the rear-seat pilot was Tian Jing.

Born in 1994, she is a Sichuan girl,

fiery in personality and even fonder of spicy food.

Above ten thousand li of cloud and sky,

"distorted" (畸变) time

and boundless space

give Tian Jing a vast freedom.

Her youth, her passion, and her very life

are destined to surge for the blue skies of the motherland.

At the Air Force Aviation University,

the first lesson she learned

was how to become a soldier —

and only then, how to become a pilot.

Grounded in the primary mission of preparing for and winning wars (备战打仗主责主业),

she gradually grew into a combat fighter (战斗员).

When an unexpected aerial situation arose,

she said:

"You raise troops for a thousand days to use them in a single moment.

Today is the day I have been waiting for."

In order to transition to the J-16

and blaze a trail for girls who want to become pilots,

Tian Jing and her comrades signed a military pledge (军令状).

Passing through dense cloud with extremely low visibility,

she and the front-seat pilot flew over Tiananmen in the J-16

with not a meter or a second's deviation.

"Every pilot wants to fly the best, most advanced aircraft."

"I want to tell our predecessors: today, our aircraft no longer need to fly the route twice."

Closing the canopy,

ranging freely across the sky,

Tian Jing thought of a kite.

"One end of the string is me and my fighter jet;

the other end is held by the motherland and the people.

No matter how far I fly, I can follow that string back home."

Sun rises, moon sets.

Clouds gather, clouds drift.

Tian Jing and her comrades

stand guard over this quiet sky.
