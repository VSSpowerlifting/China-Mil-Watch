About this analysis
Summary
The Army Engineering University 'Red Torch' Strong-Army Story Lecture Team, a cadet-led political education unit of fewer than 100 members, has conducted more than 160 sessions across 20 provinces—including Beijing, Tibet, and Xinjiang—covering over 100,000 kilometers and reaching more than 80,000 people online and offline since its first off-campus lecture in 2021; the team also conducted a joint session on Kaishan Island with a 'Spark' political instructor lecture group from the Jiangsu Coast Guard Bureau. The article documents the PLA's effort to address a recognized problem in military ideological education—that top-down, one-way indoctrination fails to engage younger officers and soldiers raised in a high-information environment—by repositioning cadets as producers and transmitters of political content rather than passive recipients. The framing points to an institutional push to embed Xi Jinping Thought on Strengthening the Military through peer-to-peer narrative rather than lecture, a model the commentary explicitly presents as a template for 铸魂育人 (forging the soul and nurturing people) across the force.

The "Red Torch" Is Spreading Like Wildfire

■ Guo Zijin, PLA Daily Reporter Shi Yu, Correspondent Xiao Liming

Members of the Army Engineering University "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team deliver a presentation. Photo by Jiang Xinyu

Audience comment cards collected by the Army Engineering University "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team. Provided by the authors

This is a "youth formation" (青春方阵) of fewer than a hundred people, yet it surges with a force that moves hearts. Members of the Army Engineering University "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team travel year-round between campus and grassroots units, between classrooms and training grounds. They are both "faithful listeners" to stories of army-building and "resolute torchbearers" of the red gene (红色基因), building bridges for the transmission of spirit in the posture of youth.

"Young students narrate strong-army stories in the voice of youth—full of vitality and deeply infectious," said Dai Jianfeng, a cadre of a certain cadet battalion. He noted that the team's footprint has spread across 20 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities including Beijing, Tibet, and Xinjiang; cumulative travel has exceeded 100,000 kilometers; more than 160 lecture sessions have been conducted; and online and offline audiences have surpassed 80,000. From a single offline session that packed 7,800 seats, to an online broadcast that drew the attention of ten thousand viewers across 162 Hope Primary Schools along the Long March route, the "Red Torch" has already taken on the force of a prairie fire.

The Torch First Ignites

As long as the telling is truthful, close to life, and emotionally resonant, strong-army stories have the power to pierce the heart

Not long ago, the atmosphere inside the honor room of the "Liu Laozhuang Company" of a certain brigade of the 82nd Group Army was blazing.

On the stage, Li Bingshu, a member of the Army Engineering University "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team, delivered a vivid and emotionally charged account of the loyalty and steadfast commitment of frontier soldiers in the new era. Below the stage, iron-willed officers and soldiers held their breath, eyes brimming with tears.

Four members' two consecutive days of presentations earned wave after wave of applause.

This was not a moment of glory achieved overnight. Its origins can be traced back to a "debut" that made team leader Zhang Weishi and political instructor Ding Pengfei break into a cold sweat several years ago.

In the bitter winter of 2021, the lecture team stepped outside the school gates for the first time, arriving at Houbiaoning Primary School in Nanjing. Facing civilian teachers and students, could these military academy cadets convey the positive energy of strong-army stories?

On the stage, team member Zhang Yixin's palms were drenched in sweat. Just before she went on, Ding Pengfei gently patted her shoulder: "Don't be nervous—just speak the sincerity in your heart." Those words became the key that unlocked her.

Though her delivery was somewhat unpolished and her sentences occasionally faltered, when Zhang Yixin spoke of soldiers persisting on patrol in temperatures of minus 40 degrees Celsius, and of peacekeeping officers and soldiers guarding peace in foreign lands, the auditorium fell completely silent, and many children quietly wiped away tears.

Practice proved: as long as the telling is truthful, close to life, and emotionally resonant, strong-army stories have the power to pierce the heart.

From that point on, the lecture team organized into small squads of three to five, using spare time to travel to military camps, schools, and communities. They abandoned rote recitation, studied content deeply, and carefully built five core modules: "loyalty, responsibility, dedication, struggle, and peace." They innovated a "menu-style" (点餐式) lecture format—grassroots units place orders according to their needs, and the team delivers tailored sessions.

Warm echoes continued to resonate. From the first lecture to the present, the children of Houbiaoning Primary School in Nanjing have established a lasting connection with frontier company officers and soldiers thousands of li away. The children write naive yet sincere greetings, which lecture team members personally deliver to the snow-covered frontier; the soldiers' reply letters are carried back to the school by team members. A single sheet of correspondence bridges children's hearts and the military camp, planting love of family and country (家国情怀) in the hearts of the next generation.

Inside the cadet battalion's lecture team exhibition hall, walls of honorary certificates gleam. Zhang Weishi pointed to the densely packed "message columns" in the display cases and said with satisfaction: "These are the heartfelt words written by officers, soldiers, and children after the lectures." On the cards are soldiers' reflections—"After hearing the story, I stand guard with more spirit"—and children's drawings captioned "When I grow up, I want to join the People's Liberation Army."

But more precious than the messages are the eyes lit up by faith—and that is precisely the driving force that sends lecture team members across mountains and seas.

Passing the Torch

To truly understand the spirit behind heroes' stories, and to carry forward genuine feeling, reverence, and faith together

Zhang Yixin, about to graduate, solemnly handed a thick stack of lecture manuscripts to a new member. The accumulation of more than 110 lecture sessions was, in that moment, transformed into a silent entrustment.

"Lecturing is absolutely not simply reading from a script. You must understand the spirit behind heroes' stories and carry forward genuine feeling, reverence, and faith together—move hearts with sincerity, and illuminate the original aspiration (初心)." Zhang Yixin said softly.

"The wind is 'iron horses in the autumn wind' (铁马秋风); the flowers are 'chrysanthemums on the battlefield' (战地黄花); the snow is 'warships in the night snow' (楼船夜雪); the moon is 'the cold moon over the frontier pass' (边关冷月)." When Zhang Yixin reinterpreted this soldier's romanticism, new cadet Li Zhuoxi in the audience felt a surge of emotion—a resonance from the depths of the soul.

Li Zhuoxi recalled the experience of paying respects at the Kangxiwar Martyrs' Cemetery in middle school. In the wind and snow of the Kunlun Mountains, facing tombstones bearing the names of fallen heroes whose average age was under 25, the word "sacrifice" was no longer an abstraction from a textbook but cold stone carvings and scalding blood.

In that moment, Li Zhuoxi, seated in the audience, clenched her fists and resolved to take up the weighty microphone and pass on the heartbeat within the strong-army stories.

The reporter holds a quarto-sized lecture booklet with a simple, substantial cover. Opening the pages, one is immediately met with the ice shards on patrol routes, the frost on guard posts, and the ice flowers that cannot be scraped clean from windshields. These words with "granularity" (颗粒度) make the image of the soldier tangible and palpable, give heroes flesh and blood, and make the distant frontier no longer abstract.

Yet the road from being a listener "illuminated by the light" to a speaker "carrying the light forward" is far more rugged than imagined.

Cadet Wang Wanchen once thought lecturing was nothing more than reading beautiful prose aloud. Not until new-recruit training, when she witnessed a lecturer on stage performing with split-second precision and words that reached the heart, did she understand: "one minute on stage, ten years of work off stage."

Later, after being selected into the lecture team, Wang Wanchen endured six months of tempering to make that "one minute" shine. She rewrote her lecture script eight or nine times and remade video materials five or six times. At first she mechanically recited; later she dug into details like an archaeologist, even traveling to the frontier during holidays to verify facts. Yellowed diaries, blurred group photographs, candles in tents that could not be kept straight—one by one these details were brought back to life, and her presentations were no longer hollow but filled with the warmth of history.

In the lecture team, this kind of near-relentless (折腾) refinement is the norm.

In late-night preparation rooms, the sound of keyboards often continues until the early hours. To verify a single detail about heating conditions at a high-altitude outpost, they collected more than a dozen reference journals; to enhance the impact of a training exercise video, they taught themselves editing software and adjusted it frame by frame. This meticulousness stems from reverence for truth, and even more from veneration of heroes.

From the biting cold winds of Kangxiwar to the clear sound of reading in the ancient capital Jinling; from the trembling of hands touching a tombstone to the steadiness of hands gripping a microphone—these young military academy cadets are conducting a spiritual relay race that transcends time and space. As Li Zhuoxi wrote in her lecture script: "Frontier soldiers cannot walk out of the mountains, but their stories must walk out of the mountains."

Forged into One's True Character

The young people who once interpreted steadfast commitment on stage have begun to write new stories of steadfast commitment

In the blazing summer of 2024, the parasol trees on campus rustled. Lecture team member Liu Shilin stood before the mirror in the "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team exhibition hall and straightened his uniform for the last time. In that moment, with solemn ceremony, he bid farewell to a chapter of his life.

Several months later, in a highland river valley at an elevation of more than 3,000 meters, Liu Shilin—now a platoon leader—bent over communications equipment as sweat dripped onto the frozen highland soil. The young man who had once interpreted steadfast commitment on stage had begun to write new stories of steadfast commitment.

This crossing was no accident. Three years of lecturing: the more deeply he spoke of others' steadfast commitment, the clearer the call within him became. When graduation assignments were made, Liu Shilin solemnly signed his name on a volunteer application to serve in Tibet.

"I don't feel it's hard. The real frontier is far harder than this," Liu Shilin said calmly over the phone. He advanced with his unit to a certain highland—a "forbidden zone of life" (生命禁区) that even eagles struggle to cross. Traveling through wind and snow, he felt a long-absent sense of groundedness: "Just like the stories I told—steadfast commitment is simply a habit."

In the northwestern corner of the motherland, in extreme cold of minus 50 degrees Celsius, Yang Aoqi, a 2025 graduating cadet, wrapped her coat tightly and raced along the frontier line with her unit.

"You will see a sentry turn around, and there is a face sculpted by ice and snow yet still radiant with a smile." Over the phone, Yang Aoqi's voice carried a smile, and also the chill of wind and snow. The images that once existed only in lecture scripts have now become everyday footnotes in her diary. The Kazakh grandmother who always comes to visit them carrying warm naan has gone from a figure in source material to a neighbor.

What lecturing gave them was not only burning passion but also the ability to "live life as poetry" (把日子过成诗). Liu Shilin, excelling across the board, often organizes cultural and recreational activities in his company, continuing his stage mission in new ways—warming the people in the stories. Yang Aoqi applies the sharp powers of observation honed while writing lecture scripts to documenting the real frontier, attempting to prove to the world: in the land of extreme cold, there is not only sacrifice and dedication, but also scalding faith and pure joy.

Liu Shilin gazed at the snow-capped mountains outside the window and gave a platoon leader's answer: "Once I spoke of steadfast commitment from the stage; now I guard the mountains and rivers from my battle position."

A Joint Lecture on Kaishan Island

■ Guo Zijin, PLA Daily Reporter Shi Yu

Deep in the Yellow Sea, waves crash against the shore.

Kaishan Island, with an area of only 0.013 square kilometers, stands isolated between sea and sky, long known as the "Yellow Sea Outpost."

Recently, the Army Engineering University "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team and representatives of the "Spark" (星火) theoretical lecture service group for political instructors boarded a boat from Yanwei Port in Lianyungang, broke through the waves, and landed on this spiritual high ground to carry out an immersive educational practice activity.

This journey was a mutual pilgrimage to spiritual origins: "Torch" (薪火) brought vivid stories, tracing the roots of loyalty; "Spark" (星火) carried the light of theory, taking deep root in the fertile soil of practice; and the island garrison's day-and-night vigil was a silent yet soul-shaking lesson in ideological and political education (思政课).

At dawn, the five-starred red flag rose slowly in the sea breeze as all members stood in solemn attention. "What rises is the national flag; what is held is the position." Zhang Yang, the officer leading the group, said that in that moment, the word "loyalty" in the lecture scripts suddenly carried the weight of a thousand jun.

Afterward, led by "Model of the Era" (时代楷模) Wang Shihua, everyone walked the small path that Wang Jicai had patrolled day after day for 32 years, entering the outpost and the flag-raising platform. Sea winds had polished the worn stone steps; inside the simple barracks, a kerosene lamp, an old wooden bed, and several well-thumbed diaries silently told of day after day of steadfast commitment.

"Only by walking the hero's path today do I truly understand the mountain-weight mission carried by the words 'guarding the island is guarding the nation.'" "Red Torch" lecture team member Jiang Xinyu stood beside Wang Shihua, her heart surging and her eyes moist.

A few days earlier, Jiang Xinyu had spoken to a certain unit about the life-and-death trials faced by pilots executing missions. Now, standing beside this 64-year-old woman whose heart is bound to the island, she suddenly understood: the light of heroes shines not only in the magnificent moment of takeoff, but also settles in the ordinary years of day after day, making the island one's home—the ordinary forges the great, and steadfast commitment is loyalty.

This profound realization was swiftly transformed into powerful resonance in the lecture. That afternoon, a distinctive joint lecture unfolded on a makeshift stage.

Emotion and reason intertwined; stories and theory resonated together, like two clear streams flowing into the heart.

"This kind of lecture goes straight to the soul—it has the warmth to move hearts and the depth to enlighten minds," said a cadre from the Jiangsu Coast Guard Bureau who came to observe and learn, deeply moved.

This "integrated emotion and reason" (情理交融) joint lecture model allowed educational practice to truly take root. "Torch" conveyed warmth through genuine feeling; "Spark" conferred elevation through theory. The torch ignites faith; the spark illuminates the course—the two converged on Kaishan Island, kindling an inextinguishable spiritual flame.

"One visit to the island benefits a lifetime; seeing the worthy and striving to emulate them is the only way to move steadily and far." Zhang Yang said that Kaishan Island is not only a spiritual monument but also the "fertile spiritual soil" that nourishes the lecture team. Here, team members personally experienced the patriotic and dedicated spirit of "guarding the island is guarding the home; only when the nation is secure can the home be secure." This steadfast commitment and loyalty that spans the years has tempered their ideals and convictions, and has infused the lecture work with vivid energy and a weighty foundation.

As night fell, the transport boat slowly left the island. Looking back across the sea and sky, the outline of the red-roofed white tower gradually blurred, but the five-starred red flag billowing in the wind had been deeply imprinted in the hearts of lecture team members, transformed into an eternal spiritual beacon pointing the way forward.

Let Young Officers and Soldiers Become Torchbearers

■ Yu Pengfei

Today, young officers and soldiers are growing up in an era of information explosion and pluralistic thought, and they easily feel a sense of distance from one-way indoctrination and conceptual lecturing. How can Xi Jinping Thought on Strengthening the Military (习近平强军思想) truly enter the mind and heart, and be seen in action and results? The vivid practice of the Army Engineering University "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team provides a powerful answer: only by激发 activating the agency of youth, and making officers and soldiers the protagonists of education, can the internal driving force of ideological and political education be activated.

Transform the audience into the subject—active participation is what generates vitality. The key breakthrough point for ideological and political education in the new era lies in激发 activating the internal driving force of those being educated. The "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team has broken the traditional model of "instructor lectures, cadets listen," driving young officers and soldiers from "listening below the stage" to "speaking from the stage," becoming discoverers, narrators, and disseminators of stories. In order to tell stories well, members proactively go deep into frontline units, conduct in-depth interviews with those who lived through events, carefully simulate battlefield scenarios, and repeatedly refine compelling details. This process itself is a profound tempering of thought and a spiritual baptism. When peers share sincerely from personal experience, education is no longer external indoctrination but is internalized as officers' and soldiers' conscious identification and pursuit.

Transform one-way transmission into interaction—mutual engagement is what makes a single spark start a prairie fire. A successful education must not only be delivered brilliantly but must also be able to ignite the spark in the audience's heart. At "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team sessions, officers and soldiers are often moved to wipe away tears, and listeners who are deeply touched often walk up to the stage on their own initiative to share their own stories. Education thereby forms a virtuous cycle of "the speaker's conviction grows stronger—the listener's heart resonates—the transmitter acts with conscious purpose," achieving the passing and continuation of the flame.

Today, the footprint of the "Red Torch" Strong-Army Story Lecture Team has spread across frontier outposts, grassroots companies and battalions, and schools across the country. In recent years, the team has continuously expanded its educational terrain, co-building educational bases with seven red-themed venues. The lecture team's flag and emblem have been collected by the Military Museum of the Chinese People's Revolution.

The essence of ideological and political education lies not in filling a container but in kindling a flame and illuminating the path of the heart. When young officers and soldiers truly become the protagonists of the educational stage, when vivid stories become the living vehicle for arming minds with theory, when sincere interaction replaces one-way lecturing, strong-army stories are no longer distant legends but become vivid practice that officers and soldiers can feel and touch around them—a living model for forging the soul and nurturing people (铸魂育人) in the new era.

Altay's Winter Is Very "Warm"

■ Yang Aoqi, Platoon Leader of a Certain Unit

"Altay's winter temperatures can drop as low as minus 58 degrees Celsius; it is known as China's 'Second Pole of Cold.'" A year ago, I described this land from the lecture stage. Below, pairs of attentive eyes looked up at me. Now, I have gone from narrator of the story to a person inside the story.

When I first took up my battle position, the cold wind cut like a blade. Before, it was "watching others hold the line"; now, it is "I myself must endure." The first time I led a team to execute field communications support training, the temperature plummeted to more than minus 40 degrees Celsius. Within half an hour, violent headaches, numb limbs, and ear pain like needle pricks came in waves; every breath felt like swallowing ice shards. As platoon leader, with soldiers' trusting eyes at my back, I had no choice but to stand firm.

Just as I was gritting my teeth to hold on, a squad leader quietly moved close. His hat brim and scarf were encrusted with ice crystals and icicles, his face barely recognizable. He said softly: "Platoon leader, it's your first winter here—it's completely normal not to be adapted. If you're really cold, jump around, smile, rub your hands, touch your ears, and the warmth will come." Looking at the sincere smiles of comrades in the wind and snow, I felt the chill in my chest quietly dissipate.

I had imagined that guarding the border meant riding horses through snow and patrolling the frontier; only after truly putting down roots here did I understand that technical support is equally critical. Faced with unfamiliar communications equipment and complex circuit diagrams, I—once a theoretical backbone—became a technical novice. The veteran squad leaders held nothing back, teaching hand-in-hand the essentials of wiring, frequency adjustment, and troubleshooting. Their hands, covered in thick calluses, silently told of hardship. I asked: "How many times does it take to get calluses like these?" The veteran smiled calmly: "Hundreds, thousands of times, I suppose. When a mission comes, there's no room for hesitation—you must be fast, accurate, and steady!" They have studied communications technology for more than a decade, long since reaching a state of mastery, able to operate with precision even with their eyes closed, yet they still diligently pursue more efficient networking methods. "Technology changes, missions change—we cannot sleep on our existing skills." Plain words, conveying a scalding drive to improve.

Late at night, as I organized equipment logs in the equipment room, a knock came at the door. A soldier came in carrying a bowl of freshly brewed hot ginger tea: "Platoon leader, drive out the cold!" As I cradled the bowl, the rising steam instantly blurred my vision.

In that moment, I deeply felt that what I had described from the lecture stage was, in the end, far too thin. The "warmth" of Altay is by no means the reading on a thermometer—it is condensed in a word of care spoken in wind and snow, inscribed in the thick calluses on an old squad leader's hands, rising from a bowl of scalding ginger tea, and rooted even more deeply in these officers' and soldiers' burning, unwavering commitment and relentless pursuit of their duty and mission. This warmth flowing from the depths of the spirit is enough to withstand the harshest cold in the world.

李卓曦想起初中时在康西瓦烈士陵园祭拜的经历。昆仑风雪中，面对墓碑上平均年龄不足25岁的英魂，“牺牲”二字不再是教科书上的概念，而是冰冷的石刻与滚烫的热血。 那一刻，坐在台下的李卓曦攥紧拳头，决心接过沉甸甸的话筒，将强军故事里的心跳传递下去。 记者手中有一本16开宣讲册，封面简约厚重。翻开内页，扑面而来的是巡逻路上的冰碴、哨位上的寒霜、挡风玻璃上刮不净的冰花。这些带着“颗粒度”的文字，让军人形象可感可触，让英雄血肉丰满，让遥远的边关不再抽象。 然而，从“被光点亮”的听众，到“擎光而行”的主讲，这条路远比想象崎岖。 学员王婉琛曾以为宣讲不过是朗读美文。直到新训时，目睹宣讲人在台上分秒不差、声入人心的风采，才明白“台上一分钟，台下十年功”。 后来，自己被选进宣讲团后，为那“一分钟”绽放，王婉琛历经半年磨砺。讲稿推翻重写八九遍，视频素材重制五六次。起初是机械复述，后来如考古学家般深挖细节，甚至利用假期奔赴边关考证。泛黄的日记、模糊的合影、帐篷里烧不直的蜡烛……诸多细节被一一唤醒，她的讲述不再空洞，而是充盈着历史温度。 在宣讲团，这种近乎“折腾”的打磨是常态。 深夜备课室，键盘敲击声常至凌晨。为核实一个关于高原哨所供暖的细节，他们收集了十几份资料期刊；为增强一段演习视频的冲击力，他们自学剪辑软件逐帧调整。这份较真，源于对真实的敬畏，更是对英雄的尊崇。 从康西瓦的凛冽寒风，到金陵城的琅琅书声；从抚摸墓碑的震颤，到手握话筒的笃定。这群年轻的军校学员正进行一场跨越时空的精神接力。正如李卓曦在讲稿中写道：“边防军人走不出大山，但他们的故事，必须走出大山。” 熔铸本色 曾在舞台上诠释坚守的青年，开始书写新的坚守故事 2024年盛夏，校园里梧桐树的叶子沙沙作响。宣讲团成员刘适林站在“红色薪火”强军故事宣讲团展厅镜前，最后一次整理服装。此刻，他以庄重的仪式感，向一段岁月告别。 数月后，海拔3000多米的高原河谷。已任排长的刘适林俯身通信设备前，汗水滴落在高原冻土上。那个曾在舞台上诠释坚守的青年，开始书写新的坚守故事。 这种跨越，绝非偶然。3年宣讲，越是深情讲述他人的坚守，内心召唤便越发清晰。毕业分配时，刘适林在赴藏志愿书上郑重签名。 “我不觉得苦，真正的边防，远比这艰苦。”电话中，刘适林语气平静。他随队挺进某高地——那个连鹰都难越的“生命禁区”。风雪中穿行，他感到久违的踏实：“就像我讲述的故事，坚守，本就是一种习惯。” 祖国的西北角，零下50℃的极寒中，2025届毕业学员杨瑷琪裹紧大衣，跟随分队奔袭在边防线上。 “你会看到哨兵转身时，那张被冰雪雕琢却依然灿烂的笑脸。”电话里，杨瑷琪的声音带着笑意，也透着风雪的凛冽。这些曾存于讲稿中的画面，如今成了她日记中的日常注脚。那位总揣着热馕看望他们的哈萨克族阿妈，也从素材人物变成了邻里。 宣讲赋予他们的，不仅是炽热情怀，更是“把日子过成诗”的能力。刘适林因综合素质过硬，常组织连里的文体活动，以新方式延续讲台上的使命——温暖着故事里的人。杨瑷琪则将写讲稿时磨砺出的敏锐观察力，用于记录真实边防，试图向世界证明：极寒之地，不仅有牺牲奉献，更有滚烫的信仰与纯粹的快乐。 刘适林凝望窗外雪山，给出一名排长的回答：“曾经我在台上讲坚守，如今我在战位守山河。” 开山岛上的联合宣讲 ■郭子瑾 解放军报记者 石 宇 黄海深处，惊涛拍岸。 面积仅0.013平方公里的开山岛，孤悬海天之间，素有“黄海前哨”之称。 近日，陆军工程大学“红色薪火”强军故事宣讲团与“星火”理论宣讲服务政治教员群体代表，自连云港燕尾港乘艇破浪，登上这座精神高地，开展沉浸式教育实践活动。 此行，是一场双向奔赴的精神溯源之旅：“薪火”带来鲜活故事，追寻忠诚根脉；“星火”携理论之光，深扎实践沃土；而守岛人的日夜坚守，则是一堂无声却震撼灵魂的思政课。 清晨，五星红旗在海风中冉冉升起，全体成员庄严肃立。“升起的是国旗，守住的是阵地。”带队干部张扬说，那一刻，宣讲稿中的“忠诚”二字，骤然有了千钧重量。 随后，在“时代楷模”王仕花的带领下，大家踏上王继才32年如一日巡守的小路，走进哨所、升旗台。海风磨亮了斑驳的石阶，简陋的营房内，一盏煤油灯、一张旧木床、几本翻烂的日记，无声诉说着日复一日的坚守。 “今天重走英雄路，才真正读懂‘守岛就是守国’这几个字承载的如山使命。”“红色薪火”强军故事宣讲团成员姜欣雨站在王仕花身旁，心潮澎湃，眼眶湿热。 几天前，姜欣雨在某部讲述了飞行员执行任务时的生死考验。此刻，站在这位心系海岛的64岁老人身边，她忽然明白：英雄之光不仅闪耀在壮烈起飞的瞬间，更沉淀在日复一日、以岛为家的平凡岁月里——平凡铸就伟大，坚守即是忠诚。 这份深切的感悟，迅速转化为宣讲中的强烈共鸣。当天下午，一场别开生面的联合宣讲在临时搭建的讲台上展开。 情与理交融，故事与理论共振，如同两股清泉汇入心田。 “这样的宣讲直抵心灵，既有温度暖人心，又有深度启人思。”前来参观见学的江苏海警局一名干部深有感触。 这种“情理交融”的联合宣讲模式，让教育实践真正落地生根。“薪火”以真情传递温度，“星火”以理论赋予高度。薪火点燃信仰，星火照亮航程——二者在开山岛上交汇，燃起一簇不灭的精神火炬。 “登岛一次，受益终生；见贤思齐，方能行稳致远。”张扬说，开山岛不仅是一座精神丰碑，更是滋养宣讲队伍的“精神沃土”。在这里，团队成员亲身感悟“守岛就是守家，国安才能家安”的爱国奉献精神。这份跨越岁月的坚守与忠诚，淬炼了他们的理想信念，更为宣讲工作注入鲜活的力量与厚重的底色。 夜幕降临，交通艇缓缓离岛。回望海天之间，红顶白塔的轮廓渐渐模糊，但那面猎猎飘扬的五星红旗，已深深烙印在宣讲团成员心中，化作指引航向的永恒精神航标。 让青年官兵成为播火者 ■于鹏飞 当前，青年官兵成长于信息爆炸、思想观念多元的时代，对单向灌输、概念说教容易产生距离感。如何让习近平强军思想真正入脑入心、见行见效？陆军工程大学“红色薪火”强军故事宣讲团的生动实践给出了有力回答：唯有激发青年主体力量，让官兵成为教育的主角，才能激活思想政治教育的内生动力。 变受众为主体，主动参与才能活力迸发。新时代思想政治教育的关键突破点，在于激发受教育者的内生动力。“红色薪火”强军故事宣讲团打破“教员主讲、学员听讲”的传统模式，推动青年官兵从“台下聆听”走向“台上主讲”，成为故事的发掘者、讲述者、传播者。为讲好故事，成员们主动深入一线部队，深度走访亲历者，精心模拟战场情境，反复打磨动人细节。这个过程本身就是一场深刻的思想淬炼和精神洗礼。当同龄人以亲身经历真诚分享，教育便不再是外在灌输，而是内化为官兵自觉的认同与追求。 促单向为互动，双向奔赴才能星火燎原。一场成功的教育，不仅要讲得精彩，更要能点燃受众心中的火种。“红色薪火”强军故事宣讲团宣讲现场，常有官兵感动拭泪，也常有听众深受触动后，主动走上讲台分享自己的故事。教育由此形成“讲者信念愈坚—听者心灵共鸣—传者行动自觉”的良性循环，实现了火种的传递与接续。 如今，“红色薪火”强军故事宣讲团的足迹已遍布边关哨所、基层营连和各地校园。近年来，团队持续拓展教育阵地，与7家红色场馆共建教育基地。宣讲团团旗（徽）被中国人民革命军事博物馆收藏。 思想政治教育，其精髓不在于填满容器，而在于点燃火焰、照亮心路。当青年官兵真正成为教育舞台的主角，当鲜活故事成为理论武装的生动载体，当真诚互动取代单向说教，强军故事便不再是遥远的传说，而成为官兵身边可感可触的生动实践，成为新时代铸魂育人的鲜活样本。 阿勒泰的冬天很“温暖” ■某部排长 杨瑷琪 “阿勒泰的冬天最低温度可达-58℃，被誉为中国‘第二寒极’。”一年前，我在宣讲台上这样描述这片土地。台下，是一双双专注的眼睛。如今，我从故事的讲述者，成了故事中的人。 就在我咬牙坚持时，一位班长悄悄靠近，他的帽檐和围脖上结满冰晶冰柱，几乎难以辨认面容。他轻声说：“排长，第一次在这儿过冬，不适应很正常。实在冷，跳一跳、笑一笑，搓搓手、摸摸耳朵，暖意就来了。”看着风雪中战友们真挚的笑脸，我感觉胸口的寒意悄然散去。 原以为戍边就是骑马踏雪、巡逻边境，真正扎根于此，才知技术保障同样关键。面对陌生的通信设备和复杂的线路图，我这个曾经的理论骨干成了技术新兵。老班长们毫无保留，手把手传授接线、调频、排障要领。他们布满厚茧的双手，无声诉说着艰辛。我问：“这茧子，得练多少次？”老兵淡然一笑：“成百上千次吧。任务来了，容不得犹豫，必须快、准、稳！”他们钻研通信技术十几年，早已炉火纯青，闭眼都能精准操作，却依然孜孜以求更高效的组网方法。“技术在变，任务在变，我们不能躺在现有技能上睡大觉。”朴实的话语，传递着滚烫的进取心。
