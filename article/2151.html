The Old Barracks Silently Bears the Glory, Dreams, and Sweat of Generations of Officers and Soldiers

老营房无声承载着数代官兵的光荣、梦想与汗水
PLA Daily (解放军报) 6 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A company of the People's Armed Police Zibo Detachment is relocating from barracks built in the 1980s to a new facility, with the piece written as a farewell narrative by an officer named Long Jiale. This is institutional nostalgia content — a genre the PAP uses to reinforce unit cohesion and transmit values (hard striving, vigilance, camaraderie) to junior personnel during transitions that can disrupt unit identity. Its value is as a record of how the PAP frames collective memory and loyalty at the company level, not as evidence of any change in posture or capability.
Translation

Old Barracks ■ Long Jiale

Beneath the setting sun, the old barracks of a certain company of the People's Armed Police Zibo Detachment stands in quiet stillness. Built in the 1980s, its mottled, peeling walls, worn-smooth doorsteps, and deep-grooved brickwork silently bear the glory, dreams, and sweat of generations of officers and soldiers. Now, those officers and soldiers are about to bid it farewell and move into new barracks.

Walking slowly through the compound, my gaze drifts across the sentry post, the drill ground, the vegetable garden, the dormitories — the loyalty, striving, camaraderie, and warmth of home that have settled into every corner gradually blur my vision.

I gently push open the door of the honors room, and the company's glorious history rushes to meet me. Medals, commendations, and trophies fill the display in abundance. Inside a glass case, a rust-spotted hoe, wrapped in the weight of years, pulls my thoughts back forty years.

At the time, the company's construction faced enormous difficulties due to tight funding. To cut costs, all officers and soldiers worked as one — leveling ground, hauling bricks, carrying sand, spreading cement. The sun blazed overhead, dust filled the air, sweat soaked through their clothes and dried into patches of salt frost. Driven by that spirit of hard striving, the barracks rose from the ground in just half a year. After the main structure was completed, the officers and soldiers took up their hoes again, breaking new ground, building walls, and cultivating seedlings, achieving self-sufficiency in vegetables and fruit.

Without realizing it, I had drawn near the sentry post at the compound gate, where a bugle of dull, darkened color caught my eye. Through long stretches of time, it had again and again greeted the morning sun and roused the sleeping barracks; again and again reminded soldiers of regular routines, urged them to sharpen their arms and feed their horses (厉兵秣马), and called them to charge into battle.

Now, though the years have stripped it of its former luster, the company still places it solemnly beside the duty position. It reminds the sentries at every moment: stay vigilant, defend the homeland!

In the Fourth Squad dormitory, I met old squad leader Sun Zhonghui. When the subject of the move came up, he was overcome with feeling: "Before we got word of the move, I always longed to live in new barracks. Now that the move is actually here, I can't bring myself to let go of this old place."

Sun Zhonghui recalled that when he first joined the company, conditions were hard — there was no running water, and all water for daily use had to be pumped and stored. In winter, heating water was no easy matter, and hot water was precious. Squad leader Hu Xuguang always rushed to wash up and clean with bone-chilling cold water, saving the hot water for the new soldiers. His large, rough hands were cracked all across the backs — it was painful just to look at them.

Squad leader Hu was also skilled with a needle and thread; most of the mending in the squad fell on his shoulders. Each time he helped everyone sew and patch their clothes, he would wet the thread end with the tip of his tongue, give it a twist between his fingers, pass it through the needle, and then fly through the stitching — quick and steady.

Homesickness is the hardest thing every new soldier endures. For a stretch of time after lights out, Sun Zhonghui would toss and turn through the night, unable to sleep. Squad leader Hu noticed, came and sat on the edge of his bunk, and said: "Little Sun, missing home, aren't you? In a few days, your squad leader has a surprise for you." A few mornings later, a stone appeared on the grass in front of the mess hall, with the character "home" (家) carved into it in vivid red. Officers and soldiers gathered around, trading remarks: "There was no stone carving here yesterday, was there?" "Who carved this?" Only Sun Zhonghui, staring at the stone, felt his throat tighten, and for a long while could not find words.

The evening breeze moved through the corridors, and a few low, muffled barks drifted over. Following the sound, I looked and saw, beside the stone carved with the character "home," an old dog lying quietly, its silhouette merging with the dusk. Its name was "Xiaobei." It had accompanied the old barracks for more than ten years — its years of service longer than those of many currently serving officers and soldiers. Years ago, the company had brought in a puppy to strengthen sentry duties. When it first arrived at the compound, it ran curiously everywhere, lively and bounding with joy. As the days passed one by one, Xiaobei gradually grew familiar with its duty position. Whether in scorching summer or bitter winter, it always followed close on the heels of the officers and soldiers, heading to the sentry post.

Its mission fulfilled, Xiaobei was honorably retired. The officers and soldiers could not bear to send this old comrade-in-arms away, and so kept it in the compound to care for it. Now it is blind in both eyes, its steps unsteady; it no longer runs, and often lies in silence in its kennel. Perhaps it has forgotten the faces of the generations of officers and soldiers who have come and gone, but the all-too-familiar "soldier smell" (兵味) and the sound of sharp, clear calls remind it at every moment: home is still that "home," and the soldiers are still those "soldiers."

As the development of the unit's construction has advanced, the old barracks can no longer support the unit's missions or the needs of its officers and soldiers. After careful deliberation, the detachment made the decision to build new barracks.

The evening breeze passed through, and the poplar leaves rustled softly overhead, as if whispering their farewells. Reaching the compound gate, I stopped and looked back. The outline of the old barracks faded bit by bit into the dusk. All was still and silent — only at the nearest sentry post, a young figure stood perfectly straight.

老营房 ■龙嘉乐 夕阳下，武警淄博支队某中队的老营房静静矗立。它建于上世纪80年代，斑驳的墙皮、磨光的门槛、深深的砖缝，无声承载着数代官兵的光荣、梦想与汗水。而今，官兵即将告别它，搬入新的营房。 缓步行走在营区，我的目光抚过哨位、操场、菜园、宿舍……角角落落里沉淀的忠诚、拼搏、情谊与家的暖意，让我眼前渐渐朦胧。 轻轻推开荣誉室房门，中队的辉煌历史扑面而来。奖牌、奖状、奖杯琳琅满目。展柜内，一把锈迹斑斑的锄头，裹挟着岁月，将我的思绪拽回40年前。 当时，中队建设面临资金紧张的巨大困难。为节省成本，全体官兵齐心协力平土地、运砖头、搬沙子、抹水泥……烈日当空，尘土弥漫，汗水浸透衣衫，凝结成片片盐霜。靠着官兵的这股拼劲，营房在短短半年间拔地而起。主体落成后，官兵又挥起锄头，开荒垦地、垒墙育苗，实现了瓜果蔬菜的自给自足。 不知不觉间，我走近了营门哨位，一把色泽灰暗的冲锋号映入眼帘。漫长的时光里，它曾一次次迎着朝阳，将沉睡的营房唤醒；一次次提醒着规律作息，催促着厉兵秣马，召唤着冲锋陷阵…… 如今，岁月虽让它褪尽了昔日的风采，中队仍将它郑重安放在战位旁。它时刻提醒着哨兵：提高警惕，保家卫国！ 在四班宿舍，我见到了老班长孙忠辉。提起搬迁，他感慨万分：“没通知搬时，总盼着住新营房。真到要搬了，心里又割舍不下这老地方。” 孙忠辉回忆，刚下连时，中队条件艰苦，没通自来水，生活用水全靠水泵抽储。冬天烧水不易，热水金贵。班长胡旭广总是抢着用刺骨的冰水洗漱洒扫，把热水省给新兵。他那双粗粝的大手，手背上裂满了口子，看得人心疼。 胡班长还做得一手好针线活，班里缝补的活儿，大半落在他肩上。每次帮大家缝补衣物，他用舌尖轻舔线头，手指一捻一穿，接着便飞针走线，动作又快又稳。 想家是每个新兵最难熬的心事。有段时间就寝后，孙忠辉夜里总翻来覆去睡不着。胡班长看在眼里，坐到床边对他说：“小孙，想家了吧？过几天，班长给你个惊喜。”几天后的一个清晨，饭堂前的草地上多了一块石头，上面刻着一个鲜红的“家”字。官兵纷纷围观，大家你一言我一语地议论道：“昨天没见这里有石雕呀？”“这是谁刻的？”只有孙忠辉，望着石头，喉头滚动，久久说不出话来。 晚风穿廊，几声低沉的犬吠传来。循声望去，在刻着“家”字的石头旁，一条老犬静静伏卧，身影与暮色融为一体。它叫“小贝”，已陪伴老营房十多年，服役岁月比许多现役官兵都长。当年，中队为加强执勤引入幼犬。初到营区，它好奇地四处奔跑，欢快雀跃。日子一天天过去，小贝渐渐熟悉了自己的战位。无论酷暑寒冬，它总是紧随官兵脚步，走向哨位。 使命完成，小贝光荣退役。官兵舍不得送走这位老战友，便将它留在营中照料。如今它双目失明，步履蹒跚，不再奔跑，常默默伏在犬舍。或许，它已忘记了过去一茬茬官兵的模样，但那再熟悉不过的“兵味”和响亮的呼号声时刻提醒它，家依然是那个“家”，兵依然是那群“兵”。 伴随部队建设的发展，老营房已难以支撑部队任务与官兵所需。深思熟虑后，支队作出了建设新营房的决定。 晚风拂过，白杨叶在头顶细碎作响，似在低语告别。行至营门，我停步回望。老营房的轮廓一点一点融入暮色，万籁俱寂，唯有最近的哨位上，一个年轻的身影，站得笔直……
