These Servicemembers Have Fused Their Youth into the Mountains and Seas of the Motherland

这群官兵把青春融入祖国山海
PLA Daily (解放军报) 1 August 2026

About this analysis
Summary
In a feature timed to August First Army Day, PLA Daily profiles enlisted soldiers and junior officers assigned to observation and communications stations (观通站) of an unnamed brigade under the Eastern Theater Command Navy, stationed on a sub-0.1-square-kilometer East China Sea island and high-mountain posts in northern Fujian, focusing on Yang Zhou, Ding Dong, Wang Guanlong, Li Guoyong, Shen Hualin, and Zhu Pengfei. The article documents the brigade's retention mechanism — a cohort of thirty-plus long-serving veterans designated 'Mountain-Sea Guardians' (山海卫士) — as the institutional answer to converting initial enlistment motivation into multi-year commitment at remote coastal surveillance posts. This is standard political work content for the Army Day cycle; its value is as a record of how the Eastern Theater Command Navy frames retention, generational mentorship, and the 边海空防 (coastal and maritime air defense) mission to junior enlisted personnel, not as evidence of a capability or posture change — the specific brigade and station locations remain unidentified.

Salute to the August First Military Flag

■ Lin Qinggang, PLA Daily Special Correspondent Zhang Rongrong

The easternmost outpost: a sentry greets the first rays of dawn.

A company in the southern frontier: officers and soldiers pose for a group photo to celebrate "August First."

Deep in the mountains of the southern border: officers and soldiers of a Southern Theater Command Army company conduct a hazardous patrol.

An island in the East China Sea: an observation and communications station (观通站) of a brigade under the Eastern Theater Command Navy holds a swearing-in ceremony to welcome "August First."

In places others cannot see, the military flag accompanies us

At six in the morning, deep in the East China Sea, sea wind carrying its distinctive salty, briny smell awakens this island outpost, which covers a mere 0.088 square kilometers.

A patrol detachment from an observation and communications station of a brigade under the Eastern Theater Command Navy sets out along the island's perimeter road, treading on reef rocks that have been washed by waves countless times. Among the formation, one soldier stands out—dark-skinned, with a steady stride. His name is Yang Zhou, a post-2000 young man from Tangshan, who once served in a border defense company in Tibet. Early last year, he chose to re-enlist. He had just left the snow-covered plateau at an altitude of more than 4,500 meters when he arrived on this small island that cannot be found on a map.

Yang Zhou's favorite spot on the island is its southeastern corner, where a gray-white stone stele stands firm against the strongest sea winds. The four characters "领海基点" (territorial sea baseline point) carved on the stele have had their edges worn smooth by the years. Every time Yang Zhou walks to this spot, he gently touches it with his hand.

In Tibet, he guarded the boundary markers on the national border line; on this island, he guards the stone stele marking the territorial sea baseline point. Wind and sand have scoured his face; waves have beaten against his boots. The hardships differ, but the vigil is the same.

Yang Zhou still remembers when he first arrived on the island and the political instructor took the new soldiers on a "tour of the island." He was surprised to discover that, with the accelerating pace of reform and military strengthening, the island now has full 5G signal coverage, allowing officers and soldiers to video-call their families at any time. Moisture-proof boards and dehumidifiers mean the barracks are no longer damp, and soldiers can sleep soundly. When bad weather prevents the ferry from operating normally, drones can be used to deliver medicine and fresh vegetables.

"Before, there was nothing on the island but rocks and sea lavender (海芙蓉). Now we can grow vegetables—and even flowers," the political instructor said, gently touching a camellia bush with a budding flower. "The construction of coastal and maritime air defense (边海空防) builds not only fortresses, but also a 'home' where officers and soldiers can take root and grow."

Noticing that Yang Zhou had not moved from where he stood before the camellia tree, the political instructor asked what was on his mind. Yang Zhou paused and said: "I was wondering—how long does it take for a tree, from the time it is planted, to bloom?" The political instructor smiled and replied: "If you put down roots here, you'll find out naturally."

"To watch a flower bloom." With that goal in mind, the monotonous training became, in Yang Zhou's eyes, a measure marking his growth. As the sea wind blew from early spring into midsummer, his duty logbook grew from thin to thick, its pages covered layer upon layer with densely recorded航线 (track) markings. The flower had not yet bloomed, but in his heart he had already made a decision.

Not long ago, late one night, the duty room was brightly lit, and the glow of the radar screen fell across Yang Zhou's young face. At that moment, Radar Squad Leader Pan Jixu—who had served on the island for nineteen years—was also on duty. After processing a batch of air situation (空情) reports, he heard Yang Zhou say quietly: "Squad Leader, I want to stay on."

"Work hard." Pan Jixu patted him on the shoulder and watched him adjust the equipment parameters. In the duty room, the faint hum of operating equipment seemed, in that moment, to merge into the relay vigil of two generations of island guardians.

From the "Roof of the World" to the East China Sea outpost, the color of the uniform changed, the battle station underfoot changed, the starry sky overhead changed. But the original aspiration Yang Zhou made when he put on his uniform shines as brilliantly as the August First Military Flag flying over the mountains.

That summer when Yang Zhou was waiting for the flower to bloom, newly graduated officer Ding Dong climbed into a pickup truck headed for a high-mountain observation and communications station. He had ranked second in his specialty at school and could have had more choices, but when the classmate who ranked first chose the Nansha Islands, he chose the high mountain and sea island.

Early this year, Ding Dong went to sea to carry out a far-seas training mission. On one occasion, he discovered that the contact logbook on board for handling complex problems prominently listed the telephone number of his observation and communications station. In that moment, he felt immense pride.

Before the Spring Festival, Ding Dong returned to the mountain. Red lanterns hung in the garrison area, and the smell of food drifted from the mess hall. These moments of perseverance that he had once taken for granted suddenly carried a different weight after he had been to sea and seen that telephone number. His seemingly ordinary post was truly connected to the vast maritime frontier spanning ten thousand li.

From campus to the mountains, from officer-cadet to sub-station commander—his identity changed, his environment changed. Yet, just like Yang Zhou, the solemnity Ding Dong felt when he first saluted the August First Military Flag has never changed.

One is on an island, the other on a mountain, but the way they found the answer in their hearts is the same: in places others cannot see, the military flag accompanies us.

Carving "love" into the reef, and weaving it into the warp and weft of the military flag

There are many more officers and soldiers like Yang Zhou and Ding Dong in this brigade. They have come to high mountains and climbed onto sea islands to salute the August First Military Flag.

How to turn "I want to stay" into "I am able to stay"—how to transform initial drive into day-after-day resilience? For the confusion that often weighs on the minds of today's officers and soldiers, this unit has its own set of methods.

The brigade selected more than thirty "old high-mountain hands" and "old island hands" with sixteen or more years of service to serve as "Mountain-Sea Guardians" (山海卫士), setting a benchmark for new soldiers on the road ahead. Staff Sergeant Second Class Wang Guanlong is a "Mountain-Sea Guardian" at a certain island observation and communications station. In the garrison area, almost all the stone carvings were made by his hand: "Take the Station as Home" (以站为家) beside the basketball court, the "Roots Grove" (扎根林) behind the barracks, "Military Soul" (军魂) to the left of the dormitory…

"After so many years, I've grown accustomed to island life. Deep in my heart, I have long since made this place my home." This veteran island guardian used a carving knife to etch the character "love" (爱) into the reef—and wove it into the warp and weft of the military flag.

"Mountain-Sea Guardian" is a title, but more than that it is a sentiment—a conviction of putting down roots in the cracks of stone, a spirit of merging the individual into these mountains and seas.

Master Sergeant Li Guoyong, stationed at a high-mountain observation and communications station in northern Fujian, completes thirty years of service this year. By seniority, he is the most qualified person at the station to hold the title of "Mountain-Sea Guardian," yet when faced with the honor, he recommended a younger backbone soldier, Zheng Xinran: "He understands the post-2000 and post-2005 generation better than I do. New comrades can more easily see a reflection of themselves in him."

Last month, Li Guoyong compiled his experience and achievements into a volume—hundreds of thousands of characters of professional material spanning the full length of his military career, every word and sentence infused with thirty years of this island guardian's dedication.

"Do today's work well, prepare for tomorrow's work, and summarize yesterday's work." Even on the day he is demobilized, thirty years of experience must remain at the station, to be passed on and continue to shine.

Squad Leader Shen Hualin at a certain high-mountain observation and communications station does not have a high level of education. Things that others can memorize after reading once, he has to read many times. So he used the most basic method—copying while memorizing.

He copied a professional textbook from cover to cover. After finishing one copy, he extracted the key points and copied them again. As he copied, he came to understand more and more.

Later, he was awarded an individual third-class merit citation, earned a junior college diploma, and grew from a new soldier who only wanted to go home and start a business into the most senior squad leader in the station's diesel engine squad. The words he wrote on the flyleaf of his notebook reflect the patriotic sentiment (赤子情怀) of more than a decade of military service: "Guard the battle station underfoot; be a qualified island-defense soldier."

At the end of last year, the station's officers and soldiers used their rest time to build a stone path of more than sixty meters leading straight to the monument at the highest point of the mountain summit.

More than fifty years ago, the first generation of officers and soldiers carried equipment up the mountain on their shoulders. After that, wherever they walked, a path appeared. Today, the path underfoot is different, but the posture of climbing upward has not changed, and the posture of striving has not changed.

Giving the best years of one's life to the August First Military Flag, to these mountains and seas

The path was built, but harder than building the path is letting those who come after know how this path was built—and why it absolutely had to be built.

Corporal Zhu Pengfei, born in the year 2000, did not know any of this when he first came up the mountain. He had loved watching the TV drama "Blue Flame Commandos" (火蓝刀锋) since childhood. The day he put on the "wave white" (浪花白) naval uniform, he thought he was finally going to board a warship and cut through the waves—but instead he found himself climbing into a large truck headed up the mountain.

The gap between his ideals and reality left him perpetually dispirited. After just one week shadowing his squad, he made a mistake through carelessness. When the notification came down shortly afterward, Zhu Pengfei broke into a cold sweat. Squad Leader Chen Huaiji saw his remorse and, while comforting him, proactively wrote an account of the situation and a self-criticism on his behalf.

That evening, Zhu Pengfei went to apologize to his squad leader. Chen Huaiji told him the story of how, in the "August 6th" Naval Battle (八六海战), a radar detachment guided warships to sink an enemy vessel, and said earnestly: "If this were wartime, missing a single target in your report could cost a whole group of comrades their lives."

Zhu Pengfei lay in his bunk, those words seemingly carved into his mind. He thought of his own negligence, of the expectant look in his squad leader's eyes, of the glorious achievements of his predecessors… He could not sleep all night. As dawn approached, he said to himself: "There will never be a next time!"

Before the reveille sounded, Zhu Pengfei was already sitting in the study room. Chen Huaiji passed by and glanced through the crack in the door—he did not go in, and said nothing.

In the subsequent retention assessment, Zhu Pengfei won top honors in multiple rounds of signal-tracking evaluations and never again missed a single target report. His radar detachment also achieved first place in the brigade-wide skills competition that year.

After Zhu Pengfei took first place, Chen Huaiji never praised him to his face. But someone at the station noticed that, on that day, Chen Huaiji paused as he walked past the honor wall and stood before the radar detachment's competition award certificate—and smiled, from the heart… Later, Zhu Pengfei heard that when Chen Huaiji was a new soldier, his own squad leader had mentored him in exactly the same way.

At this small high-mountain station, what has been passed down through the generations is not only skill, but also a deep affection for "taking the station as home."

The officers and soldiers designed and built a "High-Mountain Garden." The garden wall is built in the shape of a drawn bow and arrow, symbolizing the observation and communications station standing guard over the maritime defense line like an arrow nocked and ready to fly. The thirty-four protruding stones on the wall represent comrades-in-arms from all parts of the country. Stone slabs on the lawn are arranged in the shape of an "ear," signifying that observation and communications soldiers are the "clairaudient ears" (顺风耳) of the motherland's maritime defense line.

In one corner of the garden stands a "Unity Monument" (团结碑), engraved with the names of the officers and soldiers who participated in its construction. Many of them have already taken off their uniforms and left the mountain, but in this form they have remained here.

The military flag speaks no words, yet it watches over the frontier soldiers. Standing beneath the August First Military Flag, standing before the radar screen, standing beside the territorial sea baseline point stele—these servicemembers' youth has been fused into the mountains and seas of the motherland.

敬礼，八一军旗 ■林庆港 解放军报特约记者 张容瑢 东极哨所，哨兵迎接第一缕朝阳。 南疆某连，官兵为庆祝“八一”合影留念。 南陲深山，南部战区陆军某连官兵涉险巡逻。 东海某岛，东部战区海军某旅某观通站举行迎“八一”宣誓仪式。 在别人看不见的地方，军旗陪伴着我们 清晨6点，东海深处，海风裹着特有的咸腥味，唤醒了这座面积仅0.088平方公里的海岛前哨。 东部战区海军某旅某观通站一支巡逻分队踏上环岛路，脚下是被浪花千万次冲刷过的礁石。队伍中，一个肤色黝黑、步伐稳健的战士格外引人注目——他叫杨州，一名“00后”唐山小伙，曾服役于西藏某边防连。去年初，他选择了二次入伍，刚刚离开海拔4500多米的雪域高原，就来到了这座地图上找不到的小岛。 杨州最喜欢这座海岛的东南角，那里有一方灰白色的石碑屹立在海风最劲处。石碑上“领海基点”四个大字被岁月侵蚀得边角圆润，杨州每每走到此处，都会用手轻轻触碰。 在西藏时，他守护的是国境线上的界碑；在这座海岛，他守护的是领海基点的石碑——风沙磨过他的脸，海浪打过他的靴，磨砺不同，守望相同。 杨州还记得刚来岛上时，指导员带新兵“逛岛”，他惊讶地发现，随着改革强军步伐的加快，如今的海岛，全岛5G信号覆盖，官兵随时能和家人视频通话；防潮板、抽湿机的配备让营房不再潮湿，官兵可以酣然入睡；遇到天气不好，班船无法正常通航，可以依托无人机运输药品和新鲜蔬菜。 “以前岛上除了石头就是海芙蓉。现在不仅能种菜，还能种花。”指导员边说边轻触一株打苞的山茶花，“边海空防建设，建的不只是堡垒，更是能让官兵扎根、成长的‘家’。” 见杨州站在山茶树前没走，指导员问他怎么了，他愣了一下说：“我在想，一棵树从种下去到开花，要多久。”指导员笑着答道：“你要是扎下根来，自然就知道了。” “看一朵花盛开。”有了这个目标，枯燥的训练，在杨州眼里成了标记成长的刻度。海风从初春吹到盛夏，他的值班记录本从薄变厚，上面密密麻麻的航线标记叠了一层又一层，花还没开，但他心里已有了决定。 不久前的一个深夜，值班室的灯光明亮，雷达屏幕的光，映在杨州青涩的脸上。这一刻，在海岛服役19年的雷达班班长潘积绪也在值班。处理完一批空情，他听到杨州轻声说：“班长，我想留队。” “好好干。”潘积绪拍拍他的肩膀，看着他调整设备参数。值班室里，设备运行的轻微嗡鸣，仿佛在这一刻融入了两代守岛人的接力坚守中。 从“世界屋脊”到东海前哨，军装的颜色变了、脚下的战位变了、头顶的星空变了。但杨州在穿上军装时许下的初心，一如山间飘扬的八一军旗般耀眼夺目。 去年，杨州等待花开的那个夏天，新毕业学员丁东坐上了去高山观通站的皮卡车。他在学校专业排名第二，本可以有更多选择，但排名第一的战友选择了南沙，他便选了高山海岛。 今年初，丁东出海执行远海实习任务。有一次，他发现船上处理复杂问题的联络本上，赫然写着他所在观通站的电话，那一刻，他无比自豪。 春节前，丁东回到山上。营区里挂起红灯笼，食堂飘出饭菜香。这些他从前觉得稀松平常的坚守时光，在出过海、看过那个电话号码之后，忽然有了不一样的分量。他那看似平凡的岗位，真的和万里海疆连在一起。 从校园走进大山，从学员变成分站长，身份变了，环境变了，和杨州一样，丁东第一次向八一军旗敬礼时的那份郑重，从未变过。 一个在岛上，一个在山上，但他们找到内心答案的方式是一样的——在别人看不见的地方，军旗陪伴着我们。 把“爱”刻进礁石里，也织进军旗的经纬中 像杨州和丁东这样的官兵，在这个旅队还有很多。他们来到高山、登上海岛，向八一军旗敬礼。 如何把“我想留下来”变为“我能留下来”，如何让最初的冲劲变成日复一日的韧劲？对于时下官兵心中常有的困惑，这支部队有一套自己的办法。 旅队遴选30余名兵龄16年以上的“老高山”“老海岛”担任“山海卫士”，为新战士们立起前行路上的标杆。二级军士长王关龙是某海岛观通站的“山海卫士”。营区中，篮球场旁的“以站为家”、营房后的“扎根林”、宿舍左侧的“军魂”……站里几乎所有的石刻都出自他的手。 “这么多年已经习惯了海岛生活，在心底早已把这里当成了自己的家。”一位守岛老兵，用刻刀把一个“爱”字刻进礁石里，也织进军旗的经纬中。 “山海卫士”是一个称号，更是一种情怀——一种把根扎进石头缝里的信念，一种把个人融入这片山海的精神。 闽北某高山观通站一级军士长李国勇，今年服役满30年，论资历他在全站最有资格担任“山海卫士”，可面对荣誉，他却推荐了比自己年轻的骨干郑鑫然：“他比我更懂‘00后’‘05后’，新战友们从他身上更容易看到自己的影子。” 上个月，李国勇把自己经验成果汇编成册，数十万字的专业资料，时间跨度覆盖了他的军旅生涯，一字一句都倾注了这位守岛兵30年的执着。 “做好今天的事，准备好明天的事，总结好昨天的事。”即使有一天退伍了，30年的经验也要留在站里，继续传承下去发光发热。 某高山观通站班长沈华林，学历并不高。别人看一遍就能记住的东西，他要看很多遍。他就用最笨的办法——一边抄、一边背。 一本专业书籍，他从头抄到尾。抄完一遍，又把要点摘出来再抄一遍。抄着抄着，很多东西就懂了。 后来他荣立个人三等功，考取了大专文凭，从一心只想回乡做生意的新兵，成长为站里油机班资历最老的班长。他在笔记本扉页上写下的这句话，映照着他从军十数载的赤子情怀：“守好脚下战位，当一名合格守岛兵。” 去年底，该站官兵利用休息时间修了一条60多米的石头路，直通山顶海拔最高处的纪念碑。 50多年前，第一代官兵用肩膀把设备扛上山顶。在那之后，他们走过的地方就有了路；今天，脚下这条路不一样了，但往上走的姿势没变、奋斗的姿态没变。 将最好的年华交给八一军旗，交给这片山海 路修通了，但比修路更难的，是让后来的人知道这条路是怎么修出来的，为什么非修不可。 千禧年出生的下士朱鹏飞刚上山的时候，并不知道这些。他从小喜欢看《火蓝刀锋》。穿上“浪花白”那天，他以为自己终于要上舰劈波斩浪，结果却坐上了上山的大卡车。 理想和现实的落差让他总也提不起劲，跟班一周，他就因为粗心犯了错。不久后通报下来，朱鹏飞惊出一身冷汗。班长陈怀骥看出了他的愧疚，一边宽慰他，一边主动写情况说明和检讨书。 当晚，朱鹏飞找班长道歉。陈怀骥给他讲起了“八六”海战中雷达分队引导舰艇击沉敌舰的故事，语重心长地说：“如果在战争年代，你漏报一个目标，可能就会牺牲一群战友。” 朱鹏飞躺在宿舍床上，那句话仿佛刻在脑子里。他想起自己的疏忽，想起班长期待的目光，想起前辈创下的辉煌……一夜难以入眠，天快亮的时候，他在心里对自己说了一句话：“再也不会有下一次了！” 起床哨还没响，朱鹏飞已经坐在学习室里。陈怀骥路过时从门缝看了一眼，没进去，也没说话。 在后来的留队考核中，朱鹏飞摘下多批信号跟踪考核桂冠，再也没有漏报过一个目标。他所在的雷达分队，也在那年取得全旅比武第一名的好成绩。 朱鹏飞拿了第一之后，陈怀骥没当面夸过他。但站里有人注意到，那天陈怀骥路过荣誉墙时停下脚步，站在雷达分队的比武奖状前，由衷地笑了……后来朱鹏飞听说，陈怀骥当新兵时，班长也曾这样帮带过他。 在这个高山小站，传承至今的不只有本领，还有一份“把站当家”的深情。 官兵们设计修建了一处“高山花园”。花园围墙垒成弓箭的形状，象征观通站扼守海防线如箭在弦、引而待发；围墙上34块凸起的石头，代表来自全国各地的战友；草地上的石板拼成“耳朵”模样，寓意观通兵是祖国海防线上的“顺风耳”。 花园一角还有块“团结碑”，上面刻着参与建设官兵的名字。他们中许多人已经脱下军装离开了大山，但他们又以这种形式留在了这里。 军旗无言，注视着边关军人。站在八一军旗下，站在雷达屏幕前，站在领海基点碑旁，这群官兵的青春便融入了祖国山海。 敬礼，八一军旗！ 图片由作者提供
