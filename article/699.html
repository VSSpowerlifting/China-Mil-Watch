A Single Order from the Detachment Draws Attention: Reflections on One Comrade's "Fall and Rise"

支队一纸命令引发关注，从一名战友“一落一起”说开去
PLA Daily (解放军报) 26 May 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A political commissar at a high-altitude garrison detachment — operating above 3,000 meters with oxygen at 70 percent of plains levels — describes how Xue Peng, demoted from chief of the Combat Service Planning Section during restructuring and stagnant in rank for years, was restored to that position in March after developing a logistics support plan for garrison training that was adopted detachment-wide. The article documents how the unit's 深化政治整训 (deepening political rectification training) campaign translated into concrete cadre management measures: reassigning five officers assessed as 躺平 (lying flat), promoting twelve frontline personnel, instituting quarterly motivational 'portraits' of cadres linked to standing committee members, and running ideological education at historical sites tied to the Qinghai-Tibet Highway Spirit. The framing points to a persistent institutional problem — how to sustain officer motivation under promotion bottlenecks and remote hardship postings — with this piece serving as a record of how political work organs are expected to diagnose and correct motivational decline at the unit level, not as evidence of a new policy line.
Translation

Reflections on One Comrade's "Fall and Rise" ■ Zhou Haiyan

In March of this year, a single order from the detachment drew considerable attention — Xue Peng, the former chief of the Combat Service Planning Section, was reappointed to that same position. His "fall and rise" is also a window through which I have observed the results of the detachment's deepening political rectification training (深化政治整训).

Shortly after I arrived at the detachment, I heard other leaders mention Xue Peng: "Two years ago, during the restructuring, he was reassigned from chief of the Combat Service Planning Section to assistant of the Transportation and Delivery Section. He didn't say a word and worked even more conscientiously. But later, because of an officer overage, he went a long time without a transfer or promotion, and gradually the life just went out of him." I looked through his work records and found that the ledgers he kept during the first two years were neat, meticulous, and thorough, but the entries from early this year grew progressively sloppier, and the content began to feel perfunctory. At one headquarters handover meeting, I asked about the progress of a certain task. Only after being called on did he slowly say: "Still working on it."

I knew this was not a question of ability — his drive had been worn down. One day I called him into my office: "Xue Peng, do you feel like there's nothing to look forward to?" He was silent for a long time before he raised his head: "Commissar, it's not that I'm afraid of hardship or hard work — I'm afraid that no matter how hard I work, no one will notice." I didn't rush to lecture him. Instead, I talked about my own experience: after being promoted to full regimental grade (正团) at my previous unit, I served in that position for nine years before being promoted again. By the sixth year in particular, watching comrades around me advance one by one, I felt restless too. My old commissar told me: "If you lie flat now, won't all the suffering of those first six years have been for nothing? Even if the unit is dissolved or reorganized, your own drive must not diminish." Later, I settled my mind and did the work in front of me steadily and well, and three years after that the promotion order came. I told Xue Peng: "The problem is temporary — the organization is resolving it step by step. But if you give up now, when the opportunity comes, will you still be able to stand tall before the officers and soldiers?"

I then assigned him the task of drafting the logistics support plan for high-altitude garrison training. He spent a solid week in his office, going through records and visiting the grassroots. One night at 11 o'clock, I passed by his office and saw the light still on. I pushed the door open and found him marking supply points on a map. He said: "Commissar, I want to develop a 'modular support checklist' that fixes the personnel, materiel, and time limits for every node." That plan was later rolled out across the detachment, and garrison training support efficiency improved significantly. I knew his spirit had come back.

In March of this year, after rigorous assessment, Xue Peng was reappointed as chief of the Combat Service Planning Section. The day the order was announced, he said: "I am grateful the organization did not give up on me. I will prove through my actions that I didn't 'lie' my way back — I worked my way back."

This brought to mind an episode from the life of founding General Zhang Aiping. During the Red Army period, Zhang Aiping was reduced from division commander to regimental commander due to a military reorganization. His position was lowered, yet he had not a word of complaint and continued to lead his regiment with full vigor. When someone spoke up for him, he waved it off: "Being a division commander is fighting; being a regimental commander is also fighting. As long as the rifle is in hand, what does rank matter? Leading the troops well and winning the battle — that's worth more than anything." Looking at the present, some individual cadres lose heart and lie flat (躺平) at the first setback. When all is said and done, their vision is too narrow and their character too shallow. General Zhang Aiping's story tells us: a person's worth does not lie in the height of their position, but in whether they always have drive, are in the right state, and dare to fight hard.

Xue Peng's transformation strengthened the detachment Party committee's resolve to reinvigorate the spirit of officers and soldiers in their work and undertakings. Combining this with the deepening of political rectification training, we launched a coordinated set of measures (组合拳) in cadre education and management: we established the clear orientation of "performance earns position" (有为有位), reassigned five cadres who had been "lying flat" for an extended period, promoted and placed in key positions twelve frontline backbone personnel with outstanding records, including three — like Xue Peng — who "fell and got back up"; we established a "state early-warning" mechanism, with standing committee members individually linked to grassroots units, conducting quarterly "portraits" of cadres' mental and motivational states, with early reminders and early assistance; we organized cadres for on-site instruction at the Kunlun Mountain Pass and the former site of General Mu Shengzhong's building, to revisit the "Qinghai-Tibet Highway Spirit" (青藏公路精神), and we conduct quarterly selections of "High-Altitude Vanguard" (高原先锋), so that those who work hard gain both recognition and honor.

Comrade Mao Zedong said: "A person must have a certain spirit." In soldiering on the plateau, that spirit and vitality (精气神) are especially important. Our detachment's garrison area has an average elevation of over 3,000 meters, with oxygen content at only seventy percent of that in the plains. Some comrades joke: "Just lying still is the equivalent of carrying a load for five kilometers in the interior." Yet it is precisely this kind of environment that most tests a person's state of being — whether one treats hardship as an excuse to lie flat, or as a whetstone for tempering oneself. Political rectification training is about rectifying work style, rectifying discipline, and rectifying one's state of being (整风、整纪、整状态). When a person's spirit is settled and their state is good, even the greatest difficulties can be overcome; when every member of a collective is in the right state, there is no snow mountain that cannot be crossed, no wasteland that cannot be traversed.

Not long ago, I saw Xue Peng leading a headquarters element through a combat readiness rapid-deployment exercise, busy and covered in sweat and dust. He said cheerfully: "Commissar, we've developed a 'high-altitude rapid ration resupply method' — it's nearly a quarter of an hour faster than before." I patted him on the shoulder and said: well done, well done. On the way back to the unit, I kept thinking: as commissar, my greatest wish is to see every cadre be like the poplar (胡杨) on the plateau — no matter how fierce the wind and sand, drive the roots deep; no matter how cold the wind and snow, stand straight. Because our state of being is connected to the unit's combat effectiveness; our spirit and vitality is the hardest backbone on the plateau.

Original Chinese
从一名战友“一落一起”说开去 ■周海燕 今年3月，支队一纸命令引起了不少关注——战勤计划科原科长薛鹏，重新被任命为该科科长。他的“一落一起”，也是我观察支队深化政治整训成效的一扇窗口。 我来支队后不久，就听其他领导提到了薛鹏：“前两年编制调整，他从战勤计划科科长调整为运输投送科助理员，二话没说，工作更加认真负责。可后来因为干部超编，一直没有调职晋升，慢慢地整个人就蔫了。”我翻看工作记录，发现他记录的台账前两年都工工整整、严谨细致，可今年年初的那几页字迹逐渐潦草，内容也开始敷衍。一次机关交班会，我问起他某项工作的进展情况，他被点名后才慢吞吞说了一句：“还在弄。” 我知道这不是能力问题，是心气儿被消磨了。一天，我把他叫到办公室：“薛鹏，是不是觉得没奔头了？”他沉默了许久才抬起头：“政委，我不是怕苦怕累，是怕再苦再累也没人看见……”我没有着急讲大道理，而是聊起了自己的经历：我在原单位晋升正团之后，一干就是9年才再次提拔。尤其到了第6年，看着身边同志一个个进步，心里也不甘。老政委对我说：“你现在要是躺平了，那前6年的苦不就白吃了？即使单位撤销并改，你自己的干劲儿也不能减。”后来，我沉下心把手头的事踏实干好，3年后迎来了晋升命令。我告诉薛鹏：“问题是暂时的，组织在一步一步解决。但如果你现在放弃，等机会来了，还能在官兵面前立得住吗？” 随后，我把制订高原驻训后勤保障方案的任务交给他。他连续一周泡在办公室，翻记录、跑基层，有一天晚上11时，我路过他办公室，看到灯还亮着。推门进去，他正对着地图标记补给点。他说：“政委，我想搞一个‘模块化保障清单’，把每个节点的人、物、时限都定好。”这份方案后来在支队推广，驻训保障效率有了显著提升。我知道，他的状态回来了。 今年3月，经过严格考核，薛鹏重新被任命为战勤计划科科长。宣布命令那天，他说：“感谢组织没有放弃我。我会用行动证明，我不是‘躺’回去的，是干回来的。” 这让我想起开国上将张爱萍的一段往事。红军时期，张爱萍因部队整编从师长降为团长，职务降低了，他却毫无怨言，照样把团队带得生龙活虎。有人替他抱不平，他摆摆手：“当师长是打仗，当团长也是打仗，只要枪在手，职位高低有什么关系？把部队带好、把仗打赢，比什么都强。”反观当下，个别干部遇到一点挫折就泄气躺平，说到底还是格局小了、境界低了。张爱萍将军的故事告诉我们：一个人的价值不在于职位高低，而在于是否始终有干劲儿、在状态、敢拼搏。 薛鹏的转变，让支队党委更加坚定了提振官兵干事创业状态的决心。我们结合深化政治整训，在干部教育管理上打出一套“组合拳”：立起“有为有位”的鲜明导向，调整了5名长期“躺平”的干部，提拔重用12名实绩突出的一线骨干，像薛鹏这样“跌倒再爬起来”的就有3人；建立“状态预警”机制，常委分头挂钩基层，每季度给干部精神状态“画像”，早提醒、早帮助；组织干部到昆仑山口、慕生忠将军楼旧址现地教学，重温“青藏公路精神”，每季度评选“高原先锋”，让实干者有名又光荣。 毛泽东同志说过：“人是要有一点精神的。”在高原当兵，精气神尤为重要。我们支队驻地平均海拔超3000米，含氧量只有平原地区七成。有同志开玩笑：“躺着不动都相当于在内地负重5公里。”可正是这样的环境，最检验一个人的状态——是把艰苦当作躺平的借口，还是把艰苦当作磨砺的砥石？政治整训是整风、整纪、整状态。一个人心气儿顺了、状态好了，再大的困难也能克服；一个集体人人都在状态，就没有翻不过的雪山、越不过的荒原。 前不久，我见到薛鹏正带着机关分队搞战备拉动演练，忙得满头是汗、满身是土。他兴高采烈地说：“政委，我们搞了个‘高原快速给养补给法’，比过去快了将近一刻钟。”我拍拍他的肩，连声说好。回单位的路上我一直在想：作为政委，最大的心愿就是看到每一个干部都像高原上的胡杨——风沙再大，也要把根扎深；风雪再冷，也要挺直腰杆。因为我们的状态，连着部队的战斗力；我们的精气神，就是高原上最硬的脊梁。
