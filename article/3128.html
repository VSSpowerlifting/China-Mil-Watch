The Finest Revolutionary Soldiers of the New Era | From "Top Martial Scholar" to "Joint Nerve Center" — A Profile of Central Theater Command Joint Staff Officer Li Can

最美新时代革命军人风采丨从“武状元”到“联合芯”——记中部战区联合参谋李灿

About this analysis
Summary
Li Can, a Central Theater Command joint staff officer, transferred in 2023 from an Army battalion commander position into the Joint Staff Department, where he has worked on joint operations planning, the July 2023 Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei flood relief command coordinating tens of thousands of troops, and the 2025 parade joint command organization overseeing ten thousand personnel and a thousand pieces of equipment. The article documents the institutional friction of cross-service integration at the theater command level — specifically, that officers arriving from single-service backgrounds must rebuild their operational logic from scratch before they can function in 一体化联合作战 (integrated joint operations), the PLA's declared basic operational form. The profile's value is as a record of how PLA media frames the ideal joint staff officer: technically rigorous, self-directed in cross-domain learning, and oriented toward grassroots implementability rather than doctrinal abstraction.

The Finest Revolutionary Soldiers of the New Era | From "Top Martial Scholar" to "Joint Nerve Center" — A Profile of Central Theater Command Joint Staff Officer Li Can

■ Photos by Wu Jingya, Huang Gaixia, Fu Chao; Graphics by Wu Jingya

Step inside the Central Theater Command Joint Operations Command Center: screens flicker, data streams surge. On the main display, multi-domain situational awareness updates in real time, as contests in the invisible domain feed back simultaneously with the thunder of armor rolling hundreds of kilometers away. Between the workstations, keyboard strokes fall dense as a sudden downpour, and orders flow from here directly to the front lines of exercises. Joint staff officer Li Can is one member of this nerve-center formation.

Li Can (first from left) discussing work with comrades. Photo by Fu Chao

A plan is never the best — only better

In 2023, Li Can passed successive rounds of selection and transferred from an Army battalion commander position into a department of the Central Theater Command Joint Staff Department. Crossing from a "single-service" role into "all-domain joint" operations, a hard battle of capability transformation lay before him.

At his first joint command war game, the commander led in-depth discussion on force disposition and fire coordination. When the conversation turned to air fighter cover for ground assaults and the coordination between armored elements and air power, Li Can found that his head full of force data was momentarily useless. This made him realize: memorizing data and mastering equipment was far from enough — he had to thoroughly understand the underlying operational logic of each service and branch before he could truly enter the joint realm and embed himself in actual combat. "Integrated joint operations (一体化联合作战) have become the basic operational form (基本作战样式). To avoid talking like an outsider and to avoid dropping the ball, I had to urgently supplement my joint thinking (联合思维)."

Fighting for what is needed, learning whatever is lacking. When he could not meet face-to-face with frontline troops, he called them on the phone, asking grassroots commanders and fighters to help him work through his questions, again and again. Colleagues nearby and experts from academies all became teachers for this "joint newcomer." He was a constant presence in the theater command's joint lecture hall. He carved out time to study Air Force, Rocket Force, and other service and branch knowledge, familiarizing himself with the tactical performance of various equipment.

Li Can studying materials in the reading room. Photo by Fu Chao

A joint staff officer must function like an "external brain," providing support for the commander's decisions, while also acting like the "central nervous system," transmitting the commander's intent precisely to the last node. Li Can said: "I have been a cadet, a squad leader, a platoon leader, a staff officer, and a battalion commander. Coming up from the grassroots, I understand the frontline better; having served as a principal officer, I can better understand the commander's intent. For a joint staff officer, the key word is 'joint.'"

What he cares about is whether a plan can actually be implemented at the grassroots level, at the front line. During one research effort, he read through hundreds of thousands of characters of tactical case studies, went through the plan line by line to cut redundancies, added multiple practical combat-handling measures, and the revised plan became a practical blueprint for grassroots combat training and war preparation. Someone suggested compiling classic tactics into a volume with fixed standards; he saw it differently: "A framework can serve as a foundation, but it cannot be used directly to fight a war. Modern warfare changes too fast." To improve efficiency, he designed his own "tactical map-marking multipurpose ruler," dramatically increasing the speed of hand-drawn map marking. Whenever he could join an exercise unit, he squeezed his way in; whenever he could join a training session, he never missed it. Comrades assessed him: "Anything that passes through his hands is rock-solid." Sometimes, after a plan had already been submitted to superiors for approval, he would suddenly conceive a better approach, communicate with comrades and leadership, and go back to report it again — "In Li Can's eyes, a plan is never the best, only better."

Striving to run his own leg of the relay

During the 2015 parade commemorating the 70th anniversary of the victory of the Chinese People's War of Resistance Against Japanese Aggression and the World Anti-Fascist War, Li Can served as a staff officer in the equipment formation command. In 2025, he became a core member of the parade joint command organization, shouldering the burden of overall planning.

Ten years earlier, his colleague Jiang Guo had sat at that same workstation. Jiang Guo said he had noticed this serious and grounded young man at the time. Ten years — this was not merely a succession of workstations, but a solid passing of the baton. Li Can, tempered through years at the grassroots and in staff organs, steadily caught that baton.

"People must be forged through tasks." At the grassroots level, Li Can honed his staff skills and his ability to lead troops in war preparation; after arriving at the theater command, he "took initiative, pursued excellence, communicated well, and always achieved coordinated results in his work," and was "adept at identifying patterns." When a mission arrived and the organization needed someone, he always stepped up — a "mutual commitment running in both directions (双向奔赴)."

Li Can himself felt it: "The weight on my shoulders is different now." During the parade mission, he never took leave to go home, frequently working until two or three in the morning. Late at night, he and his comrades used their desks as beds, catching brief moments of sleep while their minds continued to run through the plans. Comrade Yuan Xiaoqing said: "Li Can can often advance multiple tasks simultaneously on his own." The sustained high-intensity effort caused his blood pressure to rise, and on one occasion he fainted at the war-gaming site. Leadership ordered him to rest; after a brief recovery, he quietly returned to his post and delivered a new version of the plan first thing the next morning. It was through exactly this kind of meticulous calculation that, on the day of the parade, ten thousand personnel and a thousand pieces of equipment converged into a torrent of steel and passed before Tiananmen without a fraction of deviation.

The assessment comrades offered most often was: "In the face of a mission, he always steps forward and takes responsibility." In July 2023, extreme flooding struck Beijing, Tianjin, and Hebei. Li Can was locked to his duty watch position, fighting day and night for more than ten consecutive days, assisting commanders in organizing and directing tens of thousands of rescue troops, continuously integrating massive volumes of hydrological, disaster, and force data, and responding at any moment to changes in the disaster situation and operational requirements. He understood clearly: with thousands of troops looking to the command, at the critical moment of life-and-death rescue, the more precise the command, the more efficient the action, and the sooner the people in the disaster area would receive proper relief. Theater command leadership gave full affirmation: "This is what a joint staff officer should look like!"

Li Can guiding grassroots backbone training. Photo by Fu Chao

"Washing clothes in a dark room" — always ready

At the time, Li Can was serving as a staff officer in a headquarters organ when higher authorities organized an all-military staff officer skills competition. Li Can, with his solid professional skills, rigorous work style, and outstanding overall quality, was successfully selected for the intensive training team.

The training team was full of top performers. In the early phase, Li Can's overall scores held steadily near the top, but the competition was scored on individual events, and when formal selection came, he was not chosen — he was designated as a reserve member. He did not complain. He said calmly: "If I can train alongside them and learn more, it won't have been a wasted trip."

"Training every day alongside the main squad with no guarantee of getting on the field — what's the point?" Faced with incomprehension, Li Can told a story: a person washes clothes in a dark room — no light, no audience — and can only settle down and scrub, again and again. No one sees the process, but as long as you persist until dawn, the moment you push open the door and step into the light, the clean clothes are the best proof. He told this story to his comrades, to his family, and to himself.

When the main squad trained, he followed every session, watching closely to learn from others' strengths; when the main squad rested, he continued to sharpen every staff comprehensive subject — map marking, tactical documents, scenario exercises, and all the rest. On the day of the competition, he performed outstandingly across all subjects, with zero errors throughout. The team took first place overall, and he personally topped the field in the operational calculation subject.

Afterward, someone asked how he had persevered. He said there was nothing special about him — he simply had not given up on his efforts, and had remained in a state of constant readiness for war.

A theater command staff officer's work is buried in countless late nights — no spotlight, no exhilaration of charging across the battlefield, only meticulous verification repeated again and again, micro-adjustments and optimizations one after another, and round after round of bottom-line war gaming. At his desk and in the war-gaming room, Li Can studied and worked through plans repeatedly, dissecting them with precision slice by slice, benchmarking against actual combat, and converting historical experience into planning capability usable in the present. This work appears unremarkable, yet it is solidly woven into the answer sheet of winning battles.

Li Can often uses "washing clothes in a dark room" as a metaphor for the state of war preparation: war may break out tomorrow, but the battlefield never turns on its lights in advance. "Only by repeatedly refining and bottom-lining every link and every detail can you achieve the ability to fight at any moment, and to fight and win (随时能战，战则必胜)."

Carrying this ever-taut sense of crisis and urgency, Li Can, grounded in the theater command's actual mission requirements, took the lead in sorting out multiple sets of contingency response measures, refined full-chain standards, supplemented and improved content for handling extreme special situations (极端特情), and enhanced the fault tolerance and stress resistance of the war-preparation system.

Comrades say: "Working alongside Li Can, you gain capability and get things done."

最美新时代革命军人风采丨从“武状元”到“联合芯”——记中部战区联合参谋李灿 ■吴静雅 黄改霞 付超 摄；吴静雅 制图 走进中部战区联合作战指挥中心，荧屏闪烁，数据奔流。大屏幕上，多域态势实时更新，无形空间的较量与千里之外的铁甲轰鸣同步回传。席位间，键盘敲击声密如骤雨，一道道指令从这里直达演兵一线。联合参谋李灿，就是这中枢方阵中的一员。 李灿（左一）与战友研讨工作。付超 摄 方案没有最好，只有更好 2023年，李灿通过层层选拔，从陆军营长岗位调入中部战区联合参谋部某局。从“单一军种”跨入“全域联合”，一场能力转型的硬仗摆在面前。 第一次参加联合指挥推演，指挥员围绕兵力布势、火力协同组织深入研讨，谈及空中战机掩护地面突击、装甲分队与空中力量配合时，李灿满脑子的兵力数据一时派不上用场。这让他认识到：光背熟数据、搞通装备远远不够，必须吃透各军兵种作战运用的底层机理，才能真正走向联合、嵌入实战。“一体化联合作战已成为基本作战样式，要想不说外行话、不掉链子，必须恶补联合思维。” 为战务实，缺什么就学什么。不能直接与一线部队官兵面对面的时候，他就通过电话，一遍遍向基层指战员求解困惑。身边的同事、院校的专家，都成了这位“联合新兵”的老师。战区的联合讲堂里，总能看到他的身影。他挤出时间钻研空军、火箭军等军兵种知识，熟悉各类装备的战术性能。 李灿在学习室研读资料。付超 摄 联合参谋要像“外脑”一样为指挥员决策提供支撑，又要像“中枢神经”一般，把指挥员意图精准传导至末端。李灿说：“我当过学员、班长、排长、参谋，也当过营长。从基层走来，我更懂一线；有了主官经历，更能理解指挥员的意图。联合参谋，关键在‘联’。” 他在意的是方案能不能在基层、在一线落地。一次攻关，他研读了数十万字战法案例，对方案逐条删减冗余，新增多项实战处置措施，修订后的方案成了基层练兵备战的实用蓝本。有人建议把经典战法汇编成册、固定标准，他却有不同看法：“框架可以打底，但不能直接用来打仗。现代战争变化太快了。”为了提高效率，他自制“战术标图综合作业尺”，让手工标图速度大幅提升。只要演习能跟队，他就挤上去；能跟训，就绝不落下。战友评价：“他经手的东西，很托底。”有时方案已经报送上级签批，他突然有了更优的想法，跟战友、领导沟通后，又回头去汇报——“在李灿的眼中，方案，没有最好，只有更好。” 奋力跑好属于自己的这一棒 2015年纪念中国人民抗日战争暨世界反法西斯战争胜利70周年阅兵时，李灿是装备方队指挥部参谋。2025年，他成为阅兵联指机构骨干，扛起整体筹划的重担。 十年前，他的同事蒋郭也坐在那个席位。蒋郭说，他当时就注意到了这个认真扎实的年轻人。十年，不仅是一次席位的接续，更是一次扎实的传承。李灿用在基层和机关的淬炼，稳稳接住了这一棒。 “人要在事上练。”在基层，李灿锤炼了参谋技能和带兵备战本领；到战区后，他“主动担当，追求极致，善于沟通，工作总能联动到位”，而且“善于发现规律”。任务来临，组织需要时，他总能顶上去，这是一种“双向奔赴”。 李灿自己感到：“身上的担子不一样了。”执行阅兵任务期间，他从未请假回家，常常工作到凌晨两三点钟。深夜，他和战友们把办公桌当床，短暂合眼，脑子里还在推演。战友袁晓庆说：“李灿常常可以一个人同步推进多项工作。”连续高强度奋战，让他的血压升高，有一次晕倒在推演现场。领导命令他休息，他稍作平复后，又悄悄回到岗位，第二天一早便交出新版方案。正是在这样的精推细算下，阅兵当天，万人千装汇作钢铁洪流，分毫不差地通过天安门前。 战友评价最多的是：“他在任务面前总能主动挺膺担当。”2023年7月，京津冀突发极端汛情，李灿铆在值班席位，连续十余天昼夜奋战，协助指挥员组织指挥数万救援兵力，不间断整合水文、灾情、兵力等海量数据，随时响应灾情变化和行动需求。他深知，这是千军万马看指挥，在生死营救的关键时刻，指挥越精准，行动越高效，灾区人民越早得到妥善救援。战区领导给予充分肯定：“这才是联合参谋该有的样子！” 李灿指导基层骨干训练。付超 摄 “黑屋子里洗衣服”，时刻准备着 彼时李灿在机关任参谋，上级组织全军参谋业务比武。李灿凭借业务扎实、作风硬朗、综合素质拔尖，顺利入选集训队。 集训队里高手如云。初期，李灿综合成绩稳居前列，但比武比的是单项成绩，正式选拔时他没有选上，只被定为预备队员。他没有抱怨，平静地说：“能跟着练、多学东西，就不白来。” “天天陪着主力练，都不一定有上场机会，图啥？”面对不解，李灿讲起一个故事：一个人在黑屋子里洗衣服，没有灯光，没有观众，只能沉下心来一遍遍搓。没人看见过程，但只要坚持到天亮，推开门迎着光亮的那一刻，干净的衣服就是最好的证明。这个故事，他讲给战友，也讲给家人，更讲给自己。 主力队员训练，他全程跟进，紧盯细节学习他人长处；主力队员休息，他继续打磨要图标绘、战术文书、想定作业等所有参谋综合课目。比武当天，他各课目发挥出色，全程零失误，团队取得团体第一名，个人也在作战计算课目上拔得头筹。 事后有人问他怎么坚持下来的，他说自己没什么特别的，只是没有放弃努力，时刻待战备战。 战区参谋的工作藏在无数个深夜里，没有聚光灯关注，没有驰骋沙场的痛快，只有一遍遍细致核对、一次次微调优化、一轮轮兜底推演。书桌旁、推演室里，李灿一遍遍研究推案，切片式地精准拆解、对标实战，把历史经验转化为当下可用的筹划能力。这些工作，看似不起眼，却扎扎实实融入到胜战的答卷里。 李灿常以“黑屋子里洗衣服”比喻备战状态，战争或许明天打响，但战场从来不会提前亮灯。“只有把每一个环节、每一个细节反复打磨、兜底，才能做到随时能战，战则必胜。” 带着这份时刻紧绷的危机感和紧迫感，李灿结合战区任务实际，牵头梳理多套情况应对措施，细化全链条规范，补充完善极端特情处置内容，提升备战体系的容错性和抗压能力。 战友们说：“跟李灿共事，长本事、能成事。”
