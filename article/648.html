About this analysis
Summary
The Long March 2F Yao-23 rocket launched the Shenzhou 23 crewed spacecraft from Jiuquan at 23:08 on May 24, carrying commander Zhu Yangzhu, Zhang Zhiyuan, and Li Jiaying — the first astronaut from Hong Kong — on the 40th mission of China's crewed spaceflight program, bringing the total number of Chinese astronauts who have reached orbit to 30. The article's most operationally significant disclosure is the November 2025 porthole-crack incident: a debris strike on Shenzhou 20 forced the program's first emergency crew-transfer operation, left the Shenzhou 22 crew designation permanently vacant, and drove a 16-day emergency launch-readiness cycle for the Long March 2F Yao-22 rocket. The framing — threading Li Jiaying's Hong Kong identity, Qian Xuesen's 1955 return through Luohu, and the Long March anniversary into a single nationalist narrative — points to a deliberate effort to use the mission to reinforce Hong Kong's integration into the PRC's state-prestige story.
Translation

Shenzhou Soars Again Ninety Thousand Li, Seven Decades of Thunder and Storm — Written on the Occasion of the Successful Launch of the Shenzhou 23 Crewed Spacecraft

Xinhua News Agency Correspondents

Thousand-year-old Euphrates poplars stand tall, bearing witness to the heroic act of reaching for the heavens; the boundless Gobi Desert speaks in silence, lifting heroes as they set out on their mission.

At 23:08 on May 24, the Long March 2F Yao-23 carrier rocket carrying the Shenzhou 23 crewed spacecraft ignited and lifted off.

This was the 40th launch mission of China's crewed spaceflight program, the 644th flight of the Long March rocket, and the 23rd flight of the Shenzhou spacecraft.

The thunderous roar was like ten-thousand-ton wind and lightning. Looking back along the road traveled, history's long river has been marked by such earth-shattering moments: on October 8, 1956, the Fifth Research Institute of the Ministry of National Defense was established, and China's space program was born.

Seventy years — thunder and storm have shaken the land of China. From Jiuquan to Taiyuan, from Xichang to Wenchang, China's space workers have used the sky as their canvas and exhaust flames as their brush, painting a magnificent trajectory of advance toward becoming a space power.

The spark of the dream of flying to the heavens, kindled in individual hearts, spread like wildfire in the spring breeze of the era.

In June 1986, on the banks of the Yellow River, Zhang Zhiyuan was born into a family of teachers in Baiyin, Gansu. Three months later, along the Grand Canal, an ordinary farming family in Peixian, Jiangsu, welcomed their eldest son, Zhu Yangzhu. In November, in Tsuen Wan, Hong Kong, where the bauhinia blooms, Li Jiaying celebrated her fourth birthday.

They came from across the length and breadth of the motherland, each with a different life trajectory, yet they stood shoulder to shoulder and set out together on a journey to the stars and the sea — late at night on May 24, 2026, the Shenzhou 23 crew, composed of Zhu Yangzhu, Zhang Zhiyuan, and Li Jiaying, flew off toward the Milky Way.

On October 15, 2003, Yang Liwei set out from Jiuquan into space, fulfilling the Chinese nation's thousand-year dream of flying to the heavens.

Li Jiaying, then a student at the University of Hong Kong, watched Yang Liwei's spaceflight video over and over again. Zhang Zhiyuan, who had already passed the initial pilot selection, was jokingly called by his classmates a "future astronaut." The deeply moved Zhu Yangzhu later, with the stubborn determination of "refusing to accept reassignment," gained admission to the National University of Defense Technology's Department of Flight Vehicle Systems and Engineering, saying, "If I can't go to space myself, then I'll study the things that go to space."

Those dreams that seemed so far out of reach were brewing with infinite possibility in the spring breeze of the era. On a land of China changing with each passing day, one spacecraft after another was lifted into space — from one person for one day to multiple people for multiple days, from working inside the cabin to spacewalking…

In May 2018, China launched the selection process for its third cohort of astronauts. Military academy instructor Zhu Yangzhu applied; Air Force pilot Zhang Zhiyuan entered the selection; both stood out from approximately 2,500 candidates and joined the People's Liberation Army Astronaut Corps.

Four years later, China launched the selection of its fourth cohort of astronauts, and for the first time selected payload specialists from Hong Kong and Macao. Among the 120 application forms received by the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, the photograph of Li Jiaying showed her smiling broadly.

Those who pursue their dreams with their feet planted firmly on the ground travel this arduous road to reach the stars.

In an orbit more than 300 kilometers above the surface, the Tianhe core module, the Wentian experiment module, and the Mengtian experiment module are assembled like building blocks into a "T" configuration. The Chinese Space Station, once drawn only on blueprints, has become a "Chinese star" patrolling the heavens day and night.

In 2023, Zhu Yangzhu became the first among the third cohort of astronauts to fly to space. He said: "It is the new era that gives us dreams to pursue and makes those dreams achievable!"

When the Shenzhou 23 crew departed in the dead of night, the 40th launch mission of the crewed spaceflight program got underway. With Zhang Zhiyuan and Li Jiaying entering space, China now has 30 astronauts who have fulfilled their dream of reaching the heavens.

"The space station missions have cultivated a team of astronauts who have executed space missions and possess rich spaceflight experience, which can provide a solid reserve of talent for the selection of astronaut crews for subsequent crewed lunar landing missions," said Zhang Jingbo, spokesperson for the China Manned Space Program.

A half-moon hangs obliquely at the edge of the sky — that is the distant destination the Chinese people are about to reach.

The journey is often met with wind and rain, but nothing can stop the footsteps of conviction.

The crew designation "Shenzhou 22" will remain forever vacant in the sequence — and the story begins with a fragment of space debris barely visible to the naked eye.

In early November 2025, before the Shenzhou 20 crew returned to Earth, a crack caused by a space debris impact was discovered on the spacecraft's porthole. Everyone drew a sharp breath: could a spacecraft with a crack still guarantee the safety of the astronauts?

The Space Station Operations Management Committee decided to postpone the return mission of the Shenzhou 20 crewed spacecraft; all systems strictly carried out various tests and joint debugging and testing according to procedures. Tens of thousands of space workers, with no time to savor the joy and relief of having just sent Shenzhou 21 into orbit, plunged headlong into the new mission.

On November 4, the first emergency operation in the history of China's crewed spaceflight was initiated — the Shenzhou 20 crew returned to the Dongfeng Landing Site aboard the Shenzhou 21 crewed spacecraft; the Shenzhou 22 spacecraft was placed on a 16-day emergency launch readiness; and the Shenzhou 20 spacecraft was to return unmanned at an opportune time.

Spaceflight has always been an undertaking full of risk and challenge.

Shenzhou 1 once had its heat shield base opened for comprehensive inspection to eliminate a technical hazard. The launches of Shenzhou 4 and Shenzhou 15 both faced the test of extremely low temperatures and bitter cold. The Shenzhou 2 rocket-spacecraft assembly unexpectedly collided with the mobile launch platform…

Ordinary mortals venture far; conviction knocks at heaven's gate.

When Shenzhou 5 ascended, Yang Liwei experienced 26 seconds of resonance that made him believe for a moment he was on the verge of death. But he said: "Even if it costs one's life to realize the dream of a nation, it is worth it."

During Shenzhou 7's execution of China's first extravehicular activity, a "fire in the orbital module" alarm suddenly sounded. In that life-or-death instant, astronaut Zhai Zhigang's resolute choice was to exit the spacecraft: "Even if we cannot return, we must let the Five-Starred Red Flag fly in space."

When the return to Earth was put on "pause," the Shenzhou 20 crew immediately declared they would subordinate themselves to the overall mission situation, defer to ground-based decisions, and accept any outcome.

The interests of the motherland above all else — this is not only the conviction of the astronaut community, but also the conviction of the more than 700 martyrs resting eternally in the Dongfeng Revolutionary Martyrs Cemetery, and the shared conviction of all of China's space workers.

On November 14, 2025, hatch opener Liu Wenbo opened the hatch of the Shenzhou 21 return capsule, and the Shenzhou 20 crew returned home safely. On November 25, Liu Feng of the China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation watched as the Long March 2F Yao-22 rocket ignited and soared skyward — this rocket had transitioned from standby to launch-ready status, completing testing, final assembly, propellant loading, and launch within 16 days. On January 19, 2026, the Shenzhou 20 spacecraft successfully landed; at that moment, ten thousand rays of morning light shone over the Dongfeng Landing Site.

Astronaut crew designations are normally consistent with the mission number of the spacecraft they fly, and so the Shenzhou 22 crew designation will remain forever vacant in the sequence. "It reminds us never to think we have achieved a perfect score of 100." said Jia Shijin of the China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation.

The Shenzhou spacecraft team replaced the porthole on Shenzhou 23 and enhanced its protective capability against space debris impacts. The rocket system validated the Long March 2F rocket's emergency launch operational capability and continuously raised the level of product quality control. The launch site system summarized mission experience and optimized work procedures and position handoffs…

The story has not yet reached its end. In wind and rain, people hold up the umbrella and press forward.

The space dream and the dream of a strong nation are concentric circles in the hearts of hundreds of millions of Chinese sons and daughters.

Luohu Port, Shenzhen — the core hub of integrated development in the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area.

More than 70 years ago, there was only a single Luohu Bridge here. In October 1955, Qian Xuesen, dressed in a black suit, walked onto the bridge — he had just broken through layer upon layer of American obstruction and returned to the motherland via Hong Kong.

Shortly after returning to China, he was tasked with establishing the Fifth Research Institute of the Ministry of National Defense. A group of people "vanished without a trace" in the autumn of 1956, founding the space program of New China.

In 1958, Sun Jixian — who had led Red Army soldiers to force a crossing of the Dadu River during the Long March and had served as deputy commander of the 20th Corps of the Chinese People's Volunteers — led the officers and soldiers of the 20th Corps who had "mysteriously disappeared" from the Korean battlefield on a march of thousands of li into the northwestern desert, to establish China's first comprehensive missile test range, opening a "new Long March" aimed at space.

In 1960, China's first domestically produced surface-to-surface missile — the Dongfeng-1 — rose into the sky. In 1964, China's first atomic bomb was successfully detonated. In 1967, China's first hydrogen bomb air-burst test succeeded. In 1970, China's first artificial satellite was successfully launched, and the melody of "The East Is Red" resounded through the heavens… With each thunderous roar, the world was compelled to look anew at "the East."

The wheel of history rolls ever forward.

On July 1, 1997, the Chinese government resumed the exercise of sovereignty over Hong Kong.

In the jubilant crowds on the streets of Tsim Sha Tsui, 15-year-old Li Jiaying bore witness: the flag of the People's Republic of China and the flag of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region were raised on the banks of the Xiangjiang River. After Hong Kong's return, she was able to go back with her parents to visit relatives in Shunde, Guangdong, passing through Luohu Port along the way.

"Hong Kong has always shared the same fate as the motherland through thick and thin, bound together by ties of blood. We are not only witnesses to the motherland's development, but participants and contributors." As the first astronaut from Hong Kong, she said with deep feeling at a collective meeting with Chinese and foreign media reporters: "Let us all work hard together — there will surely be a better tomorrow!"

"Shenzhou" flies to the heavens, "Tiangong" patrols the skies, "Chang'e" journeys to the moon, "Zhurong" explores Mars, "Xihe" chases the sun… This is the better tomorrow that the older generation of space workers longed for.

"Exploring the vast universe and building China into a space power is the space dream we pursue without ceasing." This is the better tomorrow that the space workers of the new era strive for.

On May 24, Sun Dongning, son of Sun Jixian, came to the Dongfeng Revolutionary Martyrs Cemetery and poured a bottle of water drawn from the Dadu River before Sun Jixian's grave.

"This year marks the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Red Army's Long March and the 70th anniversary of the founding of China's space program. From the Long March road to the space road, you and countless revolutionary forebears walked out a 'Long March for eternity' with your entire lives." Standing before his father's grave, Sun Dongning said.

At midnight, the rocket soared into the sky, its flames cleaving the heavens, and the letters "CZ" — representing "Long March" — gleamed brilliantly on the milky-white body of the rocket. This is a fresh mark on the journey of the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation, another magnificent expedition of China's space program toward the stars and the sea. (Correspondents Li Guoli, Liu Yi, Huang Yichen, Yang Ru, Liu Yinuo)

(Xinhua News Agency, Jiuquan, May 24)

神舟再上九万里 风雷激荡七十年——写在神舟二十三号载人飞船成功发射之际 新华社记者 千年胡杨挺立，见证问天壮举；万里戈壁无言，托举英雄出征。 5月24日23时08分，搭载神舟二十三号载人飞船的长征二号F遥二十三运载火箭点火发射。 这是中国载人航天第40次发射任务、长征火箭的第644次飞行、神舟飞船的第23次飞行。 隆隆巨响如万钧风雷。回首来路，历史长河中不乏这样石破天惊的时刻：1956年10月8日，国防部第五研究院成立，中国航天事业由此发轫。 70载，神州风雷激荡。从酒泉到太原、从西昌到文昌，中国航天人以苍穹为纸，以尾焰作画，绘出一道向航天强国迈进的壮丽轨迹。 飞天梦的星火在个人胸中点燃 在时代春风里燎原 1986年6月，黄河岸边，张志远诞生在甘肃白银的教师家庭。3个月后，京杭大运河畔，江苏沛县一个普通农家迎来长子朱杨柱。11月，紫荆花开的香港荃湾，黎家盈迎来4岁生日。 他们来自祖国大江南北，拥有不同人生轨迹，却并肩踏上星辰大海的征途——2026年5月24日深夜，由朱杨柱、张志远、黎家盈组成的神舟二十三号航天员乘组飞赴星河。 2003年10月15日，杨利伟从酒泉出征太空，中华民族千年飞天梦圆。 在香港大学读书的黎家盈，反复观看杨利伟的飞天视频；已过飞行员初选的张志远，被同学们开玩笑是“未来的航天员”；心情激动的朱杨柱，后来以“不服从调剂”的执着，考入国防科技大学飞行器系统与工程专业，说“上不了天，那就研究上天的东西”。 那些看似遥不可及的梦，在时代的春风里酝酿着无限可能。日新月异的神州大地，托举一艘艘飞船进入太空——从一人一天到多人多天，从舱内工作到太空行走…… 2018年5月，我国第三批航天员选拔工作启动。军校教员朱杨柱报名、空军飞行员张志远参选，从约2500名候选对象中脱颖而出，步入中国人民解放军航天员大队。 4年后，我国启动第四批航天员选拔，并首次在港澳地区选拔载荷专家。香港特区收到的120份报名表中，照片里的黎家盈笑意盈盈。 脚踏实地的追梦人，循此苦旅，以达星际。 距离地面300多公里高的轨道上，天和核心舱、问天实验舱、梦天实验舱像积木一样拼成“T”字构型。曾画在蓝图里的中国空间站，已变成一颗昼夜巡天的“中国星”。 2023年，朱杨柱在第三批航天员中率先飞天。他说：“是新时代让我们有梦可追、追梦可成！” 当神二十三乘组星夜启程，载人航天工程第40次发射任务拉开帷幕。张志远、黎家盈进入太空后，我国已有30名航天员圆梦九天。 “空间站任务培养了一支执行过空间任务、拥有丰富太空飞行经验的航天员队伍，可为后续载人登月任务航天员乘组选拔提供坚实人才储备。”中国载人航天工程新闻发言人张静波说。 半轮明月斜挂天边，那是中国人即将抵达的远方。 征途常有风雨 但没有什么能阻挡信念的脚步 “神舟二十二号”这个乘组编号永远空缺，要从一块人类肉眼难以捕捉的空间碎片说起—— 2025年11月初，神二十乘组返回地球前，飞船舷窗惊现一道被空间碎片撞击的裂纹。所有人都倒吸一口凉气：有裂纹的飞船，还能确保航天员安全吗？ 空间站运行管理委员会决定，神舟二十号载人飞船返回任务推迟实施；各系统严格按流程开展各项测试和联调联试。成千上万名航天人顾不上刚把神二十一送上天的喜悦与疲惫，一头扎进新的任务。 11月4日，中国载人航天史上首次应急行动启动——神二十乘组乘坐神舟二十一号载人飞船返回东风着陆场，启动神舟二十二号飞船16天应急发射，神舟二十号飞船择机空船返回。 航天，从来就是一项充满风险与挑战的事业。 神舟一号曾为排除技术隐患，打开防热大底进行全面检测；神舟四号和神舟十五号发射都面临过低温严寒条件的考验；神舟二号船箭组合体意外与活动发射平台相撞…… 以凡躯赴远，以信念叩天。 神舟五号升空时，杨利伟曾遭遇26秒共振，让他一度以为自己濒临死亡。但他说：“就算付出生命，去实现一个民族的梦想，这是值得的。” 神舟七号执行我国首次空间出舱活动期间，“轨道舱火灾”的报警突然响起，航天员翟志刚在生死瞬间的坚定选择是出舱，“即使我们回不去，也要让五星红旗在太空飘扬”。 当返回地球按下“暂停键”，神二十乘组第一时间表态，服从任务大局，服从地面决策，接受任何结果。 祖国利益高于一切，不仅是航天员群体的信念，也是长眠在东风革命烈士陵园中700多位英烈的信念，更是中国航天人共同的信念。 2025年11月14日，开舱手刘文博打开神舟二十一号返回舱舱门，神二十乘组安全回家。11月25日，中国航天科技集团刘烽目送长二F遥二十二火箭点火升空，这枚火箭从待命转入待发，在16天时间里完成测试、总装、加注、发射。2026年1月19日，神舟二十号飞船成功着陆，那一刻，东风着陆场晨光万道。 航天员乘组编号通常与执行的飞船任务编号一致，因而神二十二乘组在序列中永远空缺。“它警醒着我们，永远不要以为做到了100分。”中国航天科技集团贾世锦说。 神舟飞船团队为神舟二十三号进行了舷窗更换，提升抵御空间碎片撞击的防护能力；火箭系统验证了长二F火箭应急发射实战能力，不断提高产品质控水平；发射场系统总结任务经验，优化工作流程、岗位衔接…… 故事并没有画上句号。风雨中，人们撑起前行的伞。 航天梦与强国梦 是亿万中华儿女心中的同心圆 深圳罗湖口岸，粤港澳大湾区融合发展的核心枢纽。 70多年前，这里只有一座罗湖桥。1955年10月，穿着黑色西服的钱学森走上桥头——他刚冲破美国的重重阻碍，经香港回到祖国。 归国后不久，他受命组建国防部第五研究院，一群人在1956年秋天“销声匿迹”，创建新中国的航天事业。 1958年，曾在长征途中率领红军战士强渡大渡河的中国人民志愿军第20兵团副司令员孙继先，带着从朝鲜战场上“神秘消失”的第20兵团将士千里挺进西北大漠，筹建我国第一个导弹综合试验靶场，开启了一场面向太空的“新长征”。 1960年，我国第一枚国产地地导弹——东风一号腾空而起。1964年，我国第一颗原子弹爆炸成功。1967年，我国第一颗氢弹空爆试验成功。1970年，我国第一颗人造卫星成功发射，《东方红》乐曲响彻苍穹……世界在一声声风雷巨响中，重新正视“东方”。 历史的车轮滚滚向前。 1997年7月1日，中国政府对香港恢复行使主权。 尖沙咀街头欢庆的人潮中，15岁的黎家盈见证：中华人民共和国国旗和香港特别行政区区旗在香江之畔升起。香港回归后，她得以和父母常回广东顺德老家探亲，途经的也是罗湖口岸。 “香港始终与祖国风雨同舟、血脉相连。我们既是祖国发展的见证者，更是参与者和贡献者。”作为首个来自香港的航天员，她与中外媒体记者集体见面时深情地说，“大家一起努力，一定会有更好的明天!” “神舟”飞天、“天宫”巡弋、“嫦娥”奔月、“祝融”探火、“羲和”逐日……这是老一辈航天人期盼的、更好的明天。 “探索浩瀚宇宙，建设航天强国，是我们不懈追求的航天梦。”这是新时代航天人为之奋斗的、更好的明天。 5月24日，孙继先之子孙东宁来到东风革命烈士陵园，将一瓶取自大渡河的水洒在孙继先墓前。 “今年是红军长征胜利90周年，也是中国航天事业创建70周年。从长征路到航天路，您和无数革命先辈用一生走出了‘永远的长征’。”站在父亲墓前，孙东宁说。 子夜，火箭腾空而起，烈焰划破长空，乳白色的箭体上代表“长征”的“CZ”字样熠熠生辉。这是中华民族伟大复兴征途上的崭新印记，是中国航天向着星辰大海的又一次壮丽出征。（记者李国利、刘艺、黄一宸、杨茹、刘一诺） （新华社酒泉5月24日电）
