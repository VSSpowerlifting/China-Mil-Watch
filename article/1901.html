A Correct View of Political Achievement, Practiced in the Barracks | Record of Building a Wall in the Deep Mountains

正确政绩观 践行在军营丨深山筑墙记

About this analysis
Summary
A warehouse under the Shenyang Joint Logistics Support Center spent roughly a decade—beginning in 2008—having its officers and soldiers hand-carry more than 3.6 million bricks, 900 tons of cement, and 4,530 cubic meters of sand and gravel up mountain ridges to build a 20-kilometer perimeter wall around a dispersed strategic-materials storage complex inaccessible to construction machinery; the unit has since layered on an unmanned intelligent perimeter alarm and fire-monitoring system to counter drone intrusion and aerial surveillance threats. The article is political education content tied to an ongoing campaign on 正确政绩观 (establishing a correct view of political achievement); its analytical value is as a record of how the PLA frames long-horizon institutional commitment and cross-generation continuity to cadres, using a logistics security case to reinforce the slogan 功成不必在我，功成必定有我. The warehouse's identity below the Shenyang Joint Logistics Support Center level is not disclosed, leaving its specific stored materials and precise location unknown.

Record of Building a Wall in the Deep Mountains

■ Wang Haitao, Tao Jiapeng; PLA Daily Special Correspondent Wang Xuechao; Illustration: Fang Han

In the early days of summer, amid the shade of trees and grass, sentry Dai Wei sits before an intelligent-equipment monitoring console in the duty room of a certain warehouse under the Shenyang Joint Logistics Support Center, eyes fixed on the surveillance screens, vigilantly scanning the situation around the warehouse perimeter.

Following Dai Wei's gaze, a winding protective wall is clearly visible on the screens. Crossing mountains and ridges, spanning gullies and obstacles, threading through forests and streams, this protective wall stretching more than 20 kilometers wraps around the dispersed warehouse areas like a pair of outstretched arms, holding them in a firm embrace.

The green mountains say nothing; the surrounding wall speaks no words—yet both bear witness to the story of successive Party committee leaderships and generation after generation of officers and soldiers passing the baton to build this wall. Over nearly a decade, several generations of warehouse guards, relying on their shoulders, arms, and backs, hauled bricks up the mountain and built wall upon wall, erecting a solid barrier for the warehouse.

The story begins 18 years ago.

This warehouse sits amid a range of mountains, covering dozens of square kilometers. To facilitate management, officers and soldiers installed wire fencing around the warehouse perimeter for access control, but a small number of uninformed civilians still crossed the barrier to enter the restricted zone to gather firewood, graze livestock, and forage. The restricted zone was not truly restricted, and the hidden dangers could not be eliminated—the warehouse's officers and soldiers were deeply troubled by this.

"The warehouse stores strategic materials vital to national defense security. Once a fire or deliberate sabotage occurs, the consequences would be unthinkable. We must find every possible way to fundamentally resolve the protection hazards." In the early spring of 2008, the warehouse Party committee deliberated and decided to build a protective wall with solid defensive capability around the warehouse area. However, the warehouse area was surrounded by mountains—the peaks high, the slopes steep, the gullies crisscrossing—making it impossible for large construction machinery to enter and operate. The warehouse contacted multiple local construction teams in succession; after learning of the construction difficulties, each declined.

"We guard this warehouse. We cannot be satisfied with merely holding on to what we have—we must guard it well, guard it firmly, guard it as solid as a fortress." The warehouse's then-director, an "old mountain-valley hand" with 30 years of military service, argued forcefully at the Party committee meeting that the officers and soldiers should build the wall themselves. Some raised concerns: construction deep in the mountains would not only be arduous, but the timeline would be long and results slow; with only the warehouse's limited manpower, it simply could not be built in a short time. If it ended up as a "half-finished project," it would be better not to start at all.

"As long as it is good for the warehouse's security, it is worth doing. Even if we have to carry it on our backs, we will carry a wall into existence!" In the end, the warehouse Party committee reached consensus, established the construction goal of "build one section per year, achieve full enclosure in ten years," and issued the call to all warehouse officers and soldiers: "If one generation cannot finish it, the next generation carries on."

On the day of the groundbreaking oath-taking ceremony, the warehouse's then-Party committee leadership strapped on a brick-carrying frame, took up shovels and pickaxes and other tools, stood at the very front of all the officers and soldiers, and led the pledge: "Carry bricks and stones up the mountain, build walls of bronze and iron."

The difficulty of hauling bricks and stones up steep mountain slopes can well be imagined. Thorns grew in abundance; there was no path underfoot; one moment of inattention could send a person tumbling down the mountain. The warehouse's officers and soldiers did not retreat. Some carried loads on their backs, some on their shoulders, some in their arms, calling out work chants to encourage one another. Seen from a distance, the silhouettes of figures bent under loads of bricks, straining to climb, merged into a magnificent torrent of camouflage.

The warehouse's then-director was not tall, yet each time he would carry approximately 140 jin (roughly 70 kilograms) of red bricks to lead the charge. His wife urged him: "You're about to retire—why are you still pushing yourself so hard? Take it easy!" But his drive never diminished: "Building the protective wall will benefit us for the long term. As long as I am in post for one more day, I must take the lead!"

Senior Engineer Xu, past fifty, had just undergone surgery and was still in recovery. Leadership showed consideration for him and told him not to go up the mountain, but he quietly joined the brick-carrying team. When the officers and soldiers discovered this and urged Senior Engineer Xu to go back down and rest, he said: "If I can't carry 20 bricks, I'll carry 10. No matter how many I can carry, I want to fight alongside everyone."

Staff Officer Wang Baozhu, then serving in the warehouse's headquarters section, worked overtime late into the night every Friday to clear his workload, so that come Saturday, at first light, he could carry bricks up the mountain. "Senior Engineer Xu set the example—we young officers should all the more shoulder our responsibilities!" he said.

……

Officers set the example; soldiers competed to do their part. Vanguard teams of Party members and reserve teams of Communist Youth League members formed spontaneously; subunit challenge-and-response competitions and brick-carrying contests were actively held; soldiers about to be discharged volunteered to work until the day before they left; new soldiers, barely settled into the barracks, threw themselves into the wall-building effort… Every officer and soldier refused to fall behind. By some estimates, certain soldiers carried more than ten thousand red bricks up the mountain each year, from the day they enlisted to the day they were discharged.

The warehouse Party committee leadership changed one term after another; officers and soldiers came and went generation after generation—yet the work of carrying bricks and building the wall never stopped. Over nearly a decade, the warehouse's officers and soldiers, carrying loads on their backs and shoulders, transported more than 3.6 million red bricks, more than 900 tons of cement, and more than 4,530 cubic meters of sand and gravel up the mountain ridges, and at last erected a protective wall more than 20 kilometers long around the warehouse perimeter.

On the day of the enclosure ceremony, when the final red brick was set into the protective wall, the previous several leaders who had participated in building it had already been transferred or retired, and most of the soldiers who had joined the first wave of construction had been demobilized and returned home. They were unable to witness this moment with their own eyes, but their sweat and effort had long since been fused into every red brick of the protective wall.

"Those who came before planted the trees; those who come after cannot simply enjoy the shade." The warehouse leadership made their position clear: the "enclosure" of the protective wall does not mean they can "rest easy on their laurels." They organize officers and soldiers to carry out regular maintenance and repairs each year. Moreover, in recent years, as unmanned intelligent equipment has developed rapidly, new security threats such as drone intrusions and aerial intelligence theft have become increasingly prominent. After the new warehouse Party committee took office, it actively introduced technical measures including a fire monitoring and early-warning system and an intelligent perimeter alarm system, building an unmanned intelligent security system with broader coverage and faster response, using technological means to erect a constantly-sensing "electronic protective wall" for the warehouse.

"Success need not be achieved on my watch, but success must have my contribution (功成不必在我，功成必定有我)." The warehouse leadership told reporters that, in connection with the ongoing study and education campaign on establishing and practicing a correct view of political achievement (正确政绩观), they have taken this wall-building experience as educational material to guide Party member cadres in continuously and firmly establishing a correct view of political achievement, and in carrying out all tasks with thoroughness, substance, and results.

"Carry On" and "Keep Breaking New Ground"

■ Zhang Di

A certain warehouse under the Shenyang Joint Logistics Support Center spent nearly a decade, with generation after generation of officers and soldiers passing the baton—carrying loads on their backs and shoulders—to erect a solid security barrier deep in mountains beyond the reach of construction machinery. This experience of "building a wall in the deep mountains" is a vivid embodiment of the view of political achievement captured in the phrase "success need not be achieved on my watch, but success must have my contribution (功成不必在我，功成必定有我)."

True political achievement requires "carrying on." Over nearly a decade, the warehouse Party committee leadership changed one term after another, and officers and soldiers came and went generation after generation. Even though they might not be able to witness the moment of the protective wall's "enclosure," they kept their eyes fixed on the same goal and fulfilled the same responsibility, using tenacity and perseverance to "see one blueprint through to the end (一张蓝图绘到底)."

True political achievement also requires "keep breaking new ground." The warehouse's new Party committee did not "lean on a big tree and enjoy the shade," but instead responded with acuity to the new threats of the age of information and intelligence, introduced an unmanned intelligent security system, and erected an "electronic barrier" atop the physical wall—transforming "those who came before planted the trees, those who come after enjoy the shade" into "those who came before planted the trees, those who come after nurture the trees," continuously writing new chapters in the relay of hard work in pursuit of political achievement.

One's view of political achievement is the "steering wheel" of Party member cadres, determining the direction and effectiveness of one's work and endeavors. "Carry on" and "keep breaking new ground"—these are precisely the lessons this "wall" imparts to us: be willing to do the work of laying foundations, without seeking immediate credit; and boldly open new frontiers for the age, without neglecting the responsibilities of one's post. Only in this way can the baton of our cause be received steadily and passed on well, one handoff after another.

深山筑墙记 ■王海涛 陶佳鹏 解放军报特约记者 汪学潮 插图：方 汉 初夏时节，草木掩映间，沈阳联勤保障中心某仓库值班室内，哨兵戴维坐在智能设备监控台前，紧盯监控屏幕，警惕地巡视库区周边情况。 循着戴维的目光望去，屏幕上，一道蜿蜒的防护墙清晰可见。翻山越岭、跨沟过坎、穿林涉水，这道绵延20余公里的防护墙，如同张开的双臂，紧紧环抱着散落分布的库区。 青山无言，围墙不语，却见证了仓库几任党委班子和一茬茬官兵接力筑墙的故事——历时近10载，几茬守库官兵靠肩挑、手抱、背驮，运砖上山、筑墙为垒，为仓库筑起一道坚固屏障。 故事要从18年前说起。 这座仓库坐落于群山之中，占地数十平方公里。为便于管理，官兵在仓库周边安装了铁丝网进行封控，但仍有少数不知情的群众越过防护网，进入禁区砍柴、放牧、采摘。“禁区不禁”、隐患难绝，仓库官兵对此忧心忡忡。 “仓库储存的都是事关国防安全的战略物资，一旦遭遇火灾或蓄意破坏，后果不堪设想。必须想方设法，从根本上解决防护隐患。”2008年初春，仓库党委研究决定，围绕库区修筑一道具备坚实防护能力的围墙。然而，库区内群山环绕、山高坡陡、沟壑纵横，大型工程机械难以进场施工。仓库先后联系了多个地方工程队，对方了解施工难度后，纷纷婉拒。 “我们守仓库，不能满足于守住了摊子，而要守好、守牢，守得固若金汤。”仓库时任主任是一名有着30年兵龄的“老山沟”，他在党委会上力主官兵自己动手筑墙。有人提出顾虑：在大山深处施工，不仅条件艰苦，而且工期长、见效慢，仅凭仓库这点人手，短时间内根本建不起来。万一成了“半截子工程”，还不如不干。 “只要对仓库安全有好处，就值得干。哪怕是背，咱们也得背出一道墙来！”最终，仓库党委一班人达成共识，确立了“一年建一段，十年全封围”的建设目标，并向全库官兵发出了“一茬人干不完，下一茬接着干”的号召。 开工誓师那天，仓库时任党委领导背着背砖架，拿着铁锹、镐头等工具，站在全体官兵最前面，带头喊出“背砖扛石上山，筑就铜墙铁壁”的誓言。 往陡峭的山坡上搬运砖石，难度可想而知。荆棘丛生，脚下无路，稍不留神就可能摔下山去。仓库官兵没有退却，有的用背驮、有的用肩挑、有的用手抱，喊着号子相互加油鼓劲。远远看去，一个个弯腰背砖、奋力攀爬的背影，汇成一道壮观的迷彩洪流。 仓库时任主任个子不高，每次却要背140斤左右的红砖打头阵。妻子劝他：“马上就退休了，还这么拼命干啥，悠着点儿！”他却干劲不减：“建造防护墙功在长远，只要在任一天，我就得带头干！” 年过半百的徐高工当时刚做完手术，身体仍在恢复期。领导体恤他，不让他上山，他却悄悄加入搬砖队伍。官兵发现后劝徐高工下山休息，他说：“我背不了20块就背10块，不管能背多少，我都要跟大家战斗在一起。” 仓库时任机关参谋王保柱为了挤出更多时间背砖，每周五晚上加班处理手头工作直到深夜，周六天一亮就背砖上山。“徐高工做了榜样，我们年轻干部更应该有担当！”他说。 …… 干部立榜样，战士争着干。自发成立党员突击队、团员预备队，积极开展分队挑应战、背砖小竞赛；即将退伍的老兵主动干到离队前一天，新战士入营不久就投入筑墙战斗……官兵个个不甘落后。据估算，有的战士从入伍一直干到退伍，每年要背上万块红砖上山。 仓库党委领导换了一任又一任，官兵走了一茬又一茬，可背砖筑墙工作始终没有中断。近10年时间里，全库官兵背驮肩挑，将360余万块红砖、900余吨水泥、4530余立方米沙石运上山脊，终于在库区外围筑起一道长达20余公里的防护墙。 举行封围仪式那天，当最后一块红砖被砌进防护墙，参与筑墙的仓库前几任领导都已调离或退休，首批参加筑墙的战士们多数也复员回乡。他们没能亲眼见证这一时刻，但他们的汗水和付出，早已融入防护墙的每块红砖。 “前人栽树，后人不能只乘凉。”仓库领导态度明确，防护墙“封围”并不意味着“高枕无忧”。他们组织官兵每年定期维护整修，不仅如此，近年来，随着无人智能设备快速发展，无人机闯入、高空窃密等新型安全威胁日益凸显，新一届仓库党委班子上任后，积极引入火情监控预警系统、智能周界报警系统等技术手段，建成覆盖领域更广、反应速度更快的无人智能化安防体系，用科技手段为仓库筑起一道时时感知的“电子防护墙”。 “功成不必在我，功成必定有我。”仓库领导告诉记者，他们结合正在开展的树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育，将这段筑墙经历作为教育素材，引导党员干部持续树牢正确政绩观，把各项工作做细做实做出成效。 “接着干”与“接着闯” ■张 迪 沈阳联勤保障中心某仓库历时近10载，几茬官兵接力背驮肩挑，在工程机械难及的深山里筑起坚固的安全屏障。这段“深山筑墙”的经历，正是“功成不必在我，功成必定有我”政绩观的生动体现。 真正的政绩需要“接着干”。近10年光阴里，仓库党委领导换了一任又一任，官兵走了一茬又一茬，即便未必能见证防护墙“封围”的时刻，但他们始终紧盯同一个目标、践行同一份担当，用坚韧与执着把“一张蓝图绘到底”。 真正的政绩还须“接着闯”。仓库新一届党委没有“背靠大树好乘凉”，而是敏锐应对信息智能时代的新威胁，引入无人智能化安防体系，在实体墙之上筑起“电子屏障”，让“前人栽树、后人乘凉”变为“前人栽树、后人育树”，不断续写接力实干谋政绩的新篇章。 政绩观是党员干部的“方向盘”，决定着干事创业的方向与成效。“接着干”与“接着闯”，正是这面“墙”给我们的启示：既甘做铺垫之事，不求一时之功；又勇开时代新局，不失岗位之责。如此，事业的接力棒才能棒棒接得稳、传得好。
