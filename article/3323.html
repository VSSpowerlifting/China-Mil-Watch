About this analysis
Summary
A mission detachment from an unnamed Western Theater Command Air Force communications unit conducted high-plateau mobile deployments at altitudes above 5,000 meters, operating in temperatures below minus 30 degrees Celsius, while the parent unit simultaneously ran a campaign on correcting officers' 政绩观 (view of political achievement) — framing latent, foundational work as the proper measure of merit. The article documents how the unit's Party committee used the campaign to address two concrete institutional problems: passive 'honor preservation' behavior in a model women's company that had stopped taking training risks, and the difficulty of maintaining standardized Party-branch construction across dispersed and mobile garrison locations. The framing — 'latent-merit work,' relay leadership, and soldier-initiated software development for field site-selection — points to a deliberate effort to institutionalize a culture where unglamorous support and infrastructure work is recognized and rewarded, though the specific unit designation, order of battle, and operational mission scope remain undisclosed.

Struggle Without Words, Victory Without Form — An Investigative Report from a Unit of the Western Theater Command Air Force

■ Liu Chang, PLA Daily Reporter Zhang Leifeng, Contributing Reporter Deng Dongzhi

Summer. The high plateau of the western frontier. Howling winds, sand filling the sky. Upon receiving an urgent support order, a mission detachment from a unit of the Western Theater Command Air Force moved out swiftly. Laying cable, calibrating equipment… an information link was quickly established.

"The formless defeats the formed; the soundless defeats the sounded; the nameless defeats the named." During the unit's study and education campaign on establishing and practicing a correct view of political achievement (政绩观), the words of one leader struck a chord: "Fighting on the invisible battlefield, we must keep our eyes on the long term and on fundamentals — doing more work that lays foundations, builds staying power, and serves the long haul."

For whose sake is political achievement built, what kind of political achievement should be built, and on what basis? The answers lie in the unit's personnel's repeated mission-assurance tasks, and in one ordinary post after another.

Focused on the high-quality development of the force, they have adopted "struggle without words" as a value orientation and "strengthening the military to win wars" as the measuring standard. With the perseverance of "accumulating sand into a tower (聚沙成塔)," they guide officers and soldiers to forge ahead on the new-domain, new-quality (新域新质) track and to make their mark on the invisible battlefield.

[Caption: The unit's high-plateau mission detachment on a mobile march (archival photo). Photo by Yang Binhua.]

"'Not being seen' is the best form of 'being seen'"

In this unit, there are two types of posts where people do "invisible" work.

One is the technical station monitoring room — the post "closest to the leadership." Officers and soldiers sit behind the large operational display screens every day, just steps away from the leadership. The workload is heavy and the standards are high, yet the leadership can rarely directly sense their presence. Engineer Shi Chaowei put it vividly: "We're like the electric lights in a room — you flip the switch and they come on, and you take it for granted. Only on the day they fail to light up do you realize they exist."

The other is the mobile communications detachment — the post "farthest from the headquarters." To carry out missions, they must venture deep into uninhabited areas of the snow-covered high plateau. There, oxygen content is less than half that of the plains, and nighttime temperatures drop below minus 30 degrees Celsius. Even so, officers and soldiers who volunteer for missions keep coming in an unbroken stream. Veteran soldier Xia Mingxiao has stood watch multiple times at positions above 5,000 meters in altitude. He said: "Communications support cannot have even the slightest problem. I'm the most experienced veteran in the detachment — if not me, who?"

One is right under the leadership's nose; the other is a thousand li away. Yet their work is strikingly similar — do it well and no one knows; let a problem arise and everyone can see it. What they do is latent-merit work (潜功) — imperceptible to others, yet something no one can do without for even a moment.

Shi Chaowei has worked in the monitoring room for five years, leading his team to successfully complete all manner of support tasks — but few people understand his contributions. "Being in this post, the greatest sense of achievement isn't being seen by others, but having the equipment and systems running normally at all times," Shi Chaowei said.

Xia Mingxiao has gone up to the plateau to carry out missions multiple times around the Spring Festival. While others reunite with their families, he is calibrating equipment in a zone hostile to life. When asked whether it is hard, he was blunt: "It's hard. But when the signal comes through, it's worth it!"

They never thought about being seen — but the organization saw them. At a unit office meeting, the unit's Party committee leadership set a clear orientation for all officers and soldiers: ensure that every "invisible" contribution can be seen.

From then on, for every major mission, the unit arranges for a leader to accompany the monitoring room on duty shifts — not to inspect work and add pressure, but to help solve problems and relieve pressure. Over time, the Party committee gained a thorough understanding of the monitoring room personnel's workload, stress, and rhythm. One unit leader said: "They never say it's hard, but we need to know."

At the end of last year's "Dual Competition (双争)" evaluation, Shi Chaowei was awarded an individual third-class merit citation, and the monitoring room received a collective commendation. At the awards ceremony, Shi Chaowei — standing in the spotlight for the first time — said: "'Not being seen' is the best form of 'being seen.'"

At a battlefield-stories sharing session, Xia Mingxiao was invited to the podium. He recounted several ordinary episodes from his missions with great feeling, and the applause from the audience rose again and again. The unit also adapted his story into a dramatic sketch. On the day of the performance, he spoke from the heart: "I never imagined my story could be seen by so many people. All I want to say is: on post for one minute, do a full sixty seconds of work!"

Today in this unit, doing latent-merit work is gradually becoming the conscious action of officers and soldiers. As one young platoon leader put it: "As long as you're not afraid of hardship and are willing to dedicate yourself, no matter what post you hold, the organization will see you."

"Honor is not preserved — it is fought for"

A certain women's company in the unit is a model company for grassroots-level building in the Theater Command Air Force. The walls of its honor room are covered with dozens of advanced-unit plaques: "National Women's Merit-Building Advanced Collective," "National March 8th Red Banner Collective," "National Women's Civilization Post," and others gleam brightly.

These visible honors nearly became an invisible "burden."

The praise from outside once gave the company's officers and soldiers a sense of superiority. Over time, some developed the passive mindset of "seeking stability and guarding the status quo (求安稳、守摊子)": training followed the syllabus — neither raised nor lowered; tasks were carried out by the book — neither risky nor erroneous.

This phenomenon alarmed the unit's Party committee. Unit leaders led headquarters personnel down to the company, held heart-to-heart talks with officers and soldiers, and listened to their inner concerns. They sat in on the company's organizational life sessions (组织生活会), directly pointing out problems and digging to the root causes. After thorough analysis, officers and soldiers laid bare the contradictory mindset of "wanting to preserve the honor yet fearing to tarnish the plaque," and brought various passive thoughts out into the open.

Having identified the crux of the problem, the unit's Party committee immediately helped the company Party branch clarify its thinking: "Honor is not preserved — it is fought for. What is fought for is not the face of being advanced, but the substance of combat effectiveness."

Change began on the training ground. In the past, the company's training followed old routines — each specialty trained separately, one person per post. "That's the safest approach — no mistakes." But as the mission load grew heavier, Company Commander Zhao could no longer sit still: "If each person only trains in one specialty, there won't be enough hands when things get busy!"

The company Party branch deliberated and decided: break the baseline-protection training model (保底训练模式), promote one-specialty-multiple-skills and cross-post rotation training, require each backbone member to master two or more specialty skills, and have each specialty rotate on duty on a regular basis. Some worried: "What if it goes wrong — we won't be able to hold onto the advanced-unit plaque." Company Commander Zhao said firmly: "Failing to complete missions and being unable to fight hard battles — that is what would tarnish the plaque."

The company started with its backbone members — one person learning multiple specialties, cross-training with each other. Everyone channeled their energy: practicing hands-on skills during the day, studying materials at night. Several months later, a communications support mission arrived — multiple lines to be activated simultaneously, multiple tasks to be followed up on at once. This time, the company completed the support mission with high quality and zero errors.

The old advanced unit revitalized itself. The unit's Party committee guided the momentum, holding up the company's experience of turning the blade inward (刀刃向内) in ideological transformation as a model, and encouraging each company to conduct comparative self-examinations: check for the inertia of lying flat and "living off past achievements (吃老本)"; check for the habit of passively "playing it safe."

Today, the unit's officers and soldiers have reached a consensus: true honor is not on the wall but in one's hands; true political achievement cannot be waited for or demanded — it must be fought for one step at a time.

"Letting the great tree take deep root is our shared political achievement"

In the unit's headquarters, a map showing the distribution of the force's garrison locations stands out prominently: it includes both fixed positions in the interior of the motherland and mobile detachments on the high plateau and in the desert.

"One static, one mobile — easy to manage separately." The unit's Party committee believes that the more dispersed and divided the work between interior and exterior, the greater the need to unify and integrate. After conducting research, they upgraded their approach to building dispersed and mobile forces (动散抓建), consolidating the scattered Party organization strength into a unified whole and incorporating dynamic mission deployments into standardized construction.

Building dispersed and mobile forces through regulations and systems, and consolidating the foundation — this is a long-term undertaking: slow to show results, long in cycle.

"What benefits the force is what should be done. Doing more foundational work is the lasting political achievement." The unit's Party committee was resolute in this stance. In accordance with the higher-level assistance-building mechanism, the unit's standing committee members took the lead, bringing backbone personnel to visit positions, assess conditions, strengthen Party branches, and resolve difficulties — participating throughout and providing front-line supervision.

The leadership worked with solid effort; officers and soldiers followed suit. Political Instructor Bai of the mobile communications battalion at the time ran to every point under the battalion's jurisdiction, thoroughly surveying the baseline data of each station: which station's equipment was aging, which position's signal was unstable — each was recorded one by one and sorted by category. Research during the day, analysis at night. Someone advised him: "Building-up work requires sustained effort — why go all the way to the bottom?" He countered: "Without putting in hard effort, how can you sustain it?"

With the support of the unit's Party committee, the problems Instructor Bai recorded in his notebook gradually acquired actionable, implementable solutions. The Party branch was strengthened; the systems were rationalized. Officers and soldiers said: "Before, when we went on missions we only knew how to set up lines. Now we have mission standards, and we have training objectives in mind."

When he departed, Instructor Bai left his notebook to the next political instructor. The incoming Instructor Long, upon taking over, further refined the approach to problems not yet resolved, turning the framework into systems and implementing those systems at the grassroots level.

"Does this count as your political achievement, or the previous instructor's?" Faced with this well-meaning observation, Instructor Long smiled and said: "One generation plants the trees; the next waters them. Letting the great tree take deep root — that is our shared political achievement."

When the orientation is correct, the drive is strong. Standing committee members take the lead in going deep; principal officers carry the work forward in relay; everyone pulls together as one: doing long-term work, doing foundational work.

In this atmosphere, officers and soldiers' sense of proactive responsibility has also grown stronger. Soldier Ou Yunbo is a communications operator. During one field training exercise, he noticed that the traditional site-selection method was inefficient and error-prone. Having studied programming before enlisting, he conceived the idea of designing software to improve work efficiency.

He set to work immediately. Ou Yunbo performed his normal duty shifts during the day and self-studied and wrote code at night. Before long, he designed an intelligent site-selection software that significantly reduced site-selection time. After the force promoted it, the quality and efficiency of field training improved substantially.

The unit seized on this example, comprehensively promoting the "mining potential from existing equipment (旧装挖潜)" initiative, and made clear: "As long as it contributes to combat effectiveness, regardless of post or identity, anyone can do it — and anyone who does will be supported."

Not counting "the merit of a single term," but seeking "lasting benefit." In this force that devotes itself wholeheartedly to war preparation, every "invisible" act of steadfast duty is accumulating strength for future victory; every practice of "laying foundations and serving the long haul" is a firm step forward on the march to strengthen the military.

奋斗无言 制胜无形 ——来自西部战区空军某部的调查报告 ■刘畅 解放军报记者 张磊峰 特约记者 邓栋之 夏日，西陲高原，狂风呼啸，漫天飞沙。接到紧急保障指令，西部战区空军某部任务分队迅疾出动。放线、调试……一条信息链路很快被贯通。 “无形胜有形，无声胜有声，无名胜有名。”在该部树立和践行正确政绩观学习教育中，一名领导的话直抵人心，“战斗在无形战场上，我们要着眼长远、着眼根本，多做打基础、增后劲、利长远的工作。” 政绩为谁而树、树什么样的政绩、靠什么树政绩？答案，就藏在该部官兵一次次保通任务里，藏在一个个平凡战位上。 聚焦部队高质量发展，他们把“奋斗无言”作为价值导向、把“强军胜战”作为检验标尺，以“聚沙成塔”的毅力，引导官兵在新域新质赛道上砥砺奋斗，在无形战场上建功立业。 该部高原任务分队机动行军（资料照片）。杨斌华摄 “‘看不见’我们，就是最好的‘看得见’” 在该部，有两种岗位都在做“看不见”的事。 一种是“离领导最近”的技术站监控室。官兵每天坐在作战大屏背后，距离领导咫尺之遥，工作强度大、标准高，但领导却很难直接感受到他们的存在。工程师史朝卫形象地说：“我们就像房间里的电灯——按下开关它亮起，你觉得理所当然。哪天出了问题不亮了，你才意识到它的存在。” 另一种是“离机关最远”的机动通信分队。为了执行任务，他们要深入雪域高原的无人区。那里空气含氧量不足平原的一半，夜晚气温低至零下30多摄氏度。即便如此，主动请缨执行任务的官兵仍络绎不绝。老兵夏明肖多次在海拔5000多米的阵地上值守，他说：“通信保障不能出一点问题，我是分队里最有经验的老兵，我不上谁上？” 一个在眼皮子底下，一个在千里之外，他们的工作却惊人的相似——干好了没有人知道，出问题谁都看得见。他们做的都是潜功——让人察觉不到，却又须臾离不开。 史朝卫在监控室一干就是5年，带领团队圆满完成各类保障任务，但了解他贡献的人不多。“身处这个岗位，最大的成就感不是被人看见，而是设备系统一直正常运行。”史朝卫说。 夏明肖多次在春节前后上高原执行任务。别人阖家团圆，他却在生命禁区调试设备。被问及苦不苦，他直言不讳：“苦。但信号通了，就值了！” 他们从没想过要被看见，但组织看见了。单位办公会上，该部党委一班人向全体官兵立起一个导向：让每一份“看不见”的付出都能被看见。 此后每次重大任务，该部都会安排一名领导去监控室跟班值勤——不是检查工作给压力，而是帮着解决问题、释放压力。久而久之，党委对监控室官兵的工作强度、压力、节奏，摸得一清二楚。该部一名领导说：“他们从来不说辛苦，但我们得知道。” 去年底“双争”评比，史朝卫荣立个人三等功，监控室获集体嘉奖。颁奖晚会上，首次站在聚光灯下的史朝卫说：“‘看不见’我们，就是最好的‘看得见’。” 一次战地故事分享会，夏明肖被请上讲台。他声情并茂地讲述了任务中的几件平凡小事，台下的掌声一次又一次响起。单位还将他的故事改编成情景剧。演出那天，他说出了心里话：“从没想过，我的故事能被这么多人看见。我只想说，在岗1分钟，干好60秒！” 如今在该部，做潜功正逐渐成为官兵的行动自觉。正如一名年轻排长所说：“只要不怕吃苦、甘于奉献，无论在什么样的岗位坚守，组织都会看得见。” “荣誉不是保出来的，是拼出来的” 该部某女兵连，是战区空军基层建设的模范连队。荣誉室里，数十面先进单位奖牌挂满墙壁：“全国巾帼建功先进集体”“全国三八红旗集体”“全国巾帼文明岗”等牌匾熠熠生辉。 这些看得见的荣誉，差点成为看不见的“包袱”。 外界的赞誉，一度让该连官兵生出优越感。久而久之，一些官兵滋生了“求安稳、守摊子”的消极思想：训练按大纲来，不拔高也不降标；任务按部就班，不冒险也不出错。 这种现象引起该部党委警觉。部领导带领机关人员下沉该连，与官兵谈心交流，倾听内心顾虑；列席连队组织生活会，面对面指出问题、深挖根源。经过深入剖析，官兵把“想保荣誉、又怕砸牌子”的矛盾心理倒了出来，把各种消极思想摆上了台面。 摸清症结，该部党委第一时间帮助连队党支部理清思路：“荣誉不是保出来的，是拼出来的；拼的不是先进的面子，而是战斗力的里子。” 改变从训练场开始。过去，该连训练按老套路展开——各专业单练，一人盯一岗。“这样最稳妥，不会出差错。”但随着任务越来越重，该连赵连长坐不住了，“一个人只练一个专业，忙起来人手不够！” 连队党支部研究决定：打破保底训练模式，推行一专多能、跨岗轮训，要求每名骨干掌握2项以上专业技能，各专业定期交叉值勤。有人担心：“万一搞砸了，先进单位的牌子可就保不住了。”赵连长坚定地说：“完不成任务，打不了硬仗，那才是砸了牌子。” 连队从骨干抓起，一人学多个专业，互相交叉训练。大家憋着一股劲，白天练实操，晚上啃教材。几个月后，通信保障任务来了——多条线路同时开通，多个任务同时跟进。这一次，连队高质量、零差错完成保障任务。 老先进焕发新活力。该部党委因势利导，将该连刀刃向内的思想改造经历树为榜样，鼓励各连开展对照自查：看有没有躺平“吃老本”的惰性，看有没有消极“保安全”的惯性。 如今，该部官兵达成共识：真正的荣誉，不在墙上在手上；真正的政绩，不能等不能要，要靠一步一个脚印拼出来。 “让大树深深扎根，是我们共同的政绩” 在该部机关，一张部队驻守分布图格外醒目：既有祖国内地的固定阵地，也有高原大漠的机动分队。 “一静一动，容易分而治之。”该部党委认为，越是动静分离、内外分工，越需要统起来、合起来。他们经过调研，升级动散抓建做法，把分散的党组织力量统成一个整体，把动态任务部署纳入规范建设。 动散抓建是建章立制、固本强基的长远之事，见效慢、周期长。 “对部队有益的事，就是应该干的事。多做打基础的工作，才是长久的政绩。”该部党委态度坚决。按照上级的帮建机制，该部常委牵头，带着骨干跑阵地、摸情况、强支部、解难题，全程参与、一线督导。 领导扎实干，官兵跟着做。时任机动通信营白教导员，跑遍了营属各个点位，把各台站的底数摸了个遍：哪个台站设备老化、哪个点位信号不稳，逐一记录在案，分门别类逐条梳理。白天调研，晚上分析。有人劝他：“抓建工作久久为功，何必一竿子插到底？”他反问：“不下硬功夫，如何久久为功？” 在部队党委支持下，白教导员记在本子上的问题，逐步有了可操作、可落地的解决措施。支部建强了，制度理顺了，官兵说：“以前出任务只会架设线路，现在有了任务规范，心里也有了训练目标。” 离任时，白教导员把笔记本留给了下一任教导员。新任的龙教导员接手后，针对尚未解决的难题，进一步优化思路，把框架变成制度，将制度落实到基层。 “这算是你的政绩，还是上一任教导员的政绩？”面对善意的提醒，龙教导员笑着说：“前人栽树、后人浇水，让大树深深扎根，是我们共同的政绩。” 导向正则干劲足。常委带头扎下去，主官接力干下去，大家拧成一股绳：干长远的事、打基础的事。 在这样的氛围中，官兵主动担当作为的意识也更强了。战士欧运波是一名通信值机员，一次野外训练，他发现传统选址模式效率低、易出错。入伍前学过编程的他，萌生了设计一款软件提高工作效率的想法。 说干就干。欧运波白天正常值勤，晚上自学、写代码。不久，他设计出一款智能选址软件，大幅缩短选址时间。经部队推广，野外训练质效大大提高。 该部抓住这个典型，全面推广“旧装挖潜”活动，并明确：“只要对战斗力有贡献，不分岗位、不分身份，谁都可以干、谁干都支持。” 不计“一任之功”，但求“长久之利”。在这支潜心备战的部队里，每一份“看不见”的坚守，都在为未来的胜战积蓄力量；每一个“打基础、利长远”的实践，都是在强军征程上迈出的坚实步伐。
