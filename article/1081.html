About this analysis
Summary
A dispatch center under the Southern Theater Command has restructured military representative offices into 区域型军代室 (regional military representative offices) that consolidate rail, water, road, and air transport coordination under single command nodes, while deploying an 智能调度系统 (intelligent dispatch system) that captures real-time transport capacity data and auto-generates support plans—changes the article credits with enabling same-day high-speed rail recruit transport from Yan'an and direct rail-to-port equipment transfer without road relay. The article documents the Joint Logistic Support Force's post-reform effort to close the implementation gap left by the 军队运输投送条例 (Regulations on Military Transport and Projection), which took effect January 1, 2024, by converting a legal framework into functioning joint-approval mechanisms and integrated infrastructure. The framing points to persistent friction in military-civilian coordination as the binding constraint on strategic projection speed, with the specific problem of disconnected sub-bureau approval standards and siloed transport-mode offices now partially addressed but, by the director's own account, still not fully resolved in capacity mining and resource network optimization.
Translation

**Editor's Note:** As the modernized projection system continues to improve, our military has continuously advanced the construction of the strategic transportation system, achieving a new leap in strategic projection and comprehensive support capabilities. How can the three-dimensional projection network that sustains victory in war be woven ever denser, and the speed of cross-regional, cross-branch force projection run ever faster? In recent years, dispatch departments at all levels of the Joint Logistic Support Force have used institutional restructuring to break through the "last mile" of military-civilian coordination, infrastructure upgrades to connect the battlefield support chain, and service upgrades to make projection support both forceful and warm. "Regional Military Representative Offices (区域型军代室)" have clenched scattered fingers into a fist, coordinating transport capacity in an integrated manner, while "intelligent dispatch systems (智能调度系统)" apply precise force, allowing information flows to lead steel flows to new speeds. Today, at every transportation hub across the motherland, military representatives dig for potential, optimize operations, and lay foundations—connecting dots into lines and weaving a denser network through successive breakthroughs in military transport projection efficiency. This ever-denser three-dimensional projection network is converting powerful projection potential into war-winning capability, laying a solid foundation for victory on future battlefields.

**Reporter Investigation**

**Accelerating Projection: Iron Torrents Cross Ten Thousand Rivers and Mountains**

■ Wei Xing, Xiao Bing, PLA Daily Reporter Lai Yuhong

*A military representative office opens a "Strong Army (强军号)" themed air route, ensuring efficient and convenient transport of new recruits.*

"In the past, sending new recruits into Xinjiang and Tibet meant transferring between multiple modes of transportation along the way. Now the 'Strong Army' flight goes direct in a single leg—you can reach the foot of the Tianshan Mountains within a single day!" Standing beside the tarmac at Guiyang Longdongbao Airport, watching new recruits lined up in formation preparing to board, Huang Caiwang, a military representative at a dispatch center under the Southern Theater Command, spoke with deep feeling.

In recent years, the dispatch center has coordinated with local airlines to open flights for new recruit transport, providing one-stop projection support for new recruits. From the bumpy, multi-transfer rail journey to efficient, direct air travel, what has changed is not only the mode of transportation but a comprehensive leap in military transport projection support capability.

Transport projection connects the battlefield and is tied to winning. Modern warfare contests speed, precision, and coordination—every minute faster in force projection, every increment smoother in materiel transfer, means one more measure of advantage on the battlefield.

Faced with problems including poor military-civilian coordination, cumbersome approval processes, and disconnected infrastructure nodes, the officers and soldiers of the dispatch center took the initiative to tackle these issues: using institutional reform to clear bottlenecks, using hardware upgrades to reconnect broken links, and improving service quality and efficiency to warm the hearts of soldiers. One "slow link" after another has become a "fast channel"; one "choke point" after another has become a "smooth point"—delivering real efficiency gains and running out a new "acceleration" in transport projection.

**Breaking Through with Mechanisms: From "Running Your Legs Off" to "One Form," Concrete Measures to Speed Up Approvals**

"I never expected the equipment transport approval—from application to authorization—to come through so quickly!" Major Yang, chief of the transport and projection section of an Army brigade, still remembers one emergency projection experience.

At the time, the unit ended its field training early due to an urgent mission, and higher command required the return to garrison to be completed within one week. Upon receiving the notice, Major Yang was deeply anxious: "Loading and securing equipment requires coordination with multiple railway departments, plus submitting materials and coordinating approvals—in the past that would take at least a week…" Wasting no time, he immediately coordinated military and civilian units to advance the mission.

The next day, Ma Xiaohu, a military representative from a dispatch center responsible for coordination and support, came to the door on his own initiative. One sentence put Major Yang's mind at ease: "The unit and the railway department have signed a joint projection mechanism. Railway sub-bureaus across regions have now achieved information sharing. Your mission requires only one submission and one approval—a single plan valid for the entire process."

Submit materials, system verification… Major Yang quickly received authorization. He exclaimed repeatedly: "In the past, handling the approval process meant 'running your legs off.' Now it's application, service, and authorization all on 'one form'—equipment projection is far more efficient!"

The acceleration has a formula, rooted in the determination of military transport projection departments to solve problems and tackle hard challenges.

"In response to the complaints most frequently raised by mission units—too many approval levels, too many coordinating departments, and information that doesn't flow between them—we negotiated repeatedly with local departments." The dispatch center's leadership believes that after the promulgation of the *Regulations on Military Transport and Projection (军队运输投送条例)*, the institutional framework was in place, but how to break through the "last mile" of policy implementation still required the establishment of new mechanisms.

A process of institutional restructuring began. The dispatch center took the lead in signing agreements with transportation departments, integrating dispersed forces, and establishing "one deliberation and coordination mechanism, two working leadership groups, and two-level joint command structures," progressively clarifying "who leads, who supports, who approves, and how long it takes to complete." To address the problem of "inconsistent loading plan standards across railway sub-bureaus," they worked with railway departments to establish uniform standards and procedures, promoting the concept of "measure once, plan valid throughout"—eliminating the need for units to repeat verification and approval.

"In the past, coordinating a single mission meant military representatives submitting materials without end. Now, with fixed mechanisms and standardized procedures, everyone follows the rules, coordinates efficiently, saves effort, and improves efficiency." Chen Xiaoyi, a military representative who has served mission units for twelve years, has witnessed the projection acceleration brought about by institutional innovation.

The changes go further. A review of the dispatch center's annual transport planning and deliberation records shows that in the past, a single mission required military representatives to coordinate with more than twenty units; now, with optimized procedures, the process is handled by core units through "joint review, handled together." For urgent missions such as combat readiness alerts at all levels, a green channel has been opened—urgent matters handled quickly, special matters handled specially—ensuring that units can move the moment the order is given.

"Institutions are not provisions hanging on a wall—they are the 'engine' that accelerates and increases efficiency on the projection chain." The dispatch center's leadership stated that from "running around to coordinate" to "mechanism-enabled empowerment," from "each managing one segment" to "clenching fingers into a fist," only when all levels are truly twisted into one rope can the "acceleration chain" of transport projection be fully opened.

**Road Network Upgrades: From "Half a Road" to "Rail-Water Connectivity," Support Chains Reach the Battlefield**

"In the past, after equipment came off the train, it had to be transported by road to a waterway port… Now, with road network upgrades, rail-water-road multimodal transport has put equipment transport on an accelerated track." After completing a cross-sea projection mission, Staff Officer Liu of an Army combined-arms brigade was deeply moved.

Recalling past mission experiences, Staff Officer Liu was full of feeling: "When the unit conducted cross-regional field training, it went from rail to road to waterway. Applying for traffic control required coordination with local transportation departments; road conditions en route were complex; equipment suffered wear and tear from the rough ride, and the unit's mobility speed was slow…"

With national road network construction upgrades, the mode of cross-regional unit projection has now changed. He explained that during this cross-sea projection mission, the military train went directly to the vicinity of the port; equipment was unloaded and transferred for transport in a single stop. "From 'half a road' to 'rail-water connectivity,' unit mobility is smooth and efficient—we have far greater confidence in winning!" said Staff Officer Liu.

"Whether transport projection is blocked or not ultimately depends on whether the nodes are connected; whether support capability is strong or not fundamentally depends on whether the road network is linked." The dispatch center's leadership explained that in the past, military transport facilities were outdated and hub connections were poor—railways didn't reach ports, roads didn't reach stations—creating bottlenecks constraining military transport projection.

Whatever the battlefield needs, build it; wherever units face inconvenience, fix it. The dispatch center conducted joint military-civilian research and planning, embedding military requirements into local transportation construction. A comprehensive hardware upgrade of rail-water-road multimodal transport to "fill shortfalls, clear blockages, and strengthen the network" was fully launched:

The road network extended toward ports, docks, training grounds, and garrison areas, achieving military transport "reaching the edge and the sea"; coordination with ports to optimize military berths and improve loading and unloading facilities, enhancing roll-on/roll-off rapid loading and unloading capability; promoting the addition of military transport markings and emergency lanes on key road sections to ensure priority passage for unit mobility; deepening military-civilian aviation coordination, opening themed flights and dedicated channels for faster force projection.

"Multimodal transport is smoother—unit projection capability has gotten on the fast track." Wang Shaolong, a military representative who has participated in exercise and training support many times, remarked that the "rail-water-road-air" integrated support network is being woven ever denser, the support chains for force projection, materiel transfer, and equipment transport are fully connected, and military strategic projection capability has reached a new level.

**Soldier-Benefiting Support: From "Ensuring Transport" to "Warming Soldiers' Hearts," Support That Is Both Forceful and Warm**

"One free ferry ticket, warming the heart." During this year's Spring Festival, Staff Sergeant Xie Guangyuan of a unit stationed in Hainan was leaving the island for home leave. At the harbor terminal, preparing to board the ferry, he unexpectedly received a soldier-benefiting gift—no need to queue, no need to buy a ticket; a valid ID was sufficient for priority boarding.

This was the "free ferry service for officers and soldiers of units stationed in Hainan leaving the island" launched jointly by the dispatch center and local ferry operators, allowing officers and soldiers to experience warm travel support during the time of family reunions.

"In the past, officers and soldiers leaving the island by ferry had to buy tickets in advance and queue to wait for the boat. During holiday peak travel periods, they often waited half a day." Military representative Huang Huizheng recalled that during unit research visits, fellow soldiers always raised the problem of "tickets being nearly impossible to get" for island departures during holidays.

They actively coordinated with local authorities, formulated a plan for free ferry service for military personnel, and broke through the "last mile" of convenient travel for officers and soldiers. Xie Guangyuan said with feeling: "What was saved was only the cost of a ferry ticket, but what was felt was warm service. More and more soldier-benefiting support has strengthened our resolve to guard the island."

The dispatch center has continuously expanded the scope of support, comprehensively upgrading the service and support model of military supply stations, launching "one-stop" services including free accommodation, free meals, emergency resupply, and medical assistance—polishing the service brand of "when you have difficulties, find the military representative" through concrete action. They have worked with local transportation enterprises to create military-supporting metro lines. From smooth transport to honorable service, soldier-benefiting support is embedded in every detail and every link, making the soldier-benefiting chain of transport projection both forceful and warm.

From institutional breakthroughs to hardware quality improvements, from service that warms the heart to orienting toward war, a dispatch center under the Southern Theater Command has, through successive successful cases of accelerated projection, opened up the institutional chain, facilities chain, and service chain of military transport projection.

"There is no finish line in supporting victory in war—continuous acceleration never stops." The dispatch center's responsible official stated that they will always keep their sights fixed on the demands of war preparation and combat, deepen military-civilian coordination, strengthen technology empowerment, and optimize service and support—forging an iron fist for victory that can be deployed, can advance, can be sustained, and can win.

**Authoritative Interpretation**

**Innovating Institutional Mechanisms to Seize the Initiative for Victory**

*—A Joint Logistic Support Force Coordination Center Director Answers Reporter Questions*

■ Pang Yu, PLA Daily Special Correspondent Zhang Yanxin

Powerful projection capability is a prerequisite for seizing the initiative for victory and gaining the upper hand in war. It is imperative to accelerate the pace of building projection forces, equipment means, and transportation infrastructure to achieve a comprehensive leap in projection capability. On this topic, a PLA Daily reporter spoke with the director of a coordination center under the Joint Logistic Support Force.

**Reporter:** National transportation construction is advancing by leaps and bounds, and military modernization is accelerating in quality and speed. Military representative institutions are changing in the course of reform. What is the significance?

**Director:** The significance is to comprehensively drive high-quality development of transport projection work. The *Regulations on Military Transport and Projection* took effect on January 1, 2024, providing systematic standardization in terms of institutional mechanisms, functional positioning, and organizational processes, offering strong support for improving projection quality and effectiveness. Military representative institutions at all levels, standing at their posts, have conducted beneficial exploration in exercises and major missions, accumulating experience in building smart projection (智慧投送), three-dimensional projection (立体投送), and efficient projection (高效投送). In the next phase, we will continue to focus on war preparation and combat, adhere to reform and innovation alongside system design, and drive projection to be faster, more efficient, and better supported.

**Reporter:** In day-to-day work, how is this exploration specifically practiced?

**Director:** As functional missions and supporting conditions have undergone major changes, our primary task is to conduct in-depth investigation and research, gaining a clear picture of the strength of mission units and transport enterprises and institutions within our jurisdiction. On that basis, combined with routine management, construction, and support missions, we strengthen hardware construction, promote joint exercises, training, and support, and form a combined force.

**Reporter:** In recent years, what changes has the development of our military's strategic projection construction brought about?

**Director:** As the modernized projection system continues to improve, the construction of the strategic transportation system and projection efficiency in key directions have continuously improved, and strategic mobility and comprehensive support capability have achieved a phased leap. System restructuring has built joint projection; multimodal transport has improved the three-dimensional network; technology empowerment has driven precise support. The time and effort mission units and officers and soldiers spend on cross-regional mobility have been greatly reduced; faster speed and better support serve the generation of combat capability more effectively.

**Reporter:** Regarding the bottlenecks constraining projection capability, how will they be cleared in the next step?

**Director:** Transport projection support capability has improved considerably compared to before the reform, but there is still room for improvement in mining transport capacity potential, coordinating multiple modes of transport, and optimizing resource network layout. In the next step, we will actively introduce emerging technologies and strengthen capabilities in real-time dynamic awareness of transport projection, intelligent decision support, and precise effectiveness support.

**Keyword: "Regional Military Representative Office (区域型军代室)"**

**Railway Projection Reaches Directly into Port Areas**

■ Lin Meina, PLA Daily Special Correspondent Zhang Yanxin

At an eastern port, a summer breeze blows. A group from an Eastern Theater Command dispatch center and local transportation departments ventures deep into the railway loading and unloading station in the port hinterland for research.

Beside the platform, Staff Officer Huang of the dispatch center, looking at the railway tracks under repair, poses a question to the experts: "To carry out rail-water combined transport and ensure rapid transfer during the transport process, how should this railway be renovated?" Back in the conference room, they discuss how to embed military projection requirements into port construction, offering suggestions one by one and setting measures item by item.

This scene is a microcosm of the dispatch center's efforts to build a three-dimensional projection network and comprehensively survey loading and unloading capabilities within its jurisdiction. Joint projection cannot be achieved without a three-dimensional grid. Yet in the past, military representatives for "rail, water, road, and air" each belonged to separate offices managing their own domains, and achieving "three-dimensionality" was not easy.

One military representative recalled: "In the past, when units at all levels executed urgent missions, they had to interface one by one with military representative offices of different responsibilities. The responsibilities of the various offices urgently needed to be integrated." After organizational structure adjustments, "Regional Military Representative Offices" were established at transportation hub locations, consolidating railway, waterway, aviation, and road transport capacity under one office. From that point on, planning routes and arranging transport capacity for cross-regional projection of mission units was no longer a matter of "each sweeping in front of one's own door," but rather comprehensive consideration and scientific organization.

Taking rail-water combined transport as an example, the "Regional Military Representative Office" took the lead in conducting joint on-site surveys with railway departments and port operating units, coordinating the opening of military transport qualifications, and improving loading and unloading support capability; a green channel was opened at the port, with priority berths and full-process support designated for mission units, weaving a seamlessly connected rail-water-road three-dimensional projection grid.

From "fighting separately" to "clenching fingers into a fist," the "Regional Military Representative Office" enables all types of transport capacity and loading and unloading conditions within the jurisdiction to be coordinated and precisely allocated. Today, all types of equipment of mission units can travel directly from railway to port area, unloading nearby and loading onto ships quickly—long-distance road relay transport is gradually becoming a thing of the past. A company commander from an Army brigade, after completing a loading mission, remarked: "Less transfer in transport, less running around for the unit, support delivered in one stop."

Currently, as "Regional Military Representative Offices" are built and put into operation, the three-dimensional projection grid is being woven ever denser. The dispatch center focuses on dynamically collecting unit support requirements while simultaneously surveying the support capabilities of railway, waterway, aviation, and road transport—as one military representative put it: "Wherever supply and demand are mismatched, we coordinate promptly, quickly clear bottlenecks and choke points, and progressively optimize and upgrade the three-dimensional projection grid to ensure units arrive at mission areas efficiently."

**Keyword: "Intelligent Dispatch System (智能调度系统)"**

**New Recruits Board High-Speed Rail to Join Their Units**

■ Guo Chen, PLA Daily Special Correspondent Zhang Yanxin

At dawn, the boarding announcement echoes through Yan'an Railway Station. A group of new recruits in uniform, red flowers pinned to their chests, shoulder their packs, board the high-speed train, and head for the barracks.

Wu Pei, a military representative at the Yan'an Military Representative Office of a Central Theater Command dispatch center, reflected that this was the first time Yan'an Station had initiated high-speed rail transport for new recruits, with recruits arriving at their destination the same day. In previous years, traveling in the same direction, they could only take a conventional-speed train to Xi'an and then transfer, resulting in low projection efficiency.

Behind the change lies both the support of the newly opened Xi'an–Yan'an High-Speed Railway and the precise application of an intelligent dispatch control system. A senior staff officer at the dispatch center explained that this system can capture real-time transport capacity data for railway, aviation, and waterway, gaining first-hand awareness of the latest developments, while simultaneously formulating scientific transport plans based on unit requirements and assisting in generating support plans.

For this year's new recruit transport, the dispatch center coordinated requirements in advance, surveyed transport capacity, added more than twenty railway stations and two airports, and effectively reduced the friction of road-rail transfers. The proportion of new recruits traveling by high-speed EMU trains exceeded 90 percent, and repeated transfers between road and rail were significantly reduced.

In a transport dispatch duty room, the reporter observed the system displaying a route map on screen, with station car storage conditions and support capabilities visible at a glance, and warning information flashing in the upper right corner. "The ability to generate warnings relies on dynamic data capture and intelligent situational analysis," said Staff Officer Miao, responsible for system development. It can precisely identify in-transit military transport plans from real-time railway information and automatically fill in the arrival times at each point.

In the past, every experienced dispatch duty officer had to carry a "living map" in their head—routes, loading and unloading capabilities, and hub traffic conditions all depended on accumulated experience. During peak mission periods, repeated telephone confirmations were needed to track real-time status; information feedback was delayed, making it very difficult to handle anomalies in the first instance.

"Today, relying on this system, the focus of dispatch duty is shifting from 'finding information' to 'optimizing decisions.'" Wu Pei said that it can also automatically deduce support missions, plan operating routes, and generate support plans based on mission unit requirements or traffic conditions; duty officers then concentrate their energy on optimizing plans, and transport projection efficiency continuously improves as a result.

When information flows truly run, transport projection gains wings of intelligence. "Converting transport capacity potential into support capability is laying the foundation for combat capability." The dispatch center's leadership introduced that they are currently exploring using the system to complete transport capacity coordination and automatically generate duty logs, further liberating dispatchers at all levels from data retrieval and statistical work.

“过去官兵离岛乘船需要提前购票、排队候船，遇到节假日客流高峰，往往一等就是大半天。”军代表黄慧政回忆，他在部队调研时，总有战友反映假期离岛“一票难求”。 他们积极对接地方，制订军人乘船免费方案，打通官兵便捷出行“最后一公里”。谢广元感慨道：“节省的只是船票费用，感受到的却是温暖服务，越来越多的惠军保障让我们坚定了守岛决心。” 该调度中心持续拓展保障范围，全面升级军供站服务保障模式，推出免费入住、免费餐饮、应急补给、医疗帮扶“一站式”服务，以实际行动擦亮“有困难，找军代”的服务品牌。他们与地方交通企业打造拥军地铁线路。从顺畅运输到尊崇服务，把惠军保障落到每一个细节、每一个环节，让运输投送的惠兵链路既有力度、更有温度。 记者：日常工作中，这些探索是如何具体实践的？ 主任：随着职能使命和支撑条件发生重大变化，我们的首要工作是深入调查研究，摸清辖区内任务部队与运输企事业单位的实力。在此基础上，结合日常管理建设和保障任务，加强硬件建设，推动联演联训联保，形成合力。 记者：近年来，我军战略投送建设发展带来了哪些变化？ 主任：随着现代化投送体系不断完善，战略运输体系建设、重点方向投送效率持续提升，战略机动与综合保障能力实现阶段性跨越。体系重塑打造联合投送，多式联运健全立体网络，科技赋能推动精准保障。任务部队和官兵跨区机动花费的时间、精力大幅减少，更快的速度、更优的保障，更好地服务战斗力生成。 记者：针对制约投送能力的堵点，下一步如何打通？ 东部某港口，夏风习习。东部战区某调度中心与地方交通运输部门一行人，深入港口腹地的铁路装卸载站点调研。 站台边，该调度中心黄参谋望着正在检修的铁轨，向专家发出疑问：“开展铁水联运，确保运输过程快速中转，这条铁路如何整修？”回到会议室，他们围绕如何在港口建设中嵌入军事投送需求，一条条提建议、一项项定措施。 这一幕，正是该调度中心着力构建立体投送网络，全面摸清辖区内装卸载能力的缩影。联合投送，离不开立体网格。然而过去“铁水公空”军代表各归各室、各管一摊，实现“立体”并不容易。 一位军代表回忆：“过去各级执行紧急任务，需要逐个对接不同职责军代室，各军代室职责急需整合。”编制体制调整后，各交通枢纽点位建起“区域型军代室”，将铁路、水路、航空、公路运力归于一室。从此，为任务部队跨区域投送规划路线、筹措运力不再是“各扫门前雪”，而是通盘考量、科学组织。 以铁水联运为例，“区域型军代室”牵头联合铁路部门和港口运营单位实地勘察，协调开通军事运输资质，完善装卸载保障能力；在港口开辟绿色通道，明确任务部队泊位优先、全程保障，织密无缝衔接的铁水公立体投送网格。 从“各自为战”到“攥指成拳”，“区域型军代室”让辖区内各类运力和装卸条件得以统筹协调、精准调配。如今，任务部队各型装备实现从铁路直达港区，就近卸载、快速装船，长距离公路倒运逐步成为过去式。陆军某旅一位连长完成装载任务后感慨道：“运输少中转，部队少跑路，保障一站达。” 清晨，延安火车站响起检票广播，一队身着戎装、胸佩红花的新兵背上行囊，登上高铁，奔赴军营。 中部战区某调度中心延安军代室军代表吴培感慨，这是延安站首次开启高铁新兵运输，新兵当日抵达目的地。往年走同一方向，只能坐普速列车到西安再中转换乘，投送效率不高。 变化的背后，既有西延高铁开通的支撑，更有一套智能调度控制系统的精准发力。该调度中心高参谋介绍，这套系统能实时抓取铁路、航空、水路的运力数据，第一时间掌握最新动态，同步根据部队需求科学制定运输计划，辅助生成保障方案。 今年新兵运输，该调度中心提前对接需求、摸清运力底数，新增使用20余个车站和2个机场，有效减少公铁转运矛盾。新兵乘坐高铁动车组的比例超90%，公路、铁路间反复换乘的情况明显减少。 在某运输调度值班室，记者看到，该系统把线路图搬上屏幕，车站存车情况和保障能力一目了然，右上角还闪烁着预警信息。“能够实现预警，靠的是动态数据抓取和态势智能分析。”负责系统研发的苗参谋说，它能从铁路实时信息中，精准锁定在途军事运输计划，自动补全到达各点位的时间。 过去，每名成熟的调度值班员都得在脑子里装一张“活地图”，线路、装卸能力、枢纽通行状况全靠经验积累。任务高峰期，为掌握实时状态，需要反复电话核准情况，信息反馈滞后，很难第一时间处置异常。 “如今，依托这套系统，调度值班的重心正从‘找信息’向‘优决策’转移。”吴培说，它还能根据任务部队需求或交通情况，自动推演保障任务、规划运行路径、生成保障方案，值班员则集中精力优化方案，运输投送效率随之不断提升。 当信息流真正跑起来，运输投送便插上了智慧翅膀。“把运能潜力转化为保障力，就是为战斗力筑基。”该调度中心领导介绍，目前他们正探索用系统完成运力统筹、自动生成值班日志，让各级调度员进一步从数据查找统计中解放出来。
