How Can an Old Blueprint Leverage a Leap in Support Capability? A Naval Brigade Provides the Answer

一张老图纸，何以撬动保障力跃升？海军某大队给出答案
PLA Daily (解放军报) 27 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A brigade of the Northern Theater Command Navy used decades-old architectural blueprints stored in its archive room to diagnose structural unknowns in aging barracks and observation-communications station (观通站) facilities, enabling renovations that had stalled for years—including one where new-type equipment had sat without adequate storage for four years. The brigade framed the initiative explicitly within 政治整训 (political rectification training), using the shift from passively 'waiting for people to come' to proactively 'bringing services to the door' as a concrete test of work-style reform, and produced Standardized Wartime Emergency Repair and Construction Plans (《标准化战时抢修抢建方案》) derived from blueprint data and simulated battlefield damage scenarios. The article documents a recurring structural problem the PLA has not fully solved: older storage and support facilities built to earlier standards cannot accommodate new-type equipment, and the brigade's framing suggests the bottleneck is as much institutional inertia—等靠要 ('waiting, relying, and demanding')—as it is an engineering gap.
Translation

A Change in Work Style from "Waiting for People to Come" to "Bringing Services to the Door"——

How Can an Old Blueprint Leverage a Leap in Support Capability

■ Zhao Linhao, Ren Zijun, PLA Daily Reporter Qian Xiaohu

"The rebar specifications used to pour the load-bearing walls back then were recorded clearly in the margins of the blueprint. It's thanks to you bringing those old archives to our door to 'diagnose the problem' that this old building has been given a new lease on life." Recently, Wang Yang, an engineer with a certain brigade of the Northern Theater Command Navy, received a phone call of thanks from a battalion commander. On the other end of the line, the newly renovated multipurpose building had passed its completion inspection and was about to be officially put into use.

Behind that phone call of thanks, a blueprint stored for decades not only gave a building "nearly half a century old" a new life, but also triggered a profound transformation within the brigade regarding ideological work style and support models.

This "nearly half-century-old" barracks building had lost its architectural blueprints, leaving the routing of concealed pipelines and the layout of the main load-bearing structure a complete mystery. The renovation plan went through multiple revisions, repeatedly caught between "can't be changed" and "dare not change." At a loss for what to do, the battalion learned that the unit which had originally designed and built the barracks had, through multiple reorganizations and transfers, become a certain brigade of the Northern Theater Command Navy.

Holding onto a slim hope, they came to the door seeking help. The brigade's leadership immediately arranged for engineer Wang Yang to serve as the point of contact. He retrieved design blueprints that had been gathering dust for decades, verified parameters one by one, supplemented data gaps through on-site surveys, and scientifically formulated a renovation plan. The old building was thus completely transformed.

The urgent matter was resolved, but the brigade Party committee's reflection did not stop there. In conjunction with deepening political rectification training (政治整训), they analyzed and examined themselves: passively "waiting for people to come to the door" reflects ideological laxity; proactively "bringing services to the door" is the true measure of responsibility and action. Blueprint archives are not merely static technical heritage—they are living combat-power resources that must be thoroughly understood. And more pressing than the technical baseline is the ideological baseline and the work-style baseline (思想底数和作风底数)—only by purging the inertia of "waiting, relying, and demanding" (等靠要) and establishing the self-discipline of "taking the initiative" can one truly achieve the synchronized resonance (同频共振) of support capability and combat power.

An initiative to optimize the support model through ideological rectification (思想整风) quietly got underway. The brigade formed a technical services task force, divided responsibility by geographic area, and—drawing on the vast collection of architectural design blueprints stored in the blueprint archive room—conducted "physical examination–style" screening of aging barracks buildings and military facilities one by one: comparing building service lifespans, investigating structural aging risks, and cataloguing functional shortfalls. They proactively visited units to flag hidden hazards and simultaneously provided full-chain technical support from plan design through on-site construction.

Task force member Wang Lei, while reviewing the design blueprints for a certain observation and communications station (观通站), discovered a generational gap between the original construction standards of the station's equipment storage building and the storage requirements of new-type equipment. He immediately traveled to the site to investigate and learned that four years earlier the station had initiated a plan to field new equipment, but the renovation project had been repeatedly delayed due to three compounding bottlenecks: substandard warehouse conditions, unclear structural design, and the harsh construction environment on the island. New-type equipment had long been left with "hardware but no home." After identifying the root causes, Wang Lei quickly studied the technical and tactical parameters of the new equipment, scientifically revised the renovation plan in light of on-site hydrological and geological conditions, and saw it through from blueprint verification to construction completion. The new-type equipment was successfully installed, and combat readiness support effectiveness achieved a leap forward.

At the project summary meeting, Wang Lei candidly noted that the station's predicament was by no means an isolated case. As new-quality combat capabilities (新质战斗力) accelerate in their development, many older storage buildings constructed in earlier years have become increasingly unable to match the operational requirements of new-type equipment in terms of floor space, protection standards, and pipeline capacity. The structural contradiction of "new equipment waiting for new warehouses" is growing ever more pronounced.

The brigade Party committee used this as a mirror for further reflection, focusing sharply on the salient contradiction of "aging facilities constraining the release of new equipment's effectiveness," and conducting a thorough examination of whether the "load-bearing walls" (承重墙) of ideological work style were sound: Is there inertia of "guarding one's own turf" (守摊子)? Is there dependence on "waiting for orders" (等指令)? Is there buck-passing born of "fear of accountability" (怕担责)? Is there an attitude of "none of my business" toward other units' combat power development?...

The deeper the rectification training went, the more resolute the action became. Using the design blueprints preserved in the blueprint archive room as a data foundation, they kept a close eye on the usage scenarios of new-type equipment, precisely coordinated with each unit, and clarified renovation requirements and technical pathways item by item. At the same time, they proactively moved the safety checkpoint forward, systematically studying the damage characteristics of typical structures and modern battlefield strike patterns, conducting deductions simulating actual combat conditions such as fragment penetration and artillery blast impact, and going through walls, pipelines, load-bearing components, and other protective shortfalls structure by structure and item by item, compiling categorized facility upgrade lists and renovation recommendations.

Engineer Liu Xinmei, while leading the technical feasibility review for a unit's equipment storage building renovation, repeatedly deduced more than ten damage scenarios against the original blueprints, precisely identifying deep-seated vulnerabilities including weak exterior wall protection and insufficient blast resistance of supporting pipelines. After the rectification plan was implemented, the building's efficiency in transitioning between peacetime and wartime modes improved substantially, and the goal of "capable support in peacetime, able to withstand the test in wartime" was preliminarily achieved.

One old blueprint activated incremental gains in combat power and also illuminated the shortfalls and the reshaping of ideological work style. Drawing on the original parameters of different types of structures and the patterns of battlefield damage, they compiled categorized Standardized Wartime Emergency Repair and Construction Plans (《标准化战时抢修抢建方案》), progressively improving contingency plan reserves and driving the transformation of the support model from "experience-led" to "data-driven." The brigade's leadership told the reporter: "Blueprints are not history sitting in a cabinet—they are the basis for supporting future victory. Political rectification training (政治整训) is not an exercise in abstraction; it must be applied to every concrete matter. Understanding history thoroughly, doing the present work meticulously, and planning for tomorrow with rigor—that is the duty we must fulfill."

Original Chinese
从“等上门”到“送上门”的作风之变—— 一张老图纸，何以撬动保障力跃升 ■赵林浩 任子郡 解放军报记者 钱晓虎 “当年浇筑承重墙的钢筋规格，图纸边栏里记得一清二楚。多亏你们带着老档案上门‘把脉’，让这栋老楼焕发青春。”近日，北部战区海军某大队工程师汪洋接到某营营长致谢电话。话筒那头，新改建的综合楼已通过竣工验收，即将正式投入使用。 致谢电话的背后，一张存放了数十年的老图纸，不仅让一栋“年近半百”的老楼重获新生，更引发了该大队一场关于思想作风与保障模式的深刻变革。 这栋“年近半百”的老营房，因建筑图纸散失，隐蔽管线走向、主体承重布局均成谜团，改建方案几易其稿，始终在“改不动”与“不敢改”之间反复徘徊。一筹莫展之际，该营得知，当年设计承建这栋营房的单位，经多次调整转隶为北部战区海军某大队。 抱着一线希望，他们登门求助。该大队领导当即安排工程师汪洋对接，调取尘封数十年的设计图纸，对照参数逐一复核，结合现场踏勘补全数据缺口，科学制订改建方案，老楼由此脱胎换骨。 救急的事办妥了，大队党委的思考却未止步。结合深化政治整训，他们剖析反思：被动“等上门”折射的是思想上的懈怠，主动“送上门”才是担当作为的标尺。图纸档案不仅是静态的技术遗产，更是鲜活的战斗力资源，必须彻底掌握底数；而比技术底数更紧要的，是思想底数和作风底数——只有把“等靠要”的惰性清出去，把“主动干”的自觉立起来，才能真正实现保障力与战斗力的同频共振。 一场以思想整风引领保障模式优化的行动悄然推开。大队组建技术服务专班，按区域划分挂钩点位，依托图纸档案室存储的海量建筑设计图纸，逐一对年代久远的老旧营房与军事设施展开“体检式”筛查——比对建筑服役年限、摸排结构老化风险、梳理功能短板底数，主动登门提示隐患，并同步提供从方案设计到现场施工的全链条技术支持。 专班成员王磊在翻阅某观通站设计图纸时发现，该站装备库房原始建设标准与新式装备存放需求存在代际差距。他随即赶赴实地调研得知：4年前该站曾启动新装备列装计划，却因库房条件不达标、设计构造不明、海岛施工环境恶劣三重瓶颈，改建工程一拖再拖，新型装备始终“有器无巢”。摸清症结后，王磊迅速研究新装备技战术参数，结合现场水文地质条件科学修订改建方案。从图纸复核到施工落地，新型装备顺利入驻，战备保障效能实现跃升。 项目总结会上，王磊坦言，该站的困局绝非孤例——随着新质战斗力加速生成，不少早年配建的老旧库房在场地尺寸、防护标准、管线容量等方面已难以匹配新装备作战需求，“新装备等新库房”的结构性矛盾日益凸显。 大队党委以此为镜鉴，进一步聚焦“老旧设施束缚新装备效能释放”这一突出矛盾，深入查摆思想作风上的“承重墙”是否牢固：是否有“守摊子”的惰性，是否有“等指令”的依赖，是否有“怕担责”的推诿？是否有别的单位战斗力建设“事不关己”的思想…… 整训越深入，行动越坚决。他们以图纸档案室存续的设计图纸为数据底座，紧盯新型装备使用场景，与各单位精准对接，逐项明确改造需求与技术路径；同时主动前移安全关口，系统研究典型建筑毁伤特性与现代战场打击样式，模拟破片侵彻、炮火冲击等实战工况开展推演，逐栋逐件梳理墙体、管线、承重构件等防护短板，分门别类形成设施升级清单与改造建议。 工程师刘新梅在牵头某部装备库房改建技术论证时，对照原版图纸反复推演十余种毁伤场景，精确判明外墙防护薄弱、配套管线抗毁性能不足等深层隐患。整改方案落地后，库房平战转换效率大幅提升，“平时保障有力、战时经得起考验”的目标初步实现。 一张老图纸，激活了战斗力的增量，也照见了思想作风的短板与重塑。他们结合不同类型建筑原始参数与战场损毁规律，分类编制《标准化战时抢修抢建方案》，逐步完善预案储备，推动保障模式由“经验主导”向“数据驱动”转型。大队领导告诉记者：“图纸不是放在柜子里的历史，而是保障未来打赢的依据。政治整训不是空对空，要落到每一件具体事上。把历史读懂、把当下做细、把明天谋实，才是我们应尽的职责。”
