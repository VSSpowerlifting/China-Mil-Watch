A Certain Brigade's "Engineer Red First Company" of the 72nd Group Army: Forever the Pathfinding Vanguard That Follows the Party

第72集团军某旅“工兵红一连”：永做跟党走的开路先锋
PLA Daily (解放军报) 6 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
The 'Engineer Red First Company' of an unnamed brigade of the 72nd Group Army—founded in 1930 from Anyuan coal miners and designated the 'Pathfinding Vanguard Company' in 1979—received collective second-class merit and two Theater Army commendations in 2024–2025, including recognition as an 'Advanced Unit in Combat Readiness Training (练兵备战先进单位).' The article documents how the company absorbed two force-structure conversions—from road-and-bridge to obstacle-breaching and back again after 2017 restructuring—while maintaining assessed combat capability, and records field innovations including a 'layered compaction and progressive advance (分层垫压、逐段推进)' technique for tidal-flat road-laying that the article claims produced a surface capable of supporting helicopter operations. As political-work content timed to the 90th-anniversary Long March commemorative cycle, its primary value is as a record of how the 72nd Group Army frames Party loyalty and technical competence as mutually reinforcing obligations for junior NCOs, not as evidence of a capability shift.

A Certain Brigade's "Engineer Red First Company" of the 72nd Group Army——

Forever the Pathfinding Vanguard That Follows the Party

■ Tao Lintao, PLA Daily Special Correspondents Tong Zujing and Lu Dongyu

Company Profile

Founded personally by Mao Zedong and Zhu De on October 7, 1930, in Ji'an, Jiangxi, on the basis of 148 Anyuan coal miners, this is our military's first engineer company. It was praised by Mao Zedong as the "Revolutionary White Dragon Horse." During the Long March, the company was tasked with clearing the way and building bridges—on the banks of the Yudu River, the Chishui River, the Dadu River—their figures could be seen everywhere. In 1979, the company was awarded the honorary title "Pathfinding Vanguard Company" by the former Guangzhou Military Region. Since the new era, the company has successively completed multiple major exercise and training missions, demonstrating through concrete action the "White Dragon Horse Spirit (白龙马精神)" of "fearing no hardship, remaining absolutely loyal and trustworthy, persevering through every setback, pressing ever forward, and carrying the revolution to victory."

The "Engineer Red First Company" organizes officers and soldiers to conduct a themed Party Day activity in front of the "White Dragon Horse" sculpture. Photo by Qin Xingfeng.

Light of Victory · Front-Line Visits

In the blazing summer heat, waves of scorching air rolled across a southeastern training ground.

On a tidal flat beside a great river, officers and soldiers of the "Engineer Red First Company" of a certain brigade of the 72nd Group Army were conducting training. The mud was soft and waterlogged; heavy equipment had no footing. Officers and soldiers sank their feet into the mud, laying stone fill and iron plates, fighting to open a path forward.

Looking at the map and tracing upstream for a thousand li, a name comes into view—the Yudu River.

In October 1934, the curtain rose on the Central Red Army's strategic withdrawal from this very place.

"It was our company's forebears who built the first bridge of the Long March across the Yudu River," said Liu Wei, the company's 55th political instructor.

More than 90 years ago, their forebears set out on the long road of the Long March, sustained by an unwavering conviction to follow the Party.

For more than 90 years, generation after generation of the company's officers and soldiers have steadfastly followed the Party, always serving as that White Dragon Horse straining forward on the revolutionary road—carrying the mission, carrying victory, never veering from the course by a single step.

Officers and soldiers of the company conduct engineering surveys using simple observation equipment. Photo by Pan Guangbo.

"The Party Says Build a Bridge, We Build a Bridge; the Party Says Lay a Road, We Lay a Road"

During a training break, this reporter witnessed a special video call.

Pingxiang, Jiangxi. Anyuan Coal Mine Square. A bronze statue stands quietly: a young Mao Zedong holds an oiled-paper umbrella, his gaze fixed on the distance.

"In the autumn of 1930, right here, 148 miners answered the Party's call, shouldered a hundred loads of explosives, joined the Red Army, and formed the Red Army's first engineer company." On the other end of the video, a docent slowly recounted how the company's officers and soldiers followed the Central Red Army across ten thousand li—opening roads through mountains, building bridges over rivers—and won illustrious battle honors.

The officers and soldiers listened in silence. Many had reddened eyes.

In October 1934, tens of thousands of Central Red Army soldiers assembled on the banks of the Yudu River.

On the ground were the Nationalist pursuing troops; in the sky were aircraft conducting constant reconnaissance. The fate of the Central Red Army hung on this one river.

Wang Yaonan—the "Engineer Red First Company's" first company commander and then-commander of the Red Army engineer detachment—received an urgent order: within five days, build five pontoon bridges across the Yudu River to ensure the entire army could cross.

For an engineer company of barely a hundred men, this was a nearly impossible task.

—The river was 600 meters wide; each pontoon bridge required 240 planks, 1,200 lashing points, and 360 stone anchors. There was no specialized equipment, no ready-cut timber. If any single point snapped, the bridge would no longer be a bridge.

—Enemy aircraft circled overhead. To avoid detection, the bridges could only be built after dark and had to be dismantled before dawn.

Yet once the order was issued, no one in the company raised any conditions. Wang Yaonan took the lead and plunged into the icy river.

Five days and four nights—bridges built, dismantled, built again. The ropes cut bloody gashes into the officers' and soldiers' hands; soaking in the cold water, no one cried out in pain, no one took a single step back. In the end, they ensured the Central Red Army's successful crossing.

"No matter how great the difficulties, this company has always listened to the Party and followed the Party." Liu Wei produced a map of the company's campaign route—the Yudu River, the Xiang River, the Wu River, the Chishui River, the Dadu River—wherever the Party Central Committee went, the company followed.

When Mao Zedong visited the engineer troops, he was deeply moved. At an emotional moment, he told them the story of the "White Dragon Horse" from Journey to the West: "The White Dragon Horse's spirit of seeking neither fame nor gain and working with quiet dedication is worth learning from. In my view, you engineers should also be a fine steed for the Red Army—carrying the revolution to victory."

"Listen to the Party, follow the Party, do whatever the Party asks"—this became a conviction fused into the very blood of the company's officers and soldiers.

In the early twenty-first century, in response to the needs of military development, the company was converted from road and bridge engineering to obstacle-breaching. Though still engineers, they went overnight from "earth engineers" who laid roads and built bridges to "fire engineers" who opened fire and operated guns.

Then in 2017, military restructuring and reform converted the company back from obstacle-breaching to road engineering. Many officers and soldiers were troubled: "We finally got to be the 'main act'—why are we back behind the scenes laying roads again?"

The political instructor at the time did not lecture them with grand principles. Instead, he brought the entire company to the company history museum: "According to records, by the time the Long March ended, of the company's 148 officers and soldiers, only around 20 survived. All they thought about was getting the main force across the river, getting the revolution to victory. We are the 'White Dragon Horse'—seeking neither fame nor gain, working with quiet dedication. When have we ever grabbed the spotlight? When have we ever picked and chosen our tasks?"

A revolutionary soldier is like a brick—to be placed wherever needed. With no ready-made equipment, they borrowed old equipment from sister units to train with. With no technical backbone, everyone learned the skills and became instructors. With study rooms too few, they set up study rooms on every floor, veterans and new soldiers alike studying together deep into the night.

In just one year, the company passed its combat capability assessment. Multiple subjects—including mountain excavator operations and combat grouping—were demonstrated at the Army's engineering and CBRN defense standardization training, and their experience was promoted across the entire Army.

"The Party says build a bridge, we build a bridge; the Party says lay a road, we lay a road." Liu Wei said: that is our "White Dragon Horse Spirit."

"When Party Member Cadres Charge to the Front, the Soldiers Who Follow Them Step More Firmly"

"A nearly 40-degree slope, a vertical drop of more than 20 meters, a construction access road less than two fingers wider than the excavator's tracks—one moment of inattention and man and machine would roll down together." Two years on, recalling that exercise still makes Squad Leader Zhang Jing's heart race.

"The exercise had entered a critical phase; every minute of delay would hold up the follow-on forces. Backbone personnel from sister units looked at the steep slope with its crumbling loose soil and hesitated. The wind was sending pebbles rolling down. No one dared to be the first to drive an excavator up."

"I'll go!" Zhang Jing rolled up his sleeve and jumped into the excavator.

Maneuvering several tons of iron suspended on the slope, the tracks grinding through loose soil and sliding downward, Zhang Jing inside the cab was calm as still water, pushing the earthwork forward meter by meter. After a full two hours, a level platform had been cut out, clean and even. The bridge-laying vehicle drove up and completed the bridge in a single attempt; the route was opened on time.

Where does this blood-and-guts courage—this willingness to take responsibility and tackle the hardest problems—come from? This reporter found the answer in the roster of company commanders and political instructors in the company's honor room: from 1935 to 1936, due to combat casualties, the company went through four commanding officers.

"When building bridges, we are at the very front; after the troops have crossed, we cover the rear—we cannot leave until the bridge is dismantled. Our casualty rate was even higher than that of front-line infantry companies." Liu Wei said: "Precisely because of that, Party member cadres must shoulder the heaviest burdens and gnaw on the hardest bones. That is the rule left by the old company commander Wang Yaonan."

In 1935: "The Luding Bridge spans the river, its iron chains cold." When the Central Red Army arrived, the planks of the Luding Bridge had been stripped bare by the enemy; only smooth iron chains hung over the rushing current.

Twenty-two assault team members grabbed the chains and charged toward the far bank. Wang Yaonan hoisted a plank and followed close behind. Soldiers scrambled to take it from him; he turned and roared: "The first plank on the engineer company's bridge—the company commander must lay it!"

Tracing through the company's history, the image of Party member backbone personnel charging to the front appears everywhere—

On the banks of the Xiang River, they built pontoon bridges under enemy air bombardment; bombs detonated beside them, sending water columns several meters high, yet the Party member backbone personnel gripped the bridge piles and not one of them took cover. During operations to defend the motherland's frontier, Party member backbone personnel took the lead in clearing mines and breaching obstacles at cliff passes to open the route of advance.

Liu Wei told this reporter that the spiritual qualities forged in the years of war have been passed down from generation to generation among the officers and soldiers. Today, it has become the norm for Party member backbone personnel to charge to the front line, shoulder heavy burdens, and solve difficult problems.

During one exercise and training event, the exercise control group set up special situations including "enemy fire blockade" and "road section destroyed." The company Party branch committee member was the first to drive an engineering vehicle through the blockade line; the Party member assault team followed. They opened the route one-third ahead of the prescribed time. When new equipment was fielded, the Party member research team spent months on end at the training ground, mastering the extreme performance limits of the new equipment and compiling a thick volume of practical operating manuals.

"Visible in ordinary times, able to step forward at critical moments, willing to give everything at moments of crisis." Liu Wei said: "When Party member cadres charge to the front, the soldiers who follow them step more firmly."

"Loyalty Requires the Nourishment of Conviction—and Even More, the Support of Capability"

In the company's honor room, a horse lantern sits quietly. Its iron casing has rusted; the glass chimney has worn to a frosted edge; the wick still bears the black char of burning.

"On the eve of the Long March's departure, it was by the light of this horse lantern—under strict blackout conditions—that the company built the pontoon bridge spanning both banks of the Yudu River." Liu Wei told this reporter that the greatest challenge when building the pontoon bridge at that time was fear of detection by enemy aircraft. But on the pitch-black river surface, it was extremely difficult to keep the pontoon bridge aligned.

Wang Yaonan found a solution: hang a horse lantern in the middle of each boat; the faint light, connected in a line, marked the bridge's axis perfectly straight. The principle of this "horse lantern axis-marking method (马灯标定轴线法)" remains a required course for pontoon bridge units to this day.

From two historical documents—the "Field Army Plan for Crossing the Gong River Southward" and the "Field Army Action Schedule from October 10 to 20"—this reporter could see detailed notations of bridge-building positions, operational sequences, and force deployments, reflecting throughout the outstanding skill and professional competence of the engineer forebears.

"Loyalty requires the nourishment of conviction—and even more, the support of capability." Wang Tao, who once served as the company's 44th company commander, said: each generation has its own Long March; each generation has its own examination paper.

Entering the new era, Chairman Xi's "question of winning wars (胜战之问)" rang out like a thunderclap, and a wave of combat-realistic exercises and training swept through the entire military.

The commander's orders are the direction of troop training. Over the years, the company has trained against the performance boundaries of its equipment, independently organized effectiveness trials, measured more than 300 sets of live-equipment data from start to finish, and mastered the potential of every type of equipment. Officers and soldiers have carried out technical innovations in connection with their missions; one "golden idea" after another has been converted into real, tangible combat power.

During one exercise and training event, the road surface had been soaked soft by heavy rain; the bends were sharp and the slopes steep, and heavy equipment wheels would spin and sink the moment they slipped. Senior Sergeant Second Class He Zhongping crouched by the roadside and studied the situation for a long time. The training manual clearly stated that mechanized road surfaces could only be laid in straight lines—but this road twisted and turned; laying it straight simply could not reach the position.

He did not confine himself to the manual. A few people crouched in the mud and held a brief meeting, tried removing one end's fastener, and used dual-plank traction to link sections progressively. They managed to lay a smooth, even route through the sharp bend, and the equipment behind them drove through one vehicle after another without incident.

During another exercise and training event, the tidal flat was so soft that stepping on it sank you half a leg deep; conventional methods simply could not lay a road. Squad Leader Yuan Bo led his people, soaking in the mud, and tested more than ten types of fill material. They mastered the load-bearing range of the silt and worked out a method of "layered compaction and progressive advance (分层垫压、逐段推进)." The surface they constructed was hard enough to support helicopter takeoffs and landings.

In recent years, 15 personnel from the company have won gold and silver at higher-level skills competitions. In 2024, the company was commended by the Theater Army as an "Advanced Unit in Combat Readiness Training (练兵备战先进单位)." In 2025, it was commended by the Theater Army as a "Model Unit in Implementing the Goal of Strengthening the Military (践行强军目标标兵单位)" and was awarded a collective second-class merit citation.

At the close of the interview, as this reporter left the company's training ground, the words at the company's entrance—"Success need not be achieved by me, but success will surely be achieved with me (功成不必在我，功成必定有我)"—shone brilliantly in the light of the setting sun.

Voices from the Heart

On the Eve of an Exercise, Reflections on "What Changes and What Does Not"

■ "Engineer Red First Company" Senior Sergeant Second Class He Zhongping

In those years, our company's forebears opened the first crossing of the Long March, building pontoon bridges through the night with simple equipment to open the route of advance for the Long March army. Now, with an exercise imminent, facing entirely new equipment and complex terrain conditions, we must work in concert to master the key points of operation and complete road-surface laying and recovery operations to a high standard, ensuring the smooth movement of the follow-on main force. From crossing rivers and building bridges in those days to overcoming every difficulty today—equipment changes with each passing day, but what does not change is the responsibility of the pathfinding vanguard. I will lead my squad's comrades in carrying forward the company's fine traditions and continuing the red bloodline through concrete action.

Carrying Forward the Company Spirit on the Road to a Stronger Military

■ "Engineer Red First Company" Senior Sergeant Second Class Qin Xiang

In October 1934, following the failure of the Fifth Counter-Encirclement Campaign, the Red Army was compelled to undertake a strategic withdrawal. Our company's forebears received an urgent order, ensured the Red Army's successful river crossing, and set out on the 25,000-li Long March. The road ahead was uncertain, dangers loomed on all sides, yet no one retreated. Because in their hearts there was only one conviction: follow the Party, and there is hope! Reflecting on the past and looking at the present, how fortunate we are—the nation is strong, our equipment is excellent. This "fortune" was bought by our forebears with their blood and sacrifice. Pressing forward on the road to a stronger military, we must resolutely listen to the Party and follow the Party, carry forward the "White Dragon Horse Spirit" of "fearing no hardship, remaining absolutely loyal and trustworthy, persevering through every setback, pressing ever forward, and carrying the revolution to victory," and contribute our strength to achieving the centenary goal of army-building on schedule.

Narrating · My War History Lesson

The First Plank on the Bridge or Road—Party Member Cadres Must Lay It

■ "Engineer Red First Company" Political Instructor Liu Wei

[Photo caption: Archival photograph]

In our engineer company, there is an iron rule: the first plank on any bridge or road—Party member cadres must lay it.

This was established by the first company commander, Wang Yaonan. On the Long March, more than 20 rivers and more than a hundred bridges—most of them bear the traces of his footsteps. In his later years, someone asked him: "After a lifetime of building bridges, which one is most unforgettable?" He was silent for a long time, then said: "The Luding Bridge."

On May 28, 1935, the 4th Red Regiment received a life-or-death order: march 240 li day and night and seize the Luding Bridge. The engineer company's mission was two words: keep it open.

At dawn on the 29th, when the troops seized the western bridgehead position at the Luding Bridge, only 13 iron chains remained. At four o'clock in the afternoon, the general assault signal sounded. Twenty-two assault team members climbed onto the iron chains; the old company commander also hoisted the first door plank. Soldiers scrambled to go up; he turned sharply and said with iron resolve: "The first plank on the engineer company's bridge—the company commander must lay it!"

Enemy bullets struck the iron chains and struck sparks. Soldier Zhang Jinyuan—the youngest man in the company—missed his footing and fell. Squad Leader Li Wenbin saw this and grabbed Zhang Jinyuan's wrist; but the enormous momentum pulled both men downward together. At the critical moment, Platoon Leader Chen Yimin lunged forward and grabbed Li Wenbin's belt in time, linking the three into a chain. But the plank that Chen Yimin's left hand was gripping was too old and cracked; it could not bear the weight of three people. Zhang Jinyuan's face turned purple; he looked at his two comrades, then at the old company commander, and called out: "Company Commander—the road ahead, everyone will have to lay it for us!" Chen Yimin gritted his teeth; blood seeped from the corner of his mouth: "Hold—hold on—" Before he could finish, a bullet struck Chen Yimin; his fingers loosened, and all three fell together into the Dadu River.

The old company commander turned back in grief, forcing out one word at a time: "Grip the chains, reinforce the planks—no one stops!" The soldiers, eyes red, buried their heads and worked; their fingertips wore through and bled freely, yet not one person stopped. Seeing the Red Army drawing ever closer, the enemy set fire to the eastern end of the bridge. Waves of heat and thick smoke came surging. The old company commander turned to look—and what he saw moved him deeply: one man had been shot in the lower leg, kneeling on one knee, one hand propping up his body, the other still tying hemp rope; another man's hands were a mass of bloody flesh, yet he was using his forearms to clamp a plank and inch forward. He later recalled: "The fire had turned the iron chains red; the blood flowing from our hands was also red—you couldn't tell them apart."

The bridge was taken! The route was opened! After two o'clock in the early morning of the 30th, Red Army Chief of Staff Liu Bocheng walked onto the bridge. He walked from one end to the other, then to the very middle, and stamped three heavy times. He let out a long breath; his voice carried a catch: "Luding Bridge—we have won!"

Many years later, the old company commander Wang Yaonan returned to the Luding Bridge. He said nothing, wrote nothing—only stood at the bridgehead, facing the Dadu River, and raised a salute.

The river still flows; the iron chains still sway. But the planks laid that night have long since become the open road of an army pressing forward.

Looking Back · My View of the Long March

Willing to Be the "Paving Stone" for a Stronger Military

■ PLA Daily Reporter Kang Zizhan

"The Red Army fears not the trials of the Long March, treating a thousand mountains and ten thousand rivers as nothing"—there are the heroic deeds of those who charged into battle, and there is also the silent, tenacious act of bearing others up.

On the Long March, the "Engineer Red First Company's" officers and soldiers always fought at the front and bore the pressure at the front. On the banks of the Yudu River, their forebears used the faint light of a horse lantern to mark the bridge axis, built pontoon bridges through the night, and laid a passage for the Central Red Army's covert breakout. On the banks of the Chishui River, they repeatedly built bridges and opened routes at key crossings, creating the miracle of troops appearing as if from heaven. On the Dadu River, under a hail of bullets, they laid bridge planks and opened a lifeline for the follow-on forces.

Generation after generation of red engineers sought no fame or credit; they carried an extraordinary mission from an ordinary post, supported the overall victory through silent perseverance, and vividly embodied the fine conduct of revolutionary soldiers—taking the overall situation into account, making selfless contributions, and daring to fight and win.

The smoke of battle has cleared, but the flame is unextinguished. Though the new-era journey to strengthen the military holds none of the life-or-death extremity of the snow mountains and grasslands, it remains full of risks, challenges, hardships, and obstacles. To carry forward the great Long March Spirit, what matters most is holding fast to the original aspiration and taking root in one's combat post—to be like the "Engineer Red First Company," continuing the "White Dragon Horse Spirit" of "fearing no hardship, remaining absolutely loyal and trustworthy, persevering through every setback, pressing ever forward, and carrying the revolution to victory," willingly serving as the "paving stone" for a stronger military, and walking well the Long March that belongs to our generation.

毛泽东到工兵部队时，深受触动，说到动情之处，给大家讲起《西游记》里“白龙马”的故事，“白龙马这种不计名利、埋头苦干的精神，是值得学习的。依我看，你们工兵也应该做红军的一匹骏马，驮着革命走向胜利。” “听党话、跟党走，党让干啥就干啥”，成了融进连队官兵血脉里的信念。 21世纪初，因军队建设发展形势需要，连队从道路桥梁专业调整为破障专业。虽同为工兵，但一下子就从铺路架桥的“土工兵”，成了开火打炮的“火工兵”。 然而2017年，军队调整改革，连队从破障专业改回道路专业。不少官兵心里想不通：“好不容易当了回‘主角’，咋又回后台铺路了？” 时任指导员没讲大道理，而是把全连带到了连史馆：“据统计，长征结束时，连队148名官兵，只有20多人幸存……大家想的就是让主力部队过河，让革命胜利。我们是不计名利、埋头苦干的‘白龙马’，什么时候抢过主角？什么时候挑过活？” 革命军人是块砖，哪里需要哪里搬。没有现成装备，就找兄弟单位借老旧装备练；没有业务骨干，就人人学技术、当教员；现有的学习室不够用，就在每层楼开设学习室，老兵新兵一起钻研到深夜。 仅一年，连队就通过了作战能力评估，山地挖掘机作业、战斗编组等多个课目，在陆军工程防化规范化集训上作示范，经验在全陆军推广。 “党叫架桥，我们就架桥；党叫铺路，我们就铺路。”刘威说，这就是我们的“白龙马精神”。 “党员干部冲在最前面，战士们跟随的脚步更坚定” “近40度的陡坡，垂直高度20多米，施工便道比挖掘机履带宽不到两指，稍不留神连人带装就会滚下去。”时隔2年，再次忆起那场演习，班长张静仍心有余悸。 “演习进入关键阶段，晚一分钟后续部队就会被卡住。兄弟单位的骨干看着掉松土的陡坡都犯难，风刮得碎石子往下滚，没人敢第一个开挖掘机上去。” “我来！”张静把袖子一挽，跳上了挖掘机。 操纵着几吨重的铁疙瘩悬在坡上，履带碾着松土直往下滑，操作室里的张静心如止水，一米一米往前推土方。整整两个小时，平台整整齐齐开出来，架桥车开上去一次架设成功，通路准时打通。 这种敢于担当、敢于攻坚的血性胆气从哪里来？记者在连队荣誉室里的连主官名录上找到了答案——1935年到1936年，由于战斗牺牲，一连换了4任主官。 “架桥的时候我们在最前面，部队过完我们断后，拆完桥才能走，伤亡率比一线步兵连还高。”刘威说，“越是这样，党员干部越要挑最重的担子，啃最硬的骨头，这是老连长王耀南留下的规矩。” 1935年，“大渡桥横铁索寒”。当中央红军赶到时，泸定桥面上的木板被敌人拆得精光，只剩光溜溜的铁索悬在激流之上。 22名突击队员攀着铁索往对岸冲，王耀南扛起木板紧随其后，战士们抢着要替他，他回头吼道：“工兵连第一块板，必须连长铺！” 沿着连史探寻，党员骨干冲锋在前的身影处处可见—— 湘江边，他们在敌机轰炸下架浮桥，炸弹在身边炸起几米高的水柱，党员骨干扶着桥桩，谁也不躲；保卫祖国边疆作战期间，党员骨干带头在悬崖隘口排雷破障、开辟进军通路…… 刘威告诉记者，发轫于战争岁月的精神品质，在官兵身上代代传承。如今，党员骨干冲在一线挑重担、解难题已成常态。 一次演训，导调组设置“敌火力封锁”“路段被炸”特情，该连党支部委员第一个驾驶工程车辆冲过封锁线，党员突击队跟着上，比规定时间提前1/3打通通路；新装备列装，党员攻关组连续数个月泡在训练场，摸透新装备极限性能，编出了厚厚一本实操教材。 “平时看得出来，关键时刻站得出来，危急关头豁得出来。”刘威说，“党员干部冲在最前面，战士们跟随的脚步更坚定。” “忠诚需要信仰浇筑，更需要能力支撑” 连队荣誉室内，一盏马灯静静放着。铁壳锈了，玻璃罩磨出了毛边，灯芯上还留着烧黑的炭痕。 “长征出发前夕，连队正是靠着这盏马灯，在严控灯火条件下架起了横跨于都河两岸的浮桥。”刘威告诉记者，当年架设浮桥时，最大难题是怕被敌军飞机看见。可黑漆漆的河面上，很难保证浮桥对齐。 王耀南找到了办法：在每条船中间挂一盏马灯，微弱的灯光连成一线，便把浮桥的轴线标得笔直。这种“马灯标定轴线法”的原理，至今仍是舟桥部队的必修课。 从《野战军南渡贡水计划表》《野战军由十月十日至二十日行动日程表》两份历史资料中，记者看到，上面详细标注着架桥点位、作业时序、兵力部署，处处展现出工兵先辈的过硬本领与专业素养。 “忠诚需要信仰浇筑，更需要能力支撑。”曾任连队第44任连长的汪涛说，一代人有一代人的长征，每代人有每代人的考卷。 进入新时代，习主席发出的“胜战之问”振聋发聩，全军部队实战化演训掀起热潮。 统帅的号令，就是练兵的方向。这些年，连队盯着装备性能边界练，独立组织效能试验，前前后后测了300多组实装数据，摸透每型装备的潜力；官兵们结合任务开展技术革新，一个个“金点子”，转化成了实打实的战斗力。 一次演训中，路面被大雨泡得稀软，弯急坡陡，重型装备轮一打滑就要陷进去。二级上士何中平蹲在路边瞅了半天——教范里明确说机械化路面只能直线铺设，可这路七拐八弯，直着铺根本到不了阵地。 他没有拘泥于教范，几个人蹲在泥里开了个短会，试着拆了一端固定器，用双板牵引逐段衔接，愣是在急弯上铺出了平整通路，后面的装备一辆接一辆稳稳开了过去。 一次演训，滩涂软得踩一脚陷半腿，常规办法根本铺不了路。班长袁博带着人泡在泥里，试了十几种垫料，摸透了淤泥的受力范围，摸索出“分层垫压、逐段推进”的办法，构筑出的地面硬度，能够保障直升机起降。 近年来，连队先后有15人在上级比武中摘金夺银，2024年被战区陆军表彰为“练兵备战先进单位”，2025年被战区陆军表彰为“践行强军目标标兵单位”、荣立集体二等功。 采访结束，记者离开该连驻训地，只见连队门口“功成不必在我，功成必定有我”几个大字，在夕阳的映照下光彩夺目。 心语回响 演练在即感悟“变与不变” ■“工兵红一连”二级上士 何中平 当年，连队前辈开设了长征第一渡，使用简易器材连夜架设浮桥，为长征大军开辟前进通道。如今演练在即，面对全新装备和复杂地域条件，我们一定齐心协力钻研操作要点，高标准完成路面铺设和撤收作业，保障后续大部队顺利机动。从昔日遇水架桥，到今朝攻坚克难，装备日新月异，不变的是开路先锋的担当。我将带着班里的战友传承好连队的优良传统，以实际行动赓续红色血脉。 强军路上传承连队精神 ■“工兵红一连”二级上士 覃 湘 1934年10月，第五次反“围剿”失利，红军被迫战略转移。我们连队的前辈临危受命，保障红军成功渡河，踏上二万五千里长征。前途未卜，危机重重，无人退缩。因为他们心中只有一个信念：跟着走，就有希望！抚今追昔，我们何其有幸，国家强盛，装备精良。这份“幸福”，是先辈用热血与牺牲换来的。奋进强军路，我们要坚决听党话、跟党走，传承好“不畏艰险、矢忠守信、百折不挠、勇往直前，把革命驮向胜利”的“白龙马精神”，为如期实现建军一百年奋斗目标贡献力量。 述说·我讲战史课 架桥铺路第一块板，必须党员干部铺 ■“工兵红一连”指导员 刘 威 资料图片 在我们工兵连，有个铁规矩——架桥铺路第一块板，必须党员干部铺。 这是首任连长王耀南定下的。长征路上20余条江河、一百多座桥，大多留下了他的足迹。晚年有人问他：“架了一辈子桥，哪座最难忘？”他沉默了很久说：“泸定桥。” 1935年5月28日，红四团接到死命令：昼夜奔袭240里，夺取泸定桥。工兵连的任务就2个字：保通。 29日拂晓，部队占领泸定桥西桥头阵地时，桥只剩13根铁索。下午4点，总攻号响。22名突击队员攀上铁索，老连长也扛起第一块门板。战士们抢着要上，他猛地回头，说得很硬气：“工兵连第一块板，必须连长铺！” 敌人的子弹打在铁索上激出火星。战士张金元，连里最年轻的兵，一脚踩空往下坠。班长李文斌见状一把抓住张金元的手腕，然而，巨大的惯性拉着两人一起下落。危急时刻，排长陈亦民扑了过来，及时抓住李文斌的腰带，3个人连成了一串。怎奈陈亦民左手抠住的那块门板因过于老旧而开裂，根本承受不住3个人的体重。张金元脸憋得发紫，看向两个战友，又看向老连长，他喊道：“连长，后面的路，得大家帮我们铺了！”陈亦民咬着牙，血从嘴角渗出来：“抓……抓紧……”没说完，一颗子弹正中陈亦民，手指一松，3个人一起坠入了大渡河。 老连长悲痛地转回头，嘴里一个字一个字往外砸：“抓紧铁索，加固门板，谁都不许停！”战士们红着眼眶埋头干，指尖磨破了鲜血直流，没有一个人停手。敌人眼看红军越来越近，便在桥东头放起了火。火裹着热浪和浓烟一阵阵袭来，老连长回头一看，那一幕令他动容——有人小腿中弹，单膝匍匐，一只手撑着身子，另一只手还在捆麻绳；有人双手血肉模糊，就用胳膊夹着板子往前挪。他后来回忆说：“烈火把铁索烧红了，我们手上流的血也是红的，分不清了……” 桥夺下了！路打通了！30日凌晨两点多，红军总参谋长刘伯承走上桥。从这头走到那头，又走到桥正中，重重跺了三脚。他长长吐出一口气，声音带着哽咽：“泸定桥——我们胜利了！” 很多年后，老连长王耀南再回泸定桥，不讲话，不写字，只是站在桥头，对着大渡河，敬了一个军礼。 河水还在流淌，铁索依旧晃动，但那一夜铺上去的门板，已经变成了一支军队前进的坦途…… 回眸·我的长征观 甘当强军“铺路石” ■解放军报记者 康子湛 “红军不怕远征难，万水千山只等闲”，有敢于冲锋陷阵的壮举，亦有沉默而坚韧的托举。 长征路上，“工兵红一连”官兵始终战斗在前、承压在前。于都河畔，先辈以马灯微光标定桥轴线，连夜架设浮桥，为中央红军隐秘突围铺就通途；赤水河边，在关键渡口反复架桥通路，造就神兵天降；大渡河上，冒着枪林弹雨铺设桥板，为后续部队开辟生命通道。 一代代红色工兵不图功名，以平凡战位承载非凡使命，用无声坚守支撑全局胜利，生动诠释了革命军人顾全大局、无私奉献、敢打必胜的优良作风。 硝烟散尽，薪火不息。新时代强军征程虽无雪山草地的生死绝境，却依然充满风险挑战、艰难险阻。传承伟大长征精神，贵在坚守初心、扎根战位，像“工兵红一连”那样，赓续“不畏艰险、矢忠守信、百折不挠、勇往直前，把革命驮向胜利”的“白龙马精神”，甘当强军“铺路石”，走好属于我们这一代人的长征路。 版式设计：方 汉、杨 磊 制图：杨 磊
