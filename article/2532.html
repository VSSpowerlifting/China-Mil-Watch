Summary
A PLA-affiliated author identified as Jun Tan lays out a formal doctrinal framework for 规则战 (rules warfare), defining it as a higher-order hybrid confrontation in which states weaponize international rules across trade, technology, law, cyberspace, ideology, and military-security domains to restrict adversaries without resorting to armed force. The article codifies five internal victory mechanisms — standards-locking, institutional regulation, discourse shaping, systemic control, and iterative rule-setting in emerging domains — and explicitly frames latecomer states as capable of achieving 弯道制衡 (flanking counterbalance) by building autonomous rule systems and multilateral coalitions. The framing points to an effort by PLA theorists to elevate rules competition to the level of operational doctrine, positioning China as the aggrieved party in an existing hegemonic order while simultaneously prescribing offensive strategies for contesting that order across every governance domain.
The article introduces and systematically defines 'rules warfare' (规则战) as a formal operational concept with named sub-mechanisms, representing a doctrinal articulation of hybrid competition that explicitly frames international law, standards bodies, trade rules, and cognitive domains as weapons—a level of conceptual codification that would interest analysts tracking PLA legal warfare (falü zhan) and broader 'three warfares' doctrine evolution.

Rules Warfare: The Higher-Order Form of Hybrid War Competition

■ Jun Tan

Editor's Note: In today's world, the once-in-a-century transformation is accelerating, the international order is undergoing profound adjustment, and great-power competition is escalating across the board. In the view of certain countries and militaries, international rules are not merely a baseline constraining war — they have themselves become weapons of competition. Rules warfare (规则战), as a new higher-order form of operations that breaks through the traditional paradigm of violence and the boundaries of cognition, has already taken the stage of history and become an important variable shaping the global landscape. In essence, it represents the systemic reconstruction of the global interest-distribution system, the governance-rules system, and the logic of great-power competition during the transition from the old international order to the new. Seizing dominance over the rules has already become the key to great powers winning long-term strategic initiative in competition.

Rules warfare refers to a hybrid confrontation form in which certain countries or blocs of countries use international rules as weapons — through formulating, exploiting, reshaping, deconstructing, circumventing, and creating various competition rules — to contest institutional dominance, legal legitimacy, and the moral high ground, thereby restricting adversaries' freedom of action, amplifying their own advantages, and reducing their own costs. This form of confrontation spans multiple dimensions including trade and economics, science and technology, law, cyberspace, climate, the military, and cognition. It is the result of the intertwined effects of multiple factors: the evolution of the international landscape, the deep adjustment of globalization, the drive of the technological revolution, the transformation of great-power competition logic, and reform of the global governance system.

The Defining Characteristics of Rules Warfare

Rules warfare differs from traditional war models that aim at territorial occupation, resource plunder, and armed annihilation. It is an asymmetric, higher-order confrontation conducted through the system of rules, standards, and institutions, and it profoundly changes the mechanisms for achieving victory and the competitive logic of international strategic competition.

A de-violentized (去暴力化) form of contest. Rules warfare completely breaks free from traditional war's path dependency on military violence. It takes the system of rules, standards, and institutions as the core of competition, achieving strategic suppression through flexible means such as sanctions barriers, compliance reviews, standards exclusion, systemic decoupling, and monopolization of the right to interpret rules. At the same time, rules warfare has a pronounced asymmetry. Hegemonic states apply pressure by leveraging their existing rules hegemony, while latecomer states can achieve "flanking counterbalance (弯道制衡)" through localized rules innovation, creation of new standards, and multilateral coalition-building, forming a pattern of "breaking hegemony through institutions, reforming the old order through new rules." This profoundly reshapes the logic of power competition in modern warfare.

A competition pattern of full-domain penetration (全域渗透). Rules warfare completely breaks through temporal, spatial, and domain limitations, achieving normalized competition that is all-domain, all-hours, and all-encompassing. In the domain dimension, it fully penetrates every domain of national survival and development — trade and finance, science and technology industries, data networks, arms control, and global governance. Any domain regulated by international rules or industry standards is a battlefield for rules warfare. In the temporal-spatial dimension, competition makes no distinction between wartime and peacetime, no distinction between front lines and rear areas; it is embedded in everyday international cooperation, economic and trade exchanges, and technological interaction, presenting an all-domain posture of "peacetime is wartime, everywhere is a battlefield," with extremely strong extensibility.

Legitimate and concealed means of contest. All confrontational actions in rules warfare are conducted through international conventions, industry standards, compliance systems, and multilateral mechanisms — carrying out strategic containment under the name of "legal compliance," with extremely strong moral packaging and institutional concealment. The initiating party typically controls the right to interpret, adjudicate, and enforce existing rules, packaging unilateral sanctions, technology blockades, systemic exclusion, and standards barriers as "compliance governance," "market rules," and "international norms." This model institutionalizes, standardizes, and legitimizes certain hegemonic behaviors, trapping the passive party in the dilemma of "violate the rules and be restricted, comply with the rules and be controlled," making it difficult for conventional competition tools to break through.

A long-term, gradual tempo of confrontation. Rules warfare is a long-cycle, low-intensity, normalized, and gradual competition with no clear start or end point. The construction, revision, subversion, and reconstruction of rule systems is a long-term process; the implementation, popularization, and iteration of standards requires time to accumulate; the replacement of orders and the transfer of power depend on the accumulation of long-term competition. Rules competition does not decide winners and losers in the short term, but is a strategic tug-of-war lasting years or even longer. Both sides of the competition, through continuously revising rules, iterating standards, building new systems, and dismantling old orders, gradually compress the adversary's development space and accumulate their own strategic advantages, ultimately achieving order reconstruction and power transition — exhibiting the long-term confrontation characteristic of "imperceptible influence, sustained effort (潜移默化、久久为功)."

A competition mode of systemic confrontation (体系对抗). Rules warfare is a structural confrontation that is comprehensive, full-process, and full-system, competing on a state's overall capacity to build institutions, lead standards, integrate systems, and mobilize internationally. The actors in competition are no longer a single military or domain, but the entire institutional system, standards system, and governance system of a state or even an alliance. Competition encompasses the full-process links of rule creation, standards export, system adaptation, multilateral binding, and risk hedging. The stronger party implements full-dimensional systemic containment by building a closed-loop, exclusionary rules system; effective breakthroughs by the weaker party also require building an autonomous rules system, forming multilateral partner coalitions, and substituting new governance systems to achieve system-level counterbalancing. This is a typical confrontation model of "victory through systems, decision through order (体系制胜、秩序决胜)."

Rules dominance as the core of victory. Rules warfare reconstructs the logic of victory: the right to define rules, authority over industry standards, international discourse power, and order dominance become the core elements of victory. Under this paradigm, military hard power serves as the ultimate guarantee. Whoever can first define new rules of competition, industry standards, and value paradigms — whoever leads the revision, interpretation, and enforcement of international rules, and consolidates multilateral consensus to promote their own institutional system — controls the underlying logic of global competition. Holding the right to define rules means being able to set industry thresholds, delineate interest boundaries, and lead the direction of governance, achieving the strategic objective of "constraining adversaries through rules, controlling advantages through standards, and maintaining dominance through order." This is the most essential logical characteristic that distinguishes rules warfare from traditional warfare.

The Multiple Forms of Rules Warfare

As the center of gravity of international competition shifts toward competition over institutional rules, rules warfare has deeply penetrated key domains of national development and has evolved into a comprehensive, systemic confrontation that integrates peacetime and wartime, is concealed and gradual, cross-domain and interconnected, and long-term in nature.

Manifested in the economic and trade domain: the frontline of interest distribution. This is the most frequently initiated, most broadly affecting, and most directly impactful form of rules warfare. Its core logic lies in manipulating, modifying, and even abusing international trade, investment, and financial rules to systematically undermine the target country's economic and trade ecosystem, sever the modernization process of industrial and supply chains, and implement deep-level economic suppression. Certain major powers frequently carry out unilateral containment under the name of "rules compliance," which is in essence a fierce contest for dominance over global economic and trade governance and discourse power over value chains. This high-intensity, normalized, and all-domain confrontation poses a severe challenge to the stability of the global economic and trade order.

Manifested in the science and technology domain: the core hub of technological dominance. This is a contest for dominance over science and technology rules without the smoke of gunpowder, and is the key to victory in rules warfare. Its core lies in controlling core technology standards, industry norms, patent layouts, and certification systems to create a technology path lock-in effect, aimed at compressing the target country's space for independent innovation and industrial upgrading and seizing the global commanding heights of science and technology. Operations run through the full chain of rules covering technology R&D, patent layout, product market access, industry norms, and technology export controls, striving to achieve strategic suppression through high-standards barriers. With its high technological content, this has become the most fundamental and long-term form of rules warfare.

Manifested in the legal and judicial domain: a precision instrument of rules competition. This form of rules warfare is highly concealed, highly precise, and professionally specialized. It uses international law and domestic law as "weapons," implementing precise "soft kills (软杀伤)" against entities in the target country through the instrumentalization of judicial procedures, the complexification of legal competition, and the politicization of compliance reviews. In essence, it highly weaponizes the judiciary — through selective application of law, malicious manipulation of procedures, and abuse of extraterritorial jurisdiction — to break through the principle of sovereignty and implement transnational legal encirclement. The core is the contest for the right to interpret international law and judicial authority. Its strategic containment nature is often concealed behind a professional veneer.

Manifested in the cyber and digital domain: an emerging high ground of the governance system. As the digital economy and cyberspace have become the core domain of strategic competition, the cyber and digital space has rapidly risen as an emerging main battlefield. Data sovereignty, cybersecurity, platform regulation, and algorithmic ethics are the core issues, with the goal of contesting dominance over global cyber and digital governance. Operations revolve around defining digital sovereignty, rules for key data flows, norms for state behavior in cyberspace, and the construction of a new digital trade system, aiming to use rules to demarcate boundaries first and control the lifeline of digital economic development. This directly concerns national digital sovereignty, development space, and future strategic positioning.

Manifested in the ideological domain: the concealed front of value competition. This is the form of rules warfare that extends deeply into the cognitive domain and the domain of values. Packaged in specific value concepts, it carries out cognitive attacks through systematic ideological infiltration, manipulation of international public opinion, and shaping of discourse narratives, aimed at weakening the target country's institutional identity, confidence in its chosen path, and international image. In essence, it perverts value issues such as human rights into political tools, constructs double standards, and contests the international moral high ground and ideological dominance through monopolizing the right to define discourse, manipulating opinion tribunals, and exporting values. This form of warfare can profoundly erode the target country's public opinion environment, social consensus, and the foundations of soft power, and is highly concealed, deceptive, and long-term in nature.

Manifested in the military and security domain: a key domain of strategic deterrence. This is the strategic extension of rules warfare into the traditional security domain, reflecting the deepening of great-power military competition toward rules-based containment. It revolves around arms control mechanisms, codes of conduct for military operations, alliance treaty systems, and security rules for emerging domains. The core is to contest the right to formulate and interpret international rules in the military and security domain, aimed at restricting other countries' military force development and compressing other countries' strategic security space. Using international military law, arms control treaties, the law of armed conflict, and alliance rules as vehicles, it constrains adversaries' capability building, limits freedom of action, and constructs exclusionary security architectures — through leading rule formulation, unilateral modification, selective compliance, or malicious withdrawal from treaties — using "rules deterrence (规则威慑)" to supplement and even partially replace traditional military deterrence. This form of warfare directly concerns core national security interests and military sovereignty. It is the form of rules warfare with the greatest strategic deterrent power, and its competition outcomes profoundly affect global strategic stability and the international security landscape.

The Internal Mechanisms of Rules Warfare

Compared with the victory logic of traditional hot war, which relies on physical force, rules warfare is a higher-order competition at the systemic, institutional, and cognitive levels. Victory or defeat depends on the capacity to control the source (源头制权能力), lead standards, employ institutions, build systems, shape discourse, and reconstruct order.

Rules domain-locking (规则锁域): achieving dimensional suppression through standards definition. The most fundamental victory logic of rules warfare lies in the right to define standards. The dominant party typically reconstructs the competitive track and locks the dimensions of competition by setting access standards in technology, trade, security, compliance, and other domains, achieving "victory before battle." This is because standards possess extremely strong exclusivity and anchoring properties — once they become an internationally accepted paradigm, they form rigid thresholds. Technology patents, digital algorithms, economic and trade access, and the definition of security behavior are all, in essence, competition yardsticks. The party that holds the right to define standards, by unifying technology pathways, evaluation systems, and access thresholds, makes its own model the "sole legitimate paradigm," forcing the adversary to either passively adapt and be controlled, or be isolated and excluded. The right to define standards supersedes force superiority; it is the core dimensional-suppression victory logic of rules warfare.

Institutional regulation (制度规制): forming systemic constraints through multilateral mechanisms. The basic confrontation form of rules warfare is a contest of systemic momentum (体系势能). The key to victory lies in converting unilateral advantages into multilateral institutional advantages — by building alliance mechanisms, regional agreements, and industry organizations to construct an institutionalized community of interests and rules network, using systemic momentum to suppress single-point strength. The dominant party frequently packages its own demands as international public rules by controlling international organizations, revising multilateral conventions, and building regional rules systems, forming an institutionalized and legitimized constraining force. On one hand, it positively binds the majority of countries into its own system, forming a large-scale alliance; on the other hand, it uses institutional sanctions, compliance reviews, and other means to precisely restrict and isolate its competition adversaries. The inner core of this mechanism lies in solidifying advantages through institutions and dissolving the adversary through systems, converting short-term power differentials into long-term institutional differentials.

Discourse shaping (话语塑造): occupying the moral high ground through cognitive dominance. Rules warfare is a combination of legal competition and cognitive competition, and victory or defeat depends even more on the degree of recognition by the international community. Discourse power, interpretive authority, and the power to characterize constitute its soft victory core, directly determining the legitimacy and enforceability of rules. The implementation of international rules requires the support of value narratives and moral definitions. The dominant party has consistently monopolized the standards of right and wrong, good and evil, legal and illegal — by controlling public opinion, academic definitions, and international evaluation systems — shaping its own governance behavior as "compliant" and characterizing the adversary's legitimate development as "non-compliant," completing dimensional suppression at the cognitive level. The essence of this mechanism is cognitive primacy and legal victory (认知先行、法理制胜); narrative and legal dominance can long-term affect the adversary's international legitimacy and development space.

Systemic control (体系控局): building full-domain counterbalancing through rules linkage. Rules warfare exhibits characteristics of multi-layer nesting and full-domain linkage; the key to victory lies in building a rules matrix that links multiple domains — economic and trade, technology, finance, security, and data — to form a full-domain counterbalancing posture. Rules in each domain are mutually nested and mutually enabling: technology standards bind trade rules, financial rules sanction physical industries, security rules intervene in technological cooperation, and data rules reshape the industrial landscape. The dominant party readily relies on the rules system to form a closed-loop counterbalancing network, implementing cross-domain precision strikes against the adversary's weak points, achieving systemic constraint of "one violation, full-domain restriction (一点违规、全域受限)." Compared with traditional single-front military confrontation, this mechanism achieves an upgrade from single-point competition to full-domain lockdown, amplifying effectiveness through rules linkage.

Iterative victory (迭代制胜): opening new competitive tracks through rules innovation. Victory in rules warfare lies in continuously iterating rules and seizing rules in new domains. The technological revolution and changes in global governance have given rise to emerging governance-vacuum domains such as outer space, artificial intelligence, quantum information, biotechnology, and digital trade. These tracks are unburdened by old rules and unconstrained by traditional entrenched hegemony, and are incremental battlefields for rules competition. The weaker party must all the more rush to intervene in the governance of emerging domains, taking the lead in exporting new proposals, new rules, and new standards, building a new rules system that is autonomous and controllable, and gradually offsetting, replacing, and transforming the existing rules system. This mechanism embodies the characteristic of rules warfare's incremental breakthrough and iterative victory (增量破局、迭代制胜): holding firm and competing on existing tracks, seizing the initiative to shape the situation on incremental tracks, and grasping the initiative in hybrid warfare competition through continuous innovation, system upgrading, and paradigm renewal.

规则战：混合战争博弈的高阶形态 ■君 潭 编者按 当今世界，百年变局加速演进，国际秩序深度调整，大国博弈全面升级。在一些国家和军队看来，国际规则不仅是约束战争的底线，其本身已成为博弈的武器，规则战作为突破传统暴力范式与认知边界的新型高阶作战形态，已然登上历史舞台，成为塑造全球格局的重要变量。其本质上是新旧国际秩序转换进程中，全球利益分配体系、治理规则体系、大国竞争逻辑的系统性重构。掌握规则主导权，业已成为大国赢得长期战略竞争主动权的关键。 规则战是指某些国家或国家集团，以国际规则为武器，通过制定、利用、重塑、解构、规避、创设各类博弈规则，争夺制度主导权、法理合法性、道义制高点，进而限制对手自由、放大自身优势、降低己方成本的混合对抗形态。这种对抗覆盖经贸、科技、法律、网络、气候、军事、认知等多维领域，是国际格局演变、全球化深度调整、技术革命驱动、大国博弈逻辑转型与全球治理体系变革等多重因素交织作用的结果。 规则战的鲜明特征 规则战区别于传统以领土占领、资源掠夺、武力歼灭为目标的战争模式，是依托规则、标准、制度体系展开的非对称高阶对抗，深刻改变着国际战略博弈的制胜机理与竞争逻辑。 去暴力化的对决形态。规则战彻底摆脱传统战争对军事暴力的路径依赖，其以规则、标准、制度体系为博弈核心，通过制裁壁垒、合规审查、标准排他、体系脱钩、规则解释权垄断等柔性方式达成战略压制。同时，规则战具有鲜明的非对称性。霸权国家倚仗存量规则霸权施压，后发国家则可通过局部规则创新、新型标准创设、多边体系联合实现“弯道制衡”，形成“以制度破霸权、以新规改旧序”的格局，深刻重塑了现代战争的实力博弈逻辑。 全域渗透的角逐格局。规则战彻底打破时空与领域限制，实现全领域、全时段、全覆盖的常态化博弈。领域维度上，全面渗透至贸易金融、科技产业、数据网络、军备管控、全球治理等国家生存发展各领域。任何受国际规则或行业标准规制的领域，皆为规则战博弈场。时空维度上，博弈无战时平时之分、无前线后方之别，内嵌于日常国际合作、经贸往来、技术交流之中，呈现“平时即战时、处处即战场”的全域态势，具备极强的延展性。 合法隐蔽的较量手段。规则战的所有对抗行为皆依托国际公约、行业标准、合规体系及多边机制展开，以“合法合规”之名，行战略遏制之实，具备极强的道义包装与制度隐蔽性。发起方往往掌控既有规则的解释权、裁量权与执行权，将单边制裁、技术封锁、体系排斥、标准壁垒包装为“合规治理”“市场规则”“国际惯例”。此模式将一些霸权行径制度化、标准化、合法化，使被动方陷入“违规即受限、合规即受制”的两难困境，常规博弈手段难以破局。 长期渐进的对抗节奏。规则战是长周期、低烈度、常态化的渐进式博弈，无明确起止节点。规则体系的构建、修订、颠覆、重构乃长期过程；标准的落地、普及、迭代需时间沉淀；秩序更替、权力转移依赖长期博弈积累。规则竞争不会短期博弈定胜负，而是持续数年乃至更久的战略拉锯。博弈双方通过持续修订规则、迭代标准、构建新体系、瓦解旧秩序，逐步挤压对手发展空间、累积自身战略优势，最终实现秩序重构与权力更迭，呈现“潜移默化、久久为功”的长期对抗特征。 体系对抗的博弈模式。规则战是全方位、全流程、全体系的结构性对抗，比拼的是国家整体制度构建能力、标准引领能力、体系整合能力与国际动员能力。博弈主体已非单一军队或领域，而是国家乃至联盟的整套制度体系、标准体系、治理体系。博弈涵盖规则创设、标准输出、体系适配、多边绑定、风险对冲等全流程环节。强势方通过构建闭环排他性规则体系，实施全维度系统性遏制；弱势方的有效突破，亦须依托自主规则体系构建、多边伙伴体系联合、新型治理体系替代，实现体系级制衡，是典型的“体系制胜、秩序决胜”对抗模式。 规则主导的制胜内核。规则战重构制胜逻辑，规则定义权、行业标准权、国际话语权、秩序主导权成为核心制胜要素。在此范式下，军事硬实力为兜底保障。谁能率先定义新竞争规则、行业标准、价值范式，主导国际规则的修订、解释与执行，凝聚多边共识推广自身制度体系，谁就能掌控全球竞争底层逻辑。掌握规则定义权，即可设置行业门槛、划定利益边界、主导治理走向，实现“以规则约束对手、以标准掌控优势、以秩序维持主导地位”的战略目标。这是规则战区别于传统战争最本质的逻辑特征。 规则战的多元形态 伴随国际竞争重心向制度规则博弈转移，规则战深度渗透国家发展关键领域，已演变为平战一体、隐蔽渐进、跨域联动、长期博弈的全域性、系统性对抗。 体现在经济贸易领域：利益分配的前沿阵地。这是规则战中发起最频、波及最广、影响最直接的形态。其核心逻辑在于操纵、修改乃至滥用国际贸易、投资与金融规则，系统破坏目标国经贸生态，阻断产业链供应链现代化进程，实施深层次经济压制。个别大国常以“规则合规”之名行单边遏制之实，本质是对全球经贸治理主导权与价值链话语权的激烈争夺。这种高强度、常态化、全域化对抗，对全球经贸秩序稳定构成严峻挑战。 体现在科学技术领域：科技主导权的核心枢纽。这是无硝烟的科技规则主导权争夺战，乃规则战制胜关键。其核心在于掌控核心技术标准、行业规范、专利布局及认证体系，形成技术路径锁定效应，旨在压缩目标国自主创新与产业升级空间，掌控全球科技制高点。作战贯穿技术研发、专利布局、产品准入、行业规范、技术出口管控等全链条规则，力求以高标准壁垒达成战略压制之效。其科技含量高，成为规则战中最具根本性、长期性的形态。 体现在法律司法领域：规则博弈的精准利器。这种规则战隐蔽性强、精准度高、专业突出。其以国际法、国内法为“武器”，通过司法程序工具化、法理博弈复杂化、合规审查政治化，对目标国实体实施精准“软杀伤”。本质是将司法高度武器化，通过选择性适法、恶意操纵程序、滥用域外管辖权，突破主权原则实施跨国法律围剿，核心是争夺国际法解释权与司法权。凭借专业外衣，其战略遏制实质常被掩盖。 体现在网络数字领域：治理体系的新兴高地。伴随数字经济和网络空间成为战略竞争核心疆域，网络数字空间迅速崛起为新兴主战场。数据主权、网络安全、平台监管、算法伦理等是核心议题，目标是争夺全球网络与数字治理主导权。作战围绕数字主权界定、关键数据流动规则、网络空间国家行为规范、数字贸易新体系构建等展开，旨在以规则先行划定边界，掌控数字经济发展命脉。其直接关乎国家数字主权、发展空间与未来战略位势。 体现在意识形态领域：价值博弈的隐蔽战线。这是规则战争向认知域和价值观领域深度延伸的形态。其以特定价值理念包装，通过系统性意识形态渗透、国际舆论操纵、话语叙事塑造实施认知攻击，旨在削弱目标国的制度认同、道路自信与国际形象。本质是将人权等价值议题异化为政治工具，构建多重标准，通过垄断话语定义权、操控舆论审判、价值观输出，争夺国际道义制高点与意识形态主导权。此战能深刻侵蚀目标国舆论环境、社会共识与软实力根基，极具隐蔽性、迷惑性和长期性。 体现在军事安全领域：战略威慑的关键领域。这是规则战在传统安全领域的战略延伸，反映大国军事博弈向规则遏制的深化。围绕军控机制、军事行动准则、同盟条约体系、新兴领域安全规则等展开，核心是争夺军事安全领域的国际规则制定权与解释权，旨在限制他国军事力量发展、压缩他国战略安全空间。其以国际军事法、军控条约、武装冲突法、同盟规则为载体，通过主导规则制定、单方修改、选择性履约或恶意退约，约束对手能力建设、限制行动自由、构建排他性安全架构，以“规则威慑”补充甚至部分替代传统武力威慑。此战直接攸关国家核心安全利益与军事主权，是规则战中最具战略威慑力的形态，其博弈结果深刻影响全球战略稳定与国际安全格局。 规则战的内在机理 相较于传统热战依赖物理力量的制胜逻辑，规则战是体系层、制度层、认知层的高阶博弈。其胜负取决于源头制权能力、标准主导能力、制度运用能力、体系建构能力、话语塑造能力和秩序重塑能力。 规则锁域：以标准定义实现降维压制。规则战最根本的制胜逻辑在于标准定义权。主导方往往通过设定技术、贸易、安全、合规等领域的准入标准，重构竞争赛道、锁定博弈维度，实现“未战先胜”。这是因为标准具有极强的排他性和锚定性，一旦成为国际通用范式，便形成刚性门槛。技术专利、数字算法、经贸准入、安全行为界定等，本质皆是博弈标尺。掌握标准定义权的一方，通过统一技术路径、评价体系和准入门槛，使自身模式成为“唯一合法范式”，迫使对手要么被动适配受制，要么被隔离出局。标准定义权优于力量优势，是规则战核心的降维制胜逻辑。 制度规制：借多边机制形成体系约束。规则战的基本对抗形态是体系势能的较量。制胜的关键在于将单边优势转化为多边制度优势，通过搭建联盟机制、区域协定、行业组织，构建制度化的利益共同体与规则网络，以体系势能压制单点实力。主导方经常通过掌控国际组织、修订多边公约、构建区域规则体系，将自身诉求包装为国际公共规则，形成制度化、合法化的约束力量。一方面正向绑定多数国家融入己方体系，形成规模同盟；另一方面运用制度制裁、合规审查等手段，对博弈对手精准限制与孤立围堵。此机理内核在于以制度固化优势、以体系消解对手，将短期实力差转化为长期制度差。 话语塑造：凭认知主导占据道义高地。规则战是法理博弈与认知博弈的结合体，胜负更取决于国际社会的认同度。话语权、解释权、定性权构成其软性制胜内核，直接决定规则的合法性与执行力。国际规则落地生效需价值叙事、道义定义支撑。主导方一直通过掌控舆论、学术定义和国际评判体系，垄断是非、善恶、合法非法的标准，将自身治理行为塑造为“合规”，将对手正当发展定性为“违规”，完成认知层面的维度压制。此机理本质是认知先行、法理制胜，叙事与法理主导能长期影响对手国际正当性与发展空间。 体系控局：靠规则联动构建全域制衡。规则战呈现多层嵌套、全域联动特征，制胜关键在于构建经贸、技术、金融、安全、数据等多领域联动的规则矩阵，形成全域制衡格局。各领域规则相互嵌套赋能：技术标准绑定贸易规则，金融规则制裁实体产业，安全规则干预科技合作，数据规则重塑产业格局。主导方动辄依托规则体系，形成闭环制衡网络，针对对手短板实施跨领域精准打击，实现“一点违规、全域受限”的体系约束。相较于传统单一军事战线对抗，此机理实现了从单点博弈到全域锁控的升级，通过规则联动放大效能。 迭代制胜：用规则创新开辟新兴赛道。规则战的制胜，在于持续迭代规则、抢占新域规则。科技革命与全球治理变革催生太空、人工智能、量子信息、生物科技、数字贸易等新兴治理空白领域。这些赛道无旧规则束缚与传统霸权固化，是规则博弈的增量战场。弱势方更要抢先介入新兴领域治理，率先输出新方案、新规则、新标准，构建自主可控的新型规则体系，逐步对冲、替代、改造旧有规则体系。此机理体现了规则战增量破局、迭代制胜的特征：在存量赛道守正博弈，在增量赛道抢先塑势，通过持续创新、体系升级、范式革新，掌握混合战争博弈主动权。
