Blockbuster Online | "Forward · Forward"

大片上线丨《向前·向前》
PLA Daily (解放军报) 29 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
China Military Online has released a four-part converged-media film series (融媒体片) titled 'Forward · Forward,' timed to the 90th anniversary of the Long March victory and the PLA's 99th founding anniversary, profiling an Army tank driver from Huining, a Navy rigging sailor (帆缆兵) from the Chishui River area, an Air Force fuel soldier from Yudu, and a Rocket Force soldier from Wuqi — each hometown mapped to a Long March landmark. The series documents the PLA political work apparatus's current formula for ideological messaging to enlisted personnel: binding service-branch identity to Long March geography and framing routine technical roles — track maintenance, aircraft carrier deck work, fuel supply, missile operations — as continuations of revolutionary sacrifice under the 建军一百年奋斗目标 (centenary army-building goal). Its value is as a record of how official military media constructs the 'red gene' (红色基因) narrative for a multi-service audience, not as evidence of any operational or capability development.

"Forward · Forward | Tracks"

The 25,000-li Long March was built up from row upon row of footprints, each following the last. Zhu Guojie, who grew up in Huining, Gansu, has spent his life alongside three generations of armored vehicles, writing 22 years of military service through track marks 15 centimeters wide. Beneath the tracks lies that very same "long road" of years past — with no end point. Forward, forward. To commemorate the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Red Army's Long March, China Military Online launches the converged-media film "Forward · Forward | Tracks," focusing on an Army tank driver's path to victory in war.

"Forward · Forward | Rigging"

The "Flying Shark" yearns to fly higher, the formation yearns to sail farther, but the rigging sailor (帆缆兵) yearns for greater stability — so that those who soar to the clouds may laugh proudly over the open sky, and those who sail far may conquer the sea. He Jialiang, who grew up on the banks of the Chishui River, holds a line in his hands: one end connects to the aircraft carrier, the other connects to the motherland. To commemorate the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Red Army's Long March, China Military Online launches the converged-media film "Forward · Forward | Rigging," depicting the deep-blue wake that a Navy rigging sailor has tied fast with rope knots.

"Forward · Forward | Upholding the Sky"

Some say the fuel lines are the arteries of the war eagle. In the eyes of fuel soldier Zhu Yong, they are a river flowing toward the sky. He grew up nourished by the waters of the Yudu River. Now, he nourishes one war eagle after another with the fuel he guards. Every drop of fuel connects to the battlefield and extends the flight path. And that flight path — how could it be limited to ten thousand li? To commemorate the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Red Army's Long March, China Military Online launches the converged-media film "Forward · Forward | Upholding the Sky," to feel how an Air Force fuel soldier helps extend the map of combat power toward the blue expanse.

"Forward · Forward | Commanding the Sword"

Some roads are not persevered in because hope is seen — rather, hope comes from persevering. Wu Yufu, from Wuqi, Shaanxi, feels that the military academy is like another "Wuqi" — a place to set down roots, a place to build strength, a place to charge forward. Behind a missile patrolling the heavens is not the strength of one person alone, just as the Long March was not one person's trek. To commemorate the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Red Army's Long March, China Military Online launches the converged-media film "Forward · Forward | Commanding the Sword," to witness the silhouette of Rocket Force soldiers bearing aloft the great nation's most powerful weapons (大国重器).
