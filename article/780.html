About this analysis
Summary
PLA Daily's 2025 social responsibility report documents the outlet's full-year propaganda output as the CMC's official organ, including coverage of the Eastern Theater Command's 'Strait Thunder-2025A' exercises around Taiwan, the commissioning of the Fujian carrier (described as 'China's first electromagnetic catapult aircraft carrier'), and three types of carrier-based aircraft completing takeoff and landing training aboard the Fujian — all treated as routine thematic reporting rather than breaking news. The report records the institutional framing PLA Daily applies to each of these events: Taiwan coverage is categorized under 'public opinion countermeasures' (舆论对策) supporting 'political, diplomatic, and military struggles,' while the Fujian coverage appears under combat training propaganda, confirming how the outlet assigns editorial priority and narrative function to major military milestones. The self-identified shortcomings — insufficient timeliness in public opinion struggle, underdeveloped pre-produced content, and weak all-media literacy — provide a rare baseline for assessing where PLA media considers its influence operations to be underperforming.
The report confirms that three types of carrier-based aircraft completed takeoff and landing training on the Fujian (electromagnetic catapult carrier) and that the carrier was commissioned on November 8, 2025—the first public confirmation of the Fujian's commissioning date and multi-type carrier aviation qualification in PLA Daily's own institutional reporting.

PLA Daily was founded on January 1, 1956, and is the official organ of the Central Military Commission (CMC). Over the 70 years since its founding, PLA Daily has consistently upheld the Party's leadership, held high the Party's banner, actively publicized the Party's theories and line, principles, and policies, vividly reflected the construction, reform, and combat-readiness practices of the armed forces, and made important contributions to carrying forward the fine traditions of our Party and our military, shaping generation after generation of revolutionary soldiers, and supporting the growth and development of the People's Army. PLA Daily has a number of well-known brands and columns, including "Jiexinping," "Strong Military Forum" (强军论坛), and "Military Camp Observations" (军营观察). In recent years, PLA Daily Press has merged with other major military news media to form the PLA News and Communication Center, which operates 91 new-media publishing terminals including the "China Military" (中国军号) platform, reaching a total user base of 360 million. The pace of integrated media development has continued to accelerate.
