New and Veteran Soldiers Face to Face: Cultivating the Root and Forging the Soul (培根铸魂) Must Begin at the "Soldier's Beginning" (兵之初)

新老兵面对面，培根铸魂就要从“兵之初”开始
PLA Daily (解放军报) 1 June 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A new recruit training brigade of the Southern Theater Command Air Force ran a structured 'New and Veteran Soldiers Face to Face' session pairing incoming recruits with outstanding NCOs — including members of a nationally recognized 'March 8th Red Banner Collective,' border-island garrison troops, and competition-winning radar troopers — and screened a video testimony from a combat veteran whose family spans four generations of military service. The brigade simultaneously launched a formal two-way mentorship structure it calls 'veterans leading newcomers, newcomers advancing newcomers' (老带新、新促新), designating re-enlisting soldiers and recruit Party members as peer mentoring group leaders. This is standard recruit political-work content; its value is as a record of how the Southern Theater Command Air Force frames ideological conditioning (培根铸魂) and unit cohesion at the earliest stage of service, not as evidence of a changed training posture.
Translation

Cultivating the Root and Forging the Soul (培根铸魂), Starting from the "Soldier's Beginning" (兵之初)

— A Report on the "New and Veteran Soldiers Face to Face" Activity at a New Recruit Training Brigade of the Southern Theater Command Air Force

■ Li Yixuan, PLA Daily Reporter Chen Dianhong, Special Correspondent Xu Qiushi

On an early-summer afternoon, sunlight filtered through the glass windows and spread a layer of broken gold across the floor of the club hall of a new recruit training brigade of the Southern Theater Command Air Force. A group of new recruits and representatives of outstanding non-commissioned officers gathered together, their eyes fixed in unison on the screen ahead. This was a scene from the brigade's "New and Veteran Soldiers Face to Face" activity.

On the screen, a 78-year-old man sat wearing a faded old military uniform, the medals on his chest gleaming brightly. He was Zhu Fengming — a combat veteran and the grandfather of new recruit Deng Peibin.

Through the video footage, the reporter observed that on the wall behind the old man hung, in neat rows, military portrait photographs spanning four generations: Deng Peibin's great-grandfather was a veteran of the Railway Guerrilla Force; his grandfather had distinguished himself in combat on the battlefield; his father had served in a naval unit; and now he himself had put on an Air Force uniform — four generations in military service, the glorious tradition of defending the homeland passed down through a red bloodline.

"When I first enlisted, I was just like you — full of passion, with a single-minded devotion to serving the country." Zhu Fengming stroked a yellowed old photograph, his thoughts seemingly drifting back to those extraordinary years of artillery fire and gunsmoke.

"My old squad leader taught me to shoot hand-in-hand in the trenches, and patrolled and stood watch with me on the position. That day, while escorting combat supplies to the front line, he was shot and killed…" Before he had finished speaking, tears were already streaming down the old man's face. In front of the screen, everyone sat upright and listened in silence; many could not help but redden around the eyes.

"The skills to win on the battlefield must be passed down from generation to generation; the burden of defending the homeland must be shouldered by one generation after another." The old man's words of exhortation carried the weight of a thousand catties, landing with force.

After hearing Zhu Fengming recount his personal experiences, an exchange between the outstanding NCO representatives and the new recruits immediately got underway.

The brigade's leadership told the reporter that the outstanding NCO representatives participating in the activity included communications specialists from a nationally recognized "March 8th Red Banner Collective" (全国三八红旗集体), soldiers who had spent years garrisoning border islands at the edges of the motherland, "post-2000" radar troopers who are multi-skilled and have won gold and silver in major competitions, and outstanding "soldier-experts" (兵专家) who have spent years in the equipment field and overcome multiple technical difficulties.

As the exchange began, Liu Chengrui — a new recruit enlisting for the second time — was the first to raise his hand and speak: "During my first term of service, I was a highland cavalryman patrolling the border line on horseback; the conviction to dedicate myself to the nation was planted deep in my heart. After completing my studies following discharge, I chose to enlist a second time and join an Air Force unit. From the saddle to beneath a missile, the unfamiliar specialty has thrown me into a panic about my own capabilities (本领恐慌)."

"There are no shortcuts on the military path — every step must be taken steadily and solidly." Radar trooper Li Anzhi picked up the thread: "When I first enlisted, I couldn't even read a basic circuit diagram. My squad leader lent me his training notebook and taught me to break each operational procedure down into individual small knowledge points — starting from a single solder joint, accumulating bit by bit from zero."

As he spoke, Li Anzhi showed everyone his annotation-filled training notebook and said with firm conviction: "Settle your mind, work diligently, never stop moving forward — even the most ordinary post can make youth shine." The reporter observed that Liu Chengrui nodded repeatedly and solemnly wrote eight characters in his own notebook: "Start over from the beginning, keep both feet on the ground (从头再来，脚踏实地)."

Communications specialist Li Kaishan shared her story of striving for zero errors and zero mistakes during duty shifts. After hearing Li Kaishan's account, Deng Peibin could not help but voice the confusion in his heart: "Before I enlisted, my grandfather repeatedly urged me to keep in mind the mission and glory of four generations in military service. But after putting on the uniform, I discovered that being a qualified soldier takes far more than enthusiasm. Your work is meticulous and tedious — how do you manage zero errors and zero mistakes?"

"When I first enlisted, memorizing call signs and practicing pronunciation and finger technique required countless repetitions, yet my scores still wouldn't improve. During that period, I cried out of frustration more than once. When I wanted to give up, my squad leader told me: the communications console may be small, but it connects to thousands upon thousands of troops; the call signs may be many, but every character bears on winning (字字关乎打赢)." Speaking of her own growth, Li Kaishan said with firm conviction: "Throughout my years of service, I have always kept my squad leader's words in mind and have successfully completed more than a hundred major exercise and training communications support missions. This perseverance does not belong to me alone — it has been passed down by one generation of communications soldiers after another."

Equipment technical backbone Tian Lian recounted his experience of improvising with available materials to keep equipment running normally during a major mission; launch technician Zhang Zihao, who has been stationed year-round on a remote border island, recalled the process of inspecting and reinforcing equipment together with his comrades before a powerful typhoon struck; communications technician Zhou Wei spoke of the experience of crawling under a vehicle to carry out repairs in the middle of a sandstorm during an air-ground confrontation training exercise… Different posts, the same mission — the stories told by the veteran soldiers moved every new recruit in the audience deeply.

"To help new recruits button the first button of their military journey (系好军旅第一粒扣子), we must start from the 'soldier's beginning' (兵之初) and inject sufficient spiritual nourishment into them." The brigade's leadership told the reporter that by organizing new and veteran soldiers to listen together to the stories of their predecessors and jointly receive education in original aspirations (初心教育), and by encouraging new and veteran soldiers to share their reflections on growth and speak freely about their experiences, the aim is to ensure that new recruits, from the very moment they enter the barracks, can see role models and find a clear direction, and take every step of their military journey in a solid and grounded manner.

At the activity, the brigade also launched a two-way mentorship mechanism of "veterans leading newcomers, newcomers advancing newcomers" (老带新、新促新): new recruits are matched with dedicated mentoring backbone personnel, while new recruit Party members and recruits enlisting for the second time are selected to serve as mentoring group leaders, leveraging their advantages of familiarity with barracks life and solid foundations, in an effort to form a virtuous cycle of "veterans leading backbone personnel, backbone personnel leading new recruits, and the advanced leading all personnel" (老兵带骨干、骨干带新兵、先进带全员).

As the activity drew to a close, new and veteran soldiers all rose to their feet and exchanged military salutes. Outside the windows, sunlight bathed the training ground. Carrying with them the hopes and exhortations of the veteran soldiers, the new recruits marched with firm steps toward their respective posts.

Original Chinese
培根铸魂，从“兵之初”开始 ——南部战区空军某新训旅开展“新老兵面对面”活动见闻 ■李艺轩 解放军报记者 陈典宏 特约记者 徐秋实 初夏午后，阳光透过玻璃窗，在南部战区空军某新训旅俱乐部地板上铺下一层碎金。一群新兵与优秀军士代表齐聚一堂，目光不约而同地注视着前方屏幕。这是该旅“新老兵面对面”活动现场的一幕。 屏幕上，一位78岁的老人身着洗得泛白的老军装，胸前佩戴的奖章熠熠生辉。他是新兵邓培斌的爷爷、参战老兵朱凤鸣。 记者从视频画面中看到，老人身后的墙上，整整齐齐挂着四代人的军装照：邓培斌的太爷爷是铁道游击队老兵，爷爷曾在战场上荣立战功，父亲曾在海军部队服役，如今他也穿上了空军军装——四代从军，保家卫国的光荣传统在红色血脉中赓续传承。 “我刚入伍时，跟你们一样，一腔热血，一心报国。”朱凤鸣老人摩挲着一张泛黄的老照片，思绪仿佛回到了那段炮火硝烟的峥嵘岁月。 “我的老班长，在战壕里手把手教我射击，在阵地上和我一起巡逻执勤。那天，为了护送作战物资运往前线，他不幸中弹牺牲……”话音未落，老人已潸然泪下。屏幕前，大家端坐静听，不少人都忍不住红了眼眶。 “战场打赢的本领要一代代传，保家卫国的担子要一辈辈挑。”老人的嘱托字字千钧，掷地有声。 听朱凤鸣老人讲完亲身经历后，优秀军士代表与新兵们的交流随即展开。 该旅领导告诉记者，现场参加活动的优秀军士代表，既有来自全国三八红旗集体的通信岗位能手，也有常年扎根祖国边陲海岛的戍边战士；既有一专多能、在大项比武中摘金夺银的“00后”雷达尖兵，也有深耕装备领域多年、攻克多项技术难题的优秀“兵专家”。 交流一开始，二次入伍新兵刘程瑞率先举手发言：“第一次服役期间，我是一名在边防线上策马巡逻的高原骑兵，以身许国的信念深植心中。退伍完成学业后，我选择二次入伍来到空军部队。从马背上到导弹下，陌生的专业让我陷入了本领恐慌。” “军旅路上没有捷径，每一步都要走稳走实。”雷达尖兵李安智接过话茬，“我刚入伍时，连基础电路图都看不懂，班长将训练笔记借给我，教我把一项项操作规程拆解成一个个小知识点，从一个焊点练起，从零开始点滴积累。” 说话间，李安智给大家展示自己写满批注的训练笔记，语气坚定地说：“沉下心、踏实干，前进的脚步不停歇，再平凡的岗位也能绽放青春光彩。”记者看到，刘程瑞连连点头，在自己的笔记本上郑重写下8个字：“从头再来，脚踏实地。” 通信专业能手李凯珊给大家分享了自己值班零差错、零失误的奋斗故事。听完李凯珊的讲述，邓培斌忍不住道出心里的困惑：“入伍前，爷爷一再叮嘱我，牢记四代从军的使命和荣光。可穿上军装才发现，当一名合格的军人，光有热情远远不够。你的工作繁琐枯燥，是怎么做到零差错、零失误的？” “刚入伍时，背记号码、发音指法都要反复练习无数遍，可成绩就是上不去。那段时间，我不止一次急得掉眼泪。想要放弃的时候，班长告诉我：机台虽小，连着千军万马；号码虽多，字字关乎打赢。”谈及自己的成长经历，李凯珊语气坚定地说，“当兵这几年，我一直牢记班长这句话，出色完成百余次重大演训保通任务。这份坚守不是我一个人的，而是一代代通信兵接续传承下来的。” 装备技术骨干田练讲述自己在一次重大任务中，就地取材保障装备正常运转的经历；常年驻守边陲海岛的发射技师张子皓回忆起强台风来临前，与战友一起检查加固装备的历程；通信技师周伟谈及一次空地对抗训练中，顶着沙暴钻进车底检修的体会……不同的战位，相同的使命，老兵们讲述的一个个故事，深深打动着台下的每一名新兵。 “帮助新兵系好军旅第一粒扣子，就要从‘兵之初’开始，为他们注入充足的精神养分。”该旅领导告诉记者，他们组织新老兵一起聆听前辈故事、共同接受初心教育，鼓励新老兵分享成长感悟、畅谈心得体会，就是要让新兵从入营之初就看得见榜样、找得准方向，扎扎实实走好军旅每一步。 活动现场，该旅还启动“老带新、新促新”双向帮带机制：为新兵匹配专属帮带骨干，同时遴选新兵党员、二次入伍新兵担任帮带小组长，发挥他们熟悉军营、基础扎实的优势，努力形成“老兵带骨干、骨干带新兵、先进带全员”的良性循环。 活动接近尾声，新老兵全体起立，互敬军礼。窗外，阳光洒满训练场。带着老兵的期望和嘱托，新兵们迈着坚定的步伐，走向各自战位。
