The Finest Revolutionary Soldiers of the New Era | Academy of Military Sciences Researcher Chang Chao: Boldly Venturing into the "No-Man's Land" of Scientific Research

最美新时代革命军人风采丨军事科学院研究员常超：勇闯科研“无人区”
PLA Daily (解放军报) 10 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Chang Chao, a researcher at the Academy of Military Sciences' Defense Science and Technology Innovation Research Institute, left a Stanford postdoctoral position in 2013 to join a PLA research base in western China, then in 2018 voluntarily shifted from high-power microwave research to terahertz science — standing up the institute's first optical ultra-clean room laboratory within six months. The article is a profile in the PLA's 'finest revolutionary soldiers of the new era' series; its value is as a record of how AMS frames individual sacrifice and willingness to sit on the 冷板凳 ('cold bench,' meaning unglamorous long-term work) as the institutional ideal for defense researchers. The terahertz focus is substantive — the technology has directed-energy and sensing applications — but the article documents the messaging posture, not the research results.
Translation

Night has fallen, the lights burn low. In a laboratory of the Defense Science and Technology Innovation Research Institute under the Academy of Military Sciences, researcher Chang Chao is bent over his equipment making adjustments, his eyes fixed on the oscillating curves of an oscilloscope. He occasionally raises his head to think, then buries himself in the work again, as if engaged in a silent contest with some problem in physics.

In 2018, Chang Chao was transferred to the newly established Defense Science and Technology Innovation Research Institute. At that time, his research direction shifted from high-power microwave—a field he had cultivated for many years—to terahertz science, which was still in its early stages, and he faced numerous difficulties. A colleague asked him: "Switching tracks and starting over from scratch—is it worth it?" His answer was resolute: "Seizing the commanding heights of terahertz technology concerns national security. Someone always has to be the one to blaze the trail."

During that period, after reporting to his superiors, he and his colleagues converted an idle room in the research institute into a stronghold for tackling key problems. He led several young researchers, setting up the space while simultaneously carrying out research. Six months later, the institute's first optical ultra-clean room laboratory began operations. Thereafter, Chang Chao and his team worked together to tackle major national research projects; their findings were published in top international journals and earned recognition from the field. These "pioneers" fought day and night, overcame multiple difficult problems, and blazed a new path through this "no-man's land."

"One must pursue first-rate endeavors and be the backbone of China." At a critical crossroads in his life, Chang Chao chose to dedicate himself to his country.

When he completed his postdoctoral fellowship at Stanford University in 2013, a well-compensated faculty position was laid before him. He left behind the words "wherever the motherland needs me is the finest stage for one's life," and resolutely returned to China to take up a post at a research institute at a base in the western region.

In 2018, having already made a name for himself in his original research field, he voluntarily "reset to zero" and moved into the field of terahertz science research. Colleagues jokingly called him "Chang Chao—truly superhuman (非常超人)." He waved it off: "There's nothing superhuman about it. I'm simply willing to sit on the 'cold bench' (冷板凳)."

Being willing to sit on the "cold bench" (冷板凳) had long since become the norm for Chang Chao in his studies and research. When pursuing his doctorate at Tsinghua University, classrooms and laboratories occupied nearly his entire life; during his daily commute, the bus became his "mobile thinking pod"; even at rest, he never stopped thinking—whenever inspiration struck, he would immediately write it down. Many important ideas published in certain research journals were born in precisely these moments of "flashing inspiration."

On the bookshelf in Chang Chao's office, a stack of cutting-edge terahertz literature stands in a row. To outsiders, he is an expert, a regular presence in top-tier journals. But to Chang Chao himself, scientific research is simply day after day of bending over a desk, experimenting, and thinking. In the future, he will continue to work with quiet dedication, pressing forward without pause toward the high ground of independent innovation in defense science and technology.

Original Chinese
夜幕低垂，灯光微亮。军事科学院国防科技创新研究院某实验室里，研究员常超正俯身调试设备，目光紧盯示波器上跳动的曲线。他偶尔抬头凝神思索，又再度埋头攻关，像在与某道物理难题进行无声较量。 2018年，常超调入新组建的国防科技创新研究院。彼时，他的研究方向从深耕多年的高功率微波，转向尚在起步阶段的太赫兹科学，面临诸多困难。同事问他：“换个赛道从头再来，值吗？”他态度坚定：“抢占太赫兹制高点，关乎国家安全，总要有人蹚路先行。” 那段时间，他请示上级后，和同事们一起把研究院一处闲置房间改造成攻坚阵地。他带领几名年轻科研工作者，一边设置场地，一边开展研究。半年后，全院首个光学超净间实验室开始运行。此后，常超和团队一起攻关国家重要科研项目，研究成果登上国际顶级期刊，赢得业界认可。这群“拓荒者”日夜鏖战，攻克多个难题，在这片“无人区”蹚出一条新路。 “要做第一等的事业，要做中国的脊梁。”在人生的关键岔路口，常超选择以身许国。 2013年斯坦福大学博士后出站时，一份待遇优厚的教职岗位摆在眼前，他留下一句“祖国需要的地方，就是最好的人生舞台”，毅然回国奔赴西部某基地研究所。 2018年，已在原研究领域有所建树的他，主动“归零”，转入太赫兹科学研究领域。同事笑称“常超，非常超人”。他摆摆手：“哪有什么超人，不过是肯坐‘冷板凳’罢了。” 甘坐“冷板凳”，早已成为常超学习和科研的常态。在清华大学读博士时，课堂和实验室几乎占满他的全部生活；每天通勤路上，公交车成了他的“移动思考舱”；休息时，他也没有停止思考，一旦迸发灵感，便立即记录下来。不少发表在某科研刊物上的重要构想，就诞生于这种“灵感闪现”的时刻。 常超的办公室书柜里，一摞太赫兹前沿文献并排而立。在外人看来，他是专家、是顶级期刊的“常客”。可对常超而言，科研就是日复一日地伏案、实验、思考。未来，他会继续潜心耕耘，向着国防科技自主创新高地不断冲锋。
