Summary
PLA Daily reporter Feng Yunhe profiles founding general Chen Geng through interviews with his daughter Chen Zhijin, visits to his former residences in Beijing and Xiangxiang, and reporting from the National University of Defense Technology (NUDT), where Chen Geng established the Military Engineering Institute in 1952. The piece is standard political-work content in the PLA's 'roots journey' (寻根之旅) genre — its value is as a record of how the institution frames command excellence, selflessness, and willingness to fight (打仗) for the current officer corps, not as evidence of a new policy line. The closing 'Reporter's Notes' section makes the contemporary targeting explicit, addressing 'great-power battle commanders of the new era' and linking Chen Geng's example directly to studying and preparing for war amid 'profound transformation in the form of warfare.'

Tracing the footsteps of Founding General Chen Geng, reflecting on the way of command for senior commanders——

"You Have Always Been on the Road"

■ PLA Daily Reporter Feng Yunhe

"You have always been on the road… how I wish I could become like you…"

In the blazing summer heat, at Chen Geng Square of the National University of Defense Technology, cadets about to graduate sang this song together as they posed for photographs with the statue of General Chen Geng.

The statue stands tall and imposing, the general striding forward. The cadets said: this is a portrait of General Chen Geng's entire life — he was always rushing about with passion, until the very last moment of his life.

Today, 65 years have passed since General Chen Geng's death. What kind of man was this founding general, who time and again turned the tide in the revolutionary years and struck terror into the enemy? What unique qualities did he possess? What is it that makes military academy students today still think of him so often, and long to become him?

Recently, the Military and Political Basic Education College of the National University of Defense Technology organized a themed Party Day activity at Chen Geng Square. Photo by Bu Cancan.

Disabled Legs That Traversed Ten Thousand Mountains and Rivers

On the morning of June 3, the reporter visited Chen Geng's daughter, Chen Zhijin, at her home.

The moment the door opened, the reporter's heart gave a start — in a trance, it was as if Chen Geng himself stood there. The elderly woman, past seventy and slight of frame, had eyes strikingly like her father's, full of life and spirit.

"Our understanding of our father deepened step by step," she said. Chen Geng had died when Chen Zhijin was only eleven, and she had not yet been able to comprehend her father's magnificent life. She said: "Father rarely spoke to us about his wartime experiences. The one he recounted in some detail was the story of rescuing Chiang Kai-shek during the Eastern Expedition, and Chiang Kai-shek's later attempt to persuade him to surrender."

That was October 1925, during the Second Eastern Expedition of the National Revolutionary Army. In one battle, Commander-in-Chief Chiang Kai-shek was surrounded by the enemy. Chen Geng, who had graduated from the Whampoa Military Academy and was serving as a company commander, carried Chiang Kai-shek — whose legs had gone weak — out of danger on his back.

Chiang Kai-shek held Chen Geng in high regard and transferred him to serve as an aide-de-camp: following close behind him whenever he went out, free to enter and leave his quarters at will. Many people said: "Chen Geng is about to soar to great heights." Unexpectedly, before long, Chen Geng submitted a letter of resignation to Chiang Kai-shek.

"Father was a man of clear loves and hates. He once spoke to us with contempt about the reactionary nature of Chiang Kai-shek that he had seen through, and he bitterly denounced the traitor Gu Shunzhang, who had sold his soul," said Chen Zhijin.

Years later, when Chen Geng saw Chiang Kai-shek again, he had gone from honored guest to prisoner. In 1933, betrayed by a traitor, Chen Geng was unfortunately arrested. The enemy escorted him to Nanchang, where Chiang Kai-shek personally appeared to persuade him to surrender, offering him the titles of "Front Commander-in-Chief" and "Nanjing Garrison Commander." Chen Geng stood firm with righteous indignation and furiously rebuked Chiang Kai-shek: "I will absolutely never serve as an official under you. If you want to beat me, beat me; if you want to kill me, kill me!"

There is a memory that has never left Chen Zhijin's mind: her father once arranged for his children to visit the ruins of the prison where he had been held captive. The forbidding, cold dungeons and water cells were bone-chilling. The story of their father singing "The Internationale" again and again in prison educated and influenced them for the rest of their lives.

On July 1, the reporter accompanied Chen Zhijin to Chen Geng's former residence in Beijing — No. 41 Lingjing Hutong. After so many years, traces of Chen Geng's life there are difficult to find. Leafing through a thick old photo album, the reporter came across a little-known photograph. In the photograph were Chen Geng's legs, covered in the wounds of war — the middle of the left tibia protruded, the muscles twisted and deformed; on the outer side of the leg were three scars, the marks left by bullets that had passed through the bone.

In 1927, after Chen Geng participated in the Nanchang Uprising and marched south with the troops, his left leg was struck by bullets three times. Blood poured out and he could not move. Watching the enemy close in step by step, he had the presence of mind to strip off his military uniform, hold his breath, and feign death — and so narrowly escaped.

On May 22, 1949, Chen Geng led his forces to liberate Nanchang. Walking into her father's old study, Chen Zhijin took a stack of "battlefield diaries" from a suitcase. The reporter opened one of them — a black hardcover volume marked "1949" — and learned of Chen Geng's state of mind that day: "This time I come here in the posture of a victor. The three previous times I entered this city, I was either a fugitive, or a prisoner, or unable to hold my ground… Without those three previous times, there would be no today's glory of the people."

Over decades of military life, Chen Geng dragged his disabled legs along the long road of the Long March, fighting from the Land Revolution War all the way through the War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea. Were the trials he endured limited to those "three previous times"?

"I can hardly imagine how he made it through. Father was simply that kind of pure man — from the very beginning he gave everything he had to the revolutionary cause, and long since transcended himself…" said Chen Zhijin.

Battle-Hardened Steps That Crossed the Threshold of Victory

In the television drama "Bright Sword," there is a scene in which the enraged Japanese forces write in Chinese on an armored vehicle: "Specifically targeting the 386th Brigade's Independent Regiment."

This was not fiction. In battles such as Shentouling, Xiangtangpu, and the Qinyuan Campaign, the 386th Brigade of the Eighth Route Army's 129th Division always struck by surprise and defeated larger forces with smaller ones, cumulatively annihilating tens of thousands of enemy troops, making the Japanese forces both hate and fear them.

The commander of the 386th Brigade who directed this series of battles was none other than Chen Geng.

During the Long March, the "Cadre Regiment" he led was the "vanguard blade" that fought the hardest battles; during the War of Resistance, the 386th Brigade and Taiyue Column he led were the "blade behind enemy lines" that shook all of North China; during the War of Liberation, the "Chen-Xie Corps" he led was the "mobile iron fist" that decided the outcome in Central China… Why was it that no matter what combat mission they were assigned, the forces under Chen Geng's command always won resounding fame?

The reporter, carrying this question, made a special visit to Chen Geng's former residence in Xiangxiang, Hunan. The biographical exhibition hall inside the residence silently tells the story of Chen Geng's outstanding military command abilities.

In the special exhibition area on "Mastery of Ambush Warfare," the reporter noticed a comment made in January 1938 by Evans Carlson, counselor of the U.S. Embassy in China: the 386th Brigade was "the best brigade in China."

By coincidence, the Nationalist Army also had a unit that styled itself "the Number One Brigade Under Heaven." More coincidentally, before reorganization that brigade had been the 1st Division, and its former commander Hu Zongnan was Chen Geng's old classmate from the Whampoa Military Academy.

In 1946, Hu Zongnan led the "Number One Brigade Under Heaven" and other elite forces to attack the Taiyue Liberated Area, intending to settle accounts with his old classmate. The outcome left him utterly humiliated — the "crack force" he was so proud of was annihilated to the last man.

The "Number One Brigade Under Heaven" was no pushover. The brigade was entirely equipped with American weapons, had many veteran soldiers, possessed strong combat power, and its commanders generally held ranks one grade higher: Brigade Commander Huang Zhengcheng was a lieutenant general, and the two regimental commanders under him were both major generals.

War is also a contest of commanders. Shi Huaibi, who served as deputy commander of the temporary rear-area command during this campaign, once wrote a memoir article titled "The Art of Chen Geng's Command in Battle," in which he wrote: "He dispatched many capable and reliable scouts to ascertain the enemy's route of march and supply trains in detail… General Chen Geng calculated that the enemy would encamp at Dayang on a certain day… and made specific deployments for each regiment as to from which direction and along which road they would carry out the encirclement."

Superior command art is always built upon thorough preparation for war. Looking at the Nationalist Army by contrast, the captured Huang Zhengcheng still could not understand: "Your way of fighting is very strange. By rights you should have struck one of the two flanks; our 1st Brigade was in the middle, yet you struck us…"

A general without courage leaves the three armies without sharpness; a general without strategy brings the three armies to utter ruin.

After the battle, when Chen Geng's subordinate Zhou Xihan came face to face with Huang Zhengcheng, Huang Zhengcheng insisted on seeing Chen Geng in person. Zhou Xihan immediately replied: "Why use a butcher's cleaver to kill a chicken? To capture you, I, Zhou Xihan, am more than enough!" This battle commander, who had followed Chen Geng in battle from the time he served as operations section chief of the 386th Brigade, killed or captured 61 enemy generals over the course of his life.

The reporter contacted Shi Lailiang, the combat hero who had captured Huang Zhengcheng alive. He had fought under Zhou Xihan's command and is now 99 years old, living in a military rest home in Nanchang, Jiangxi. Speaking of Chen Geng and Zhou Xihan, the old man said with admiration: "There are no weak soldiers under a strong general. Not a single person trained by Commander Chen was wishy-washy — they were all fierce commanders. Back then, I was just a soldier and didn't understand tactics, but following them into battle, I felt very confident!"

In that campaign, the three main brigade commanders Chen Geng dispatched were Zhou Xihan, Li Chengfang, and Chen Kang — all battle commanders who had fought alongside Chen Geng for many years, and all three were awarded the rank of lieutenant general in 1955.

Each of them carried Chen Geng's imprint. Behind Chen Geng stood a constellation of shining stars.

Chiang Kai-shek once asked his subordinates: both sides came from Whampoa — why was there not a single Nationalist who could defeat Chen Geng? His generals fell collectively silent.

One good general is worth more than a million brave soldiers. Chen Zhijin said she had heard that senior Nationalist general Yu Hanmou, on his deathbed in Taiwan, said: "The reason we lost is that men like Chen Geng all went to the Communist Party."

A Burning Heart That Forged a Mighty Formation

On the banks of the Xiang River, summer heat steams and rises; the surging river rushes eastward.

The reporter came to the National University of Defense Technology, feeling as if touching the strong, beating pulse of cutting-edge national defense science and technology.

During the visit, the reporter's gaze was drawn to a set of satellite equipment. On the equipment, a household tape measure with a slightly worn surface was being used as an antenna, looking entirely out of place amid the surrounding precision high-end instruments.

This is the actual object of "Tiantu-1," the world's first single-board nano-satellite, independently developed by the university. Zhao Yong, who served as chief designer at the time, explained: "Traditional antennas are expensive. Through repeated verification, we found that the tape measure material fully met the requirements, saving hundreds of thousands of yuan in development costs."

After interviewing multiple faculty and students at the university, the reporter felt that "daring to walk roads no one has walked before, daring to think thoughts no one has thought before" seemed to be a quality they all shared. Luo Yougan, head of the university's history museum, said: "When General Chen Geng founded this school, he set the example for us of daring to venture into 'uncharted territory' (无人区)."

"Starting a new undertaking is difficult, but everything in the revolution is built from nothing… If I cannot run the Military Engineering Institute well, I swear I will not be fit to call myself a man!" This oath, sworn by Chen Geng in 1952 when he was ordered to establish the Military Engineering Institute (known as "Harbin Military Engineering Institute" or "Ha Junggong"), is now inscribed in the "Chen Geng's School Administration" exhibition area of the university's history museum.

No school buildings, no teaching materials, no faculty, no equipment… Chen Geng led faculty and students to overcome these difficulties one by one — in just one year, "Ha Junggong" rose from the ground.

In the years away from the smoke of battle, those disabled legs still did not rest, and that heart — made fragile by electric torture — still beat with fierce and ardent passion.

In December 1957, Chen Geng suffered a massive myocardial infarction and fell into a coma; the first words he spoke upon regaining consciousness were to ask about the matter of training personnel for the "Two Bombs" program. Two years later, Chen Geng suffered a second myocardial infarction, yet still constantly kept the cause of cutting-edge national defense on his mind. Chen Zhijin said that because her father's chest was often in pain, he would constantly rub his chest with his hand while working; over time, a patch of his shirt was worn through.

In the National University of Defense Technology's history museum, the reporter saw a photocopy of the "Report on Adjusting the Mission of the Military Engineering Institute" submitted by Chen Geng to the Central Military Commission. On that occasion, he put forward the concept of "concentrating on cutting-edge [programs], dispersing conventional [programs]" (尖端集中、常规分散) — separating some specializations and faculty from "Ha Junggong" to establish several new military academies, in order to meet the growing demand for national defense science and technology personnel; the restructured "Ha Junggong" would focus primarily on cultivating talent for cutting-edge technology research and design, with emphasis on providing personnel support for the "Two Bombs" program.

The reporter noted that this report was submitted in November 1959 — less than six months after Chen Geng had been discharged from the hospital…

Entering the School of Aerospace Science at the National University of Defense Technology, the reporter visited the "Talent Forest Phenomenon" exhibition hall. This school developed from the Missile Engineering Department established in 1959 and has to date produced more than ten academicians. Many of the science and technology innovation achievements on display in the hall have reached world-leading levels.

"Today, the original five departments of 'Ha Junggong' have taken root and flourished across the country, becoming the 'roots' of many military and civilian institutions. Most of the university's current colleges developed from the newly established specialized departments of that era, and they continue to this day to supply research personnel for key fields such as aerospace and electronic countermeasures," said Luo Yougan with deep feeling. "How far the general could see!"

Those deep, penetrating eyes saw far into today, and seemed also to see the end of life. In Chen Geng's former residence, beneath a final family photograph of Chen Geng with his family, these words are printed: "My time may not be long. Let me do more work. Tell the Central Health Bureau not to keep too tight a watch over me!"

On March 15, 1961, Chen Geng's condition flared again and he collapsed to the ground. Just the day before, despite hands and feet that were ice-cold, he had been bent over his desk writing a "Summary of Combat Experience," having just completed the "Preface" section: "In modern warfare, firepower has developed; new weapons such as atomic bombs and missiles have great destructive power… Our national defense industry and science and technology are not yet very advanced, cutting-edge breakthroughs have not yet been achieved, and the air defense system to counter missiles has not yet been resolved…"

The next day, Chen Geng passed away, at only 58 years of age. This shining star had released all of its energy for the revolutionary cause.

"Ci Yungui," "Song Wencong," "Lin Junde"… Today, around Chen Geng Square at the National University of Defense Technology, sculptures bearing the names of dozens of academicians stand in silent solemnity, like a mighty and imposing formation.

In the clear sunlight, a group of cadets walks toward the teaching building. The general's gaze rests upon them, as if conveying the charge of those years: "Great hopes are placed in you…"

(This interview received strong support from Zhang Zhaoxing, Zhang Chao, Liu Zhuo, Shou Baoshun, and Yu Lingping.)

Reporter's Notes

Because He Was Selfless, He Is Immortal

Marshal Chen Yi once said of Chen Geng: "Chen Geng is like a glass cup — clear and transparent from inside to outside." "Chen Geng is a magnet — witty and humorous, able to unite the great majority of people in revolution together." "Chen Geng is a cannon of our Party. It is a pity that we now have fewer such cannons; I hope there will be more like him"… During the reporter's interview, Chen Zhijin said: "To achieve these three things, one must first be selfless."

Because he was selfless, he is immortal. Selflessness is an outstanding quality of Communist Party members, and also an indispensable quality of a great-power battle commander. Only through selflessness can one face with pure conviction the trials of life and death, the trials of battle, and the trials of self-interest; only through selflessness can one pour one's lifelong passion into the journey of building a strong nation and a powerful military.

A thousand-weight burden on one's shoulders, a thousand armies and ten thousand soldiers at one's back. In today's era of profound transformation in the form of warfare, great-power battle commanders of the new era should carry a strong sense of historical mission and responsibility — studying war with meticulous care, preparing for war with single-minded focus, and throwing themselves into the battlefield with wholehearted ardor. This is the image that revolutionary forebears left for history, and it is also the image that great-power battle commanders of the new era should embody.

记者追寻开国大将陈赓的足迹，感悟高级指挥员为将之道—— “你一直在路上” ■解放军报记者 奉云鹤 “你一直在路上……我多想变得和你一样……” 炎炎夏日，国防科技大学陈赓广场，即将毕业的学员们在与陈赓大将雕像合影留念时，一齐唱起这首歌。 雕像巍然矗立，将军阔步前行。学员们说：这是陈赓大将一生的写照——他始终热情地奔忙着，直至生命最后一刻。 如今，距离陈赓大将逝世已过去65年了。这位在革命岁月数次力挽狂澜、令敌闻风丧胆的开国大将，究竟是一个什么样的人？有着怎样独特的品质？是什么原因让今天的军校学子仍时常想起他、渴望成为他？ 近日，国防科技大学军政基础教育学院在陈赓广场组织主题党日活动。补璨灿 摄 伤残的双腿，走过万水千山 6月3日清晨，记者登门拜访陈赓之女陈知进。 门开的瞬间，记者心头一颤，恍惚间竟似见到陈赓大将本人——年逾古稀、身形消瘦的老人，那双眼睛酷似父亲，神采奕奕。 “我们对父亲的认识是一步一步加深的。”陈赓逝世时，陈知进才11岁，还读不懂父亲波澜壮阔的一生。她说：“父亲很少和我们讲战争经历，讲得比较详细的，是东征时救蒋介石和后来蒋介石劝降他的那段往事。” 那是1925年10月，国民革命军第二次东征。一次战役中，总指挥蒋介石遭敌包围。黄埔军校毕业后担任连长的陈赓，背着双腿发软的蒋介石脱离了险境。 蒋介石对陈赓甚是欣赏，将他调作侍从参谋：出门紧随其后，住所随意出入，很多人都说：“陈赓就要飞黄腾达了。”令人意想不到的是，没过多久，陈赓却向蒋介石递交了“辞职书”。 “父亲是一个爱憎分明的人。他曾用轻蔑的口气，给我们讲他所识破的蒋介石反动本质，还痛骂过出卖灵魂的叛徒顾顺章。”陈知进说。 多年后再见蒋介石，陈赓从“座上客”变成了“阶下囚”。1933年，因叛徒出卖，陈赓不幸被捕。敌人将他押解到南昌，蒋介石亲自出面劝降，许以“前敌总指挥”和“南京卫戍司令”的官衔。陈赓大义凛然，怒斥蒋介石：“我决不做你们的官。要打就打，要杀就杀吧！” 陈知进的脑海里，始终有一段挥之不去的记忆：父亲曾安排子女们去过他当年遭受囚禁的监狱旧址。森严阴冷的地牢、水牢，令人毛骨悚然。父亲在狱中一次次高唱《国际歌》的故事，教育影响了他们一生。 7月1日，记者同陈知进前往陈赓在北京的旧居——灵境胡同41号。经年历久，陈赓在此生活的痕迹已难寻觅。翻开一本厚重的老相册，记者看到了一张鲜为人知的照片。照片里是陈赓那双布满战伤的腿——左腿胫骨中段隆起，肌肉扭曲变形；腿外侧有3处创痕，那是子弹穿透腿骨后留下的印记。 1927年，陈赓参加南昌起义后随部队南下征战时，左腿3次中弹，鲜血直流，无法行动。眼看敌人步步逼近，他急中生智脱下军衣，屏住呼吸装死，这才逃过一劫。 1949年5月22日，陈赓率部解放南昌。走进父亲的旧书房，陈知进从一个行李箱里取出一摞“战地日记”。记者翻开其中一本标有“1949”的黑色硬壳本，了解到陈赓当日的心境：“这次则以胜利者姿态来此。前三次入城，或为亡命客，或为阶下囚，或者站不住……无有前三次，则无今日人民之光荣。” 数十年的戎马生涯，陈赓拖着伤残的双腿，走过漫漫长征路，从土地革命战争一路征战至抗美援朝，其中考验又何止是这“前三次”？ “我难以想象他是怎么走过来的。父亲就是这样一个纯粹的人，从一开始就把自己的一切都交给了革命事业，早就超越了自我……”陈知进说。 善战的脚步，跨越胜利门槛 电视剧《亮剑》中有这样一幕：气急败坏的日军，在装甲车上用中文写着“专打386旅独立团”几个大字。 这并非虚构。在神头岭战斗、响堂铺战斗、沁源战役等战斗中，八路军第129师386旅总是出其不意、以少胜多，累计歼敌数万人，令日军既痛恨又忌惮。 指挥这一系列战斗的386旅旅长，正是陈赓。 长征期间率领的“干部团”，是专打硬仗的“开路尖刀”；抗战期间率领的386旅、太岳纵队，是威震华北的“敌后利刃”；解放战争期间率领的“陈谢兵团”，是决胜中原的“机动铁拳”……为何无论担负何种作战任务，陈赓指挥的部队总能打出赫赫威名？ 记者带着这样的疑问，专程探访坐落于湖南湘乡的陈赓故居。故居内的生平陈列馆，无声诉说着陈赓卓越的军事指挥本领。 在“善打伏击战”专题展览区中，记者注意到1938年1月美国驻华使馆参赞卡尔逊的一句评语：386旅为“中国最好的一个旅”。 巧合的是，国民党军也曾有一支自诩为“天下第一旅”的部队。更巧的是，该旅整编前为第一师，曾任第一师师长的胡宗南，正是陈赓在黄埔军校的老同学。 1946年，胡宗南率“天下第一旅”等精锐部队进犯太岳解放区，欲与老同学一决高下，结局让他脸面丢尽——他引以为傲的这支“劲旅”，最终被全歼。 “天下第一旅”并非“软柿子”。该旅装备全是美械，军中老兵多，部队战斗力强，指挥官的军衔也普遍高一级：旅长黄正诚是中将，下辖两个团，团长均为少将。 打仗即打将。在此次战役中担任临时后勤司令部副司令员的史怀璧，曾写过一篇回忆文章《陈赓指挥作战的艺术》，文中写道：“他派出许多精干可靠的侦察员，把敌人的行程、辎重调查清楚……陈赓将军算好了敌人要在某日宿营大阳……并将每个团从哪个方向哪条路进行包围均作了具体部署。” 高超的指挥艺术，永远建立在对战争的充分准备之上。反观国民党军，被俘的黄正诚依旧不清醒：“你们的打法很怪。按道理你们应该打两翼中的一翼，我们第一旅在中间，你们却打了我们……” 将无勇，则三军不锐；将无谋，则三军大倾。 战后，当陈赓的部下周希汉见到黄正诚时，黄正诚执意要见陈赓本人。周希汉当即回道：“杀鸡焉用牛刀？捉你，我周希汉足矣！”这位从担任386旅作战股股长起就跟随陈赓征战的战将，一生曾毙擒61名敌将。 记者联系到当年活捉黄正诚的战斗英雄时来亮。他曾在周希汉麾下战斗，如今已是99岁高龄，居住在江西南昌某干休所。谈起陈赓和周希汉，老人敬佩地说：“强将手下无弱兵。陈司令员带出来的人，没有一个拖泥带水的，都是一群猛将。当年，我还只是个战士，不懂什么战术，但跟着他们去打仗，心里很有底气！” 此次战役中，陈赓派出的3个主力旅旅长分别为周希汉、李成芳、陈康，都是跟随陈赓作战多年的战将，1955年均被授予中将军衔。 他们的身上，都有陈赓的影子。陈赓的身后，是一群闪耀的将星。 蒋介石曾问过他的手下：同是黄埔出身，为什么国民党没有一个打得赢陈赓？他的部将集体沉默。 得一良将，胜于百万雄兵。陈知进说，她曾听闻国民党高级将领余汉谋在台湾临终前讲过一句话：“我们之所以失败，是因为陈赓那样的人都到共产党那儿去了。” 炽热的丹心，带出雄壮方阵 湘江之滨，暑气蒸腾；滔滔江水，奔流向东。 记者来到国防科技大学，仿佛触摸到国防前沿科技强劲跳动的脉搏。 采访期间，记者的目光被一套卫星设备吸引。设备上，一把表面略有磨损的家用卷尺被充当天线，与周边精密的高端装备显得格格不入。 这是该校自主研制的世界首颗单板纳星“天拓一号”卫星实物。时任总设计师赵勇介绍：“传统天线价格昂贵。我们经过不断验证，发现卷尺材质完全符合要求，节约了数十万的研制经费。” 采访多位该校师生，记者感到，“敢走别人没走过的路，敢想前人未想过的事”，似乎是他们共同的品质。该校校史馆负责人罗有敢说：“当年陈赓大将创建这所学校时，就为我们树立了敢闯‘无人区’的榜样。” “创业是艰难的，但是革命的一切都是从无到有的……我要是办不好军事工程学院，誓不为人！”1952年，陈赓受命创办军事工程学院（即“哈军工”）时立下的誓言，如今镌刻在该校校史馆“陈赓办学治校”展区。 一无校舍、二无教材、三无师资、四无设备……这些难题，都被陈赓带领师生逐一攻克——仅过了1年，“哈军工”便拔地而起。 远离硝烟的岁月，那双伤残的腿仍不停歇，那颗因受过电刑而脆弱的心脏，仍在壮怀激烈地跳动。 1957年12月，陈赓突发大面积心肌梗塞，陷入昏迷，醒来后的第一句话就问起为“两弹”事业输送人才一事。两年后，陈赓第二次突发心肌梗塞，但仍时时牵挂国防尖端事业。陈知进说，父亲因为胸口常常疼痛，工作时不停地用手抚摸胸口，日子一长，衬衣都被磨破了一片。 记者在国防科技大学校史馆里，看到陈赓向中央军委呈报的《关于调整军事工程学院任务的报告》影印件。那一次，他提出“尖端集中、常规分散”的思路，从“哈军工”分出部分专业和师资力量，新建多所军事院校，以满足日益增长的国防科技人才需求；调整后的“哈军工”，主要培养尖端技术研究设计人才，重点为“两弹”事业提供人才支撑。 记者发现，这份报告呈报时间为1959年11月，距离陈赓出院还不到半年…… 走进国防科技大学空天科学学院，记者参观了“人才森林现象”展馆。该学院由1959年设立的导弹工程系发展而来，目前已培养出10多名院士。展馆内展示的科技创新成果，有不少达到世界领先水平。 “今天，‘哈军工’最初的五大系在全国遍地开花，成为众多军地院校的‘根’。目前学校的几个学院，多数是当年新设立的专业系发展而来，至今还在为航天、电子对抗等关键领域输送科研人才。”罗有敢感慨道，“将军看得多么遥远啊！” 那双深邃的目光看到了遥远的今天，仿佛也看到了生命的终点。陈赓故居里，一幅陈赓与家人的最后合影下方，印着这样一段文字：“我的时间可能不多了，让我多做些工作吧。告诉中央保健局，不要把我管得太严了！” 1961年3月15日，陈赓病情再次发作，颓然倒地。就在前一天，他不顾手脚冰凉，还伏案撰写《作战经验总结》，才刚刚写完《序言》部分：“现代作战，火力发展，原子、导弹等新武器杀伤力大……我们的国防工业和科学技术还不很发达，尖端尚未突破，对付导弹的防空体系还没有解决……” 第二天，陈赓离世，年仅58岁。这颗将星，为了革命事业，释放了全部能量。 “慈云桂”“宋文骢”“林俊德”……今天，在国防科技大学陈赓广场周围，铭刻数十名院士姓名的雕塑默然肃立，犹如威武雄壮的方阵。 明澈的阳光下，一群学员向教学楼走来，将军的目光凝视着他们，仿佛在诉说当年的嘱托：“你们是被寄予厚望的……” （采访得到张照星、张超、刘卓、受保顺、俞玲萍大力支持） 记者手记 因为无私，所以不朽 陈毅元帅曾评价陈赓：“陈赓像一个玻璃杯，从里到外都是清楚的、透明的”“陈赓是一块磁铁，风趣幽默，能够团结大多数人一起革命”“陈赓是我们党的一门炮。可惜现在我们这种炮少了一些，希望能有更多的这样的炮”……记者采访时，陈知进说：“要做到这三点，首先要做到无私。” 因为无私，所以不朽。无私，是共产党人的优秀品质，也是大国战将不可或缺的重要素质。唯有无私，方能以纯粹信念直面生死考验、战火考验、利益考验；唯有无私，方能将毕生热忱倾注于强国兴军的征程。 身上千钧重担，身后千军万马。在战争形态深刻变革的今天，新时代大国战将应胸怀强烈的历史使命感和责任感，一丝不苟钻研战争，一门心思准备战争，一腔热血投身战场。这是革命先辈给历史留下的样子，也是新时代大国战将应有的样子。
