About this analysis
Summary
Garrison personnel from a Navy Nansha Islands unit produced a short-video series titled 'I'll Tell the Nansha Story,' accumulating more than ten million views across dozens of episodes; the series was named a '2025 Strong-Military Positive-Energy Online Excellence Work' (2025强军正能量网络精品) and was conceived after Corporal Li Xinyu encountered online comments denying any Chinese presence on the reefs. The article documents an institutionally endorsed information-operations model in which frontline garrison soldiers serve simultaneously as content creators and sovereignty messengers, with unit leadership explicitly framing the video work as a form of 'steadfast watch' (坚守) equivalent to physical reef duty. The accompanying commentary piece directly addresses the 'dereliction of duty' objection and advances the concept of 'sovereignty expression' (主权表达) in cyberspace, confirming that this framing is official, not incidental.

I Tell You About the Nansha Islands

■ Huang Xinlong

On the reefs and islands of the South China Sea, there is a group of people telling moving stories in plain language. A series of short videos titled "I'll Tell the Nansha Story" is quietly making its way into the view of countless netizens through the "cloud." In the videos, garrison officers and soldiers recount the history of defending sovereignty and the steadfast watch kept amid the ebb and flow of the tides, bringing the stories of the Nansha Islands into thousands of homes.

For a long time, many people's understanding of the Nansha Islands has been suspended in the air. It is the expanse of deep blue at the southernmost tip of the motherland's territory—a distant place repeatedly invoked in grand narratives yet always faceless and indistinct. People respect the perseverance of the officers and soldiers there, but it is hard to truly reach the core of that perseverance.

The Nansha Islands are too far from us—so far that most Chinese people have never truly come to know them. And this series of videos has accomplished something remarkable: it has brought that distant sea before our very eyes.

There is no ornate narration in the videos, no deliberate sentimentality. Young Nansha officers and soldiers simply look at you and tell you about the reefs they have guarded, the sea they have watched, the home they have thought of. These stories are small—small enough not to stir anyone—yet when they accumulate one by one, you come to understand: what we call home and country has never been an abstract concept. It is built inch by inch from countless such concrete and minute days, from ordinary yet extraordinary people. The power that truly moves us often lies not in how loud a voice is, but in how true it is.

That is also why I want to recommend it to you. It gives warmth to a distant territory and gives a face to nameless perseverance. After watching, "Nansha" will no longer be a symbol you look up to from afar, but a group of people you think about, miss, and are willing to feel a surge of warmth for. That sea will, because of these voices, quietly take up residence in your heart.

So please, open it. Give it a little time, and give yourself a little time, to listen to those unpolished voices coming from the Nansha Islands. I believe that, just like me, at some offhand remark, you will suddenly find your eyes going red.

Stories of Youth, Stacked into a "Spiritual Reef"

■ Huang Xinlong

Officers and soldiers of a Navy Nansha garrison unit on duty. Photo by Li Xinyu

The morning sun over the Nansha Islands always leaps from the sea with impatient urgency.

At dawn, before the first golden rays have spread across the sea and sky, Corporal Li Xinyu of a Navy Nansha garrison unit has already jogged to the easternmost point of the reef. He sets up his tripod and adjusts the camera angle again and again, until the lens steadily "frames" the figure, the reef rocks, and the sea about to be ignited by the rising sun.

In the frame, Wang Jingwen, deputy political instructor of a certain company, draws a deep breath, and her clear voice cuts through the sea wind: "Listening to the stories of Nansha, reading the history of Nansha—hello everyone, I am your narrator, Wang Jingwen…"

This is an ordinary scene from the filming of "I'll Tell the Nansha Story." Since it began airing last year, dozens of episodes have accumulated more than ten million views. A group of young people with an average age under 25, armed with a few cameras and burning hearts, have turned the days of reef duty into stories and told the story of Nansha's steadfast watch to countless netizens in the "cloud."

The lens records stories. Behind the lens, there are even more stories.

---

Telling the Nansha Story Well Is Also a Form of Steadfast Watch

Everything began with a comment that stung.

Last year, Li Xinyu, returning to his unit after leave, was scrolling through short videos on a high-speed train when he came across one introducing the Nansha Islands. In the comment section, one remark cut him to the quick: "Where are the Nansha Islands? Aren't they all uninhabited reefs?"

Li Xinyu gripped his phone tightly. The scenery outside the window flashed past, but his thoughts drifted back to that distant expanse of blue.

"So many of us are guarding the Nansha Islands, and there are still people who think it's an uninhabited zone!" After lights out, lying in his bunk, Li Xinyu told his comrades what had happened.

The dormitory fell instantly silent. In the darkness, someone turned over: "My parents still can't figure out where the Nansha Islands are… I told them I'd been assigned to Nansha, and my mom stared blankly for a long time and asked whether Nansha was right next to Hainan Island."

"If you search for Nansha online, you really can't see us—real, living people—or what we do every day." Another voice joined in.

That night, the dormitory talked until very late. Outside, waves beat against the reef, each one sounding like a sigh.

What truly ignited action was a lament from a new recruit.

In September of last year, Private Tian Kuiyuan saw off a veteran squad leader who had guarded the Nansha Islands for twelve years, his eyes reddening: "The squad leader has watched over this sea for so long, but besides us, who knows his story? What a shame."

Yes. What a shame.

On the reef, every person is the protagonist of a story: a radar operator keeping lone watch over a screen deep in the night, wrestling with solitude; officers and soldiers protecting sea turtle nests and releasing hatchlings back into the ocean; everyone planting seedlings and trees, cultivating a "South Sea oasis" on the reef…

These ordinary yet burning stories are like scattered pearls. If no one picks them up, strings them together, and tells them, they will ultimately be scattered by the sea wind and swept away by the waves.

A thought took root in the hearts of these young officers and soldiers: tell these stories to the netizen who asked that question, and to everyone who does not know the Nansha Islands.

The idea quickly won support from leadership. "The 'Nansha guardians' of the new era must not only guard the reefs well, but also make more people know about Nansha, pay attention to Nansha, and love Nansha." The unit's leadership was unequivocal: "Every inch of reef in the Nansha Islands is a symbol of national sovereignty. Telling the Nansha story well is also a form of steadfast watch."

The officers and soldiers saw it clearly at once: what they were filming was not an ordinary "life vlog." Every episode is a calling card showcasing the bearing of Nansha servicemembers; every participant is a messenger conveying love of home and country.

---

Everyone Is a Creator, Everyone Can Be a Narrator

The enthusiasm burned like fire, but only when they actually got to work did they realize how hard it was.

No professional equipment, no directing experience—at the first meeting, the soldiers sat around the table staring at one another.

Someone suggested filming training scenes to look formidable; someone wanted to film scenery for an aesthetic effect; others argued for focusing on combat readiness and demonstrating capability. Everyone argued heatedly, faces flushed.

"Film history!" The unit leadership made the call, settling the matter with a single decisive word.

With the direction set, ideas flowed freely. Squad leaders dug out long-stored old stories; new recruits contributed fresh ideas; the headquarters staff provided a "story collection"—all of it became precious "ammunition."

Once the scripts were written, filming presented its own major challenges: the Nansha sun is brutally fierce, and cameras exposed to it for even a short time would shut down. The sea wind howled, and microphones were constantly filled with noise. By the time evening finally came and the light softened somewhat, the tide would surge in and equipment had to be pulled back.

No one retreated. With the drive to "cut a road through every mountain," they ground their way to a set of filming techniques unique to island-reef conditions.

The hardest part was appearing on camera. Most narrators were grassroots officers and soldiers who frequently froze up in front of the lens. Staff Sergeant Second Class He Qian, a signals soldier, froze more than ten times during a segment of less than one minute. After several failures, she stamped her feet in frustration, her voice on the verge of tears.

"Our equipment is basic, our technique is rough, but we have something others don't—authenticity!" the squad leader in charge of filming reassured her. "Netizens love watching because we don't perform. Every detail is real. Authenticity is the most powerful thing."

He Qian steadied her breathing and took her position again. Facing the camera, she smiled, turned the script into her own words, and delivered her narration in one unbroken take.

"That's a take!" Under the blazing sun, several deeply tanned young soldiers smiled brighter than the sunlight itself.

Shoot after shoot, adjustment after adjustment, the officers and soldiers found their footing and fell into a natural division of labor: those with a good writing hand polished the scripts, aiming for "the smell of earth" as well as "love of home and country"; those with agile minds devised creative ideas to find novelty in the everyday; those with a steady hand held the camera, keeping watch by the sea from five in the morning to capture the empty establishing shots of sunrise; others specialized in finding "details"—the stone mill of an old reef fortification, a national flag tattered by the sea wind—all of which became moving footnotes in the videos.

Everyone is a creator; everyone can be a narrator. Training breaks and off-duty hours became "creative time." Not beautiful enough? Reshoot. Not satisfied with the script? Revise again. Flip open the notebook of one of the team's lead creators and you find dense clusters of ideas alongside storyboard sketches.

---

Creation Gradually Became a Collective "Journey to the Roots"

The more stories they filmed, the deeper the officers and soldiers wanted to dig. And so they became "treasure hunters" of Nansha history.

They plunged into the military history gallery and the hall of honors, brushing dust from old photographs and artifacts; they approached veterans who had guarded the reefs for many years and listened to them recount the hardships of living in "stilted structures" and drinking "sky-gazing water" (rainwater collected by looking up at the sky); they pored over historical records, noting down one by one the ironclad evidence that the Nansha Islands have belonged to China since ancient times, engraving it in their hearts.

"The deeper you dig into the history, the more awe-struck you feel, and the more glorious the mission seems." Narrator Wang Jingwen spoke from deep personal experience. To tell the history of the reefs well, she did thorough preparation and for the first time came to understand in full the journey by which her predecessors surveyed and named the Nansha Islands. In the process, she suddenly understood: what she was narrating was not merely the story of one reef or one person, but a weighty history concerning sovereignty and dignity.

Creation gradually became a collective "journey to the roots" (寻根之旅). The officers and soldiers came to understand that what they guard is not only the reef, but also a history, a legacy, and a heavy responsibility.

The full story of the land beneath their feet took deep root in the hearts of the officers and soldiers, growing into the "root system" (根脉) that sustains their continued watch.

In the past, soldier Liu Zihan was somewhat impetuous. After participating in filming the videos and deeply exploring Nansha history, he seemed like a changed person—his character became steadier, his training drive stronger, and he wanted "to stay in the military for the long term." In March of this year, he was successfully promoted to Corporal.

He Qian, who used to freeze up so often, now speaks Mandarin with increasing precision and carries herself with greater confidence in front of the camera. More importantly, the repeated acts of narrating and looking back gave her a fundamentally different understanding of her mission: "Before, I simply thought that guarding the reef meant keeping a good watch. Now I understand—what we guard is the fruit of thousands of years of our ancestors' cultivation of the South China Sea, generation after generation."

Unit leadership was gratified to discover that after the "I'll Tell the Nansha Story" series was released, the awareness that "you could become the protagonist of a story at any moment" transformed the officers and soldiers' spirit and bearing: they push harder on the training ground, stand watch with greater vigilance, their drive grows stronger, and their passion burns brighter.

Comments from netizens across the country gave the young officers and soldiers enormous encouragement. A student left a message: "Someday I want to enlist and serve in the Nansha Islands." A parent said: "After watching the videos, I can finally feel at ease about my child serving there." A demobilized veteran sent a message: "Thank you for continuing to guard and tell the story in our place."

Every comment was solemnly noted down. The officers and soldiers said: "The recognition of netizens is our motivation to keep telling the story."

In early 2026, "I'll Tell the Nansha Story" was named a "2025 Strong-Military Positive-Energy Online Excellence Work" (2025强军正能量网络精品). When the news reached the Nansha Islands, the officers and soldiers were overjoyed.

Telling the story was the original intent; winning the award was a surprise. The Nansha story continues. Now they have new ideas: filming "four scenes of Nansha," conducting "veteran revisit" segments, and engaging in deeper interaction with netizens.

"We are thinking about how to tell the story with more thought and more depth." As a next step, they plan to dig deeper into the history, culture, and spiritual lineage of the Nansha Islands, weaving sovereignty consciousness and love of home and country more naturally and skillfully into vivid stories.

"No slogans—but we want everyone who watches the video to understand from the bottom of their heart: why this sea must be guarded by Chinese people; why this steadfast watch is something every Chinese person can be proud of." This is the shared voice of the officers and soldiers who participated in the filming.

The setting sun melts into gold; another episode wraps up filming.

Li Xinyu and his comrades sit on the edge of the reef. The evening glow dyes the sea orange-red; the national flag on the reef snaps in the wind like a leaping flame.

"What do we talk about next episode?" someone asks.

"Let's talk about this flag." Li Xinyu points to the national flag, vivid as fire. "Starting from the day the first Five-Starred Red Flag was raised over the Nansha Islands, it has been raised on time every day, rain or shine. Even if only we can see it, I want to tell that story to everyone."

The sea wind brushes their faces—carrying salt and bitterness, but carrying burning heat as well. On this territory at the southernmost tip of the motherland, a group of young people are using unassuming lenses and sincere narration to guard and transmit China's Nansha story.

This is the declaration of sovereignty written by the new-era Nansha officers and soldiers in cyberspace; this is the invisible yet indestructible "spiritual reef" (精神礁盘) stacked up by a group of soldiers with their youth and their love.

---

The Lens Is Also a Weapon

■ Cheng Yuanjin

"I'll Tell the Nansha Story" has won an award. Behind the applause, there are also questions: a soldier's duty is to prepare for war and fight it—is it "dereliction of duty" (不务正业) for reef-garrison soldiers to film short videos?

To answer this question, one must first recognize a fundamental truth: today, the battlefield for defending national sovereignty has long extended beyond the reef beneath one's feet. When questions about the Nansha Islands continue to circulate online, silence is loss of voice, and loss of voice may mean loss of ground.

When reef-garrison soldiers raise their lenses, it may look like capturing light and shadow, but in reality they are gripping another kind of "weapon"—what they guard is the invisible territory beyond the tangible land, a territory equally inviolable. This is absolutely not "dereliction of duty"; it is precisely a demonstration of understanding the "primary duty" more deeply and practicing it more concretely.

The deeper value lies in completing a successful act of "sovereignty expression" (主权表达). These young officers and soldiers keenly perceived that cyberspace is equally a battlefield for defending sovereignty. They use their lenses to declare to the world: there are people here, there are stories here—this is China's inalienable territory! When hundreds of millions of netizens come to pay attention to and love the Nansha Islands because of these videos, a powerful emotional consensus and sovereign identity quietly coalesces. This is precisely a vivid expression of the political acuity and sense of mission of the new-era Nansha officers and soldiers—guarding the tangible position while holding the intangible public opinion front; beyond silent perseverance, sounding a powerful voice of the era.

What is worth deep reflection is the way the process of narration itself shapes the officers and soldiers. "Seeking the roots" (寻根) in order to tell the story well allows the younger generation to understand legacy by tracing history, and to anchor their faith by gazing back at the road already traveled. It offers us this insight: the best education is to make officers and soldiers the protagonists of the story rather than spectators; the deepest identification comes from personal experience and personal understanding.

The waves of the Nansha Islands beat against the reef day and night; the waves of the era surge equally through every officer and soldier. By picking up the lens, they actively embrace the era and define what steadfast watch looks like in the new era.

The Nansha story is inexhaustible; the story of a stronger military is even more so. We look forward to more officers and soldiers proactively picking up their lenses and "microphones," telling the brilliance of every reef and island and the steadfast watch at every combat post, and spreading those stories far and wide. Let every ordinary yet burning story of a stronger military become a light that illuminates the human heart!

The Reefs of Nansha, The Soldiers of Nansha

■ Yi Wenqiang

This is a reef rock. It is neither the miraculous stone of mythology that gave birth to the Stone Monkey, nor the legendary Taishan rock said to ward off evil and protect the home—it is simply the most ordinary of the hundreds of millions of rocks in this world.

But is it truly so ordinary? This is the most common type of reef rock found on the Nansha Islands. Over tens of thousands of years, scoured by time and waves, it has grown ever smoother and more lustrous, yet it has never yielded to this stretch of sea—just like the officers and soldiers who stand watch here, its "sinew and bone" (筋骨) growing ever harder.

Reading the Silence of the Reef Rocks

■ Navy Lieutenant Xu Qiwei

When I received the assignment to film the twenty-first episode of "I'll Tell the Nansha Story," I thought about it for a long time.

In the previous twenty episodes, my comrades had told so many stories. It seemed like everything worth telling had already been told. What could I still say?

One day, while walking on the reef, I suddenly noticed something I had never paid attention to before: the reef rocks scattered across the reef had, at some point unknown to me, been carved by my comrades with slogans of all sizes.

The different words on the reef rocks reflect the different feelings of the officers and soldiers. They carved the character for "home" (家) into the reef rocks, expressing the love of home and country embedded in "making the reef one's home" (以礁为家); they chiseled "Nansha Spirit" (南沙精神) into the rocks, engraving their loyalty to the motherland into the grain of the stone; they wrote battle slogans on the rocks to remind themselves to remain ever ready for combat (枕戈待旦).

I once thought reef rocks were cold and hard, without feeling. But when I read carefully the words carved into them, I understood: the garrison officers and soldiers have projected onto these silent rocks their feelings for this sea and this reef, fusing into the stone their faith in guarding the Nansha Islands and defending home and country, endowing the rocks with warmth and spiritual meaning, and distilling a reef-rock culture (礁石文化) unique to Nansha.

During recording, there was one sentence I turned over and over in my mind, and finally spoke to the camera. I said: "The soldiers of Nansha use their loyalty to give the reef rocks a soul, and the reef rocks in turn, through their eternal standing, bear witness to the officers and soldiers' steadfast watch. They are mirror images of each other, resonating with one another, together facing the wind and waves in the vast deep blue, silently guarding the peace of the motherland." That was not a performance. It was what I truly wanted to say from my heart.

On the day the video was released, I stared at the comment section and scrolled down one comment at a time. One comment made my nose sting. A netizen wrote: "I thought the hardest thing in Nansha was the reef rocks. After watching this, I know—the hardest thing is the people standing on the reef rocks."

It turns out that a silent stone can truly connect people who have never met. A thousand li away, a stranger had formed some subtle emotional resonance with us, standing watch in the Nansha Islands. I suddenly understood: bringing this expanse of blue national territory into the public eye, and moving every person who hears the story to feel it and resonate with it—that is the meaning of our narration.

The story of the Nansha Islands—I will keep telling it. I will tell it to everyone, and I will tell it to myself ten years from now—to tell her: you once did something remarkable on China's South China Sea.

官兵豁然开朗：他们要拍的，并不是普通的“生活Vlog”，每一期视频，都是一张展示南沙军人风采的名片；每一个参与者，都是传递家国情怀的信使。 人人都是创作者，人人可当讲述人 热情如火，真干起来才知不易。 没有专业设备，没有编导经验，第一次碰头会，战士们围着桌子，大眼瞪小眼。 有人提议拍训练场景，显得威武；有人想拍风景，追求唯美；还有人主张，聚焦战备、展现实力。大家争论不休，面红耳赤。 “拍历史！”部队领导拍板，一锤定音。 方向既定，思路顿开。班长们掏出“压箱底”的老故事，新兵们贡献新点子，机关提供“故事集”，这些都成了宝贵的“弹药”。 脚本写好之后，拍摄也是个大挑战：南沙烈日毒辣，相机暴晒片刻就罢工。海风呼啸，话筒常常灌满杂音。好不容易等到傍晚，光线柔和了一些，潮水却又涌上来，设备只得后撤。 大家没有退缩，凭着“逢山开路”的劲头，硬是摸索出一套岛礁专属拍摄经验。 最难的是出镜。讲述人多是基层官兵，面对镜头常常紧张“卡壳”。二级上士、通信兵贺茜，不到一分钟的镜头，竟卡壳了十几次。几次失败后，她急得直跺脚，声音里带着哭腔。 “设备差、技术糙，但我们有别人没有的——真实！”负责拍摄的班长安慰她，“网友爱看，就因为咱不表演，每个细节都是真实的。真实，最有力量。” 贺茜调整呼吸，重新站定。面对镜头，她露出微笑，把文稿化作自己的语言，讲述一气呵成。 “过了！”烈日下，几名晒得黝黑的年轻士兵，笑容比阳光还灿烂。 一次次拍摄，一次次磨合，官兵找到了门道，形成自然分工：文笔好的人负责打磨脚本，要“泥土味”，更要“家国情”；脑子活的人想创意，让日常出新意；手稳的人端相机，为拍摄日出的空镜头，凌晨5点就在海边守候；还有人专司寻找“细节”，老礁堡的石磨、被海风吹烂的国旗，都成了视频中动人的注脚。 人人都是创作者，人人可当讲述人。训练间隙、执勤之余，就是“创作时间”。镜头不够美？重拍！脚本不满意？再改。翻开一名团队主创的笔记本，只见密密麻麻的创意旁，是一张张分镜草图。 创作，慢慢成了集体的“寻根之旅” 故事拍多了，官兵就想挖掘得更深一些。于是，他们成了南沙历史的“寻宝人”。 他们扎进军史长廊、荣誉室，拂去老照片、旧物件上的灰尘；走近守礁多年的老兵，听他们讲住“高脚屋”、喝“望天水”的艰苦岁月；翻阅史料，把南沙自古属于中国的铁证，一条条记在本上，刻进心里。 “越挖掘历史，越感到震撼，越觉得使命光荣。”讲述人王婧雯深有感触。为了讲述好岛礁的历史，她做足了功课，第一次完整了解了先辈们考察、命名南沙的历程。这个过程中，她恍然明白：自己讲述的，不仅是一座岛礁、一个人的故事，而是一段关乎主权与尊严的厚重历史。 创作，慢慢成了集体的“寻根之旅”。官兵渐渐懂得，他们守的不只是礁盘，更是一段历史、一种传承、一份沉甸甸的责任。 脚下这片热土的来龙去脉，深深扎进官兵心里，长成了支撑他们继续坚守的“根脉”。 以前，战士刘子涵做事比较浮躁。参与拍摄视频并深入挖掘南沙历史后，他像换了个人，性格变得更沉稳，训练劲头也更足，“想在部队长期干下去”。今年3月，他顺利晋升为中士。 常常“卡壳”的女兵贺茜，普通话说得越来越标准，镜头前更加自信。更重要的是，一次次讲述、回望，让她对使命有了截然不同的理解：“以前，我单纯觉得，守礁就是值好班。现在，我懂了，我们守的是几千年来先民世代耕耘南海的成果。” 部队领导欣喜地发现，《南沙故事我来讲》系列视频发布后，“随时可能成为故事主角”的意识，让官兵的精气神为之一变：训练场上更拼，执勤站岗更警惕，干劲越来越足，激情越来越旺。 来自天南海北网友的评论，给了年轻官兵莫大鼓舞。一名学生留言：“以后我也想去南沙当兵。”一位家长说：“看完视频，终于放心孩子在那里当兵了。”一名退伍老兵发来消息：“谢谢你们，替我们继续守着、讲着。” 每一条留言，官兵都郑重记下，他们说：“网友的认可，就是我们讲下去的动力。” 2026年初，《南沙故事我来讲》被评为“2025强军正能量网络精品”。消息传回南沙，官兵喜出望外。 讲述是初衷，获奖是惊喜。南沙故事，仍在继续。现在，他们有了新想法：拍“南沙四景”，做“老兵回访”，与网友深度互动。 “我们在琢磨，怎么讲得更有思想、更有深度。”下一步，他们计划深挖南沙历史文化与精神谱系，将主权意识、家国情怀，更自然、更巧妙地融入鲜活故事。 “不喊口号，但要让每个人看完视频都能从心底明白——这片海，为什么必须由中国人来守；这份坚守，为什么值得每个中国人骄傲。”这是参与拍摄官兵的共同心声。 夕阳熔金，又一期拍摄任务收工。 李昕宇和战友坐在礁盘边。晚霞把大海染成橘红，礁盘上的国旗在风中猎猎作响，如一团跳动的火焰。 “下期，咱们讲什么？”有人问。 “就讲这面旗。”李昕宇指向鲜艳如火的国旗，“从第一面五星红旗在南沙升起那天开始，无论风雨，它每天都会准时升起。哪怕只有我们看得见，这故事我也要讲给所有人听。” 海风拂面，带着咸涩，更带着滚烫。在祖国最南端这片国土上，一群年轻人正用朴素的镜头、真挚的讲述，守护并传递着中国的南沙故事。 这，就是新时代南沙官兵在网络空间写下的主权宣言；这，就是一群士兵用青春与热爱，垒起的无形却坚不可摧的“精神礁盘”。 镜头也是一种武器 ■程袁锦 《南沙故事我来讲》获奖了。掌声背后，也有质疑：军人的本分是备战打仗，守礁官兵拍短视频，算不算“不务正业”？ 回答这个问题，先要认清一个根本：今天，捍卫国家主权的战场，早已超越脚下的礁盘。当一些关于南沙的疑问仍在网络流传，沉默即失语，失语或失地。 守礁官兵举起镜头，看似捕捉光影，实则紧握另一种“武器”——他们守护的，是有形国土之外那片同样不容侵犯的无形疆域。这，绝非“不务正业”，恰恰是将“主业”理解得更深、践行得更实的体现。 更深层的价值，在于完成了一次成功的“主权表达”。这群年轻官兵敏锐洞察到：网络空间，同样是捍卫主权的战场。他们用镜头向世界宣告：这里有人、有故事，是中国不可分割的领土！当亿万网友因这些视频而关注、热爱南沙，强大的情感共识与主权认同便悄然凝聚。这，正是新时代南沙官兵政治敏锐与使命担当的生动诠释——在守好有形阵位的同时，守住无形的舆论阵地；在无声的坚守之外，发出有力的时代强音。 值得深思的，是讲述过程本身对官兵的塑造。为讲好故事而“寻根”，让年轻一代在追溯历史中读懂传承，在凝望来路中锚定信仰。它启示我们：最好的教育，是让官兵成为故事的主角而非看客；最深的认同，源于亲历和亲悟。 南沙的浪，日夜拍打礁盘；时代的浪，同样激荡着每名官兵。拿起镜头，他们主动拥抱时代，定义新时代坚守的模样。 南沙故事讲不完，强军故事更讲不尽。期待更多官兵主动举起镜头和“麦克风”，将座座岛礁的精彩、个个战位的坚守讲出来、传开去。让每一个平凡滚烫的强军故事，都化作照亮人心的光！ 南沙的礁石 南沙的兵 ■易文强 这，是一块礁石。它既不是神话中孕育石猴的造化仙石，也不是传说中可以镇宅辟邪的泰山奇石，只是这个世界上亿万块石头中，最为普通的一块。 但是，它真的如此普通吗？这是南沙岛礁上最为常见的礁石。数万年来，历经时光和海浪的冲刷，它变得越发光亮圆滑，但从未向这片海域妥协，就像坚守在这里的官兵一样，“筋骨”越发坚硬。 南沙的战士，就像南沙的礁石。请扫描二维码，走近南沙的礁石、南沙的兵。 读懂礁石的沉默 ■海军上尉 徐启薇 接到拍摄第21期《南沙故事我来讲》的任务时，我思考了许久。 前面20期，战友们讲过许许多多故事，能讲的好像都被讲完了，我还能讲点什么？ 一天，我在礁上散步，忽然注意到了一件以前从未在意的事：散布在礁上各处的礁石，不知何时，被战友们刻上了大大小小的标语。 礁石上，不同的文字折射出官兵不同的情感。官兵在礁石上刻出了“家”的字样，倾诉“以礁为家”的家国情怀；在礁石上凿出“南沙精神”，把对祖国的忠诚刻进了礁石的纹路；在礁石上写下战斗标语，警醒自己时刻枕戈待旦。 我曾觉得，礁石冰冷坚硬，没有情感。可当我认真阅读刻在礁石上的那些文字，我才明白，守礁官兵将对这片大海、这座岛礁的情感投射到了沉默的礁石上，把坚守南沙、保家卫国的信仰融入礁石，赋予礁石以温度和精神内涵，凝练出了南沙特有的礁石文化。 录制时，有一句话我反复琢磨，最后讲给了镜头。我说：“南沙的兵，用忠诚赋予礁石灵魂，而礁石也以永恒的矗立，见证着官兵的坚守。他们互为镜像，彼此共鸣，在茫茫深蓝中，共同直面风浪，默默守护着祖国的安宁。”那不是表演，是我内心真正想讲出来的话。 视频播出那天，我盯着评论区，一条一条往下看。有一条评论，看得我鼻头发酸。网友评论：“我以为南沙最硬的是礁石，看完才知道，最硬的是站在礁石上的人。” 原来，一块沉默的石头，真能把素不相识的人们连接在一起。千里之外，一名陌生的网友竟然与坚守在南沙的我们产生了某种微妙的情感共鸣。我忽然明白了，让这片蔚蓝国土走进大众视野，让每一个听故事的人都能为之感动共鸣，就是我们讲述的意义。 关于南沙的故事，我会一直讲下去。讲给所有人听，也讲给10年后的自己听——告诉她，你曾在祖国的南海，做过一件了不起的事情。
