A Record of the Chinese Navy's Task Force 83 Ship Open-House Activities in Brunei

中国海军83舰编队在文莱举行舰艇开放活动见闻
PLA Daily (解放军报) 29 July 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
Chinese Navy Task Force 83, comprising the training ship Qi Jiguang and the amphibious dock landing ship Kunlunshan, conducted ship open-house events and a deck reception at Muara Port, Brunei, in late July 2025, hosting overseas Chinese communities, local residents, Chinese-funded enterprise personnel, and diplomatic staff to mark the PLA's 99th founding anniversary and the 35th anniversary of China-Brunei diplomatic relations. The article documents a standard overseas port-visit public diplomacy package — open decks, cultural performances, Belt and Road project tie-ins — and its primary analytical value is as a record of how the PLAN frames far-seas cadet training voyages for both domestic diaspora audiences and host-country publics simultaneously. The explicit linkage of naval presence to BRI infrastructure projects (the Hengyi PMB petrochemical complex, the Temburong Bridge) extends a documented pattern of using port visits to reinforce China's economic footprint narratives in Southeast Asia.
Translation

Brunei's name carries the meaning of "land of peace." Over the past several days, the Chinese Navy's Task Force 83, currently conducting a port visit here, has held a series of ship open-house activities, inviting representatives of overseas Chinese communities, local residents, Chinese-funded enterprises, and personnel from embassies and consulates of various countries accredited to Brunei to come aboard and tour the vessels.

"So these are the PLA uncles!" Four-year-old Zhao Canyue, the moment she stepped aboard the Qi Jiguang, was immediately captivated by the precision drill movements of the naval cadets from the PLA Dalian Naval Academy. Zhao Canyue's mother, Ms. Xiang, told reporters that her daughter had been extremely excited watching the September 3rd parade at home last year. This summer holiday, she brought her daughter to Brunei to visit her father, who works at a Chinese-funded enterprise, and when they learned that a Chinese naval vessel formation was visiting, they came to see it at the first opportunity. "We hope she can feel the strength of the motherland through these mighty warships she can see and touch." Ms. Xiang's words were filled with deep emotion.

Aboard the Qi Jiguang and the Kunlunshan, 48 naval cadets participated in the parade commemorating the 80th anniversary of the victory of the Chinese People's War of Resistance Against Japanese Aggression and the World Anti-Fascist War. On this voyage, executing a far-seas comprehensive training and port visit mission with the task force, they have formed a ceremonial honor guard. Huang Quanfeng, a cadet in the navigation specialty, is one of them; during the ship open-house activities he also served as a guide aboard the Kunlunshan. "On the parade ground, my comrades and I marched with resounding steps, presenting the 'Chinese bearing' (中国排面). Venturing beyond our borders, we will absolutely display the image of the Chinese Navy with a spirited bearing." He introduced the ship's general situation, the visit's circumstances, and naval knowledge to visitors in fluent English, and his professional commentary earned repeated praise from the people of Brunei.

"It's absolutely stunning!" Brunei university student Alanrita said with feeling after touring the Qi Jiguang and the Kunlunshan. "This is my first time boarding a Chinese naval vessel. I was originally worried I wouldn't understand anything, but I never expected there would be such professional guides who taught me so much." Alanrita told reporters: "I came this time with my father, and I hope next time there will be an opportunity to bring my mother and younger brother aboard to visit."

Amid the surging crowd, Guo Zhongwei, an engineer and representative of a Chinese-funded enterprise, was from time to time introducing the Qi Jiguang's flight deck, helicopter control tower, and main gun platform to colleagues beside him. This was already his second time boarding for a visit. "When the Qi Jiguang made its first visit to Brunei in 2019, I came aboard then too—that was just when I had started working on the Hengyi Brunei PMB Island petrochemical project." Standing on the flight deck, Guo Zhongwei pointed toward Pulau Muara Besar, visible across the water from Muara Port, and said: "Seven years have passed in the blink of an eye. It's hard to imagine that place was once a desolate island covered in swamps." That project is China's largest investment project in Brunei and a key project through which both countries are jointly implementing the Belt and Road Initiative.

After finishing her tour of the Qi Jiguang, Ms. Yan, an ethnic Chinese Malaysian, had her clothes soaked through with sweat, yet she was still reluctant to leave. Ms. Yan stood on the flight deck and photographed the Temburong Bridge in the distance. "I have worked in Brunei for more than 20 years. Before, getting back to Malaysia required a detour of more than two hours; after the bridge opened, it only takes 45 minutes by car." Ms. Yan said with satisfaction. In 2020, the Temburong Bridge, built with the participation of Chinese enterprises, was completed and opened to traffic, greatly facilitating movement between Brunei and surrounding areas. "I hope the Chinese Navy gets better and better, and China gets better and better!" As she left the ship, Ms. Yan offered her sincere wishes.

On the evening of the 26th, the Qi Jiguang flew her full dress of flags, and Task Force 83 held a deck reception celebrating the 99th founding anniversary of the Chinese People's Liberation Army. Kung fu fans rose and fell like the wind, Sichuan opera face-changing performers moved with flowing sleeves, and traditional Chinese instruments played melodious and graceful tunes; the music drifted with the sea breeze toward the harbor glimmering with lights, telling the story of the deep and long-standing friendship between China and Brunei, separated yet facing each other across the sea…

"This year marks the 35th anniversary of the establishment of diplomatic relations between China and Brunei. Over the long term, the two militaries have consistently maintained a high degree of mutual trust, with fruitful results in ship mutual visits, joint training, personnel cultivation, and other areas," the task force commander stated. "The ocean is the bond connecting the world. Every time the Chinese Navy makes an overseas visit, it is committed to serving as a messenger of peace and building bridges of friendship, and at the same time we also hope to broaden the international horizons of naval cadets and lay a solid foundation for their professional development."

Original Chinese
文莱国名寓意“和平之邦”。连日来，正在这里进行访问的中国海军83舰编队举办了一系列舰艇开放活动，邀请华人华侨、当地民众和中资机构等代表及各国驻文使领馆人员登舰参观。 “这就是解放军叔叔啊！”4岁的赵灿玥一登上戚继光舰，立刻被海军大连舰艇学院学员标准的队列动作吸引。赵灿玥的母亲向女士告诉记者，孩子去年在家里观看九三阅兵时非常兴奋，这个暑假带她来文莱探望在中资机构工作的爸爸，当得知中国海军舰艇编队来访，便第一时间前来参观。“我们希望她能从这看得见、摸得着的威武舰艇上感受祖国的强大。”向女士的话语饱含深情。 在戚继光舰和昆仑山舰上，有48名海军学员参加了纪念中国人民抗日战争暨世界反法西斯战争胜利80周年阅兵。这次随编队执行远海综合实习访问任务，他们组成了一支仪仗队。航海专业的学员黄全峰便是其中一员，他在舰艇开放活动中兼任昆仑山舰的讲解员。“阅兵场上，我和战友们步履铿锵，走出‘中国排面’。走出国门，我们必将以昂扬姿态展现中国海军的形象。”他用流利的英语向参观人员介绍舰艇概况、出访情况和海军知识，专业讲解赢得文莱民众频频称赞。 “太震撼了！”文莱大学生阿兰丽塔参观完戚继光舰和昆仑山舰后感慨地说，“这是我第一次登上中国海军舰艇，本来还担心自己看不懂，没想到有这么专业的讲解员，让我了解到很多知识。”阿兰丽塔告诉记者：“这次我是和父亲一起来的，希望下次有机会带着母亲和弟弟一起登舰参观。” 攒动的人群中，中资机构代表、工程师郭中伟不时向身旁的同事介绍戚继光舰的飞行甲板、直升机塔台和主炮平台。这已是他第二次登舰参观。“2019年戚继光舰首访文莱时，我就登舰参观过，那时我刚进入恒逸文莱大摩拉岛石化项目工作。”站在飞行甲板上，郭中伟指向与摩拉港隔水相望的大摩拉岛说，“一转眼7年过去了，很难想象，那里曾经是一片沼泽遍布的荒岛。”该项目是中国对文莱最大的投资项目，也是中文两国共同落实“一带一路”倡议的重点项目。 参观完戚继光舰，马来西亚籍华人燕女士的衣服已被汗水浸透，可她依旧不舍得离开。燕女士站在飞行甲板上拍下远处的淡布隆跨海大桥。“我在文莱工作20多年，以前回马来西亚需要绕行两个多小时，大桥通车后，驾车只需45分钟。”燕女士欣慰地说。2020年，中企参与承建的淡布隆跨海大桥建成启用，极大便利了文莱和周边地区的交往。“希望中国海军越来越好，中国越来越好！”离舰时，燕女士送上真诚祝福。 26日晚，戚继光舰满旗高悬，83舰编队举行庆祝中国人民解放军建军99周年甲板招待会。功夫扇起落如风、川剧变脸衣袂轻扬、中国传统乐器悠扬婉转，乐声随海风飘向灯火阑珊的港湾，讲述着中文两国隔海相望、源远流长的深厚情谊…… “今年是中文建交35周年。长期以来，两军始终保持高度互信，在舰艇互访、联合训练、人才培养等方面成果丰硕。”编队指挥员表示，“海洋是联结世界的纽带。中国海军每一次出访，都致力于担当和平使者、架设友谊桥梁，同时我们也希望能提升海军学员的国际视野，为他们的职业发展奠定坚实基础。”
