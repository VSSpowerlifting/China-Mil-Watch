A Navy Detachment Explores a New Intensive Training Organization Model to Release Greater Effectiveness from Limited Resources

海军某支队探索集约化组训新模式，让有限资源释放更大效能
PLA Daily (解放军报) 11 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
A PLA Navy surface combatant detachment — whose ships include the Harbin, Qingdao, Urumqi, Weifang, Yantai, Anshan, Garzê, Zhumadian, and Nanchang — has implemented a 集约化组训 (intensive training organization) model that allows commanding officers from multiple ships to ride aboard whichever vessel is conducting a given assessment subject, rather than waiting for their own ship's schedule to clear. The detachment reports that over the past year multiple commanding officers completed position qualification assessments through the mechanism, which it frames as a direct response to a documented readiness problem: an accelerating fleet expansion that has outpaced the pool of fully qualified commanding officers, leaving assessment backlogs as ships cycle through high-tempo operational commitments. The article documents how the PLA Navy is managing the qualification bottleneck created by rapid force structure growth, but leaves the detachment's parent fleet command, total ship count, and aggregate qualification shortfall unspecified.
Translation

A Navy Detachment Explores a New Intensive Training Organization Model —— Same Specialty, Centralized Training; Same Subject, One-Ship Examination

■ PLA Daily Reporter Bi Xiaotian, Special Correspondent Wu Di

The detachment organizes maritime training. Photo by Li Fengxiang

Between sea and sky, with high winds and surging waves.

In the height of summer, a live combat-oriented (实战化) anti-air engagement training assessment aboard the Qingdao, a ship of a certain Navy detachment, got underway. Entering the command compartment, the reporter found that the officer in command was not the Qingdao's own commanding officer, but the deputy commanding officer of the Harbin. As soon as training began, he fixed his gaze on the integrated display console and issued a series of commands in rapid succession.

"Target attribute identification," "fire control solution input" … As the commands came through, the warship swiftly maneuvered to an advantageous battle position, and round after round of shells screamed from the barrels, precisely striking the aerial target drone.

"Target destroyed — excellent score!" The moment the examiner finished speaking, the Harbin's deputy commanding officer let out a breath of relief. "The ship's missions are heavy, and training time constantly conflicts. This assessment has been hanging over me — today I finally cleared it!" he told the reporter.

The experience of the Harbin's deputy commanding officer is not an isolated case, but rather a new development brought about by the detachment's exploration of an intensive training organization (集约化组训) model — commanding officers from multiple ships "hitching a ride" on a single vessel to sit for assessments together. The detachment's leadership introduced that, at peak times, as many as 10 deputy commanding officers have gathered aboard a single ship to complete multiple training assessments on the same platform.

This leader told the reporter that the innovation of the intensive training organization model was "forced out" by the real-world difficulties confronting live combat-oriented training. In recent years, a number of new ships have entered service with the detachment in succession, yet there are not enough commanding officers who have passed full-qualification assessments. Some ships have multiple overlapping training tasks; others have had coordinated training delayed. The accumulation of commanding officers' training metrics and their eligibility for independent-operation qualification assessments have been affected, and the contradiction between assessments that "cannot be conducted" and missions that "cannot be put off" has grown increasingly acute.

Breaking the impasse was urgent. Targeting this acute contradiction, the detachment Party committee leadership studied the matter and decided to explore a new intensive training organization model. The detachment headquarters would compile each ship's mission situation and scheduling, integrate dozens of assessment subjects into multiple sets of training plans, and assign different ships different training assessment tasks. After assessment plans were distributed to each ship, commanding officers with qualification assessment needs could apply to go aboard the ship carrying out the corresponding task to participate in training and assessment.

Since the new training organization model was implemented, the quality and effectiveness of training have shifted into a higher gear: aboard the Urumqi, deputy commanding officers from the Zhumadian and the Garzê responded flexibly and handled situations calmly, completing assessments in navigation maneuvering, mooring and unmooring from buoys, and other subjects; aboard the Weifang, the Yantai's deputy commanding officer conducted intensive and refined training (专攻精练) in anti-surface attack, anti-air defense, damage control, and other subjects, and successfully completed the assessment with close coordination among all battle stations.

To further improve training effectiveness, the detachment simultaneously launched a "training bus" (训练班车) and a "training express" (训练专车) — the "training bus" is organized around common subjects, allowing personnel of the same specialty from different ships to study together and train jointly; the "training express" focuses on specialty subjects for refined intensive training, precisely targeting the capability shortfalls of specific categories of personnel. The two approaches complement each other: a single voyage concentrates and intensifies training for a cohort of backbone personnel, allowing limited training resources to produce maximum training effectiveness.

During the reporter's visit to the detachment, the following scenes were observed: in the headquarters office, operations and training staff officers were busy compiling training requirements from different ships, coordinating suitable vessels, and working out logistical details; inside a ship's compartment, a combat watch officer was checking off pending assessment subjects one by one and reporting the training and assessment plan; on the naval port pier, a department head from a ship that had just completed a major mission rushed straight to a sister ship to make up training in formation maneuvering subjects …

Not long ago, during a live-fire missile training exercise in distant sea areas, the Anshan received three personnel riding along for training, including the commanding officer of the Garzê. During training, the ride-along personnel participated throughout in scenario planning and group deduction exercises; after training concluded, they further conducted self-assessments and mutual assessments, exchanging insights on command. "Cross-ship exchange and sparring helped me pinpoint my own shortfalls, and my command capability received genuine, concrete tempering and improvement," the Garzê's commanding officer told the reporter.

Since the "dual-track training" (训练双车) mechanism was implemented, the detachment has intensively consolidated training resources, training quality and effectiveness have improved substantially, and officers and soldiers' enthusiasm for training has continued to rise. Over the past year, multiple ship commanding officers have successfully passed position qualification assessments through this mechanism, and large numbers of multi-skilled (一专多能型) officers have distinguished themselves in major missions.

The detachment's leadership stated that they will continue to optimize the training organization model, keep a close eye on weak links in combat readiness training (练兵备战), and drive the "dual-track training" system to extend from sea to shore and expand from a single domain to multiple domains, comprehensively strengthening the cultivation and reserve of personnel for combat readiness, and continuously driving combat readiness training to new heights.

Releasing Greater Effectiveness from Limited Resources

■ Yang Yue

Victory or defeat in war lies in shaping the situation (谋势); the strength or weakness of the situation lies in overall coordination (统筹). On the training ground, the precision of resource coordination directly affects the speed at which frontline officers and soldiers generate combat power. A training organization model in which each unit fights alone and training is conducted in dispersed fashion easily leads to resource idleness and redundant investment, severely constraining improvements in training quality and effectiveness. Implementing intensive training organization is undoubtedly the key solution for activating training resources and breaking through training bottlenecks.

The core of intensive training organization is establishing the concept of treating training resources as "a single game of chess" (一盘棋). The Navy detachment's implementation of the joint-ship co-training model is a vivid practice of intensive training organization — the track of one ship becomes a forge for tempering talent across multiple ships. This has not only allowed the detachment to leave behind the extensive training困境 (困境) of "single-ship independent training and resource waste," but has also effectively resolved the practical problems facing personnel assessments. Their innovative practice shows us an effective path toward the intensive transformation (集约化变革) of military training: only by proactively breaking down organizational barriers (建制壁垒) and parochial thinking (本位思维) can limited training platforms and equipment resources be made to release greater training effectiveness.

A Record of "Hitching a Ride" to the Assessment

■ Officer of a Certain Navy Detachment, Duan Chunjie

Standing on the deck of the Qingdao, watching the waves rolling across the sea surface, I recalled my experience as a department head aboard the Nanchang. At that time, the ship carried out high-intensity combat readiness watch duties year-round, and I was on the go almost every single day.

Preparing for deployment exercises, maintaining and servicing equipment, filling out documents and reports … The complex watch duties and administrative affairs consumed the vast majority of my time and energy. Once, I had prepared well in advance for a position qualification assessment, only to be suddenly ordered to carry out an urgent mission, and had no choice but to give it up. The next assessment would mean waiting more than half a year.

Watching comrades from the same cohort pass their assessments one after another and move into key positions, my inner anxiety grew with each passing day.

The detachment's exploration and implementation of the "dual-track training" mechanism showed me an opportunity. I applied at the first opportunity to ride the Qingdao's "training bus," hoping to use it to make up for what I owed.

At first, facing the new training environment, I was somewhat out of my element. The Qingdao's command cadence was faster, and its coordination habits differed slightly from those of the ship on which I had served. During my first time commanding a navigation maneuvering training session, poor communication with the crew led to an overall slowdown in progress, and the training results fell short of expectations.

Faced with this setback, I did not lose heart. During my training period aboard the Qingdao, I familiarized myself with coordination commands with each crew member every day and refined the maneuvering procedures; during training breaks, I humbly sought guidance from the Qingdao's training backbone personnel on training organization methods and practical techniques, and gradually integrated into subsequent training.

Dedicated effort is never in vain. During the position qualification assessment, I led officers and soldiers as the commanding officer through training in navigation maneuvering, mooring and unmooring from buoys, anti-air engagement, and other subjects. Everyone coordinated closely and worked in seamless concert, and we ultimately passed with outstanding results. In that moment, I truly experienced the sense of achievement and honor that comes with being a ship's commanding officer.

(Compiled by PLA Daily Special Correspondent Wu Di)

海军某支队探索集约化组训新模式—— 同专业集中训 同科目一舰考 ■解放军报记者 毕笑天 特约通讯员 吴頔 该支队组织海上训练。李凤祥摄 海天之间，风高浪急。 盛夏时节，海军某支队青岛舰一场实战化对空抗击训练考核拉开战幕。走进指挥舱室，记者发现，坐镇指挥的并非青岛舰指挥员，而是哈尔滨舰副舰长。训练一展开，他就紧盯综合显示台，连续下达多道口令。 “目标属性判别”“发射诸元装订”……随着口令传来，战舰疾速占领有利战位，一发发炮弹呼啸出膛，精准命中空中靶机。 “目标摧毁，成绩优秀！”考官话音刚落，哈尔滨舰副舰长舒了一口气。“舰艇任务繁重，训练时间经常发生冲突，这项考核一直‘悬着’，今天总算过关了！”他对记者说。 哈尔滨舰副舰长的经历并非个例，而是该支队探索集约化组训模式带来的新变化——多舰指挥干部同乘一艘舰“搭车”赶考。该支队领导介绍，参考指挥员最多时，曾有10名副舰长齐聚一舰，同台完成多项训练考核。 这名领导告诉记者，集约化组训模式的创新，是被实战化训练面临的现实难题“逼”出来的。近几年，该支队一批新舰接连入列，通过全项资质考核的指挥员却不够用。有的舰艇多项训练任务交织，有的舰艇协同训练推迟，指挥员训练指标积累、独操合格考核受到影响，资质“考不了”与任务“拖不起”的矛盾越来越突出。 破局刻不容缓。针对这一突出矛盾，该支队党委一班人研究决定，探索集约化组训新模式，由支队机关统计每艘舰艇的任务情况、时间安排，将数十个考核科目整合为多套训练计划，让不同舰艇承担不同训练考核任务。考核计划下发各舰后，有资质考核需求的指挥员可申请前往承担相应任务的舰艇参加训练考核。 新的组训模式推行以来，训练质效换挡提速：乌鲁木齐舰上，来自驻马店舰和甘孜舰的副舰长灵活应对、沉着处置，完成航行操纵、系离浮筒等科目考核；潍坊舰上，烟台舰副舰长围绕对海攻击、对空防御、损害管制等科目展开专攻精练，在各战位密切协同下顺利完成考核。 为进一步提高训练效益，该支队同步开通“训练班车”和“训练专车”——“训练班车”围绕通用科目组织，让来自不同舰艇的同专业人员集中学习、共同训练；“训练专车”聚焦专业科目开展精细化强训，针对单类人员能力短板精准发力。两种方式相辅相成，一次出航集中强化训练一批骨干，让有限的训练资源发挥最大训练效能。 记者在该支队采访时看到：机关办公室里，作训参谋忙着汇总不同舰艇的训练需求，协调适配舰艇、沟通保障细节；舰艇舱室内，某舰作战值更官在待考核科目旁逐一打钩，报备训考计划；军港码头上，刚完成大项任务的某舰部门长，直奔兄弟舰艇参加编队运动科目补训…… 前不久的一次远海海域导弹实射训练中，鞍山舰迎来了甘孜舰舰长等3名跟训人员。训练中，跟训人员全程参与想定作业与分组推演，训练结束后进一步展开自评互评，交流指挥心得。“跨舰交流切磋，让我找准了自身短板，指挥能力得到实打实的锻炼和提升。”甘孜舰舰长告诉记者。 “训练双车”机制施行以来，该支队集约统筹训练资源，训练质效大幅提升，官兵训练热情持续高涨。过去一年间，多名舰艇指挥员依托这一机制顺利通过岗位资质考核，大批一专多能型干部在重大任务中崭露头角。 该支队领导表示，他们将持续优化组训模式，紧盯练兵备战薄弱环节，推动“训练双车”从海上向岸上延伸、从一域向多域拓展，全方位抓好备战打仗人才培养储备，不断推动练兵备战迈上新台阶。 让有限资源释放更大效能 ■杨悦 兵之胜负，在于谋势；势之强弱，在于统筹。练兵场上，资源统筹的精准程度，直接关系到一线官兵的战斗力生成速度。各自为战、分散施训的组训模式，容易导致资源闲置与重复投入等问题，极大制约训练质效提升。推行集约化组训，无疑是盘活练兵资源、破解训练瓶颈的关键解法。 集约化组训的核心，是树立训练资源“一盘棋”理念。海军某支队推行同舰共训模式，正是集约化组训的生动实践——一艘舰艇的航迹，成为多艘舰艇人才淬火的熔炉，不仅让该支队告别了“单舰独训、资源浪费”的粗放式练兵困境，更有效解决了人才考核面临的实际问题。他们的创新实践，让我们看到了军事训练向集约化变革的一条有效路径：只有主动打破建制壁垒、本位思维，才能让有限的训练平台、装备资源释放出更大练兵效能。 “搭车”赶考记 ■海军某支队干部 段春杰 站在青岛舰甲板上，看着海面翻滚的浪花，我想起了担任南昌舰部门负责人的那段经历。那时，舰艇常年担负高强度战备值班任务，我几乎每天都忙得脚不沾地。 部署操演准备、装备维护保养、文书报表填报……繁杂的值班工作和行政事务，占据了我的绝大部分时间和精力。一次岗位资质考核，我早早做好了迎考准备，却临时受命执行紧急任务，只好作罢。下一次考核，还得等半年多时间。 看着同期战友陆续通过考核、走上关键岗位，我内心的焦灼与日俱增。 支队探索推行“训练双车”机制，让我看到了机会。我第一时间申请搭乘青岛舰“训练班车”，希望借此补齐“欠账”。 一开始，面对新的训练环境，我有些“水土不服”。青岛舰的指挥口令节奏更快，协同习惯也与我服役的舰艇略有不同。第一次指挥航行操纵训练，因与号手沟通不畅，导致整体进度迟缓，训练效果不及预期。 面对挫折，我没有气馁。在青岛舰训练期间，我每天与各号手熟悉协同口令，细化操纵流程；训练间隙，我虚心向青岛舰训练骨干请教组训方法和实操技巧，逐一融入后续训练。 功夫不负有心人。岗位资质考核中，我作为指挥员带领官兵展开航行操纵、系离浮筒、对空抗击等科目训练。大家密切协同、配合默契，最终以优异成绩顺利过关。那一刻，我真正体会到作为舰艇指挥员的成就感和荣誉感。 （解放军报特约通讯员吴頔整理）
