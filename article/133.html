About this analysis
Summary
At this year's National Two Sessions, Xi Jinping addressed the PLA and PAP delegation and called for building a 'revolutionized and specialized' (革命化、专业化) talent contingent, framing political loyalty and warfighting competence as inseparable requirements. The article, published in a special issue on 政治建军 (building the military through politics), elaborates that framework into four concrete talent priorities: joint operations command, new-type combat forces, high-level scientific and technological innovation, and high-level strategic management—the '四类人才.' The framing points to sustained institutional pressure to resolve what Xi has publicly named as the 'two insufficient capabilities' (两个能力不够) problem, namely that PLA cadres cannot adequately command or fight modern warfare, with the centennial army-building deadline sharpening the urgency.
Translation

When Talent Flourishes, the Military Flourishes

■ Xin Shihong

To build a strong nation, we must build a strong military; to build a strong military, we must first build strong talent.

Talent is the key factor in driving our military's high-quality development and in seizing the initiative in military competition and future wars. In opening up a new landscape for building a strong military and invigorating the armed forces, talent is the most foundational and most critical element; in building a world-class military, talent is also the most urgent and most needed element.

At this year's National Two Sessions (全国两会), when Chairman Xi attended the plenary session of the delegation of the People's Liberation Army and the People's Armed Police Force, he emphasized the need to strengthen the building of a revolutionized (革命化) and specialized (专业化) talent contingent, to persistently use the Party's innovative theories to forge the soul and cultivate people, to solidly lay the ideological foundation for officers and soldiers to heed the Party's words and follow the Party's lead, and to ensure that modernized weapons and equipment remain in the hands of a revolutionized talent contingent.

Revolutionization determines the political character, loyalty, and value pursuits of talent; specialization underpins talent's ability to win battles, fulfill duties, and innovate. The two complement each other and are inseparable.

When talent flourishes, the military flourishes. Our military must give full play to the unique advantages of building the military through politics (政治建军), comprehensively implement the strategy of strengthening the military through talent (人才强军) in the new era, and, with more proactive, more dynamic, and more effective talent policies, forge a large-scale, structurally sound, and high-quality revolutionized and specialized military talent formation (人才方阵).

Striving through one hundred years of army-building, pressing forward toward a first-class strong military. The People's Army, which is about to celebrate its centennial, calls for an abundance of talent, a gathering of outstanding figures, elite soldiers and strong commanders, and ten thousand horses galloping forward.

I

At the founding of New China, with everything in ruins and waiting to be rebuilt, large numbers of talented people were urgently needed to serve national construction. In February 1950, Zhu Guangya, who had earned a doctorate from the University of Michigan, declined generous treatment abroad and returned to the motherland he had missed day and night. Before returning, he took the lead in drafting "An Open Letter to Fellow Students Studying in the United States," and soon 52 patriotic sons and daughters who had decided to return to the embrace of the motherland signed their names to this open letter.

Inspired by this open letter, a large number of experts, scholars, and outstanding overseas students returned to work in China, making major contributions to the development of New China's various undertakings, especially in science and technology. Today, in the Malan Martyrs' Cemetery, the tombstone of Academician Zhu Guangya—a founding contributor to the "Two Bombs, One Satellite" (两弹一星) program—stands quietly.

Talent is the foundation of a strong nation; talent is the basis for realizing dreams. In today's world, competition for talent has become an important battlefield in the contest among great powers. Whoever possesses advanced talent holds the initiative in competition and development.

National development depends on talent; national rejuvenation depends on talent. As Chairman Xi has emphasized: "We are closer than at any other time in history to achieving the great goal of the rejuvenation of the Chinese nation, and we are also more eager for talent than at any other time in history."

Victory is decided before battle is joined; victory is decided by talent. Military competition is, at its root, competition for talent. In modern warfare, without talent, with insufficient talent, or with talent of insufficient quality, it is impossible to win battles! Modernization efforts and preparations for military struggle certainly cannot do without funding and equipment support, but the most core issue is the question of talent.

In 1950, when Comrade Qian Xuesen proposed to return to China, then-U.S. Under Secretary of the Navy Dan Kimball resolutely opposed it, declaring: "Qian Xuesen is worth five Marine divisions; I would rather have him shot in America than let him leave." At that time, across the entire Korean battlefield, U.S. ground forces numbered seven divisions.

On February 1, 1956, Comrade Mao Zedong said to Qian Xuesen, who had already returned to China: "I hear the Americans consider you worth five divisions! In my view, for us, you are worth far more than five divisions!"

Talent resources are the primary resource; talent advantage is a decisive advantage. In the military domain, talent is the most dynamic, commanding, and transformative primary element; the degree to which all other elements are realized depends on the exercise of this element.

During the two years and nine months of the War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea, one of the main reasons the Chinese People's Volunteer Army was able to defeat the United Nations forces led by the United States and win the great victory of the war was that our military had been tempered through more than twenty years of revolutionary warfare, and the broad ranks of officers and soldiers had acquired the ability and experience to defeat an enemy with superior equipment using inferior equipment—and in particular, a cohort of outstanding commanders had been cultivated who dared to ride into battle with swords raised, were courageous in shouldering responsibility for the nation, and were skilled at fighting decisive battles to achieve decisive victories.

Since the 18th National Congress of the Party, in response to problems such as "two large gaps" (两个差距很大), "two insufficient capabilities" (两个能力不够), and "five don't-knows" (五个不会), Chairman Xi has repeatedly issued to the entire military the "question of winning battles" (胜战之问), the "question of values" (价值叩问), and the "question of competence" (本领拷问). A very important reason these problems have arisen is that our military personnel's capability and quality are not comprehensive enough, their structural layout is not rational enough, and their development and management are not efficient enough.

New-type warfare calls for new-type talent. Whoever possesses more high-quality, specialized, new-type military talent will be able to seize the strategic initiative in military competition and lead in future warfare. Faced with changes in technology, changes in warfare, and changes in adversaries, to achieve victory on future battlefields, we must seize talent as the decisive factor, strive to forge a talent formation matched to the form of modern warfare, and use a powerful talent advantage to win battlefield superiority.

II

"On the banks of the Yellow River, a group of outstanding sons and daughters of the Chinese nation have gathered…" Yan'an, that red land that nurtured the Chinese revolution, gave birth to a famous military academy—the Chinese People's Anti-Japanese Military and Political University (抗日军政大学, "Kangda"). In just under ten years, Kangda ran eight classes, spawned fourteen branch schools, and cultivated more than 100,000 military and political cadres of both virtue and ability. In 1955, when the People's Liberation Army held its first conferral of military ranks, six marshals, eight generals, twenty-six lieutenant generals, forty-nine major generals, and one hundred and twenty-nine brigadier generals had worked or studied at Kangda.

In 1938, Comrade Mao Zedong personally came to Kangda to lecture on multiple occasions—"a small lecture every three days, a major lecture every five days." When answering the question "What should be studied at Kangda?", he told the students: "First, learn a political direction."

Not long ago, Chairman Xi personally decided to hold a training course for senior cadres of the entire military at the National Defense University, attended the opening ceremony, and delivered an important speech. He emphasized the need to be guided by the Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era, to thoroughly implement the Thought on Strengthening the Military for the New Era (新时代强军思想), to carry out ideological rectification (思想整风), to deepen political consolidation training (政治整训), to strengthen the revolutionary tempering (革命性锻造) of our military's cadre contingent, to always maintain the purity and glory of the People's Army, and to greet the centennial of the founding of the army with a completely new political bearing. This provided a profound lesson in ideological and political education, Party spirit forging, and warning education for cadres at all levels of the military, especially senior cadres.

The political standard is the foremost standard for our military's talent; the political requirement is the most fundamental requirement for our military's talent.

Since the 18th National Congress of the Party, the entire military has deeply studied and implemented Xi Jinping's Thought on Strengthening the Military, profoundly grasped Chairman Xi's important statements on strengthening the military through talent, consistently maintained the correct political direction in talent work, persisted in cultivating, examining, and employing talent from a political standpoint, implemented the standards for good cadres in the military, cultivated "Four-Haves" (四有) revolutionary soldiers for the new era, and carried the Party's absolute leadership over the military through every aspect and the entire process of talent work, ensuring that the barrel of the gun is always held in the hands of those who are loyal and reliable to the Party.

Proletarian parties and armies have always regarded the cultivation and selection of leading cadres who possess both virtue and ability as a major issue bearing on the overall situation. Lenin pointed out: "We must study people, we must find capable workers. That is where the key lies now."

Upholding the Party's management of cadres, the Party's management of talent, and organizational selection of personnel—highlighting the political attributes of cadre management and talent management—is a precious experience repeatedly validated over the Party's hundred years of struggle, and is also the basic principle by which our Party and military select and employ personnel. Our military's cadres are the Party's cadres; they are not the private property of any particular level or any particular individual, and we must absolutely never allow them to become something decided by a single person or a small number of people.

As early as 1938, Comrade Mao Zedong put forward the important idea of "the Party manages cadres" (党管干部) at the Sixth Plenary Session of the Sixth Central Committee of the Party, establishing the principles of "selecting people on the basis of merit" (任人唯贤) and "combining virtue with ability" (才德兼备) for selecting and employing personnel, and thereby formulating and implementing a correct cadre line and cadre policy that guaranteed the victory of the revolution.

Our military is an armed group that carries out the Party's political tasks and is a strong pillar for defending the red country and upholding national dignity. Maintaining high standards and strict requirements for military talent in political terms is a concrete embodiment of our military's approach of building the military through politics and a key measure in strengthening the military through talent; it reflects the special nature of the military profession and the essential requirements of the laws governing the growth of military talent.

Strengthening the military must first mean being strong politically; being strong politically is the most fundamental form of strength. At all times, every level should run the political standard through the entire process of talent selection, cultivation, and employment; persist in cultivating, examining, and employing talent from a political standpoint; strengthen loyalty identification and political examination work; and ensure that the Party truly and firmly grasps the troops ideologically, politically, and organizationally.

III

Soldiers are born for war; talent is cultivated for war. Our military's talent work is, at its core, about preparing talent for war.

During the revolutionary war years, the Party and the military focused on everything for the front lines and everything for victory, relying on the practice of war to cultivate a large cohort of military talent who heeded the Party's command, were capable of fighting and winning, and were heroically unyielding. Compared with the war years, while our military's mission tasks have differed across different periods, the fundamental function as a fighting force has never changed, and the fundamental orientation of focusing on war preparedness and combat readiness to strengthen talent development has never changed.

In 1950, Comrade Liu Bocheng proactively requested to resign from key positions including Chairman of the Southwest Military and Administrative Committee and Commander of the Second Field Army, in order to establish military academies. That November, he was appointed President of the Military Academy. From the very beginning of running the academy, he vigorously promoted the ethos of studying war, teaching war, and learning war, putting forward: "Cultivating the talent needed to defeat present and future enemies is our sole objective. All courses must revolve around this objective." He also studied the latest battle cases and battlefield developments in connection with the then-ongoing War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea, invited commanders who had returned from the front lines to give lectures, and arranged for a cohort of outstanding students to go to the foreign front lines for experiential observation.

Talent is like the keel of a warship; talent cultivation is "the most arduous war preparation." Chairman Xi has profoundly pointed out: "The military's insufficient capability to fight modern warfare and the insufficient capability of cadres at all levels to command modern warfare are, at their root, the insufficient capability and quality of the talent contingent."

"For extraordinary achievements, one must await extraordinary people." On the new journey, to enhance the capacity and level of talent supply for war preparedness and combat readiness, we must persist in calibrating to the capability requirements of "war," establishing the cultivation standards of "war," firmly establishing the talent-cultivation philosophy of "grounded in war" (基于战), and innovating the talent-cultivation model of "skilled at war" (善于战), so that the "front sight" of talent development is always aimed at the "target" of combat effectiveness development.

"Pure and clear love, only for China." This battle cry written by "post-2000" border defense soldier Chen Xiangrong before his death moved the entire nation. "The regimental commander stood at the very front to block the foreign troops; the battalion commander rescued the regimental commander; soldiers rescued the battalion commander; squad leaders rescued soldiers." Those figures who pressed forward one after another, daring to fight and determined to win, built an indestructible steel Great Wall.

Whatever kind of talent is needed for war, that is the kind of talent to cultivate and employ. Every level should make the ability to fight and win battles the starting point and ultimate goal of talent work; persist in the combat effectiveness standard; persist in keeping a firm grip on powerful adversaries; persist in vigorously conducting realistic combat military training (实战化军事训练); thoroughly sweep away the peacetime maladies inconsistent with the requirements of war preparedness and combat readiness; select and employ the worthy without being bound by convention; continuously enhance the capacity and level of talent supply for war preparedness and combat readiness; and ensure that our military's talent is capable of commanding modern warfare and effectively fulfilling mission tasks in the new era.

In those years, the Japanese military's Okamura Yasuji once boasted: "I would rather sacrifice twenty Japanese soldiers to exchange for one Kangda student, and sacrifice fifty Japanese soldiers to exchange for one Kangda cadre." Why were Kangda students able to fight? This was due to our Party and military's emphasis on giving full play to the university's function of "winning battles" and its role as a "crucible," stressing learning warfare through warfare, regularly organizing instructors and students to directly participate in combat, and improving their capabilities through the baptism of fire.

The capability standard for winning battles is not fixed; it continuously changes with the development of war practice. Compared with winning mechanized warfare, winning informatized and intelligentized warfare (信息化智能化战争) places new demands on the capability structure of talent. This also determines that enhancing the capacity and level of talent supply for war preparedness and combat readiness has only a present tense and no past tense—it requires sustained effort and long-term commitment.

IV

The biography of Qian Xuesen recounts the following story: On the evening of February 1, 1956, Comrade Mao Zedong hosted a banquet at Huairen Hall in Zhongnanhai for all delegates attending the Second Session of the Second National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference. The invitation Qian Xuesen received showed his seat at Table 37. When he arrived at the banquet hall, however, a staff member led him to the seat at Table 1, immediately to the right of Mao Zedong—the position of the foremost honored guest in Chinese traditional etiquette. This fully reflected the Party's and the state's eagerness for and emphasis on talent, and also ignited the strong desire of more overseas scientists to return to work in China.

He who wishes to catch fish must first clear the waterway; he who wishes to attract birds must first plant trees. To forge a powerful magnetic field that attracts and concentrates talent, Party organizations and leading cadres at all levels must proactively cultivate a growth environment that respects talent and earnestly seeks the worthy; a living environment with appropriate treatment and freedom from worry; and an institutional environment of fairness, equality, and competitive selection of the best—providing strong support for talent in their work and effective guarantees in their lives, so that they can devote themselves wholeheartedly to their undertakings.

In 1978, at the opening ceremony of the National Science Conference, Comrade Deng Xiaoping said: "I am willing to serve as the logistics minister for everyone, and willing to work together with leading comrades of Party committees at all levels to do this work well." Seven years later, at the National Education Work Conference, he again stated: "I once said I was willing to serve as the logistics minister for comrades in education and science and technology departments. Today, I still hold that attitude." This spirit and work style of willingly serving as the "logistics minister" for talent not only greatly encouraged and energized the broad ranks of talented people, but also established a clear orientation of respecting knowledge and respecting talent.

Chairman Xi has pointed out: "We must create a good environment for innovation, accelerate the formation of a cultivation mechanism conducive to talent growth, an employment mechanism conducive to making the best use of talent, an incentive mechanism conducive to competitive growth and the full display of individual abilities, and a competition mechanism conducive to the emergence of all types of talent; cultivate fertile soil for talent growth, so that talent's root systems become more developed and one crop after another grows up strong."

In those years, Mr. Lu Xun, confronting the social reality of old China where "ten thousand horses stand mute—what a lament" (万马齐喑究可哀), called out loudly: to produce geniuses, one must first become the soil that nurtures geniuses—"for example, if you want tall trees and beautiful flowers, you must first have good soil"; "the merit of being soil is more immediate than demanding geniuses."

To create a good environment for identifying, loving, respecting, and employing talent, Party organizations and leading cadres at all levels must persist in the unity of strict management and caring trust; give full political trust to all types of talent, proactively guide them ideologically, create conditions for them in their work, and care for them in their lives; rectify the chronic problem of only wanting to control talent; establish the philosophy of serving talent and removing constraints on talent—thereby fully stimulating the enthusiasm, initiative, and creativity of the broad ranks of talented people in their work and undertakings.

In the course of leading the establishment of the Harbin Military Engineering Institute, Comrade Chen Geng showed great respect for experts and professors, serving them with wholehearted enthusiasm and providing convenience for their scientific research work. He often said that instructors are those who "cook the food," students are those who "eat the food," and leaders are those who "carry the plates." It was precisely with this spirit of willingly "carrying plates" for talent that the "Harbin Military Engineering Institute" (哈军工) produced generation after generation of outstanding talent.

Mountains are never too high; seas are never too deep. For all types of talent, if Party organizations and leading cadres at all levels care about both their material needs and their spiritual needs, both create conditions for them in their work and help them resolve difficulties in their lives, they will certainly be able to open up a vibrant situation in which everyone aspires to develop talent, everyone strives to develop talent, everyone can develop talent, and everyone gives full play to their talent.

V

The story of Mao Sui recommending himself is familiar to many, but few know how Mao Sui's life ended. There is a popular account that goes: Mao Sui stood out in the state of Zhao because of his outstanding eloquence, but when faced with an invasion by the state of Yan, King Xiaocheng of Zhao insisted on sending Mao Sui to lead the army into battle. The result was that Mao Sui, whose tongue was hard but whose fists were soft, was quickly and disastrously defeated—"Mao Sui recommending himself" (毛遂自荐) ultimately became "Mao Sui taking his own life" (毛遂自刎).

"Each talent has what it is suited for; use it in the right way and talent shines; use it in the wrong way and talent dims." Talent is cultivated, but even more so it is brought out through use. Simply put, this means using people according to their strengths, at the right time, and in the right position, so as to maximize the value of talent.

"In governing all under heaven, the only foundation is the use of people; all else is secondary." Selecting and employing people has always been the "root of survival or destruction, the pivot of order or chaos" for a nation and a military.

Chairman Xi has profoundly pointed out: "Employ one worthy person and all worthy persons will come; when people see the worthy and think to emulate them, it becomes a prevailing trend. Whom you select is a weather vane; it determines what kind of cadre work style there will be, and indeed what kind of Party conduct there will be."

"Employ the upright and those who do good will all be encouraged; mistakenly employ the wicked and those who are not good will compete to advance." Whether the selection and employment of people is fair, good, and accurate not only bears on the overall situation of military development and the outcome of future wars, but also bears on the cohesion of hearts and minds, and carries a very important guiding function.

By what standard to select people and what kind of people to select is the foremost question in cadre work. When those who are good at currying favor crowd out those who are good at getting things done, when those skilled at scheming displace those skilled at studying warfare, and "bad currency" openly drives out "good currency"—like discarding a great bell and amplifying the noise of a clay pot—this will inevitably inflict fatal harm on the military.

In May 1930, the Red Fifth Army surrounded Xiushui County Town, with its subordinate Fifth Column assigned the main assault. Because the city walls were solid and easy to defend but hard to attack, the column launched several charges without breaking through. At the critical moment, Huang Kecheng, Political Commissar of the Eighth Detachment, volunteered for the mission and led officers and soldiers in launching another attack. He took the lead, climbing the scaling ladder first, with officers and soldiers following close behind. After a night of fierce fighting, the Red Army annihilated the defending enemy and captured Xiushui County Town. Peng Dehuai, then serving as army commander, firmly remembered the bespectacled man who was the first to scale the city wall, and said with great admiration: "One battle lets us know one person; the battle of Xiushui let us know Huang Kecheng."

In the war years, a hundred battles produced a hundred commanders. To assess a cadre's capabilities, one looked primarily at performance on the battlefield; after several fierce and hard battles, who was superior and who was inferior was plain to see. Comrade Li Desheng once vividly said: "The standard for selecting cadres in the war years was very simple: first, whether one could wield a broadsword against the enemy; second, whether one's voice was loud enough to command and awe people."

"Of all difficult matters, none is more difficult than knowing people; of all important matters, none is more important than knowing people." In peacetime, with the yardstick of knowing people through battle gone, whether one can precisely identify outstanding cadres in all respects and employ them rationally—giving talent the optimal configuration and enabling them to play the greatest role—is undoubtedly an extremely challenging subject.

"Are there no thousand-li horses in the world? It is rare to find a Jiufang Gao among men." Party organizations and leading cadres at all levels should cultivate and select cadres according to the standards for good cadres in the military; seize on the key point and focal point of incentivizing cadres to take on responsibilities and act; and build a quality cultivation system, a system for knowing affairs and identifying people, a selection and appointment system, a strict management system, and a positive incentive system with the characteristics of our military. Persist in selecting and employing people with integrity and impartiality; put to use cadres who are loyal, clean, and willing to take on responsibilities; configure leadership bodies at all levels well; retain high-level talent and urgently needed talent; give those who run around seeking favors, those who demand things, and those who coast along no opportunity to exploit—and strive to form a healthy atmosphere in which those who want to fight remain, those who can fight are employed, and those who win battles advance.

VI

For any political party, any nation, any military, a hundred years is a major historical milestone of extraordinary significance.

Achieving the centennial goal of army-building on schedule is the military order we have pledged to the Party and the people; it is a hard task that bears on the overall situation, must be accomplished without fail, and admits no loss.

At present, our military is, in accordance with the new "three-step" strategic arrangement for national defense and military modernization, continuing to fight the decisive battle to achieve the centennial goal of army-building. Profound transformations are occurring across military theory, weapons and equipment, organizational forms, and military personnel.

The core of military modernization is the modernization of people. Faced with the new situation of international strategic competition, the new trends in the evolution of warfare forms, and the new circumstances in the development of the cause of strengthening the military, we must strengthen the sense of urgency that we cannot afford to wait and the sense of crisis that we cannot afford to slow down, ensure that the modernization of military personnel achieves major progress, and achieve major breakthroughs in talent in key domains—providing a strong talent guarantee and intellectual support for achieving the centennial goal of army-building on schedule.

— Anchor to the "four categories of talent" (四类人才) and advance systematically. Chairman Xi has explicitly pointed out the need to systematically advance the cultivation of "four categories of talent": joint operations command, new-type combat forces, high-level scientific and technological innovation, and high-level strategic management—achieving coordinated development between the capability and quality of people and the practice of strengthening the military. Different eras place different demands on military talent. Every step forward in the practice of strengthening the military is inseparable from the powerful driving force of talent; every leap in the capability and quality of talent is closely attuned to the pulse of the times in the practice of strengthening the military. These "four categories of talent" are what is urgently needed for current war preparedness and combat readiness and the focus of military development. Only by continuously strengthening the strategic layout of talent work and innovating talent cultivation models—accelerating the advancement of military talent's scientific and technological literacy to keep pace with the development of the military revolution, command literacy to keep pace with the evolution of modern warfare, and governance literacy to keep pace with the needs of high-quality development—can the military talent contingent keep up with the pace of the times, match strategic demands, and support the mission of winning battles.

— Concentrate the "trinity" (三位一体) talent-cultivation synergy. Military academy education, unit training practice, and military professional education, as the "three horses" of new-type military talent cultivation, are both interrelated and mutually connected, yet also functionally distinct and each with its own emphasis; the three constitute an organically unified whole. It takes ten years to grow a tree; it takes a hundred years to cultivate people. Party organizations at all levels should strengthen the educational concept of "the entire military runs education, jointly cultivates talent" (全军办教育、联合育人才), treat talent cultivation as a long-term plan, effectively connect the talent cultivation chain linking academies and units, theory and practice, classrooms and battlefields, give full play to the leading role of teaching, stimulate the intrinsic motivation of learning, establish the realistic combat standard of training, and enhance the comprehensive effectiveness of management—truly achieving joint cultivation, open cultivation, and full-process cultivation.

— Innovate military human resource management. To do our military's talent work well and advance the modernization of military personnel, scientific and effective management plays an important role. We should innovate management concepts and methods, comprehensively advance reform and innovation across all aspects of identifying, concentrating, cultivating, and employing talent, and drive the comprehensive transformation and upgrading of military personnel's capability and quality, structural layout, and development and management. Continuously deepen the reform of military human resource policies and systems; systematically plan career development paths for all categories of personnel; strengthen the supporting development of policies and systems; form a talent cultivation and employment model with the characteristics of our military; let the creative vitality of all types of talent burst forth in competition and their wisdom and abilities flow fully—and strive to write a new chapter in building a strong military and invigorating the armed forces that is worthy of our revolutionary forebears, worthy of the century-long foundation, and worthy of this great era.

新中国成立之初，百废待兴，急需大量人才为国家建设服务。1950年2月，获得美国密执安大学博士学位的朱光亚谢绝国外的优厚待遇，回到日夜思念的祖国。回国前，他牵头起草了《给留美同学的一封公开信》，很快就有52名决定回到祖国怀抱的赤子，在这封公开信上签下自己的名字。 在这封公开信的感召下，一大批专家、学者和优秀留学人员纷纷回国工作，为新中国各项事业特别是科技事业发展作出重大贡献。如今，在马兰烈士陵园，静静地矗立着“两弹一星”元勋朱光亚院士的墓碑。 人才是强国之本，人才是圆梦之基。当今世界，人才竞争已成为大国博弈的重要战场。谁拥有先进人才，谁就掌握了竞争和发展的主动权。 国家发展靠人才，民族振兴靠人才。正如习主席所强调的：“我们比历史上任何时期都更接近实现中华民族伟大复兴的宏伟目标，我们也比历史上任何时期都更加渴求人才。” 决胜于未战，决胜于人才。军事竞争归根到底是人才竞争。在现代战争中，没有人才、人才不足、人才质量不高，是打不了胜仗的！搞现代化建设、抓军事斗争准备，固然离不开经费和装备保障，但最核心的是人才问题。 1950年，钱学森同志提出回国，时任美国海军部副部长丹尼尔·金贝尔坚决反对，他称：“一个钱学森抵得上5个海军陆战师，我宁可把他击毙在美国，也不能让他离开。”那个时候，在整个朝鲜战场，美军的地面兵力是7个师。 1956年2月1日，毛泽东同志对已经回国的钱学森说：“听说美国人把你当成5个师呢！我看呀，对我们来说，你比5个师的力量大多啦！” 人才资源是第一资源，人才优势是具有决定性的优势。在军事领域，人才是最具活力和支配力、变革力的主体要素，其他要素的实现程度都有赖于这一要素的发挥。 在2年零9个月的抗美援朝战争中，中国人民志愿军之所以能够打败以美国为首的“联合国军”，赢得战争的伟大胜利，一个主要原因是我军经受了20多年革命战争的锤炼，广大官兵拥有了以劣势装备战胜优势装备之敌的能力和经验，特别是培养出了一批敢于横刀立马、勇于为国担当、善于决战决胜的优秀指挥员。 党的十八大以来，针对“两个差距很大”“两个能力不够”“五个不会”等问题，习主席多次向全军发出“胜战之问”“价值叩问”“本领拷问”。出现这些问题，很重要的原因就在于我军军事人员能力素质不够全面、结构布局不够合理、开发管理不够高效。 新型战争呼唤新型人才。谁拥有更多高素质专业化新型军事人才，谁就能掌握军事竞争的战略主动，执未来战争之牛耳。面对科技之变、战争之变、对手之变，要制胜未来战场，就必须抓住人才这个决定性因素，努力打造与现代战争形态相匹配的人才方阵，以强大人才优势赢得战场胜势。 二 “黄河之滨，集合着一群中华民族优秀的子孙……”延安，这片孕育了中国革命的红色热土，诞生了一所著名军校——中国人民抗日军政大学。前后不到10年，抗大办学8期，衍生出14所分校，培养出10万余名德才兼备的军政干部。1955年，中国人民解放军首次授衔，6位元帅、8位大将、26位上将、49位中将和129位少将曾在抗大工作和学习过。 1938年，毛泽东同志多次亲临抗大授课，“三天一小讲，五天一大讲”。在回答“在抗大应当学习什么”时，他告诉学员们，“首先是学一个政治方向”。 前不久，习主席亲自决策在国防大学举办全军高级干部培训班，出席开班式并发表重要讲话，强调要以新时代中国特色社会主义思想为指导，深入贯彻新时代强军思想，开展思想整风，深化政治整训，加强我军干部队伍革命性锻造，始终保持人民军队纯洁光荣，以崭新政治面貌迎接建军一百周年。这为军队各级干部特别是高级干部上了一堂深刻的思想政治课、党性淬炼课、警示教育课。 政治标准是我军人才第一位的标准，政治要求是对我军人才最根本的要求。 党的十八大以来，全军深入学习贯彻习近平强军思想，深刻领会习主席关于人才强军重要论述，始终保持人才工作正确政治方向，坚持从政治上培养、考察、使用人才，落实军队好干部标准，培养“四有”新时代革命军人，把党对军队绝对领导贯彻到人才工作各方面和全过程，确保枪杆子始终掌握在对党忠诚可靠的人手中。 无产阶级政党和军队，始终把培养选拔德才兼备的领导干部视为关系全局的重大问题。列宁指出：“要研究人，要发现有才干的工作人员。现在关键就在这里。” 坚持党管干部、党管人才、组织选人，突出干部管理、人才管理的政治属性，是我党百年奋斗历程反复验证的一条宝贵经验，也是我党我军选人用人的基本原则。我军干部是党的干部，不是哪一级、哪个人的私产，决不能搞成个别人说了算、少数人说了算。 早在1938年，毛泽东同志在党的六届六中全会上就提出“党管干部”的重要思想，确立了“任人唯贤”“才德兼备”的选人用人原则，由此制定和实施正确的干部路线和干部政策，保障了革命的胜利。 我军是执行党的政治任务的武装集团，是保卫红色江山、维护民族尊严的坚强柱石。对军事人才在政治上坚持高标准、严要求，是我军政治建军的实际体现和人才强军的关键举措，反映了军事职业的特殊性和军事人才成长规律的本质要求。 强军首先要在政治上强，政治上强是最根本的强。无论任何时候，各级都应把政治标准贯穿人才选拔、培养、使用全过程，坚持从政治上培养、考察、使用人才，加强忠诚度鉴别和政治考察工作，确保党真正从思想上政治上组织上牢牢掌握部队。 三 军人向战而生，人才为战而育。我军人才工作说到底是为战争准备人才。 革命战争年代，党和军队聚焦一切为了前线、一切为了胜利，依托战争实践，培养了一大批听党指挥、能征善战、英勇不屈的军事人才。与战争年代相比，我军虽然不同时期担负的使命任务不同，但作为战斗队的根本职能始终没有变，聚焦备战打仗加强人才建设的根本导向始终没有变。 1950年，刘伯承同志主动请求辞去西南军政委员会主席、第二野战军司令员等要职，去创办军事院校。那年11月，他被任命为军事学院院长。办学伊始，他就大兴研战、教战、学战之风，提出“培养战胜现在和将来敌人所需的人才，是我们的唯一目标。所有课程都要围绕这个目标”。他还结合当时正在进行的抗美援朝战争，研究最新的战例和战场动态，邀请从一线归国的指挥员登台授课，安排一批优秀学员赴异国前线体验见习。 人才犹如战舰之龙骨，人才培养是“最艰巨的战争准备”。习主席深刻指出：“军队打现代化战争能力不够、各级干部指挥现代化战争能力不够，说到底是人才队伍能力素质不够。” “盖有非常之功，必待非常之人。”新征程上，提升备战打仗人才供给能力和水平，必须坚持把准“战”的能力需求、立起“战”的培养标准，树牢“基于战”的育人理念、创新“善于战”的育人模式，使人才建设的“准星”始终对准战斗力建设的“靶心”。 “清澈的爱，只为中国。”“00后”边防战士陈祥榕生前写下的这句战斗口号，让举国为之动容。“团长顶在最前面阻挡外军，营长救团长、战士救营长、班长救战士。”那些前赴后继、敢打必胜的身影，筑起了一道坚不可摧的钢铁长城。 打仗需要什么样的人才，就培养使用什么样的人才。各级应把能打仗、打胜仗作为人才工作的出发点和落脚点，坚持战斗力标准，坚持扭住强敌对手，坚持大抓实战化军事训练，彻底扫除与备战打仗要求不符的和平积弊，不拘一格选贤任能，不断提升备战打仗人才供给能力和水平，确保我军人才能够驾驭现代战争、有效履行新时代使命任务。 当年，日军的冈村宁次曾叫嚣：“宁肯牺牲20个日本兵换一个抗大学员，牺牲50个日本兵换一个抗大干部。”抗日军政大学的学员为什么能打仗？这得益于我党我军注重发挥大学的“胜战”功能、“熔炉”作用，强调在战争中学习战争，经常组织教员、学员直接参与战斗，在战火的洗礼中提高本领。 能打胜仗的能力标准并非一成不变，而是随着战争实践发展而不断变化。与打赢机械化战争相比，打赢信息化智能化战争，对人才能力结构提出了新的需求。这也决定了提高备战打仗人才供给能力和水平，只有进行时、没有完成时，必须持续用力、久久为功。 四 《钱学森传》里面讲了这样一个故事：1956年2月1日晚，毛泽东同志在中南海怀仁堂招待参加全国政协二届二次会议的全体委员。钱学森收到的请柬上显示，自己的座位在第37桌。他到了宴会厅后，却被工作人员领到第一桌紧挨毛泽东的右侧座位——我国传统礼仪中的第一贵宾位置。这充分体现出党和国家对人才的渴求和重视，也点燃了更多海外科学家回国工作的强烈愿望。 欲致鱼者先通水，欲致鸟者先树木。打造吸纳人才、集聚人才的强磁场，需要各级党组织和领导干部积极营造尊重人才、求贤若渴的成长环境，待遇适当、后顾无忧的生活环境，公正平等、竞争择优的制度环境，为人才在工作上提供有力支持、在生活上提供有效保障，使其心无旁骛干事创业。 1978年，在全国科学大会开幕式上，邓小平同志说：“我愿意当大家的后勤部长，愿意同各级党委的领导同志一起，做好这方面的工作。”7年后，他在全国教育工作会议上再次表态：“我曾说过，愿意给教育、科技部门的同志当后勤部长。今天，我还是这个态度。”这种甘为人才当好“后勤部长”的思想和作风，不但使广大人才深受鼓舞、干劲倍增，更树立起尊重知识、尊重人才的鲜明导向。 习主席指出：“要营造良好创新环境，加快形成有利于人才成长的培养机制、有利于人尽其才的使用机制、有利于竞相成长各展其能的激励机制、有利于各类人才脱颖而出的竞争机制，培植好人才成长的沃土，让人才根系更加发达，一茬接一茬茁壮成长。” 当年，鲁迅先生针对旧中国“万马齐喑究可哀”的社会现状大声疾呼：欲要天才，先要做培养天才的泥土，“譬如想有乔木，想看好花，一定要有好土”“做土的功效，比要求天才还切近”。 营造识才爱才敬才用才的良好环境，需要各级党组织和领导干部坚持严格管理和关心信任相统一，对各类人才在政治上充分信任、思想上主动引导、工作上创造条件、生活上关心照顾，纠治只想把人才管住的顽症，立起为人才服务、为人才松绑的理念，从而把广大人才干事创业的积极性、主动性、创造性充分激发出来。 陈赓同志在领导创办哈尔滨军事工程学院的过程中，非常尊重专家、教授，满腔热忱地为他们搞好服务，给他们的科研工作提供方便。他常说，教员是“做饭做菜”的，学员是“吃饭吃菜”的，领导是“端盘子”的。正是凭着这种乐为人才“端盘子”的精神，才有了英才辈出的“哈军工”。 山不厌高，海不厌深。对于各类人才，各级党组织和领导干部既关心其物质需求，还关心其精神需求；既为其在工作上创造条件，还为其在生活上排忧解难，就一定能够开创人人渴望成才、人人努力成才、人人皆可成才、人人尽展其才的生动局面。 五 毛遂自荐的故事，许多人耳熟能详，但了解毛遂人生结局的人却不多。坊间有这样一种说法：毛遂因为口才出众在赵国脱颖而出，可是在面临燕国入侵时，赵孝成王执意让毛遂带领军队去迎战。结果，舌头硬拳头软的毛遂很快惨败，“毛遂自荐”最后成了“毛遂自刎”。 “人才各有所宜，用得其宜，则才著；用非其宜，则才晦。”人才是培养出来的，更是用出来的。简单地说，就是用当其长、用当其时、用当其位，实现人才价值的最大化。 “治天下惟以用人为本，其他皆枝叶事耳”。选人用人从来就是一个国家、一支军队的“存亡之本，治乱之机”。 习主席深刻指出：“用一贤人则群贤毕至，见贤思齐就蔚然成风。选什么人就是风向标，就有什么样的干部作风，乃至就有什么样的党风。” “用得正人，为善者皆劝；误用恶人，不善者竞进。”选人用人公不公、好不好、准不准，不仅关系部队建设全局，关系未来战争胜负，而且关系人心聚散，有着十分重要的导向作用。 以什么样的标准选人、选什么样的人，是干部工作的首要问题。当会来事的挤走会干事的，当善钻营的淘汰善钻战的，“劣币”堂而皇之地淘汰“良币”，如同黄钟毁弃、瓦釜雷鸣，必然会给军队带来致命的伤害。 1930年5月，红五军包围修水县城，所属第五纵队担任主攻。由于城墙坚固、易守难攻，纵队发起几次冲锋都未能突破。关键时刻，第八大队政治委员黄克诚主动请缨，带领官兵再次发起进攻。他身先士卒，抢先爬上云梯，官兵紧随其后。经过一夜激战，红军全歼守敌，占领了修水县城。时任军长的彭德怀牢牢记住了那个戴眼镜第一个冲上城墙的人，十分赞赏地说：“打一仗让我们认识一个人，打修水让我们认识了黄克诚。” 战争年代，百战生百将。看一名干部能力如何，主要看战场上的表现，几场恶仗硬仗打下来，孰优孰劣一目了然。李德生同志曾形象地说：“战争年代选拔干部的标准很简单，一是能否和敌人甩开大刀片子，二是嗓子亮不亮，能否震慑住人。” “事之至难，莫如知人；事之至大，亦莫如知人。”和平年代，少了以战识人的尺子，能否把各方面的优秀干部精准识别出来、合理使用起来，让人才得到最佳配置、发挥最大作用，无疑成为挑战性极强的课题。 “世上岂无千里马，人中难得九方皋。”各级党组织和领导干部应按照军队好干部的标准培养和选拔干部，抓住激励干部担当作为这个关键点和着力点，构建具有我军特色的素质培养体系、知事识人体系、选拔任用体系、从严管理体系、正向激励体系。坚持公道正派选人用人，把忠诚干净担当的干部用起来，把各级领导班子配备好，把高层次人才、紧缺人才保留住，让那些跑、要、混的人无可乘之机，努力形成想战者留、能战者用、胜战者上的良好风气。 六 对任何一个政党、一个国家、一支军队来说，百年都是重大历史节点，具有非凡意义。 如期实现建军一百年奋斗目标，是我们向党和人民立下的军令状，是事关全局、务期必成、不容有失的硬任务。 当前，我军正按照国防和军队现代化新“三步走”战略安排，接续打好实现建军一百年奋斗目标攻坚战，军事理论、武器装备、组织形态、军事人员等各方面都在发生深刻变革。 军队现代化的核心是人的现代化。面对国际战略竞争新形势，面对战争形态演变新态势，面对强军事业发展新情况，我们必须强化等不起的紧迫感、慢不得的危机感，确保军事人员现代化取得重大进展，关键领域人才取得重大突破，为如期实现建军一百年奋斗目标提供坚强的人才保证和智力支持。 ——锚定“四类人才”体系推进。习主席明确指出，体系推进联合作战指挥、新型作战力量、高层次科技创新、高水平战略管理“四类人才”培养，实现人的能力素质同强军实践协调发展。不同时代对军事人才有着不同要求。强军实践的每一步推进都离不开人才力量的强劲驱动，人才能力素质的每一次跃升都紧扣着强军实践的时代脉搏。这“四类人才”是当前备战打仗的急需、军队建设的重点。只有不断加强人才工作战略布局、创新人才培养模式，加快推进人才科技素养跟上军事革命发展、指挥素养跟上现代战争演变、治理素养跟上高质量发展需要，才能使军事人才队伍跟上时代步伐、匹配战略需求、支撑打赢使命。 ——凝聚“三位一体”育人合力。军队院校教育、部队训练实践、军事职业教育作为新型军事人才培养的“三驾马车”，既彼此关联、相互贯通，又功能各异、各有侧重，三者构成一个有机统一的整体。十年树木，百年树人。各级党组织应强化“全军办教育、联合育人才”的教育观念，把人才培养作为一项长期规划，有效贯通院校与部队、理论与实践、课堂与战场紧密衔接的人才培养链路，发挥教的主导作用、激发学的内生动力、立起练的实战标准、增强管的综合效益，真正做到联合育人、开放育人、全程育人。 ——创新军事人力资源管理。搞好我军人才工作，推进军事人员现代化，科学有效的管理起着重要作用。应创新管理理念和方式方法，统筹推进识才、聚才、育才、用才等各方面改革创新，推动军事人员能力素质、结构布局、开发管理全面转型升级。持续深化军事人力资源政策制度改革，系统规划各类人员职业发展路径，加强政策制度配套建设，形成具有我军特色的人才培养和使用模式，让各类人才的创造活力竞相迸发、聪明才智充分涌流，努力书写无愧于革命先辈、无愧于百年基业、无愧于伟大时代的强军兴军新篇章。
