Soul Graffiti, AI Confession Booths, Team Interactions… These Activities Unlock the "Heart Lock" of Growing Up

心灵涂鸦、智能树洞、团队互动……这些活动打开成长的"心锁"
PLA Daily (解放军报) 26 May 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
On the May 25 National College Student Mental Health Day, the People's Armed Police Engineering University's Military Psychology Teaching and Research Section ran a series of psychological health activities — including soul graffiti (心灵涂鸦), AI confession booths using a robot called 'Xiao Wu,' VR-assisted systematic desensitization, sandplay therapy (沙盘游戏), and group trust exercises — for cadets experiencing adjustment stress, sports injury trauma, and family conflict over frontier assignments. This is standard institutional mental health content; its value is as a record of how the PAP frames psychological resilience as a component of combat effectiveness and documents the specific clinical techniques — spatial psychological anchoring (空间心理锚定), graduated exposure (阶梯式暴露法), family systems therapy (家庭系统疗法) — the university is now deploying with cadets. The frontier-service storyline also documents how the PAP uses peer mentorship from serving officers, here Wang Chen of a Xinjiang General Corps detachment, to shape cadets' assignment preferences before graduation.

Helping You Unlock the "Heart Lock" of Growing Up ■ Li Zhi, Ma Ying

The People's Armed Police Engineering University carries out psychological health activities in various forms. Photos by Li Zhi and Ma Ying

Psychological health is the deep and fertile soil of spiritual strength. Only when the soil of the soul is firm enough can the green pine of ideals stand tall through wind and rain, and the flower of youth bloom in all its splendor.

Today, as psychological health has increasingly become an important component of combat effectiveness, cadets need not only to master psychological self-regulation skills at military academies — after taking up posts in units, they will equally depend on strong psychological resilience when carrying out diverse military tasks.

On the occasion of the "5·25" National College Student Mental Health Day, military academies across the armed forces launched a series of psychological health activities to care for young cadets, widely disseminating relevant knowledge and helping cadets grow and thrive.

Inside the Psychological Services Research Center of the People's Armed Police Engineering University, Huang Fei, an instructor from the Military Psychology Teaching and Research Section, drew slips of paper one by one from a "soul blind box": "I want to do everything well, but things often don't go as I wish"; "Life at the military academy is monotonous and repetitive, with no sense of novelty"… Faced with these anonymously written "youthful troubles," the cadets present either shared similar experiences, actively offered suggestions, or listened quietly in reflection, together embarking on a journey of self-growth.

Unlocking the "heart lock" of growing up, breaking through the "troubles" of growing up. Soul graffiti (心灵涂鸦), AI confession booths (智能树洞), team interactions, messages to youth (青春寄语)… Through a series of group psychological activities and behavioral training sessions, the cadets gained warm and powerful "heart" energy.

A "Water Drop" Must Merge into the "Ocean" to Avoid Drying Up

Stepping into the Psychological Services Research Center of the People's Armed Police Engineering University, a brightly colored "soul graffiti" wall catches the eye.

"A 'stick figure' with simple lines stands to one side, facing a lively crowd — this hints at the author's inner self-enclosure." Looking at cadet Su Linxin's casual doodle, Gao Li, director of the Military Psychology Teaching and Research Section, read the message behind it.

In conversation, Su Linxin candidly admitted that as the top gaokao scorer in her hometown, "excellence" had once been her defining trait. Yet after entering the military academy, she repeatedly fell behind in military training, and her theoretical studies hovered around the middle of the pack. The unprecedented sense of pressure gradually drove her toward self-enclosure.

"Because of 'upward social comparison' (上行社会比较), she feels she has gone from being excellent to being 'ordinary,' experiencing a kind of 'relative deprivation' (相对剥夺感). The key is to help her integrate into the collective and develop a sense of trust and security within it." After analyzing Su Linxin's situation, Gao Li proposed a solution.

On a raised platform, Su Linxin stood tensely, hearing the voices of her comrades behind her: "Don't worry! We will definitely catch you!"

Receiving encouragement from her comrades, Su Linxin summoned her courage and finally fell backward bravely. Her comrades formed a "safety net" with their arms and caught her steadily.

This "trust fall" (信任背摔) allowed Su Linxin to feel the warmth and strength of the collective, and a sense of belonging gradually took root in her heart.

Afterward, Gao Li deliberately organized a "praise you" activity, asking cadets to speak about each other's strengths.

"Su Linxin is especially attentive — last time I went on sentry duty without eating dinner, and when I came off duty, she had made me a bowl of hot noodles." "She is very resilient; during crawling drills her elbows were scraped raw, but she was back on the training ground the next day."… Hearing her comrades' words, Su Linxin was surprised — she had not expected that others saw so many bright points in her.

The sincere praise and appreciative gazes of her comrades swept away the gloom in Su Linxin's heart. Just as a "water drop" must merge into the "ocean" to avoid drying up, as the distance between hearts drew closer, she became more optimistic and positive.

Today, Su Linxin has become a "warm-hearted person." She proactively stands sentry for sick classmates, accompanies instructors on visits to comfort veterans of the War of Resistance Against Japan, and organizes participation in "Red Story Telling Competitions"… At the same time, she regularly consults her comrades on military training methods, jointly studies theoretical knowledge, and has made gratifying progress.

Words from the Psychology Instructor

Gao Li, Director of the Military Psychology Teaching and Research Section, People's Armed Police Engineering University: Dear cadets, upon entering the gates of a military academy, facing a new environment and unfamiliar classmates, it is inevitable to feel pressure from invisible comparisons, and even to fall into self-imposed loneliness. If you observe carefully, there will always be someone in the collective who is "just like you" — someone who likes playing basketball or listening to music. This "similarity effect" (相似性效应) can quickly close the distance between people. We can use shared interests and hobbies, or a sense of identification in ideas and values, to reduce the sense of strangeness one feels within the collective, and thereby experience the warmth that comes from belonging to it.

Turning the "Stone" in Your Heart into a "Stepping Stone" for Growth

"I can't run the 3-kilometer with my classmates." To "Xiao Wu," the robot at the Psychological Services Research Center, first-year cadet Zhou Chenhao confided a secret he had long kept buried.

When he first enrolled, Zhou Chenhao's physical fitness was poor — he could only finish the 3-kilometer run in 17 minutes. After a period of hard training, he finally brought his time down to 13 minutes, nearly within reach of the passing mark. However, during a routine training session, a classmate tried in good faith to pull him along to finish the full distance, but accidentally knocked him down, causing a torn cruciate ligament in his knee. After recovering from the injury, Zhou Chenhao developed a "psychological ailment" — he was afraid to run with other classmates. Plagued by mental pressure, his performance also regressed.

"Whether facing teachers or classmates, cadets may find it difficult to speak up due to 'herd pressure' (集群压力). Sometimes they are more willing to share their troubles with a stranger. In this regard, with the rapid development of artificial intelligence, robots have opened a new path to addressing this problem." Xie Yeda, an instructor from the Military Psychology Teaching and Research Section, explained the original intent of this "AI confession booth" (智能树洞) activity.

After learning about Zhou Chenhao's situation, "Xiao Wu" first offered verbal guidance, then played a stress-relief video. At the same time, a tailored solution was generated.

"If you're afraid to run on the training ground, you can try our VR running." Stepping onto the VR treadmill, Zhou Chenhao strapped on VR goggles and other equipment. "Xiao Wu" projected the familiar track into a virtual "world" and provided positive psychological suggestion through "spatial psychological anchoring" (空间心理锚定). "This is a classic case of post-traumatic stress disorder (创伤应激障碍) and requires systematic desensitization — letting him gradually confront his past setbacks and dissolve the anxiety and fear response in a state of relaxation," said Xie Yeda.

Watching Zhou Chenhao gradually relax, Instructor Xie continued to guide him: "At this moment, imagine you are running on the training ground." Following the instruction, Zhou Chenhao began to meditate.

"Another classmate has joined." Zhou Chenhao pressed his lips together. Instructor Xie narrated gently at his side: "This is your classmate — you can trust him completely…" Slowly, one "classmate" after another joined Zhou Chenhao, and in his mind he ran faster and faster.

After completing the desensitization and imagery training, Instructor Xie and "Xiao Wu" accompanied Zhou Chenhao to the training ground and walked with him along the track. When they reached the bend where he had been injured, Zhou Chenhao was visibly tense. "Xiao Wu" played soothing music to calm his mood, while Instructor Xie encouraged him verbally and ran alongside him…

In the days that followed, under Instructor Xie's guidance, Zhou Chenhao started running again. As he ran, familiar faces began to appear around him — the "buddy" who slept in the bunk above him, the "partner" from the study interest group… Classmates he spent day and night with chatted about light topics while running, allowing Zhou Chenhao to slowly shed his "psychological burden," his stride growing faster and faster.

"Instructor Xie, thank you — I finally passed the 3-kilometer run." Not long after, Zhou Chenhao excitedly reported his good news to the instructor.

Young cadets will inevitably encounter difficulties in the course of growing up. Setbacks and trauma in particular are like a stone in the heart — they can weigh a person down until they can barely breathe. But as long as one eliminates fear and faces them bravely, these "stones" will become "stepping stones" for growth, helping cadets climb upward from where they fell, step by step out of their psychological困境.

Words from the Psychology Instructor

Xie Yeda, Instructor, Military Psychology Teaching and Research Section, People's Armed Police Engineering University: "The best way to eliminate fear is to face it." Though this is an old saying, it is also the most straightforward truth. Injury on the road of growth is inevitable, but after confronting fear head-on, one discovers that fear is merely an amplification of subjective imagination. We can use the "graduated exposure method" (阶梯式暴露法) to break a feared thing down from large to small. For example, if you are afraid to speak in front of a crowd, you can start by speaking to yourself in a mirror, then to a friend, and finally in front of the whole class — thereby breaking the fixed pattern of psychological shadow, reducing emotional sensitivity, and through direct confrontation, accepting emotions and tempering the mind.

Growing Up Is a Long Journey Carried on the Back of Love and Dreams

"Classmate, is something troubling you?" Noticing that cadet Lin Yishu seemed distracted during a sandplay therapy (沙盘游戏) session, instructor Yang Xinyuan asked with concern.

It turned out that Lin Yishu, who was about to graduate, was determined to serve in a remote and arduous region, but her parents had expressed a different view — they felt she could not endure the hardships of the frontier.

"Why do you want to go to the frontier?" Yang Xinyuan asked.

Lin Yishu opened her phone's photo album, and a dried-leaf bookmark made from a Xinjiang spruce appeared on the screen.

The previous year, the People's Armed Police had organized a lecture by outstanding graduates who had voluntarily gone to the frontier. Their stories of taking root at the frontier and making achievements had moved Lin Yishu. After the event, Wang Chen, from a certain detachment of the Xinjiang General Corps, came to Lin Yishu's dormitory, held a group discussion with everyone, and gave classmates dried-leaf bookmarks.

Wang Chen told Lin Yishu about her experience serving in the Altay region: "It is very beautiful there — a brilliant starry sky, majestic snow-capped mountains, magnificent valleys. This leaf is one I picked up during an early-autumn field operation. But behind that beauty lies the 'hardship' of temperatures dropping to -40°C in winter and slapping a dozen mosquitoes dead with one hand in summer."

Seeing Lin Yishu's admiring gaze, Wang Chen continued: "One winter, herders encountered danger while out in the field, and the unit I was in quickly moved forward to carry out a rescue. After the herders were saved, seeing their smiles and hearing their words of gratitude, I felt my youth had real value. I guard the peace of the frontier and the happiness of the people — my heart is at ease."

Wang Chen's words planted the seed of a desire to voluntarily serve at the frontier in Lin Yishu's heart. But now, with graduation approaching, her parents' opinion had caused her to waver.

After listening to Lin Yishu's account, Yang Xinyuan did not immediately counsel her, but instead brought her to the mini "karaoke room" in the Psychological Services Research Center.

"I want to be the wind — don't worry about me, no matter where I go, I'm on the road to finding myself…" As the theme song "I Want to Be the Wind" from the film "Successor" (《抓娃娃》) played, Lin Yishu watched the music video while recalling scenes from the film, and seemed to gain some understanding of her parents' state of mind.

"Arguing cannot solve the problem. We can use 'family systems therapy' (家庭系统疗法) — first try to understand them, then give them a sense of security, and finally persuade them." Recalling the communication method Instructor Yang had just taught her, Lin Yishu decided to write a letter to her family.

"Mom and Dad, I know you feel for me — you're afraid I'll suffer hardship, afraid you'll miss me and not be able to see me. Please don't worry. Transportation is well-developed now; I promise to come home to see you every time I have leave. Border post facilities are getting better and better — not only can we eat fresh vegetables and fruit, but the barracks are warm in winter and cool in summer… Going to the frontier is your daughter's own wish; your support for me is my greatest happiness." As the words from her heart were set down on paper, the weight pressing on Lin Yishu's chest seemed to lift.

To convince her parents, Lin Yishu also found many moving videos online of young officers and soldiers defending the nation at the frontier, as well as vlogs reflecting the new look of frontier construction and development, and sent them to her parents. "This is the 'concrete evidence persuasion method' (具象化证据说服法) Instructor Yang taught me — using this empirical evidence in place of my 'empty words,' so Mom and Dad can see it with their own eyes," she said.

Not long after, Yang Xinyuan received a text message from Lin Yishu — her family had agreed to her choice, she had written her application to voluntarily serve at the frontier, and she was ready to set off for the frontier at any moment to pursue her dream.

Growing up is a long journey carried on the back of love and dreams. "With love in the heart, everything in sight is beautiful"; "Set forth now toward the stars and the sea, and may you return still young at heart"… On the activity room wall, the "messages to youth" board was covered in heart-shaped sticky notes of every color, filled with the ardent voices of the cadets.

Words from the Psychology Instructor

Yang Xinyuan, Instructor, Military Psychology Teaching and Research Section, People's Armed Police Engineering University: Parents' love often holds them to a higher standard precisely because they "love so deeply." Parents' empiricism and our youthful dreams always seem to be at odds, and love struggles to choose between "sheltering" and "letting go." I suggest everyone avoid communicating with "violent language" (暴力语言) such as "I'm going to do it my way" or "what do you know." Try "role reversal" (角色互演) with your parents — directly experiencing each other's positions and emotions. Build a bridge of the soul through mutual understanding and sincere communication, and carrying their love with you, bravely set out toward the mountains and seas that belong to you.

(At the request of those interviewed, some names in this article are pseudonyms.)

“无论面对老师还是同学，学员都可能迫于‘集群压力’而难以开口。有时候，他们更愿意将自己的烦恼和陌生人交流。在这方面，随着人工智能的快速发展，机器人为解决这一问题开辟了一条新的路径。”军事心理学教研室教员解业达解释了这次“智能树洞”活动的初衷。 了解周宸皓的情况后，“小武”先用语言开导他，然后播放了一段解压视频。同时，一份量身定制的解决方案也生成完毕。 青年学员在成长过程中，难免会遇到困扰。特别是挫折和创伤，如同内心的一块石头，会压得人喘不过气。但是，只要消除恐惧，勇敢地直面和对待它，这些“石头”将会化作成长的“阶梯”，助力学员从跌倒的地方向上攀登，一步步走出心理困境。 心理教员有话说 武警工程大学军事心理学教研室教员解业达：“消除恐惧的最好办法就是面对它。”这虽然是一句老话，却也是最朴素的道理。成长路上难免会受伤，但正视恐惧后会发现，恐惧只是主观臆想的放大。我们可以利用“阶梯式暴露法”，将恐惧的事情由大变小。比如害怕在人群面前发言，可以先对着镜子说，然后跟朋友说，最后在全班面前说，从而打破心理阴影的固有定式，降低情绪敏感度，在直面中接纳情绪、历练心智。 成长是一场背负爱与梦想的远行 “同学，有什么烦恼吗？”观察到学员林依舒在沙盘游戏中心不在焉，教员杨昕媛关心道。 原来，即将毕业的林依舒一心想去边远艰苦地区服役，但父母表达了不同看法，觉得她吃不了边疆的苦。 “为什么想去赴边？”杨昕媛问。 林依舒打开手机相册，一张新疆云杉的干叶书签出现在屏幕上。 去年，武警部队组织了一场优秀志愿赴边毕业学员宣讲会，他们扎根边疆、建功立业的故事打动了林依舒。会后，来自新疆总队某支队的王晨走进林依舒宿舍，与大家一起座谈，还给同学们送了干叶书签。 王晨向林依舒讲起在阿勒泰地区服役的感受：“那里很美，星空璀璨、雪山巍峨、山谷壮丽，这枚树叶就是我初秋出外勤时捡到的。但是，这份美丽的背后是冬天气温低至-40℃、夏天一巴掌拍死十几个蚊子的‘苦’。” 看到林依舒投来敬佩的目光，王晨接着说：“有一年冬天，牧民外出时遇险，我所在的部队迅速前出救援。牧民获救后，看着他们的笑容，听着他们感谢的话语，我感到自己的青春很有价值。我守卫着边疆安宁与人民幸福，心里很踏实。” 王晨的话，在林依舒心中种下了志愿赴边的种子。但如今毕业在即，父母的意见让她心里产生了波动。 听完林依舒的讲述，杨昕媛并没有立即开导她，而是带她来到了心理服务研究中心的迷你“K歌房”。 “我想当风，你别管我，管我去哪儿，我都在找自己的路上……”当电影《抓娃娃》的主题曲《我想当风》的旋律响起，林依舒一边看着MV，一边回忆着电影里的情节，似乎有些理解了父母的心境。 “争吵并不能解决问题，我们可以采用‘家庭系统疗法’，先尝试理解他们，再给他们安全感，最后进行劝服。”回想起刚才杨教员教给自己的沟通方法，林依舒决定给家人写一封信。 “爸妈，我知道你们心疼我，怕我受苦，怕想我却见不到我。请你们放心，现在交通发达，我保证一放假就回去看你们。边防哨所建设得越来越好，不仅能吃上新鲜蔬菜瓜果，而且营区冬暖夏凉……赴边是女儿的志愿，你们对我的支持，就是我最大的幸福。”当心里话跃然纸上时，压在林依舒心头的石块好像也落了地。 为了能让父母信服，林依舒又从网上找到了不少卫国戍边年轻官兵的感人视频，还有反映边关建设发展新面貌的VLOG发给他们。“这是杨教员教我的‘具象化证据说服法’，用这些实证代替我的‘空口白话’，让爸妈眼见为实。”她说。 不久后，杨昕媛收到了林依舒发来的短信——家人已经同意她的选择，她也写好了志愿赴边申请书，准备随时奔赴边疆、追寻梦想。 成长是一场背负爱与梦想的远行。“心中有爱，目光所及皆是美好”“此去星辰大海，愿归来仍是少年”……活动室内，“青春寄语”墙上贴着各种颜色的心形便笺纸，上面写满了学员们滚烫的心声。 心理教员有话说 武警工程大学军事心理学教研室教员杨昕媛：父母的爱，往往因“爱之深”，而“责之切”。父母的经验主义与我们的青春梦想似乎总是存在分歧，爱在“庇护”与“远行”间难以抉择。建议大家别用“我就要这样”“你们懂什么”的“暴力语言”沟通，尝试与父母“角色互演”，直观感受彼此的立场与情绪。用相互理解与真挚的沟通架起一座心灵桥梁，带着他们的爱，勇敢走向属于自己的山海。 （应采访对象要求，文中部分人名为化名）
