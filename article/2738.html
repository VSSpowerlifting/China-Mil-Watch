Listen! The Song of Sentinel Duty Soaring Above the Clouds

听！云端飞扬守哨的歌
PLA Daily (解放军报) 1 August 2026
View original source ↗
About this analysis
Summary
On the eve of Army Day (August 1st), soldiers at the Tibet Military District's Zhannangxie Post (海拔4,655米) collectively wrote and recorded an original song, gathering lyrics from a corridor submission box and incorporating field-recorded ambient sound—patrol footsteps, ice-axe strikes, snowmelt—with musical arrangement by regimental cultural cadre Wang Jiaqi. This is standard political-work content; its value is as a record of how the Tibet Military District frames morale-building and patriotic sentiment for troops at high-altitude border posts, not as evidence of a new policy or operational development. The piece documents the institutional method: channeling the physical hardship of remote garrison duty into collective cultural production as a retention and cohesion tool.
Translation

Listen! The Song of Sentinel Duty Soaring Above the Clouds

■ Song Xiaoli, Chen Weijing

On the eve of "August 1st," officers and soldiers at the Tibet Military District's Zhannangxie Post climb toward a position at an altitude of more than 5,000 meters. Photo by Zuo Yu

At Zhannangxie Post, officers and soldiers sit gathered around the "Heroes' Platform" composing a song. Photo by Zuo Yu

On the eve of "August 1st," at the Tibet Military District's Zhannangxie Post, situated at an altitude of 4,655 meters, frost and wind still cover the ground. In the study room, Political Instructor Pan stood before the blackboard and voiced an idea he had long been turning over: "Let's write a song that belongs to our post."

The soldiers below exchanged glances, their eyes flickering with anticipation. Second-Class Staff Sergeant Zhao Baosheng scratched his head and muttered under his breath: "Write a song? I'm fine shouting slogans with this big voice of mine, but don't even mention writing songs—I can't even sing in tune." That remark seemed to strike a chord with many—for soldiers who garrison the summit of a snow-capped mountain year-round, writing a song, that "romantic thing," seemed far, far removed from their daily life of guarding the border.

Political Instructor Pan did not rush to rally everyone. He walked to the window, his gaze settling on the distant, unbroken line of snow mountains, and after a brief pause said to them all: "Our daily patrols, training, and duty shifts—we record all of that in the sentinel log every day. But have you ever thought about this: those feelings that suddenly drift through your mind during a patrol or while standing watch, the longing and the worry for those you hold dear; the snow lotus flowers blooming in summer, the sound of the wind passing through the post in autumn, the starry sky you gaze up at while standing night watch in winter... these fragments of our time here—shouldn't we preserve them in some way?"

"Why do I propose writing a song? Because up here above the clouds, beyond the rifles and the mission, there is also our sincere love for the motherland." Those words fell like a seed, planted in every heart.

The next day, a cardboard box appeared in the corridor, bearing the words: "Lyrics Collection Box—Write Down Your Sentinel Reflections." Political Instructor Pan was the first to write a line and drop it in: "I look at the motherland through my binoculars, and the motherland looks back at me through the lens."

Snow began to fall over the post without warning. Private First Class Han Jianqiao and his comrades completed a grueling patrol, passing through "Tiger's Mouth"—the most treacherous stretch of cliff face, with a sheer drop of ten thousand zhang on one side and a steep slope on the other where snow could slide down at any moment. When they returned to the post, Han Jianqiao shook the snowflakes from his shoulders, skipped the oxygen and the rest, and walked straight to the box to slip in a folded piece of paper.

During a late-night watch, Private Zi Jie once again gazed up at the stars. At over 4,600 meters above sea level, the night sky above the snow mountains seemed close enough to touch with an outstretched hand. After coming off duty, he wrote by lamplight: "The stars over Zhannangxie are so bright, so bright—every single star represents a heart standing guard." The cook Chilie Duoji's reflection was plain and sincere: "Someone has to watch over the border line, and someone has to watch over the stove."

The slips of paper accumulated, the handwriting ranging from neat to clumsy, some hurried, some careful. Every one of them carried the distinctive breath of Zhannangxie Post.

One weekend evening, everyone sat gathered in the study room. The box was placed in the center. Political Instructor Pan drew out slips at random and read them aloud one by one. The love for the motherland buried deep in the heart, the heroic resolve to hold the cloud-top post, the boundless longing for family far away... line by line, like notes on a staff, they strung together the inner melody of those who keep the watch.

At the most moving passages, the room fell so quiet that only breathing remained; at the most harrowing ones, everyone smiled knowingly; when homesickness came through, some silently turned to look out the window—beyond the snow mountains lay home, lay family.

The lyrics were there, but the melody posed a new problem. As it happened, regimental cultural cadre Wang Jiaqi had come to the post for a research visit and agreed to help them compose the music. He also offered a suggestion: "The sound of snow falling and the sound of patrol footsteps that you hear every day—these are the musical notes of time; put them in the background and they will surely enrich this song."

In the days that followed, every patrol saw someone checking the battery on a voice recorder. The crisp ring of an ice axe striking an ice wall, the heavy breathing on a steep slope, the muffled thud of snow sliding off the roof, the whistle of morning assembly, the sound of a guitar played on weekends—all of it was now carefully captured by the soldiers and made part of the melody.

Political Instructor Pan already had a plan: to let this song be sung forever. "Starting from this 'August 1st,' we'll sing it on the patrol route, we'll sing it when we miss home... let the fragments of our vigil be woven into the melody and become our shared memory of youth."

At dawn, accompanied by the spirited sound of singing, the patrol detachment once again set out in full gear. The door swung open; snow reached the ankles; the white vapor of each breath instantly crystallized into a frost-mist that clung to eyebrows.

Below the post's mountain, snow lotus flowers are blooming again. This song lives in the sound of flowers opening, in the sound of snow falling, in the sound of steadfast footsteps on watch...

Original Chinese
听！云端飞扬守哨的歌 ■宋小理 陈玮靖 “八一”前夕，西藏军区詹娘舍哨所，官兵向着海拔5000多米的点位攀登。左宇 摄 詹娘舍哨所，官兵围坐在“英雄台”上创作歌曲。左宇 摄 “八一”前夕，海拔4655米的西藏军区詹娘舍哨所，依旧遍地风霜。学习室里，潘指导员站在黑板前，说出一个酝酿已久的念头：“写一首属于咱们哨所的歌。” 台下战士你看看我、我看看你，眼神里透出期待的光。二级上士赵宝生挠挠头，小声嘀咕：“写歌？我这大嗓门喊口号还行，别提写歌了，连唱歌都跑调。”这句话仿佛戳中了不少人的心思——对常年驻守雪山之巅的战士来说，写歌这件“浪漫的事”，似乎离他们的戍边日常太远，太远。 潘指导员没有急着鼓动大家。他走到窗前，目光投向远处连绵的雪山，停顿片刻，对大家说：“巡逻、训练和执勤的日常情况，咱们每天记录在守哨日志上。可你们有没有想过——那些巡逻坚守时突然从脑海飘过的感怀，内心的思念与牵挂；那夏天盛开的雪莲花，那秋天吹过哨所的风声，那冬夜站哨时仰望的星空……这些岁月点滴，我们是否应该以某种方式珍藏？” “为什么我提议写一首歌？因为在这云端之上，除了钢枪和任务，还有我们对祖国的真挚情感。”这句话像一颗种子，种在每个人心里。 翌日，走廊里多了一个纸箱，贴着“歌词征集箱——写下你的守哨感言”几个字。潘指导员率先写下一句话投入纸箱：“我在望远镜里看祖国，祖国也从镜头里看着我。” 哨所突然飘雪。上等兵韩剑桥和战友完成一趟艰难的巡逻任务，他们经过“老虎嘴”——那段最险的崖壁，一侧是万丈深渊，另一侧是随时可能落雪的陡坡。回到哨所时，韩剑桥抖抖肩头的雪花，没有吸氧休息，而是走到箱子前，塞进一张纸条。 深夜站哨，列兵字杰又一次仰望繁星。海拔4600多米雪山上的星空，仿佛近得伸手可触。下哨后，他借着灯光写道：“詹娘舍的星星很亮、很亮，每一颗星星都代表一颗守防的心。”炊事员赤列多吉的感言朴素真挚：“有人守望边防线，就要有人守好灶台边。” 纸条越积越多，字迹有工有拙，有的潦草有的端正。每一张，都透着詹娘舍哨所特有的气息。 一个周末的晚上，大家围坐在学习室里。纸箱放在中间，潘指导员随机抽出一张，逐一念给所有人听。那深藏于心海对祖国的爱、坚守云端哨位的壮志豪情、对远方家人的无尽思念……一句一句，像五线谱般串起守哨人的心曲。 读到动情处，屋里静得只剩呼吸；读到惊险处，大家会心一笑；读到想家时，有人默默望向窗外——在雪山的那边，是故乡，是亲人。 词有了，曲又让人犯愁。凑巧的是，团文化骨干王嘉齐来哨所调研，答应帮他们一起谱曲。他还出了个主意：“大家每天听到的雪落声、巡逻脚步声——这些都是岁月的音符，放在背景音里一定会为这首歌增色。” 随后几天，每次巡逻时，都有人检查录音笔的电量。冰镐敲击冰壁的脆响，陡坡上粗重的喘息，积雪从屋顶滑落的闷响，清晨集合的哨音，周末弹奏的吉他声，如今都被战士们精心收录下来，成了旋律的一部分。 潘指导员已有打算：让这首歌永远传唱。“从这个‘八一’开始，咱们巡逻路上唱，想家时唱……让坚守的点滴融入旋律，成为我们共同的青春记忆。” 清晨，伴着意气风发的歌声，巡逻分队又一次整装出发。门推开，雪没脚踝，呼出的白气立刻结成霜雾，挂在眉梢。 哨所山下，又见雪莲花盛开。这首歌，在花开的声音里，在雪落的声音里，在坚守的脚步声里……
