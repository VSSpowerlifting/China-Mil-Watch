Visiting Sentries During the Dog Days | Deep in the Dense Forest: Officers "Nailed" to the Frontmost Position

三伏探哨丨密林深处：干部“钉”在最前沿

About this analysis
Summary
A PLA Daily reporter visited a concealed sentry post (暗哨) exercise at a training ground under the Shenyang Joint Logistics Support Center, where company political instructor Liu Dong'ang led a security detachment through extended prone concealment, OPFOR UAV evasion, and a two-person capture drill in dense forest during peak summer heat. The article documents how a grassroots political officer managed pre-mission reluctance among soldiers by personally occupying the forward-most position—a leadership method the piece frames explicitly through the Northeast Anti-Japanese United Army's Wang Detai as inherited revolutionary tradition. The tactical content (low-altitude UAV threat recognition, noise-discipline capture, pre-planned covert withdrawal routes) is incidental to the article's primary function: recording how the institution expects junior political officers to enforce training standards through personal example rather than exhortation, a recurring theme in PLA political work coverage rather than evidence of a capability shift.
Translation

A Reporter Visits the "Concealed Sentry Post" at a Training Ground of a Unit Under the Shenyang Joint Logistics Support Center——

Deep in the Dense Forest: Officers "Nailed" to the Frontmost Position

■ Jin Jiliang, Man Tianxin; PLA Daily Correspondent Wang Xuechao; Illustration: Fang Han

On a scorching midsummer afternoon, the blazing sun beat down relentlessly. The reporter drove to a training ground of a unit under the Shenyang Joint Logistics Support Center to visit a "concealed sentry post" deep in the dense forest.

Upon reaching the designated location and opening the car door, a wave of steaming, humid air carrying the scent of earth—rising after the rain—hit us in the face. Looking around, the trees on all sides were lush and full-branched, layered so densely that not a breath of wind could pass through. Apart from the occasional chirping of insects and birds, almost no other sound could be heard. Staff Officer Zhang from the unit's headquarters, who accompanied the reporter, said that the troops were conducting battlefield security training deep in the forest, and that the "concealed sentry post" was fully aware of our every move.

Hearing this, the reporter walked deeper into the forest while looking around in all directions, searching for where the "concealed sentry post" might be. Suddenly, two sentries—faces painted with camouflage grease, clad in woodland camouflage—leapt up from the roadside undergrowth.

"Don't move!" A low, forceful shout instantly charged the atmosphere with tension. After identities were verified, the alert sentries quickly turned and disappeared back into the forest.

Led by Staff Officer Zhang, the reporter carefully climbed to a spot beneath a cliff face halfway up the hill. Pushing aside the dense branches, the reporter discovered a captain officer lying in wait at the sentry post, intently observing the direction of the valley through binoculars.

Officers and soldiers conducting concealed reconnaissance. Photo by PLA Daily Correspondent Wang Xuechao.

Staff Officer Zhang introduced him as Liu Dong'ang, the political instructor of a company under the unit. From the very start of the concealment training, he had been "nailed" to the frontmost sentry post. At that moment, beads of sweat the size of soybeans slid down his cheeks, and the camouflage paint had become somewhat smeared from the soaking sweat. Having lain prone for more than an hour continuously, Liu Dong'ang's neck was covered with welts from mosquito and insect bites, yet he kept his eyes fixed unwaveringly on the target area.

For this training exercise, Liu Dong'ang was responsible for leading a security detachment in executing the concealment mission. Before the mission commenced, upon learning that they would have to lie in the jungle for an extended period and endure the trials of intense heat, sun exposure, and insect bites, a few soldiers developed a reluctance to proceed. To ensure the mission was completed successfully, Liu Dong'ang not only organized motivational education sessions on multiple occasions, but also declared on the spot: he would take the lead at the frontmost concealed sentry position.

"What you require soldiers to do, officers must lead by doing it first—that is more persuasive than any amount of mobilization!" said Liu Dong'ang. He added that throughout the vast expanse of black soil where the unit is stationed, the red bloodline of revolutionary forebears has always flowed: Wang Detai, deputy commander-in-chief of the First Route Army and commander of the Second Army of the Northeast Anti-Japanese United Army, was a famously fierce general. Whenever the troops held a position, he would always go to the front line in person, even personally manning a machine gun to sweep fire at the enemy; when charging into battle, he frequently led the assault at the very front, and when ammunition ran out, he would engage the enemy in hand-to-hand combat.

"It was the same in battle as in daily life." Liu Dong'ang told the reporter that at that time, the Second Army spent long periods maneuvering against the enemy deep in the mountains and forests, where food shortages and sleeping outdoors were the norm. Faced with a harsh natural environment and a dangerous struggle, Wang Detai consistently persisted in sharing hardships and facing life and death together with his soldiers.

"In those years, our revolutionary forebears called out 'Watch me' and 'Follow me,' inspiring and rallying officers and soldiers through their actual actions, and cultivating a revolutionary comradeship of shared hardship and shared fate in life and death. Today, we must carry forward the red bloodline, and properly inherit and carry forward the fine traditions forged by our revolutionary forebears with their blood and lives." Looking toward the target area in the valley ahead, Staff Officer Zhang told the reporter that before this training mission commenced, Liu Dong'ang led his soldiers in an on-site reconnaissance to select the location of the "concealed sentry post" and plan concealed withdrawal routes; once training began, he took the lead at the frontmost sentry position, achieving "concealment as steady as a boulder, attack as swift as lightning, and withdrawal without a sound," setting a benchmark for officers and soldiers through his actual actions.

The sentry post before them commanded a wide field of view, overlooking the entire valley. The reporter could not help wanting to stand up for a clearer look, but was pulled back by Liu Dong'ang: "A miniature UAV is flying toward the airspace above us, hugging the treetops—its reconnaissance intent is obvious."

No sooner had he finished speaking than a faint buzzing sound approached from a distance. Liu Dong'ang's gaze locked onto the UAV flickering in and out of view among the treetops. About ten seconds later, the UAV flew off into the distance. Only after the buzzing had completely faded and Liu Dong'ang had confirmed that no follow-on reconnaissance assets were moving in did he lower his voice and tell the reporter: "That was a probing reconnaissance by the OPFOR. Judging from the UAV's flight path, our position has not been exposed—we must continue to lie in wait."

"That UAV was flying so low—why not use electromagnetic countermeasure equipment to capture it?" the reporter asked, watching the UAV gradually disappear.

"The core function of a 'concealed sentry post' is to covertly monitor enemy movements, guard against sudden enemy attacks, and promptly report any abnormal situations. It emphasizes concealment, early warning, and defense. Only when attacked may the sentries fire to raise the alarm, resist on order, or withdraw in an orderly manner." Liu Dong'ang explained: "Under normal circumstances, sentries must maintain concealment and observe calmly while lying in wait. Even if an emergency capture is required, it must be carried out without a sound and without leaving any hidden dangers."

Mid-conversation, the communications terminal in his hand suddenly transmitted a special situation report: "Two 'enemy' personnel in camouflage uniforms carrying reconnaissance equipment have been detected approaching your flank along the valley, at a distance of approximately 500 meters, with no follow-on forces observed. Higher command orders you to seize an opportunity to capture them!"

Upon receiving the order, Liu Dong'ang immediately maneuvered toward the flank slope while issuing low-voiced instructions: "Maintain concealment, do not expose yourselves—wait until the 'enemy' enters the ambush zone, then act on my order." The reporter noted that Liu Dong'ang's movements were both fast and light, causing no visible disturbance to the surrounding shrubbery.

After reaching the slope, Liu Dong'ang quickly concealed himself behind a thicket and raised his binoculars to observe carefully. In the valley ahead, two OPFOR soldiers were advancing cautiously, alternating between covering each other. Suddenly, the two stopped at a point approximately 60 meters from the concealed position; one of them raised binoculars and swept his gaze back and forth in Liu Dong'ang's direction. Liu Dong'ang signaled the sentries to prepare for capture, but did not issue an attack order, continuing instead to keep his eyes fixed on the two "enemies'" every move.

A moment later, the OPFOR soldier lowered his binoculars and signaled his companion to continue advancing. The two quickly moved into the optimal ambush zone of the "concealed sentry post." Liu Dong'ang decisively signaled "move in." The sentries, who had been motionless until that instant, suddenly rose, leveling their muzzles at one of the OPFOR soldiers. At the same moment, Liu Dong'ang also leapt from the flank thicket, pointing his muzzle at the other "enemy."

In a flash, the sentries swiftly subdued both OPFOR soldiers and confiscated their weapons and equipment. The entire capture was executed with complete surprise and clean efficiency.

With the capture complete, Liu Dong'ang picked up the communications terminal and reported the results to the commander. Higher command determined that the "concealed sentry post" was about to be exposed and ordered them to escort the "prisoners" and withdraw covertly along the pre-planned route……

Training concluded, and the after-action review immediately commenced. Inside the tent, the participating officers and soldiers spoke one after another, engaging in heated discussion around the problems exposed during training. Liu Dong'ang carefully recorded the soldiers' remarks, thinking about how to improve subsequent training.

"How did you manage to stay completely motionless during concealment?" Walking out of the tent, the reporter asked one of the participating soldiers. He pointed back at Liu Dong'ang: "The instructor didn't move a muscle—how could we?"

"Rallying" Requires "Leading" First

■ Wang Xuechao

"What you require soldiers to do, officers must lead by doing it first"—this is both the most straightforward principle of troop leadership for a grassroots commander, and a fine tradition forged and refined by our military through the trials of blood and fire. Faced with the many trials of concealment in the dense forest, Instructor Liu Dong'ang took the lead in holding the frontmost position, pushing through physiological limits in a harsh environment and completing the mission successfully, prompting soldiers to hold themselves to the same standard instinctively: "The instructor didn't move a muscle—how could we?"

Northeast Anti-Japanese United Army commander Wang Detai fighting shoulder to shoulder with his soldiers; General Deng Hua going by the alias "Ding Hua" to conduct night patrols along cliffs together with ordinary soldiers…… From the bloody battles of the anti-Japanese battlefields to the charges of field exercises, practice has repeatedly proven that when leading cadres share hardships and face life and death together with ordinary soldiers, actions speak louder than words, and orders are followed without fail.

"Rallying" requires "leading" first. During the dog days of summer, visiting the sentries, the reporter experienced firsthand the authenticity of officers being tempered at the sentry post on the exercise front line, felt the rigor of officers and soldiers training in earnest, and witnessed the fine traditions of our military being carried forward and inherited from one generation of officers and soldiers to the next. The reporter has every reason to believe that, striding forward in the new era and advancing on the new journey, the broad ranks of grassroots officers and soldiers will surely deliver an outstanding answer to the Party and the people through the hard capability to fight and win wars.

Original Chinese
记者探访沈阳联勤保障中心某部演训场“潜伏哨”—— 密林深处：干部“钉”在最前沿 ■金继亮 满天昕 解放军报特约记者 汪学潮 插图：方 汉 盛夏午后，烈日炙烤。记者驱车前往沈阳联勤保障中心某部演训场，探访密林深处的一处“潜伏哨”。 到达指定位置，打开车门，一股雨后蒸腾的湿热气浪裹挟着泥土的气息扑面而来。放眼望去，四周树木枝繁叶茂，层层叠叠、密不透风，除了偶尔传来的虫鸣鸟叫，几乎听不到其他声响。同行的该部机关张干事告诉记者，部队正在密林深处组织战场警戒训练，我们的一举一动，“潜伏哨”都了如指掌。 闻言，记者一边往山林深处走一边四处张望，寻找“潜伏哨”在哪里。突然，两名脸涂油彩、一身林地伪装的哨兵，从路边丛林中一跃而起。 “不许动！”一声低沉有力的喝止，让现场气氛瞬间紧张起来。验明身份后，警惕的哨兵迅速转身，重新隐入山林。 在张干事的带领下，记者小心翼翼地爬到半山腰一处崖壁下。拨开浓密的树枝，记者发现，一名上尉军官潜伏在哨位上，正聚精会神地用望远镜观察山谷方向。 官兵潜伏侦察。解放军报特约记者 汪学潮摄 张干事介绍，这是该部某连指导员刘东昂。从潜伏训练一开始，他就“钉”在最前沿的哨位上。此刻，豆大的汗珠顺着他的脸颊滑落，油彩在汗水的浸渍下已有些模糊。连续趴了一个多小时，刘东昂的脖颈处被蚊虫叮咬出不少包，但他仍目不转睛地紧盯目标区域。 此次训练，刘东昂负责带领警卫分队执行潜伏任务。任务展开前，得知要长时间趴在丛林中，面临高温暴晒、蚊虫叮咬等考验，个别战士产生了畏难情绪。为确保任务圆满完成，刘东昂不仅多次组织动员教育，还当场表态：带头潜伏哨位最前沿。 “要求战士做到的，干部带头先做到，比任何动员更有说服力！”刘东昂说，部队驻守的这片广袤黑土地上，一直流淌着革命先辈的红色血脉—— 东北抗日联军第一路军副总指挥、第二军军长王德泰是出了名的“虎将”。部队坚守阵地时，他总是亲临前线，甚至亲自架起机枪向敌人扫射；冲锋陷阵时，他经常带队冲在最前面，子弹打光了就同敌人贴身肉搏。 “打仗如此，生活中也是如此。”刘东昂告诉记者，那时，第二军长期在深山老林同敌人周旋，缺粮断炊、野外露宿是常态。面对恶劣的自然环境和危险的斗争形势，王德泰始终坚持与战士同甘共苦、同生共死。 “当年，革命先辈叫响‘看我的’‘跟我上’，以实际行动激励官兵、带动官兵，培养出甘苦与共、生死与共的革命情谊。今天，我们必须赓续红色血脉，把革命先辈用鲜血和生命凝结成的优良传统传承好、发扬好。”看着前方山谷的目标区域，张干事告诉记者，此次训练任务展开前，刘东昂带着战士一起实地勘察，选定“潜伏哨”位置，规划隐蔽撤离路线；训练展开后，他带头潜伏在前沿哨位，做到“潜伏稳如磐石、出击快如闪电、撤离悄无声息”，以实际行动为官兵立起标杆。 眼前的哨位视野开阔，能俯瞰整个山谷。记者忍不住想站起身来看得更清楚些，却被刘东昂一把拉住：“一架微型无人机正贴着树冠朝我们上空飞来，侦察意图明显。” 话音刚落，一阵细微的嗡鸣声由远及近，刘东昂的目光死死盯着树冠之间若隐若现的无人机。十几秒后，无人机向远处飞去，直到嗡鸣声彻底消失，刘东昂确认没有后续侦察力量跟进，才压低声音告诉记者：“这是蓝军的试探性侦察。根据无人机飞行路径判断，我们的位置没有暴露，还要继续潜伏。” “这架无人机飞得这么低，为啥不用电磁反制设备抓捕？”看着逐渐消失的无人机，记者疑惑地问道。 “‘潜伏哨’的核心职能是隐蔽监视敌人行动、防范敌人突然袭击、发现异常情况及时报告，强调的是隐蔽性、预警性和防御性，只有遭遇袭击时才能开枪报警、依令阻击或有序撤回。”刘东昂解释道，“一般情况下，哨兵潜伏时必须保持隐蔽、冷静观察。即使遇到紧急情况需要抓捕，也要确保悄无声息、不留隐患。” 说话间，他手里的通信终端突然传来特情通报：“发现两名身着伪装服、携带侦察装备的‘敌人’，正沿山谷向你侧翼靠近，距离约500米，未发现后续兵力。上级命令你们择机抓捕！” 接到指令，刘东昂立即向侧翼缓坡机动，同时低声下达指令：“保持隐蔽，不要暴露，等‘敌人’进入伏击范围后听令行动。”记者注意到，刘东昂的动作又快又轻，没有造成灌木丛明显晃动。 到达缓坡后，刘东昂迅速隐蔽于灌木丛后，举起望远镜仔细观察，只见前方山谷间，两名蓝军士兵正小心翼翼地交替掩护前进。突然，两人在距潜伏点位约60米处停了下来，其中一人举起望远镜，朝刘东昂这边来回扫视。刘东昂示意哨兵做好抓捕准备，但没有下达攻击命令，而是继续紧盯两名“敌人”的一举一动。 片刻后，那名蓝军战士放下望远镜，示意同伴继续前进，两人很快便进入“潜伏哨”的最佳伏击区域。刘东昂果断示意“动手”，刚才还纹丝不动的哨兵猛然起身，将枪口对准其中一名蓝军士兵。同一瞬间，刘东昂也从侧翼灌木丛中跃起，将枪口指向另一名“敌人”。 电光石火间，哨兵迅速将两名蓝军士兵制伏，收缴其武器装备，整个抓捕过程出其不意、干净利落。 抓捕完成，刘东昂拿起通信终端，向指挥员汇报战果。上级判定“潜伏哨”即将暴露，命令他们押解“俘虏”，沿预设路线隐蔽撤回…… 训练结束，复盘随即展开。帐篷内，参训官兵你一言我一语，围绕训练中暴露出的问题展开激烈讨论。刘东昂认真记录官兵发言，思考如何改进后续训练。 “你们是怎么做到潜伏时纹丝不动的？”走出帐篷，记者问一名参训战士。他指了指身后的刘东昂：“指导员都纹丝不动，我们哪能动？” “带动”先要“带头” ■汪学潮 “要求战士做到的，干部带头先做到”，这既是一名基层指挥员最朴素的带兵之道，更是我军在血火考验中凝炼升华形成的优良传统。面对密林潜伏的种种考验，指导员刘东昂带头坚守最前沿，在恶劣环境中挑战生理极限、圆满完成任务，让战士们自觉对标：“指导员都纹丝不动，我们哪能动？” 东北抗联将领王德泰与战士并肩作战、邓华将军化名“丁华”与士兵一起夜巡悬崖……从抗日战场的浴血奋战，到沙场演兵的冲锋陷阵，实践一再证明，领导干部与普通一兵甘苦与共、生死与共，就能起到行胜于言的作用，就能产生令行禁止的效果。 “带动”先要“带头”。三伏探哨，记者在演训一线体味了干部哨位淬火的真，感受了官兵真训实练的严，更见证了我军优良传统在一代代官兵身上的赓续传承。记者有理由相信，阔步新时代，奋进新征程，广大基层官兵必将以能打仗、打胜仗的过硬本领，向党和人民交出一份优异答卷。
