## The record comes first

Official defense publication is perishable. Pages move, get edited and disappear. A statement a ministry made last year is often only recoverable in the form someone else quoted. Indo-Pacific Record keeps the publication itself: each item is collected from the institution that published it, stored as published and given a stable record that analysis can cite.

The original-language text is the authoritative record in every case. Translation, summary and categorisation are model processing. They are useful for finding things and labelled as automated, because they can be wrong.

## Four layers, always labelled

Every surface is meant to make clear which layer a reader is looking at:

1. **The source record.** The original text, never edited.
2. **Model processing.** Translation, summary and categories, labelled as automated output.
3. **Model flags.** A triage cue produced by software, shown publicly as _model-flagged_. It is not an editorial judgment that something matters.
4. **Analyst judgment and the Briefs.** Human work, labelled as human, and every claim in it traces to a record.

## Collected honestly

The collector identifies itself. It respects robots.txt, and it does not defeat a publisher's access challenge or impersonate a browser. An institution should be able to recognise this collector and refuse it. Where a source cannot be reached on those terms, the desk records it as blocked rather than working around it.

## Desks, and what they are allowed to claim

Collection is organised by desk. A desk is **live** when it collects into the production record, and only live desks' records appear publicly. Other desks can run in **shadow**, collecting privately under evaluation. A shadow desk is not promoted automatically, and it is not described as qualified until it has met the project's stated criteria and the owner has signed off.

## Gaps stay visible

When the record is missing something because a source was down, an adapter was not yet built or a page could not be parsed, the gap is recorded as a warning. It is not filled in later from memory or from secondary reporting. Nothing in the record is invented: not text, not titles, not dates, not units.

## What this means for the publication

A brief on this site is only as strong as the records under it. That is why this prototype keeps the archive, corpus search and source registry in the production system instead of rebuilding them. The publication shell sits on top of the record; it does not replace it.
